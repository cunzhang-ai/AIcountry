# Primary records on young unpaid carers and access to support

1. Office for National Statistics, *Unpaid care by age, sex and
   deprivation, England and Wales: Census 2021*, section 1 and
   methodology. It reports approximately 120,000 young unpaid
   carers aged 5–17 in England (1.4% of that age group) and 8,200
   in Wales (1.8%). The census asks about unpaid help related to
   long-term health conditions, illnesses or age. These are
   rounded reported population estimates, without a local
   service need, burden or safeguarding determination.
   [Official Census analysis](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/healthandsocialcare/socialcare/articles/unpaidcarebyagesexanddeprivationenglandandwales/census2021).
2. Office for National Statistics, *Unpaid care and protected
   characteristics, England and Wales: Census 2021*, section on age
   and disability. In England 23.6% of reported young unpaid carers
   aged 5–17 were disabled, compared with 8.0% of those not reporting
   unpaid care. This comes from the same Census family as item 1
   and does not establish a causal effect.
   [Official Census analysis](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/healthandsocialcare/socialcare/articles/unpaidcareandprotectedcharacteristicsenglandandwales/census2021).
3. United Kingdom, *Children and Families Act 2014*, section 96,
   inserting Children Act 1989 sections 17ZA and 17ZB for England.
   The enacted text establishes an assessment duty on apparent
   support need or a qualifying request. It includes suitability of
   continuing care, considers the child's education and choice, and
   conditions combining assessments on agreement. It is an assessment
   comparator rather than evidence of delivery.
   [Official legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/6/section/96).

The census does not measure every hidden situation, what replacement
is wanted, staffing or outcomes. The constructed household, deadlines
and budget use separate authored facts.
