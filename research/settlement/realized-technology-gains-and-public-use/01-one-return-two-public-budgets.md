# One realized return and two public budgets

Constructed in cash units and minutes. A public investment produces
12 units of settled contractual cash, held by the legal public owner.
Of this balance, three units meet existing contractual and winding-up
costs and two remain reserved for a credible later claim. Another
four units of returned capital are held in a separately restricted
account. A valuation gain of five has produced no cash. After existing
costs and the reserve, seven units can lawfully be put to a vote.
Assume the later claim uses the full two units and no repeat technology
receipt is available next period.

The stated purpose is to restore a public assistance route at two sites
where access is poor. In the comparison, the existing ordinary budget
pays six units for six hours of staffed receiving across the two sites
in each modelled operating period. That paid capacity works. Two residents in the
fixture each spend 50 minutes on an affected journey. Under an
independent operational assessment in the stipulated case, using
nearer existing sites with equal staffed hours would reduce each
trip to 20 minutes without reducing eligibility or opening hours
for other residents. Commissioning, accessibility work and handover
would cost four units. The receiving owners can fund later operation
from the existing six-hour ordinary budget. No productivity saving
is assumed.

| Choice under the same seven-unit limit | Immediate effect | Position at the next budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-off equal payment to both residents | Pay three each and one in administration. Under stipulated existing rules, one resident's other payment falls by one. Net household gains are two and three. | No further cash is promised. The two-site travel defect remains. |
| Repair access and retain the remaining balance | Spend four on commissioning and retain three. Preserve both ordinary service budgets. | Verify the same staffed hours and each 20-minute trip. Three remain available under ordinary future authority. |
| Retain all seven pending a better supported choice | Make no new commitment. | Seven remain subject to future authority. The travel defect remains. |

Select repair in this case because the access defect is established
and qualified ongoing operation is already funded. The choice benefits
people using those sites and gives no immediate spendable cash to
either resident. The one-off cash rival gives residents direct control
over use. If basic cash hardship is the binding problem and the repair
cannot be delivered, compare that rival on its net effects. If the
authorized staffing, maintenance or accessibility test fails, retain
the balance or vote a feasible alternative.

Suppose the Assembly instead removes three units from the next ordinary
service budget and uses the remaining three from the return to buy
back the same staffing. The repair may still improve travel in that
period. The three-unit backfill has funded **zero additional hours**
and leaves a three-unit gap in the following budget if the cut
continues. Publish the travel gain separately from the source switch.
A pledge of three units of cash to both residents in the next period
also has no source on these facts. A late cost above the two-unit
reserve remains an actual liability if lawfully established, with
a separately disclosed funding gap.
