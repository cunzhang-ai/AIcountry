# One common issue and separate receipts

Design361 · Constructed comparison under
[RCR01–RCR06](../../country/C08/13/06-representative-claims-and-delivered-redress.md).

The same seller applies a disputed fee to residents in three localities.
Ten people opt into a civil proceeding with accessible assistance. Three
other people receive notice and do not join. The common issue is whether
the fee term was enforceable in the declared period. The claimants still
need individual proof of payment, earlier credits and any separate harm.
A claimant with an urgent individual request retains the local court route
under the existing rules.

Ten separate hearings could each examine the same fee term. A blanket
settlement covering all 13 could be faster but would waive rights of
people who did not agree to representation. The selected court hears
the shared issue once, with a meaningful defense and actual local
receiving. Separate losses and the three outsiders remain outside.
If the proposed lead court cannot accept the work, keep existing
cases and their dates rather than dismissing them to a paper forum.

The case stipulates that the court establishes 2 units of the same
recoverable loss per participant. Two participants already received
full same-loss reimbursement. One other participant has only been
promised reimbursement. The group therefore needs 16 further units,
not 14. An approved plan has 16 in lawful custody, with administration
funded separately. It sends 2 to each
of the eight remaining participants. Two payments are returned.

| Measure after first distribution | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total established same-loss amounts | 20 |
| Actually received before distribution | 4 |
| New amounts sent | 16 |
| New amounts actually received | 12 |
| Amount returned and still held for lawful delivery | 4 |
| Total actually received | 16 |
| Same-loss amount still owed | 4 |

The two returned payments keep their original due dates and protected
alternative contact. The payment promised earlier does not earn a
credit until actually received. If that separate payment later settles,
the executor verifies it against the same obligation before retrying a
returned transfer and keeps any distinct remedy intact. Court records
do not publish private payment details.

If one timely enrolled claim is excluded by a narrowing order, an
original filing period with 15 days left at enrollment gives way to the proposed
60-day protected period after usable notice. A claim with 75 days
left retains 75. This rule concerns the defined timely enrolled
claim, not the filing periods of outsiders or a previously expired
claim. The exercise proves neither legal authority nor actual
custody, claimant response, court capacity or recovery.
