# One accessible vehicle, two time-critical trips

*Design290 · Constructed comparison for a shared local loss.*

A disruption closes the ordinary routes. E needs an accessible
evacuation to an accepting protected site by minute 20 to prevent
grave bodily harm. V needs an accessible ride to reach a lawful
polling place by minute 28. The exercise assumes that this ride is
the remaining lawful access path for these voters. A vehicle is
useful only with the needed equipment, a paid and qualified crew,
a safe route, accepted riders and receiving. Polling access does
not require divulging a person's vote.

| Choice | Due cash units | Result under the declared closure |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| One scheduled common standby | 4 | E reaches safety at minute 16. The van is ready again at minute 30. V's access deadline is missed. |
| Common standby and an independent civic ride | 7 | E reaches safety at 16. The separate accepted ride reaches V's polling place at 24. |
| Two independently staffed dedicated public routes | 8 | Both trips work. Civic access is less exposed to a shared dispatch failure or the loss of the standby contract. |

The second route's added three units cover its independent vehicle,
route, staffing and acceptance for the declared window. The
reference local payer can commit seven units by dispatch. A weaker
payer has four and cannot count the second route. Reusing one
road, sole driver or dispatch key would also invalidate the
independence claim regardless of funding. Two fleet names and
one compatible wheelchair position still supply only one position.

The shared van is ordinarily the lower-cost option when a verified
sequence is available. In the alternate route case it is ready
after E at minute 20 and can complete V at minute 27. If there is
no acceptable destination for E, the available van cannot be
credited with averting that danger. Dispatch may still carry out
an independently feasible lawful trip and must keep seeking the
missing safe receiving for E.

A later report of a third urgent trip due at minute 34 needs
another independently usable dispatch or a funded and qualified
turnaround. Returning the initial van to its depot at minute 30
does not prove the third trip can arrive by 34. In the declared
case its required nine-minute trip finishes too late. No option
claims second-call coverage without a separately checked route
and source. Track actual missed access and remedies under the
original duties. The times and costs are illustrative. They are
neither clinical margins nor universal evacuation parameters.
