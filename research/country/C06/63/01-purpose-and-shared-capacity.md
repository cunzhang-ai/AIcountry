# IV01–IV04. Choose and schedule public investments

**IV01 — Start with the service and the assets already serving it.** Each
public asset owner maintains a proportionate condition and service inventory,
with the legal owner, responsible operator, actual use, safety findings,
repair and renewal periods, dependencies and known gaps. Record separately
what is inspected and what is estimated. Publish actionable priorities with
sensitive site and security details protected. Use the actual DB/FB budget
to state what will be maintained, what can safely wait, what risk remains
and how affected people receive lawful continuity. Prioritize existing due
services and material preventable loss. A repair backlog, without diagnosis,
does not order every asset replaced. Maintenance funding and past replacement
funds are reconciled once. An appropriation for another purpose cannot be
quietly diverted to maintenance, and maintenance balances are not free cash.

**IV02 — Compare meaningful ways of meeting a need before making it expensive
to change course.** The responsible service owner proposes the public purpose,
affected users, credible retain and repair, and a conceptually different route
where feasible. Compare safety, access, whole-life cost and timing, ecological
and land burdens, resilience and distribution. Give space to people who lose
a usable route or bear construction or closure. For substantial or hard-to-reverse
commitments, secure independent, technically qualified challenge before
selecting a preferred route and before committing its major delivery stage.
Disclose common controllers and original evidence. Publish reasons for
departing from advice. Permit the minimum lawful work needed to compare options
through a bounded preparatory authorization that leaves the right to build
undecided and keeps alternative sites and contracts practically available. Authorize ordinary repairs
through existing programme powers and handle urgent safety under sector law.

**IV03 — Put concurrent projects in one resource calendar.** At each annual
budget and before a material new commitment, reconcile the proposal with
already authorized work and continuing services. The asset/service owners
identify scarce qualified crews, dated access or shutdown windows, land,
materials and infrastructure, receiving staff and actual payer/currency.
Treasury or the competent local budget office reconciles funding and interfaces
across authorities. Count a shared capacity reservation only once and show
who would lose it if reassigned. Keep linked projects together for risk and
choice, including work under related entities and contracts. A stage can use
separate authority if it has independent safe public value or is a bounded
preparation step. If independent projects use the same scarce pool, they can
remain separate decisions with visible mutually exclusive schedules. Do not
aggregate unrelated small work merely because an owner is common. Authority,
signed supply, usable capacity and forecast procurement stay distinct.

**IV04 — Elect a purpose, gross exposure and limits before binding delivery.**
The Assembly or competent elected local body authorizes a material investment
or specifies the limits of prior delegated authority. Fix purpose, functional
scope, binding ceiling, available payment sources and dates, paid operating
owner, contingency control and return triggers. Distinguish build cash,
future operation, renewal, cancellation and contingent exposure. Include
leases, guarantees, concessions, public enterprises and joint financing on
comparable service, timing and risk terms. An expected cost cannot authorize
an uncapped obligation. A national co-financier can set lawful grant terms and
refuse its own future participation. Local elected powers, other parties' consent
and applicable territory, land and environment decisions remain separate.
Protected opposition, amendments and published elected reasons remain available.
