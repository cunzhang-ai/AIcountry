# Decision record: finance and reach in a replaceable democracy

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 49 research selection; not enacted law

## Baseline and bounded question

The [design-48 predecessor](predecessor-design-48/README.md) preserves the
working country before this choice. C03 already protects equal votes,
party/civic-list entry, public campaign support and opposition access;
C05 and C11 identify employer, market and media gatekeeping. But the
reference country has not assigned a complete, timely chain for funding a
new challenger, disclosing organized finance and paid reach, preventing
incumbent diversion of public resources, and enforcing repair before
voting. C03's candidate rule itself lists campaign money as an unresolved
dependency. The question is how political competition remains practical
when money, public assets, platforms and regulators may share controllers.

This authored research decision follows the proposed
[working framework](../FRAMEWORK.md) and
[decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md). It confers no legal power,
affected-party consent or independent reviewer status. The affected
public includes voters, new and incumbent contestants, independent
candidates, small donors at risk, public workers, media users, journalists,
contractors and people dependent on services that must not be disrupted.
None has participated in a real AIcountry election. Values in conflict
include expression, political equality, association, privacy, anti-capture,
usable opposition, fair defense and continuity of public information.

## Competing diagnoses and packages

Unequal contest may come from legal exclusion, insufficient funds,
concentrated private sponsorship, incumbent control of public assets,
dominant distribution channels, intimidation, or a finance regulator that
targets critics. These have different remedies. Original campaign journeys,
ad purchases and reach, beneficial-control records, public procurement,
worker accounts, small-donor safety, service communications and actual
court execution would discriminate among them. More reporting alone does
not prove independence or equal opportunity.

| Package | Possible mechanism and benefit | Material failure or objection |
|---|---|---|
| Retain general disclosure and courts | Light administration and broad speech freedom | Late reports and after-election damages may leave a lost contest irreversible |
| Strict public-only campaign finance and official equal airtime | Could reduce wealthy private influence and ease audit | Can entrench approved parties, constrict association, and give the state a distribution monopoly |
| Mostly private financing with disclosure | Keeps plural funding and less public spending | Wealth, contractors and platforms can dominate access; disclosure may arrive too late or expose vulnerable donors |
| **Selected: baseline public access, bounded plural finance, timely material disclosure and separate pre-vote remedies** | New contestants can enter; organized control and public-resource misuse are visible; courts can restore opportunity | Formula or regulator may favor incumbents, privacy may be lost, and a remedy may still arrive too late |
| Decentralized civic media and local support only | May reduce a central gate and reflect communities | Unequal local wealth and platforms can reproduce domination; national elections still need common contest rules |

The selected [C03 route](../country/C03/07-political-finance-and-campaign-access.md)
keeps lawmaking, ballot administration, finance inquiry, public media,
Treasury and courts separate. The [C06 account](../country/C06/11-democratic-contest-finance.md)
funds support and scrutiny as real obligations; the
[C13 incumbent-capture exercise](../country/C13/14-incumbent-campaign-capture.md)
tests the full chain under a hostile cabinet. The prior C03/04 rule on
civic and material independence remains; this selection gives one of its
highest-risk mechanisms a dedicated operating route.

## Source support and limits

[International IDEA's Political Finance Database](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/political-finance-database)
distinguishes private-income limits, public funding, spending, reporting,
oversight and sanctions, while warning that formal rules do not establish
compliance. Its [political-finance design
tool](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/tools/political-finance-design-tool)
shows alternative choices and tradeoffs; no one formula follows from it.
The [OSCE/ODIHR election-campaign
handbook](https://odihr.osce.org/election_campaign_observation_handbook)
addresses the wider political environment. The
[Venice Commission/OSCE guidelines on administrative-resource
misuse](https://www.coe.int/en/web/venice-commission/-/opinion-778)
identify the incumbency problem, and the
[Joint Guidelines on Political Party Regulation](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/?pdf=CDL-AD%282020%29032-e)
are a comparator for expression and party rights. These are official
comparative sources, not an AIcountry constitution, applicable treaty
inventory, observed election or proof that this particular split works.
No affected public, campaign accounts or current political event in an
AIcountry territory exists to confirm the mechanism.

## Authority, shared commitments and transition

Prospective elected law sets eligibility, funding formula, source and
spending rules, reporting thresholds, privacy, remedies and the effective
date. C03's election administrator decides ballot qualification under
that law; C06 Treasury pays; a separately governed finance office
investigates material flows; C11 protects source evidence and plural media;
C05 and C04 handle gatekeeping and misuse within their own powers; courts
grant serious sanctions and urgent rights relief. The finance office
cannot certify the winner, rule on ideological truth or suspend a candidate
alone. The Assembly may revise future rules, but incumbents cannot change
their current contest by an emergency classification or delayed budget.

The independence chain includes who nominates and removes election and
finance officials, controls the Treasury release, pays court and legal-aid
staff, holds donor and ad records, operates the broadcaster and platform,
and obeys an order. Shared party patrons, vendors or banks can capture
all nominally separate offices. C13 tests a noncooperating incumbent,
late payment, hidden sponsor, public-data misuse and overreaching
regulator together. Protected opposition and independent journalism
must be practically able to find evidence and introduce an alternative
before votes are cast; post-election publication is not enough.

An adopter must reconcile actual constitutional speech and association
rights, election and party law, public procurement and media duties,
privacy, employment, finance, court and emergency rules. Existing party
assets, lawful contracts, donor confidentiality, pending complaints and
valid ballot rights continue until competent law assigns successors.
Public access needs staffed venues, translation, postal/offline routes,
funded media time and real cash by due dates. A rule cannot be called
ready merely because an account exists. Prospective changes must preserve
applicable treaty and judicial obligations and fair process for accused
contestants.

Objections remain live. A baseline public grant may fund fringe candidates
or consume scarce resources; a high qualification gate can suppress them.
Disclosure may expose dissidents; a low threshold can conceal control.
Ad archives can become surveillance of lawful speech; digital rules can
become a censorship channel. Rapid courts may lack staff or overrule
voters through weak evidence. These are not reasons to declare an
unaccountable finance office benign; they are conditions for an actual
public's selection and test. A positive turnout or vote count alone
cannot prove challengers had a fair chance.

**Evidence:** comparative institutional guidance and authored design, no
AIcountry original election or affected participation. **Research
selection:** baseline usable campaign access, bounded plural finance,
timely material transparency and court-executed pre-vote repair.
**Legal authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** no electorate,
law, formula, regulator, Treasury cash, media capacity or tested order.
**Observed outcomes:** none; practical replacement is not yet proven.

The next bounded test would follow a real jurisdiction's new contestant
through nomination, fundraising, public support, major ad purchase,
state-resource complaint and pre-poll remedy, then compare public-only,
mostly private and this mixed package. It requires a lawful data and
participant-protection plan, real staff and an approved time/cost limit;
none is authorized here. Reopen the selection if new entrants remain
unviable, donor privacy fails, state assets are diverted without timely
repair, a regulator suppresses opponents or another package offers
better actual public control and expression.
