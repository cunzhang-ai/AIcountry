# Operating water, food and electricity as essential systems

3 October 2026 · Bounded national-design analysis for C04/C06–C07/C09–C10 · Research recommendation, not enacted law

## The unresolved national choice

AIcountry's [design 8](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md) promises effective access to safe
water and food and resilient electricity, while respecting ecological limits.
It now identifies elected authority for shared land and resource allocations.
That still leaves a different question: **which accountable institutions actually
operate, maintain, finance and recover the systems between elections and during
shortage?** A public right, spatial plan, ownership label or reserve target cannot
itself supply safe water, food or electricity. Conversely, an engineer or
contractor who keeps a network running cannot acquire authority to choose whose
rights disappear. The [status register](../DESIGN-STATUS.md) does not yet select
one compatible operating arrangement for these three systems.

The physical arrangements differ. Water needs protection from source to use,
quality surveillance and catchment allocation. Electricity needs a common grid,
continuous balancing and dispatch, as well as generation and distribution. Food
comes from farms, processors, traders, transport and retailers; access depends
on income and local availability as well as total stocks. A single ownership
answer across all three would confuse those differences. The selected proposal
therefore holds **democratic public stewardship and an enforceable essential
floor constant**, while using licensed utilities for water and grid bottlenecks
and plural production/distribution for food. It rejects an automatic state
monopoly or purely contractual hope that a failed provider will be replaced.

## Competing operating models

| Model | Decision and delivery mechanism | Why it appeals | Distinctive failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Integrated public utility** | Public authorities own and run water, electricity production/grid and substantial food procurement/distribution; budget and workforce are directly public. | Clear public control over core assets and continuity investment; less reliance on profit incentives. | An incumbent can capture every supply channel; public ownership does not provide expertise, replacement parts, cash or independent inspection. Taking farms, utilities or distributors into state control would create large transitional property and management duties. |
| **Regulated private/cooperative plural provision** | Private, cooperative and public operators compete where feasible; regulators set safety, access and price duties; subsidies support low-income users. | Allows local and entrepreneurial capability and avoids a compulsory public employer. | Physical water pipes and grids cannot be duplicated costlessly; a dominant network can hold customers captive. An exit clause and a regulator without a staffed replacement do not keep water or power flowing. Prices alone may exclude people from food. |
| **Decentralized commons and mutual supply** | Community water, local generation, gardens and mutual distribution rely on local governance and shared assets. | Can build local knowledge, resilience and agency, including off-grid capacity. | Geography, weather, capital and labor differ; a self-reliant locality can leave remote, poor or displaced people without a national floor. Shared catchments, grid stability, food safety and imports still need wider rules. |
| **Selected conditional reference: public stewardship with plural operation and residual capacity** | Assembly enacts rights, ecological and scarcity rules and funding; specialist public authorities secure each essential floor. Water and electricity networks operate under public-service licenses or public ownership with the same duties; generation and food production remain plural. Independent inspectors, funded alternatives and lawful step-in duties cover failure. | Fits different physical systems while preserving an accountable public guarantee and local choice of capable operators. | Requires difficult supervision, fiscal capacity and genuine alternate labor/assets. A public fallback can become a patronage monopoly. No evidence establishes it as superior in an actual jurisdiction. |

This is a **functional choice**, not a license to privatize or nationalize by
default. Local elected government may operate a water utility or choose a
cooperative/regulated concession under national rights; an electricity network
may be publicly owned or license-bound, but dispatch, access and reliability
cannot depend on the energy seller's preference. Food producers may be public,
cooperative or private, while safety, nutrition protection and emergency
procurement remain public duties. Actual asset ownership would be decided
lawfully with affected workers, users and owners; no existing title changes by
publishing this note.

## The proposed authority and delivery chain

1. **The Assembly sets the legal floor and priorities.** It enacts essential
   access, water quality and food safety duties; permitted ecological withdrawals
   and emissions within the C04/C09 territorial process; service continuity,
   tariff and assistance rules; procurement and import authority; and a
   preadopted scarcity schedule. Cabinet proposes a consolidated budget showing
   due cash, capital, repair staff, network capacity, stocks, logistics and
   foreign-currency needs. Treasury pays only lawful commitments. New scarcity
   priorities, taxes or permanent reallocation require elected authority;
   neither a regulator, utility, AI model nor emergency incident commander
   legislates them through a technical forecast.
2. **Each system has one answerable public steward and named operators.** A
   national water authority holds the guarantee and catchment-account duty while
   elected local authorities arrange distribution through publicly run or
   licensed utilities. A separate public-health water inspector tests quality
   and can issue reviewable safety orders. An electricity authority contracts
   reliable capacity and names a grid/dispatch operator with nondiscriminatory
   access and a maintenance plan; an independent safety/reliability regulator
   supervises it. A food-security authority secures physical availability and
   emergency in-kind delivery, monitors supply risk, and procures/rotates
   reserves under law. The existing social-protection authority remains
   answerable for any cash nutrition assistance, with local services arranging
   last-mile access. A separate safety authority inspects food and orders
   lawful recalls. These are **proposed roles**, not
   claims that offices or crews exist. One officer can coordinate incidents but
   may not finally judge its own operator's safety failure.
3. **Licensed operators owe performance through the asset life.** A license or
   public operating charter states quality, coverage, non-discrimination,
   metering/correction, repair time, renewal, spare parts, workforce safety,
   emergency stores, record access, outage communication and compensation/remedy
   obligations. Public enterprises face the same technical inspection and
   accounts as private licensees. The responsible steward verifies actual
   service, not a signed supply contract. Regulators publish reasons, inspect
   protected operational evidence and can order lawful correction, subject to
   independent court review; they cannot quietly rewrite an entitlement or
   acquire a political veto over a lawful Assembly budget.
4. **Affordability and maintenance are one budget choice.** Water and electricity
   tariff decisions disclose operating cost, renewal and resilience investment,
   environmental harm, cross-subsidy, household effects and any explicit public
   transfer. Law protects an essential quantity or service against termination
   solely for inability to pay, with a fair process for disputed bills and
   continued lawful debt rules. Food assistance tracks an adequate, local
   nutritious basket and can deliver money or safe food according to actual
   market availability; cash alone fails when shelves or transport fail. Tariff
   relief, remote service equalization, safety laboratories, rotating reserves
   and backup power are appropriated and staffed. A utility cannot finance an
   apparent low tariff by deferring pipe or grid renewal, nor can an agency book
   the same fuel, worker or reserve against several promises.
5. **Cross-system dependencies are audited together.** Pumping, treatment,
   refrigeration and transport depend on power; generation can depend on water,
   fuel and imported components; food production uses land, water, labor and
   trade. The Cabinet maintains one public interdependence and failure register
   from the three stewards, utilities, local authorities and suppliers. It
   reconciles physical inputs, maintenance, inventory age, contract dates,
   foreign payments, ecological limits and service priorities with the C06
   account and C04/C09 spatial decisions. This register is evidence for
   Assembly choice, not a new super-ministry with a power to ration livelihoods.
   Operators disclose common owners and critical subproviders; confidential
   details receive protected review, not immunity from oversight.

## Scarcity, rights and operational failure

The enacted scarcity schedule should protect life-sustaining household water
and food access, critical care and sanitation, and the ecological conditions
needed to sustain future supply. It must state how uncertainty, necessary
production, geographic burdens and rights are considered rather than asserting
that a fixed universal ranking solves every drought or blackout. A qualified
operator may implement immediate, **temporary** isolation of unsafe water,
load shedding or a recall within enacted limits, recording the cause, persons
affected, alternative safe supply, notice and review. It may not invent permanent
water rights, censor rationing complaints or confer favored access on its owner.
An individual can seek urgent delivery and correction; affected producers and
localities can contest disproportionate restrictions. Ecological minimums and
quality cannot be bought away by a tariff, while scientifically assessed limits
do not make an engineer the final arbiter of social distribution. If existing
rights and physical supply cannot all be met, the shortfall remains public and
requires an Assembly response under C06, not a fictitious declaration of success.

Suppose a power-system failure stops a water-treatment plant and cold storage,
while imported treatment chemicals are delayed. One utility's “backup” generator
may already be committed to a hospital, and a food reserve may be inaccessible
without electricity or transport. Under the selected model, the grid operator
performs lawful stabilization and gives cause and restoration estimates; the
water steward arranges independently tested safe distribution; the food
authority activates local deliveries and lawful reserves; local governments
provide accessible intake. Sector inspectors check safety, and the national
guarantee authorities pay under preauthorized continuity funds. Cabinet exposes
the common shortage and Treasury's cash path; the Assembly decides any new
priority or funding. If equipment, trained crews or foreign currency are
unavailable, a court can preserve claims and order lawful action but cannot
conjure them. This is an **authored failure exercise**, not a verified contingency
plan or evidence that those actors could operate together.

Before a utility concession expires or a provider fails, the steward must
have a lawful receiver/step-in route with protected customer records, asset
condition, essential spare parts, staff-transfer or recruitment rules, payment
authority and a tested alternate operator. The operator retains notice, fair
hearing and compensation where law requires; intervention targets service
continuity, not political control over workers or users. Public ownership can
also fail: a public operator requires the same incident reporting, independent
inspection and a succession route independent of the incumbent minister.
Restoration, remediation, decommissioning, existing contracts, permits and
accrued household claims remain assigned to named successors. A regulatory
order without access to staff, assets and lawful funds is not delivery.

Local councils should choose the form and siting of ordinary services where
effects are local, with funded national minimums and independent appeals.
Shared catchments, power networks and ecological limits follow the selected
[territorial-authority route](../2026-10-operating-settlement/territorial-authority.md):
interlocal preparation and an Assembly-reserved decision for material unresolved
allocation, not local unanimity or regulator choice. Neighboring communities
outside a utility district can challenge material effects and seek remedies;
cross-border imports and exports follow C10's lawful commitment and harm
account. Local public generation, storage, water systems and food commons can
strengthen resilience, but receive equivalent safety, ecology and rights checks
and do not extinguish the national floor for those unable to join them.

## Sources, objections and qualified result

The [WHO's updated drinking-water framework](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-06-2026-who-unveils-third-addendum-to-guidelines-for-drinking-water-quality)
describes health-based targets, supplier water-safety planning and independent
surveillance. The [IEA's electricity-grid assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-grids-and-secure-energy-transitions)
examines grid investment and reliability as bottlenecks in changing energy
systems. [FAO's agrifood-resilience report](https://www.fao.org/agrifood-economics/publications/detail/en/c/1457037/)
distinguishes production and supply-chain robustness from physical and economic
food access. The [OECD Water Governance Principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/water-governance/the-oecd-principles-on-water-governance-and-implementation-strategy.html)
cover water functions across ownership forms and scales. These primary
institutional sources were inspected on 3 October 2026. They identify relevant
failure modes and governance questions, not empirical performance of AIcountry
or endorsement of this public-stewardship settlement.

The public-stewardship model could cost more, deter investment through uncertain
license terms or become captured by an incumbent that appoints stewards,
regulators and fallback managers. Clear statutes, bounded licenses, protected
funding and removal rules, operator due process, opposition scrutiny, accessible
data and court enforcement can reduce those risks, but no written arrangement
proves independence. A full public utility might perform better if it has real
capability and plural checks; decentralized commons may be especially valuable
where local institutions can manage resources fairly. Those alternatives should
not be dismissed by a generic efficiency claim. The selected model should be
reopened if actual provider or ecological evidence shows its fallback is
unaffordable, its regulators cannot act independently, its tariffs cause
deprivation, or it shifts scarcity onto politically weak communities.

**Main design changes if integrated:** (1) C09 should name separate public
water, electricity and food stewards, safety/reliability reviewers and operating
channels under one enacted scarcity and interdependence account; ownership may
vary, but each network or reserve has an accountable operator and funded
replacement. (2) C06/C07 should connect essential affordability and no unsafe
disconnection to lifecycle renewal, explicit tariff/subsidy incidence, reserve
rotation, imported inputs and the standing guarantee/shortfall schedule.
These fit C04's local and territorial authority and C10's external commitment
rules; no new central body should acquire a technical veto. The status register
would need an operating-systems row and a legal-migration dependency. This note
does not itself revise the main design or any original sector act.

stewards show one service-area asset, operator, supplier and payment map that

fuel, reserves or cash? The output should include affected-user and worker
participation, legal duties, loss-bearing, independent inspection, costs and a
witnessed restoration exercise. Without those observations, do not certify
reliability or invent a national stockpile target.
