# C13. A failure across sanitation, cyber and the border

*Part of [C13 lived experience](../C13-lived-experience.md); integrated design 55 research exercise, not an observed event.*

A wastewater plant loses control-system access during a flood. Its backup
power and the clinic's cold store need the same fuel delivery. Replacement
control parts and medicines reach the border, but a data outage prevents
routine customs release. The sanitation steward must provide safe interim
containment and warn downstream users; the plant operator cannot announce
safe treatment merely because the control screen is back. The cyber
coordinator shares technical evidence and recovery help without taking over
health, court or border powers. Customs records each hold and gives a lawful
manual receipt; a protection claim by a carrier's passenger remains separate.

The local incident lead identifies safe immediate acts under prior law. C06
counts the one generator, driver, fuel stock, inspection worker and payment
route only once. If an enacted priority does not resolve the conflict, the
competent elected body makes the new material choice with rights review,
public reasons and visible unmet claims. C09's alternate communications
route must reach residents without compulsory online identity. C08 courts
preserve evidence and remedies for contamination, unlawful holds and lost
benefits; C14's public account compares delivered sanitation, medicines,
legal notice and repair rather than completed digital tickets.

This exercise cannot prove readiness. In a real place it needs original asset
maps, lawful orders, staff, physical transit time, backup integrity,
independent inspection, affected people and a funded receiver. The test fails
if one office can hide a release, if the manual route has no real operator, or
if a lawful remedy arrives after an irreversible injury.
