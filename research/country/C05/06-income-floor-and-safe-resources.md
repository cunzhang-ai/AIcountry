The candidate ordinary top-up is `max(0, N - O - A - r × max(0, E - d))`:
recognized needs **N**; received nonwork resources **O**; a lawful usable-asset
draw **A**; after-tax/contribution earnings **E**; one personal earnings
disregard **d**; and withdrawal rate **r**. The research reference selects
`r = 0.5`, `d = 0.2 × personal basket`, and a 20% disregard of received
contributory pension up to 20% of that basket. Rates are hypotheses,
subject to prospective elected revision and joint distribution/capacity review.
Actual basket values must come from the legislated goods/access method;
fictional CU values are not selected as adequate national amounts.

The [household and fiscal reference](../../settlement/income-floor/README.md)
compares individual assessment, pooled assessment and a universal base,
including actual support, private control, work costs and benefits that
disappear abruptly. It preserves the old service account and reconciles
the old transfer components under explicit inclusion/legacy branches.
The illustrated package is **not financed** by the earlier 100-million
transfer line. Cash, qualified delivery and improved lives remain unverified.

The current selection is the legal architecture and explicit accounting,
not an asserted optimal rate, launch date or welfare victory. Equal
political standing and the ability to leave dependence are reasons for
individual access; administrative burden, costly targeting and universal
or safely pooled alternatives remain material objections. A real comparison
must include people who do not claim, safe privacy and actual services.
