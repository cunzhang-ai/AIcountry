# A current receipt margin is not a pension-financing plan

*Part of [retirement comparison](README.md); alternative histories, not a transition forecast.*

Hold the old reference 15,000 older people, worker receipts 228 million,
actually payable bond interest 5 and restricted cash 25. Change only the
declared past earnings/credit history to show why future claims matter.
The basic layer at old adult basket 1,000 monthly is 180 million from general
revenue, already distinct from insurance. Nothing here changes the immutable
old 45-million schedule or classifies actual inherited claims.

| Alternative earned history for every recipient | Annual earned payments | Receipts less earned payment **before omissions** | Day-1 extra cash to pay and retain four-month normal floor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Old scheduled 250 monthly | 45 | 188 | 0 |
| Forty years at 30,000 | 225 | 8 | 68.75 |
| Twenty work/twenty eligible care years | 168.75 | 64.25 | 45.3125 |
| Forty years above the 60,000 accrual ceiling | 450 | −217 | 162.50 |

The 225 case owes 18.75 on day 1: cash 25 can pay it, leaving 6.25 against
a normal floor 75. For payments 450, day-1 due 37.5 exceeds cash by 12.5
before any new source, even though a bond appears in the asset account.
The extra-cash column funds both due payment and the continuing normal
buffer; it is not another annual expense or an actual lender commitment.
Shock use may draw reserves under prior law with replenishment; a normal
gap is not permission to withhold an already due pension.

Administration, survivor contingencies, actual new care credits, omitted
legacy promises, collection failures, reserve access, floor/tax interaction
and full liability values remain **unknown**. They are excluded from the
shown partial differences, never declared zero or fully funded. The examples
are not age cohorts, demographic forecasts or a complete national budget.
They show neither 188 nor 8 as resources available for general spending.

| Whole architecture | Reason for present disposition and retained objection |
|---|---|
| Retain vague two-layer promise | Preserves direction but leaves rights and political discretion unclear; replace with explicit candidate clauses, not an affordability claim |
| Ongoing pooled earnings layer plus individual basic | Retained research choice: protects short/interrupted careers and insures lifetime payment without requiring private investment skill. It preserves earnings hierarchy, uses compulsory contributions and remains exposed to production, demographic and political risk |
| Stronger universal basic with no new earnings layer | Genuine rival: simpler and may better fit less wage-centered futures. Requires explicit public choice on distribution, tax/service burdens and preserved earned/contractual rights; a closed-legacy cash saving cannot enact it |
| Mandatory personal funded accounts | Genuine rival: visible individual assets with investment, fee, inflation and longevity risk; transition must pay old claims while new contributions buy assets. No market return or asset account alone guarantees a person's lifetime income |

The [ILO's official explanation](https://www.ilo.org/frequently-asked-questions-social-security-minimum-standards-convention)
distinguishes contributory and tax-financed mechanisms and stresses legal,
financing and governance protections. It supplies comparison context, not
endorsement, compliance certification or evidence for this design's numbers.
No public has selected these hypotheses. No design establishes better lives
without actual qualified assessment, affected participation and outcomes.
