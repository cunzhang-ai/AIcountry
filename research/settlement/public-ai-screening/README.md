# Screening public cases without making a second eligibility system

9 November 2026 · Design210 · Country operation

Select [DP17–DP24](../../country/C11/09/05-screening-queues-and-actual-decisions.md).
The state can gain information from automated screening while keeping practical
access under the same law as the formal decision. The sector that owns a queue
also owns foreseeable displacement from that queue. Use a lawful ordinary route
as the comparator and the protected alternative. This selection allows a
relevant lead to direct available checking time under limits. It rejects use
of a raw flag as sufficient authority for additional proof or a hold on due
protection.

| Arrangement | Principal advantage | Failure and choice |
|---|---|---|
| Competent ordinary routing and qualified review | A simpler baseline with directly intelligible priorities | It can miss useful signals or preserve manual discrimination. Retain it where additional screening lacks a qualified net benefit |
| Model ranking with human decision at the end | More promising referrals may reach reviewers | Additional waiting, proof and displacement already occur before that decision. Reject it as a sufficient safeguard |
| Prohibit all probabilistic referral in covered services | Removes a class of hidden grounds and vendor dependence | Loses some beneficial leads and leaves opaque manual routes. Retain this for a particular use if its material effects cannot be bounded or reviewed |
| Protected ordinary route with scoped screening | Uses better leads in capacity that the sector can afford without displacing due work | Depends on actual staffing, valid evidence and independent remedy. Choose conditionally with fallback and a decision to remove failed uses |

separates findings about model referrals from the timing effects of review.
