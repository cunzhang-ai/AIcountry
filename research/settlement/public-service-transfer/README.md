| Arrangement | Potential benefit | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Keep authority and delivery together in the current body | Preserves familiar local relationships and known work | Need, capacity or useful reach may extend beyond its finances and scope. |
| Transfer legal and fiscal ownership and retain capable delivery by lawful agreement, selected where justified | Makes the new owner answerable while retaining actual people and service | Partial contracts may hide costs and leave responsibility for individual decisions unclear. |
| Transfer ownership and deliver directly | Can join personnel, funding and technical command | Moving employers, premises or systems can interrupt services and reduce local access. |
| Finite paid overlap with separate accepting responsibilities | Gives a valid route when an operational transition is incomplete | Costs money and staff. A shared team can fail both sides, and temporary operation needs an enforceable end. |

Choose transfer by task. Continue or repair a lawful current operation when
it works. Compare direct and delegated receiving at the same legal standard,
with each cost and deadline exposed. Use a separately paid overlap only where
it protects real service. The legal date belongs to law. Completion belongs
to observed service and lawful payment. Keep both visible.
