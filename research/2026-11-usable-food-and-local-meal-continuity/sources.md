# Evidence for usable food and local meal continuity

| Primary source | Observation | Use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [U.S. Department of Homeland Security Office of Inspector General, OIG-20-76](https://www.oig.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/assets/2020-09/OIG-20-76-Sep20.pdf) | The audit of the federal commodity response to Hurricanes Irma and Maria distinguishes shipments arriving in Puerto Rico from later delivery to staging areas or distribution points. It describes proof-of-delivery failures and reports that municipalities experienced damaged or expired meals and damaged packaging. | Count safe accepted receipt and investigate the missing last stage. Port arrival and stock figures are limited evidence of local usefulness. The audit does not track every resident's meals. |
| [U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-19-526](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-19-526) | In its 2017 hurricane response review, GAO found some food and shelter needs unmet and gaps in how agreements described available services. The [full report, figure 5](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-19-526.pdf) documents staffed school-kitchen production in Humacao as well as shelf-stable food boxes prepared for distribution. | Compare actual local preparation and direct distribution with their dependencies and paid operators. Neither example establishes relative cost or adequate diets. |

Both investigations include the same disaster family and are
partly overlapping evidence. The [worked comparison](../settlement/usable-food/README.md)
adds authored costs, need, food suitability and outage assumptions.
It establishes no nutrition standard, adopted procedure, actual
capacity, affected consent or national outcome.
