# Security and coercive power in the country design

1 October 2026 · Supporting analysis for C03, C04, C08, C10 and C14 · Research proposal, not adopted law

## The consequential gap

The integrated design protects people against many *individual* abuses: criminal
legality, independent defense, limited police force, outside-command investigation,
short emergency powers and civilian control. It does not yet allocate the country's
*whole coercive apparatus*. A government could obey the police chapter while moving
the same activity to an intelligence unit, military support mission, private security
contractor, border authority, prison operator or emergency command. Conversely, a
local police service captured by an armed or wealthy group may decline to protect
victims; a blanket national takeover can become the next form of domination. This is
an architectural gap, not merely a missing offense or response-time target.

The question for the main design is: **how can the country maintain enough lawful
protective capacity to stop violence while preventing any elected executive, local
patron, security service or contractor from using that capacity to decide who may
organize, govern or live safely?** The answer must still work during an election
handover, a local protection failure and a real external emergency. Rights in text
do not settle command, resources, weapons, records or compliance.

## Values, constraints and rival diagnoses

Protection from assault, coercive domestic control, organized violence and invasion
is a public duty. So are liberty, political equality, fair process and protection
against official violence. These values can conflict in immediate cases. The
material constraints are trained personnel, safe shelter, equipment, courts,
defense counsel, independent investigators, communications, cash and command
continuity. A court order without an accepting operator or an independent case
file is an uncompleted remedy.

Three diagnoses compete. One says the main risk is *insufficient response*: victims
remain exposed because no competent person takes responsibility. Another says it is
*concentrated command*: a cabinet or local patron can redirect armed personnel,
surveillance and detention against rivals. A third says it is *boundary evasion*:
strong police rules are ineffective when equivalent coercion migrates into military,
intelligence, procurement and private security. All may be true in different
places; no national crime, trust or staffing evidence establishes their relative
weight for a real jurisdiction. The proposed architecture targets the third while
requiring checks for the first two.

## Coherent institutional alternatives

| Package | Protective mechanism | Principal failure and transition cost |
|---|---|---|
| A. Retain the present broad civilian-control language and implement the detailed justice candidate | Fewer constitutional changes; concentrate on funded police, courts and victim services | Leaves the boundary between agencies and contractors contestable; a new executive can move suppression into an unregulated channel. The source emergency conflict still needs reconciliation. |
| B. Put all armed and intelligence functions under one national cabinet chain | Clear incident command and rapid national mobilization; easier equalization across localities | A single captured command can seize election records, silence opposition and defeat courts. Local protection knowledge and independent prosecution are weakened. Reorganizing personnel and assets is expensive and contentious. |
| C. Give each locality autonomous police and rely on national courts | Local consent and multiple power centers may resist national executive capture | Local elites can control protection and evidence; cross-border violence and a locality that refuses a court order have no credible national response. Duplicated specialist capacity and fiscal inequality remain. |
| D. Separate functions under a common enacted coercion charter, with local civilian protection, narrow national specialist and rights-protection competence, external defense, and independent case review | No office may switch legal standards by changing the agency or contractor; national protection is possible where local failure threatens rights, without permanent blanket takeover | Boundaries can cause delay or jurisdiction fights; a nominally independent national specialist service can be captured too. Requires funded duplicate *critical* capacity, interoperable records with privacy, lawful mutual aid, training and outside-command execution. |

**Recommendation for research selection: D, with a narrow retention option.** Keep A
if a real jurisdiction demonstrates that its existing allocation already closes
the evasion routes and delivers protection. D is not an instruction to multiply
armed agencies. Its value is a legally bounded division of roles and a tested route
for transfer when one part fails. It should not be combined with B's unrestricted
cabinet command or C's immunity for local authorities.

## Proposed national-design correction

1. **Apply one rights floor to all state coercion.** An enacted law, not an
   operational label, grants each power to search, surveil, detain, use force,
   restrict movement, freeze access to essential resources or compel disclosure.
   The law identifies its purpose, trigger, authorizer, operator, duration, record,
   independent review and remedy. Different powers require different grounds;
   this is no general emergency license. A public register of *legal powers and
   controllers* makes the architecture visible, while sensitive case records remain
   protected. Software updates, procurement terms and classification rules cannot
   create powers. Existing [justice provisions](../2026-09-everyday-justice/candidate-provisions.md)
   remain the detailed candidate for ordinary police procedure and force.
2. **Separate civilian protection, prosecution, intelligence and external defense.**
   Local civilian services deliver ordinary protection under national rights,
   training and funding guarantees. A national civilian capability handles legally
   defined cross-boundary crime, serious official abuse and a narrowly reviewable
   rights-protection response when a locality will not protect people. Prosecutors
   decide individual charges independently; courts decide guilt and legality;
   public defense has its own clients and resources. Intelligence can collect only
   under specific lawful purposes and external review; it has no charging, political
   eligibility or routine public-order command. The defense force has an external
   security remit. Disaster logistics may support civilians under a named civilian
   incident lead but do not confer police or political powers. If any temporary
   military law-enforcement support is legally allowed, it needs exceptional grounds,
   training, the same applicable human-rights limits and prompt independent review.
   This is consistent with the UN Human Rights Committee's [peaceful-assembly
   guidance](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=abco5XpN%2FDh62Z6HBsD%2BLXNKDCzCGvX%2B8r7d54AdcXbLIA9IZN98GoAKE54TbGqxxa%2FiSsYFfYx4FxBSr%2F5Dww%3D%3D),
   which treats military policing of assemblies as exceptional rather than ordinary.
3. **Do not outsource sovereign force.** Contractors may supply equipment, safe
   transport or other bounded services under enforceable terms; a contract must not
   confer public detention, protest-control, intelligence-targeting or lethal-force
   authority. Private guards retain only powers any person or a specifically
   enacted, reviewable civil-security law permits. A ministry cannot evade legal
   limits by calling a private armed group a vendor. Licensing and beneficial-control
   review must protect against private intimidation without criminalizing ordinary
   association, lawful self-protection or criticism.
4. **Keep operational command accountable without politicizing individual cases.**
   Elected ministers set lawful security policy and answer to the Assembly. Named
   professional commanders control particular operations within statute; they do
   not choose political winners. Neither a minister nor an opposition committee
   directs a named person's prosecution, acquittal or protected witness disclosure.
   A security oversight route needs protected opposition access to enough classified
   information to test legality, confidentiality duties, independent technical
   capacity and a way to challenge withheld access. It cannot publicly expose
   ongoing operations or private testimony. Security procurement, weapon custody,
   payroll, communications and data access must be assessed for shared control with
   the cabinet and its vendors; different office titles alone do not separate power.
5. **Investigate protectors outside their command and make relief executable.**
   Death, serious injury, torture, disappearance and major surveillance abuse require
   prompt preservation of evidence, independent investigation and prosecution
   decisions, protected witnesses and medical access, fair process for accused
   officials, and remedies that record actual delivery. The [Minnesota Protocol](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Publications/MinnesotaProtocol.pdf)
   provides an official comparison standard for investigating potentially unlawful
   deaths; it does not certify this candidate or determine its institutional form.
   An outside investigator needs independent legal access to records and scenes,
   staffing, forensic capability, cash and a lawful executor of judicial orders.
   Mere referral to another office inside the same command is insufficient.
6. **Protect people without making criminal punishment the only safety tool.**
   Accessible medical care, safe housing, conflict prevention, child protection,
   disaster rescue and income continuity operate on their own lawful grounds.
   Serious violence still receives fair investigation and, where proved, accountable
   punishment. Local failure triggers a scoped national protection duty and funding
   response, with reasons and court review; it does not automatically abolish local
   self-government. Both a victim and an accused person can challenge the part of
   the response that affects them. The response must have actual staff, transport,
   shelter and independent intake, not only a referral or AI classification.
7. **Bind external force and handover to democratic authority.** The cabinet may
   act immediately within preexisting law against an imminent attack, but sustained
   deployments, defense commitments, major security assistance and their fiscal
   exposure require Assembly authorization and review. Force abroad must be assessed
   against applicable international law, including the [UN Charter](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text);
   a domestic vote alone cannot legalize it internationally. The detailed war-powers
   trigger, classified disclosure, treaty interaction and command succession need
   lawful drafting in a real setting. No hypothetical defense guarantee is promised.
   At a lawful change of government, command, personnel records, armories, intelligence
   authorizations and communications credentials transfer to constitutional offices,
   with separately controlled custody and tested recovery. Neither a commander nor
   a vendor validates the election result.

The [UN Code of Conduct for Law Enforcement Officials](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Promotional-materials-soft/Code%20of%20Conduct%20for%20law%20enforcement%20officials-KH.pdf)
explicitly covers officials exercising police powers and stresses lawful conduct and
reporting of violations. The [Basic Principles on the Use of Force and Firearms](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/ProfessionalInterest/firearms.pdf)
provide standards for necessity, minimization and lethal force. These are comparison
sources, not observed proof that package D works or legal authority for a proposed
AIcountry agency. The existing everyday-justice study already uses the force source;
reusing it here does not create a second independent evidence family.

## Two whole-country failure traces

**A defeated cabinet manufactures an internal threat.** It calls an opposition
assembly a security emergency, orders military logistics to block access, hires
private guards at a payment gateway and asks intelligence to identify organizers.
Under D, ordinary assembly protection and criminal thresholds remain applicable;
logistics, procurement and intelligence labels do not grant police powers. Courts,
opposition reviewers, independent investigators and civil servants have defined
routes to obtain and act on evidence. The emergency still expires under the existing
democratic candidate; no new successor is inferred from delay. **Failure point:**
if cabinet, commanders, payment operator, record custodian and court enforcement
are jointly captured, paper separation does not stop repression. The design must
test actual custody, refusal channels, alternate communications and compliance.
It must not answer capture by inventing an autonomous AI veto or rival party militia.

**A locality abandons residents facing organized violence.** A local police chief
refuses reports and the elected council blocks shelter access. A national civilian
rights-protection competence can preserve reports, supply emergency safety and seek
court authority for a targeted intervention. Prosecutorial independence and local
due process prevent the cabinet from using this as a pretext to dissolve a hostile
council. **Failure point:** a court order without national field capacity or shelter
does not rescue anyone; a standing national force without independent triggers can
become an occupation. Compare actual staff, acceptance and delivery, and the ability
of affected residents to contest both local abandonment and national overreach.

These are authored cases, not trials or probability estimates. They should be
compared against actual risks before selecting commanders, budgets or thresholds.

## Transition, objections and decision status

The correction would alter C04's national/local allocation, C08's ordinary justice
chain, C10's security and defense settlement, C03's handover and C14's founding
conditions. The source security Article 1–6, criminal procedure, migration/treaty
Article 5, constitution's emergency clauses, budget, procurement, data and local
government schedules are affected consumers. A single summary amendment cannot
silently replace them. Existing cases, detention clocks, victim protection, staff
rights, pensions, contracts, evidence and valid treaty duties continue until a
lawful transition rule changes them. An existing abusive command cannot simply
inherit the new offices without review; a blanket purge is also incompatible with
individual due process and may destroy protective capacity. A legitimate transition
would need individual conflict and misconduct assessment, independent appeal,
evidence preservation and a funded overlap plan. Alleged historic abuse is not
cleared by a new institutional name.

The strongest objections remain live. Functional separation may slow urgent action;
therefore narrow, preauthorized mutual aid with post-action review is necessary.
National intervention may intimidate local communities; therefore it needs a
specific rights-protection trigger, limited scope, independent review and a return
path. Extra oversight may divert money from shelters, courts and prevention; the
budget must compare these uses in one account. Security secrecy may defeat review;
reviewers need protected evidence access, not an assertion that all files can be
public. Disarming private coercion while public forces are captured can leave people
unprotected; the public protection route must be operational before broad claims of
readiness. Legitimate self-defense and emergency rescue remain possible under law.

The next bounded design action is to decide whether the main reference country
selects this **coercion-boundary architecture** and, if so, insert the core allocation
and anti-evasion rule into C03/C04/C08/C10 and record its unresolved legal and
capacity dependencies in `DESIGN-STATUS.md`. That choice is for the integrated design
review, not this supporting study. A real jurisdiction would then need a current-law
inventory of *every* coercive actor and a shared cash/personnel/asset map before
drafting exact mandates. Revisit the recommendation if such an inventory shows a
less divided architecture protects people and democratic replacement better, or if
the proposed national protective power is itself a likely path to capture.
