# Can a grid serve people through both an outage and a market failure?

*Design193 · Comparison for [EG01–EG12](../../country/C09/19-electricity-service-and-grid-access.md).*

## Evidence that changes the design

The joint FERC and NERC staff investigation of the February 2021 cold
event found that ERCOT had to shed up to 20,000 MW of firm load. The report
describes weather-related generation failures, fuel shortages, limits on
rotation and common dependencies between gas facilities and the electricity
they needed. Its findings make a firm capacity label inadequate evidence
without the actual upstream power, usable fuel and network path. The
number describes peak ordered load shedding, rather than energy unserved
or a probability for the future country.
[Staff report](https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2021-11/Cold%20Weather%20Report_%20November%202021.pdf)

The Ofgem 2025 decision supplies a real case of moving a connection queue
from historical order toward evidence of project readiness and strategic
need. It supports a genuine alternative to holding firm capacity with
applications alone. Its strategic criteria and treatment of existing
contracted projects belong to that legal regime. They do not establish
the country design's classes or observed consumer benefits. The decision
excludes demand connecting at distribution and treats covered demand-only
projects as meeting the need criterion. EG's essential access classes are
an independent design choice.
[Ofgem decision](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/decision/decision-connections-reform-package-tm04)

Directive (EU) 2024/1711 adds provisions on flexible connections, default
supply and disconnection protections. This illustrates distinct service
and network responsibilities. The design uses its own local/national
authority, existing C07 essential floor and applicable ordinary review.
The directive does not supply actual transposition, staff or source for
the candidate. [Electricity directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32024L1711)

## One connection decision at a constrained node

This is an authored scalar comparison. A node can deliver 10 MW in
the stated ordinary period and 8 MW during a specified four-hour
constraint. Existing firm demand is 7 MW. A new essential task requests
1 MW firm. An optional workload requests 2 MW and can accept interruption
under a tested limited contract. Assume those loads are simultaneous and
separately controllable. This tests connection promises, without supplying
frequency, voltage, dispatch, feeder-outage or demand estimates.

| Path | Normal availability | Four-hour constraint | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promise both additions as firm | 10 MW demand at 10 MW capacity | 10 MW promised against 8 MW. The 2 MW gap lasts four hours. | Unsupported firm promise. Do not issue it on this source. |
| Admit only the 1 MW essential connection | 8 MW firm with up to 2 MW unused | 8 MW firm against 8 MW. Optional work stays off the node. | Viable within the scalar assumptions. Preserve as a rival where interruption or recovery is unsafe. |
| Admit 1 MW firm and up to 2 MW voluntary flexible | 8 MW firm plus up to 2 MW flexible | Serve the 8 MW firm. Interrupt 2 MW optional load, or 8 MWh relative to its stated requested profile. | Selected connection form where consent, measurement, control and useful recovery are verified. |

The example makes no spare recovery capacity at 10 MW when both
normal tranches are fully used. An 8 MWh backlog requires a later period
with actual space, or a workload able to forgo it. Contract limits
still govern how long and how often it may be curtailed. A full feeder
failure removes this path for all its loads. Protect the water, care,
communication and election tasks through qualified alternatives in their
own scopes. Two suppliers using that same failed feeder do not provide
two sources. This is different from the prior PS10 system adequacy case
and is never added to it as a national forecast.

## Compound cases and remaining tradeoffs

- **Cold outage and seller failure coincide.** The system and distribution
  operators handle physical service and the elected restoration schedule.
  The default supplier accepts continuing accounts, cash obligations and
  hedge exposure under its own authority. Pay only from usable contracted
  security and authorized public sources. No consumer debt follows a person
  from another account. Physical power, an accepting default supplier and
  proof of lawful billing each need their own remedy if they fail.
- **A protected and an optional load share a switch.** The distribution
  operator documents what can actually be isolated and rotated. If a
  single switch prevents separation, a declaration cannot create it.
  Before the shortage price sectionalizing equipment, a downstream backup
  or another lawfully accepted way of protecting the critical task. Report
  who still loses power. Ordinary PS/CP/RX scope and poll remedies apply.
- **A household is cut off by a prepaid meter while the grid is healthy.**
  The law treats this as an access and billing failure. The receiving
  body activates actual provisional protection and ordinary remedy. A
  healthy grid reading or recalculated bill is insufficient until safe
  essential service resumes.

A repaired existing arrangement can use stronger safety, fair queue
and service duties without changing ownership. An integrated public utility
could reduce buyer/supplier coordination and lower retailer-failure
exposure, while concentrating control and requiring lawful financed
transfer. A more market-led option can offer flexible contracts and price
signals, with real entry and investment benefits. It still needs an
essential floor, funded fallback and political decisions over shared
infrastructure and unequal outage exposure. The selected separation
preserves plural ownership and limits dispatch capture, with material
costs in oversight, contracting and actual independent backup.
