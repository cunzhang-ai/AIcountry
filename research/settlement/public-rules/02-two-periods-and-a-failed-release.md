# Two legal periods, one incorrect release

*Authored counterexample. This is an abstract rule and fictional
data for checking design behavior. It specifies no actual benefit,
entitlement, payment, legal advice or software deployment.*

## Separate the dates and facts

Assume a hypothetical periodic credit starts at 100 units.
Subtract verified income above the allowance for that period,
with a floor of zero. Period 1 covers days 0–9 and has an allowance
of 20. An amendment published on day 8 raises it to 40 for period 2
starting on day 10. It has no earlier effect. A decision or correction
on day 12 must still apply 20 to period 1. Only verified period
income can decide this credit. A total for both periods is
inadmissible as the sole evidence for either.

A release on day 12 erroneously uses the current 40 in both
periods and divides each person's two-period total of 100 evenly.
It outputs 90 in each period for all three people. The release
can be perfectly reproducible and still be unlawful under the
stated rule.

| Person | Verified period 1 / period 2 income | Lawful period 1 / period 2 amount | Actual flawed release |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 90 / 10 | 30 / 100 | 90 / 90 |
| B | 10 / 90 | 100 / 50 | 90 / 90 |
| C | Unknown / unknown | Requires qualified evidence and review | 90 / 90 |

Each person has the same recorded aggregate income. C has no
accepted period-specific evidence. The competent human reviewer
must apply the real burden of proof, assistance and any applicable
interim protection. Neither the incorrect output nor a new default
of zero income can establish C's lawful payments. A separate
existing entitlement is preserved where it applies.

## Correct individuals and keep the distinct legal processes

For A the released result is 60 higher in period 1 and 10 lower
in period 2. For B it is 10 lower in period 1 and 40 higher in
period 2. Under the stated proof, before any separate lawful
recovery decision, the two additional dues total 20 and the two
apparent excesses total 100. A net overpayment figure of 80 for
these two people would hide both shortfalls and say nothing about
C's still unsupported periods. Pay verified additional dues from
the lawful source and review any recovery on its own grounds.
Actual further money reaches A and B only when paid and received.

A rollback to the old allowance in both periods would reproduce
the earlier rule but underpay B by 20 in period 2. Run the
accepted ordinary route with the right law and facts for both
periods. The corrected processing and the first issuance of a
remedy do not reset the original receipt, due time or appeal.

The affected service owner scopes the failed release against all
cases that used the two mappings, including silent cases. It
preserves original inputs and release records, isolates the faulty
behavior, and keeps competent case review and previously owed
unaffected payments working. Escalate missing reviewers or funds
through the existing duty and budget routes. A batch repair does
not create legal authority to reopen a final case or a substitute
for protected individual evidence.
