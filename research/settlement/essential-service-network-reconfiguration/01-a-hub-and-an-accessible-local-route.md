# A service hub and an accessible local route

This constructed comparison tests [SNR01–SNR08](../../country/C04/08/07-essential-service-network-reconfiguration.md).
A district considers moving booked nursing follow-ups to a larger hub.
Each booked person needs one qualified visit in their existing service window.
In the first window six people can reach the hub independently and four need an
accessible return journey or a staffed local visit. In the second window
independent demand is eight and local-dependent demand stays at four.
Other services and their assigned staff and cash are already reserved.
The authority can spend at most 14 settled units for the covered work in
each window. All numbers are illustrative.

| Plan | Qualified capacity and access | Window 1 receipt and cost | Window 2 receipt and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retain and repair | Existing accessible route with 12 qualified places each window | 10 of 10 at 14 | 12 of 12 at 14 |
| Consolidate at the hub | 12 qualified places each window, with two accessible return journeys in window 1 and one in window 2 | 8 of 10 at 11 | 9 of 12 at 10 |
| Shared hub and retained local sessions | Eight qualified hub places and four qualified local places each window, with first-window paid overlap | 10 of 10 at 14 | 12 of 12 at 11 |

A qualified slot means compatible staff, time, safe space and actual patient
acceptance. The example assumes no safe substitution outside each window.
Hub-only cost includes 9 for clinical operation and 1 for each accepted
accessible journey. The hybrid uses 6 for hub work, 5 for local work, and 3
for an independently staffed first-window standby slot, including activation. Both affordable protected plans use the same opening
cash. No asset proceeds fund either. The result favors the hybrid on these
assumptions and keeps the local sessions as part of the actual funded network.

If a fifth person needs the local route in the second window, the hybrid
reaches only 12 of 13. The retained network also has only 12 places. Preserve
due claims and the existing access route, stop any further loss of capacity,
and seek additional qualified receiving by the deadline. A balance of three
units in the hybrid budget supplies no extra qualified slot by itself.

A different failure removes one qualified local place in the first window.
The standby budget preserves one independently staffed local slot at an existing
site, without drawing on the affected staff or the other reserved services.
If the standby accepts and serves the remaining person in useful time, all 10
receive a visit once at a cost of 14. If standby shares the failure or misses
the deadline, the hybrid reaches only nine. The irreversible step
stays on hold in either failed-transfer branch pending a new qualified result.
The case counts patients once, separates the continuing route from authorization
to dispose of a site, and keeps any missed visit visible.

The comparison checks availability and dated costs under stated assumptions.
It supplies no observed clinical outcomes, travel capacity, allocation of
additional staff, or real-world finance. [Reproduction](verify-network-reconfiguration.py).
