# Design430. Make ordinary small claims affordable and collectible

## Decision and baseline

Design429 is the pinned predecessor. The C08 common civil code
permits simpler forms for suitable monetary disputes under B13
and requires lawful actual execution under B18. It leaves route
allocation, cost exposure on transfer, failed notice and the link
to payment open. Select SCL01–SCL06 as the working design for that
gap. This revision used AI assistance.

## Rival choices and material objections

Funded simplification in the ordinary court can serve more kinds of
dispute and avoid separate intake. It should displace this route if
it gives equal protection and effective recovery with less combined
burden. Voluntary settlement can save time when parties can safely
refuse and obtain relief if talks fail. A provider-controlled or
mandatory settlement stage would give a repeat party leverage over
entry. A separate tribunal would need another appointment, budget,
review and enforcement chain. Choose a bounded procedure inside the
ordinary court with common support and execution. The two-basket
limit is a candidate for comparison. Its relationship to affordable
filing and available court time remains unverified.

A party with many claims could shift collection costs to the public
or overwhelm respondents. A person with a strong but low-value claim
could still lose access through repeated service attempts, costs on
transfer or lack of executable funds. Preserve review, test related
claims together, limit retroactive cost exposure and publish
privacy-protected measures of repeat use and unpaid awards. The
risk of delayed legitimate recovery after a service failure is real.
Price lawful re-service, apply the ordinary urgent route where
available, and leave the judge responsible for the finding.

## Evidence, authority and migration

[Sources](sources.md) document two statutory comparators and the
constructed example. Neither provides a local affordability or
outcomes estimate. No affected-party participation or independent
qualified review has been recorded for this change.

The elected legislature controls jurisdiction, fee authority and
appropriation, subject to constitutional access and review. Existing
independent court appointment, removal and assignment rules apply.
The executive, repeat litigants and platform suppliers cannot select
the judge, condition their pay on recovery or withhold the record
needed for challenge. Fund the independent court, legal aid, review
and statutory execution on the same dated budget. Provide the
existing independent complaints and review channels access to
necessary records within their authority. Judicial review retains
effective stay, rehearing and ordered-return powers. No adopter,
appropriation or case-level authority has been supplied by this
decision.

Apply prospectively from a lawful start. Preserve the original
receipt, appropriate evidence, ongoing urgent relief, existing review
and payments. Parties in an open ordinary case keep their route and
existing cost rights unless competent law allows a fair transition
with notice and review. A replacement service or medium needs a
lawful paid receiver and an auditable account of open balances and
protected records. Current law and constitutional holds continue.

## Next decision and reopening

Use one court catchment and the same caseload, rights and money to
compare funded ordinary simplification and a limited small-claims
route. Require observed first contacts, successful notice, cases
transferred or abandoned, both parties' outlay and time, qualified
review, actual receipts and the next urgent court calendar. Seek
participation from small claimants, respondents, interpreters,
advocates, court staff and execution staff. Keep unsupported cells
open. Reopen the choice on a demonstrated notice disparity, cost
deterrence, abusive concentration, weak collection or displacement
of urgent cases. Actual enactment, funded capacity and outcomes
remain unverified.
