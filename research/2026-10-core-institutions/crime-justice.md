# Ordinary crime and justice: a national penal settlement

2 October 2026 · Supporting decision analysis for C04, C06–C08, C10 and C14 · English research proposal, not enacted law

## Decision and baseline

The integrated country design says that criminal law must be prospective, fair and
proportionate; it separates police, prosecution, courts, defense and victim support.
It has not yet chosen a coherent **national penal strategy**. For the same assault,
the state might fund prevention, offer immediate safety, investigate and prosecute,
seek restitution, impose community restrictions, imprison, or arrange a restorative
process. Each uses different authority and resources. Without a stated hierarchy,
“rehabilitation” can excuse unsafe release, “safety” can justify indefinite
surveillance, and “restoration” can pressure a dependent victim to reconcile.

This analysis asks one core question: **when should AIcountry use criminal conviction
and confinement, and how should protection, accountability and return to ordinary
life work as a single system?** It covers ordinary interpersonal and property harm,
serious violence and official abuse. It does not draft a complete criminal code or
claim a universal empirical optimum.

The read baseline is [country design](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md), integrated design 6,
SHA-256 `8513013a92d6f74312d32029702c800dd0dda7d6b15f8327ffeb4d5fb4517229`,
and [design status](../DESIGN-STATUS.md), SHA-256

The [working framework](../FRAMEWORK.md) and [decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md)
are the proposed research method, not legal authority. The [everyday-justice

## Premises and rival explanations

**Values.** People must be safe from assault, coercion, exploitation and official
violence. A person accused of harm retains liberty, equality, defense and the
presumption of innocence. A person harmed retains safety, voice and material help
even if no one is convicted. Proportionate accountability matters; neither a
reduced prison count nor a higher conviction count is itself justice. Political
opponents and unpopular minorities must be able to challenge the penal system.

**Constraints.** Criminal rules need enacted offenses, culpability, proof, qualified
investigators, prosecution, independent defense and courts, lawful custody,
healthcare, safe accommodation, supervision and funds. An external settlement
currency and scarce skilled workers constrain the whole package in C06. A community
program without safe transport or an independent adviser is not an available option;
a prison sentence without humane capacity is not operationally ready.

**Competing causal accounts.** One account emphasizes credible detection and
consequences; another emphasizes material dependence, situational opportunity and
the ability to leave a dangerous relationship; another emphasizes the harm caused by
criminalization, exclusion and imprisonment themselves. All can be relevant, but
their relative contribution varies by harm and place. Assault, coercive control,
fraud, an official beating and a disputed low-level public-order complaint should
not be treated as one mechanism. The design needs separate evidence about harm,
reporting, case errors, service access, repeat victimization and costs. No local
observation in this project determines those causal shares.

The [UN Guidelines for the Prevention of Crime](https://www.unodc.org/documents/justice-and-prison-reform/crimeprevention/resolution_2002-13.pdf)
support examining social and situational prevention alongside criminal justice.
They are an official design reference, not evidence that a specific AIcountry
housing, policing or treatment intervention will reduce crime.

## Four coherent models

| Model | Mechanism and institution | Benefit under its assumptions | Serious failure or incompatible choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Retain the current C08/J1–J8 draft and fund it | Existing offense and procedure candidates, case-by-case prosecution, more staff and delivered victim services | Minimizes revision and may fix the dominant problem if it is unavailable competent response | Leaves the guiding choice between confinement, non-custodial accountability and prevention implicit. “More capacity” could mean more punishment without measuring safety. |
| B. Deterrence and incapacitation first | Broader offenses, high detection, longer custody and strong central enforcement | May protect against some continuing serious threats when noncustodial measures cannot | Raises wrongful coercion, family disruption, prison health, fiscal and political-capture risks; convictions and sentence length are poor substitutes for delivered safety. The government could use public-order crimes against opposition. |
| C. Community restoration first, confinement nearly abolished | Local mediation, restitution, support and reintegration replace most prosecution | May preserve relationships and avoid custody harms where participation is genuinely free and safety can be maintained | A dependent survivor may have no safe refusal; community leaders can favor a powerful accused person; serious organized or repeated violence may continue. Private mediation cannot supply public accountability for official abuse. |
| D. Differentiated protection and bounded penal power | Needs-based safety and prevention run independently; prospective criminal law addresses sufficiently serious culpable harm; fair case adjudication; the least restrictive adequate sentence; voluntary restorative routes; lawful confinement where needed with humane care and reentry | Can respond to serious harm while limiting punishment to a justified role; makes costs and errors visible across the full life cycle | Complexity, specialist workforce and overlapping duties can cause delays. “Alternatives” may widen control if people who would otherwise receive no sanction instead receive monitoring. Safety or restorative labels can conceal coercion unless independently reviewed. |

**Recommended research choice: D, as an explicit national rule rather than an
automatic combination of attractive parts.** Retain A where a real setting already
achieves the same boundaries and delivery with simpler institutions. Do not import
B's broad preventive detention into D, or C's replacement of fair prosecution for
serious violence. The affected public may value punishment, forgiveness, autonomy
and public accountability differently; evidence cannot average that disagreement
into a consent score.

## What D would mean in the country

**1. Criminalization is a high-threshold legislative choice.** The Assembly, not
police, prosecutors, a model or a restorative council, defines conduct and culpable
mental states prospectively. It must identify the protected interest, conduct,
harm or serious risk, necessity of a criminal response over civil/regulatory/service
routes, available defenses, maximum sanction, distributional exposure and actual
enforcement cost. Core categories for a complete future code include serious bodily
and sexual violence, coercion and exploitation, theft and fraud, corruption and
official abuse, and grave harms to public systems and the environment. That is a
scope for drafting, not newly enacted offenses or calibrated sentence maxima.
Poverty, homelessness, peaceful dissent, identity, refusal of treatment and a model
prediction are not crimes. A failed investment or unproved report alone is not
fraud or malicious reporting. Administrative penalties cannot evade criminal-level
fairness by changing their label. Statutes receive scheduled public review without
automatically lapsing protections for serious harm.

**2. Prevention is funded but does not adjudicate guilt.** Local health, housing,
education, care, workplace and environmental authorities may address conditions
that expose people to harm. They cannot use a criminal allegation as a blanket
eligibility test or require an accused person to accept compulsory therapy without
a separate legal basis. The national/local C04 and C07 guarantees should make
emergency accommodation, safe mobility, treatment and income usable without
waiting for conviction. Prevention investments compete for the same workers, sites
and funds as investigation and custody; C06 must compare them openly.

**3. A report opens parallel, distinct routes.** An accessible intake takes the
account and preserves evidence with a safe contact method. Immediate clinical care,
safe accommodation and advice follow assessed need. A temporary protective
restriction has its own grounds, expiry and prompt hearing; it does not determine
criminal guilt. Police investigate inculpatory *and exculpatory* material under
particularized powers. An independent prosecutor records the legal sufficiency and
public-interest reason for charging, noncharging or lawful diversion. The harmed
person can seek review of a noncharge and keep service access, but cannot compel
conviction. The accused has counsel, disclosure, challenge to automated evidence,
the presumption of innocence and an independent trial. The [ICCPR](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/ProfessionalInterest/ccpr.pdf),
especially Articles 9 and 14, is a primary comparison source for liberty and fair
trial; binding status for a future polity depends on actual legal circumstances.
The stronger [everyday-justice candidate](../2026-09-everyday-justice/candidate-provisions.md)
excludes torture and coercion-derived statements and prevents transfers from
restarting custody clocks. A prosecution decision does not end the safety duty.

**4. Disposition begins with the no-sanction comparator.** After proof, a court
gives public, individualized reasons connecting gravity, culpability, harm,
mitigation, prior lawful findings, victim safety and proportionality to the
sentence. It considers no penal sanction where law permits, restitution, a
graduated fine that protects subsistence, a time-limited community measure, or
custody. A person with money cannot buy immunity; inability to pay alone cannot
become imprisonment. Noncustodial orders have bounded duration, conditions and
intrusion, review and a hearing before material escalation. They must not turn
people who previously would have received a warning or no penalty into monitored
subjects. The [Tokyo Rules](https://www.unodc.org/pdf/criminal_justice/UN_Standard_Minimum_Rules_for_Non-custodial_Measures_Tokyo_Rules.pdf)
provide official comparison standards for noncustodial measures *and their
safeguards*; they do not establish that any proposed local alternative is effective.

**5. Confinement remains possible, bounded and costly.** For serious violence or
other grave culpable harm, a proportionate custodial sentence may be necessary for
accountability and protection when a less restrictive lawful measure is inadequate.
Sentence length cannot be determined by an opaque future-risk score or prison
capacity target. A treatment course, homelessness, nonpayment or administrative
delay cannot extend a lawful term. Within custody, humane conditions, independent
health care, defense access, family contact where safe, education and work options,
external inspection and confidential complaints are funded duties. Release planning
names actual providers for identity documents, housing, treatment, work or study,
and any lawful supervision; the person's basic services continue after release.
The [Nelson Mandela Rules](https://www.unodc.org/documents/justice-and-prison-reform/Nelson_Mandela_Rules-E-ebook.pdf)
are an official standard for humane custody and health-care continuity, not a
certificate that AIcountry can fund a compliant prison. Wrongful conviction
requires actual release, record correction and compensation, with review of
dependent orders and consequences.

**6. Restoration is an option with protected refusal.** It may be offered before
or after judgment under an enacted scheme, but never as the sole route to safety,
evidence preservation or a serious-abuse investigation. Before referral, assess
coercive dependence, threats, language, disability and who controls housing,
income or local reputation. Participants have independent advice, know the
consequences and can withdraw. Refusal or failure to reach agreement cannot
aggravate sentence or erase services. A recorded agreement has proportionate terms,
review and an actual delivery route; confidentiality does not shield ongoing
serious harm. Serious violence is not automatically converted into a private
settlement; a freely sought restorative process may coexist with public
accountability where safety and law allow. These safeguards track the [UN Basic
Principles on Restorative Justice](https://www.unodc.org/documents/commissions/CCPCJ/Crime_Resolutions/2000-2009/2002/ECOSOC/Resolution_2002-12.pdf),
which require voluntary consent and attention to power imbalances and safety.

**7. Official violence is not handled as an internal personnel matter.** Alleged
torture, serious force, disappearance and grave custodial abuse trigger prompt
evidence preservation and investigation outside the command, independent charging,
access to counsel for accused officials, witness protection and actual remedy
execution. Political leaders cannot direct a named prosecution or block it with
classified labels. Prosecutorial independence needs independently accessible
records, budget, forensics and execution, not merely a new office name. The
[Minnesota Protocol](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Publications/MinnesotaProtocol.pdf)
is a comparison standard for potentially unlawful deaths. The country must assess
the same route for abuse by contractors and national protective services under C10.

## A concrete whole-system test

Suppose a person reports repeated assault by a partner who controls their housing
and wages. The report is disputed; a child is present. Safe accommodation, medical
care and income access should begin from assessed need. A time-limited order may
protect against imminent harm with prompt review for the respondent. Investigators
preserve both inculpatory and contrary evidence; the survivor need not submit to
mediation or promise to testify to obtain safety. A prosecutor gives an independent
charge decision; a court applies criminal proof. If convicted, the sentence can
include proportionate custody when needed, but treatment and release arrangements
must not make the survivor a compelled carer or rely on an imaginary shelter bed.
If acquitted, safety and child-protection decisions still have their own lawful
bases and review. If an officer ignored reports or used unlawful force, an
outside-command investigation runs separately. None of these conclusions can be
inferred from a risk score or a referral marked “complete.” This is an authored
case, not evidence of observed effectiveness.

The opposing failure test is an allegation that is false or mistaken. Immediate
support may still be justified without branding the accused guilty. Unlawful
detention, overbroad protective restrictions and disclosure of private data need
prompt challenge and correction; a complainant is not punished solely because
proof fails. This prevents the safety path from becoming a route to arbitrary
coercion while preserving the ability to report real harm.

## Authority, capacity, dissent and migration

The Assembly authorizes offenses, sentencing ranges, procedure, victim support and
budgets. Independent courts and prosecutors decide individual cases. Elected bodies
can demand aggregate accounts of harm and error, hear affected people and replace
general policy; they cannot choose a verdict. AI can help disclose evidence limits,
identify service gaps or compare policy but cannot classify a person as guilty,
create a criminal norm or silently set an individualized sentence. Protected
opposition access and a usable court route are required if the executive or its
police are the target of a complaint.

An opening **justice account** must join intake, investigation, forensics,
prosecution, defense, interpreters, courts, witness and victim services, shelters,
prison places, independent health care, community supervision, legal review,
reentry and compensation. It must show time-of-day coverage, geographical travel,
pending cases and prison maintenance, not only annual totals. The prior
[synthetic capacity exercise](../2026-09-everyday-justice/capacity-and-evaluation.md)
found shortfalls under invented inputs; it is not a real caseload or budget. Case
closure, a conviction, completion of a program and a paid remedy are different
events. If capacity is missing, rights and pending claims continue, shortages are
reported, and the Assembly must choose lawful financing or scope; a court cannot
summon absent clinicians or defenders. If safe custody capacity is missing, the
state must seek lawful, reviewable alternatives or build capacity rather than
silently detain people in unlawful conditions.

Material objections remain. Survivors of repeated violence may see “least
restrictive” as a reason to leave them exposed; the test is adequate *actual*
protection, with custody available where justified. Defendants may experience
diversion, electronic monitoring or treatment as punishment without trial;
therefore entry thresholds, no-sanction comparison and review matter. A public
prosecutor may use “public interest” to shield allies; reasons, recusal, victim
review, protected disclosure and independent official-abuse investigation must
cover that power. Communities may prefer restorative or traditional forms that
state procedure marginalizes; their practices can remain available when consent,
equality, safety and legal review are real. A punitive majority may reject low
custody as insufficient censure; that is a legitimate value disagreement to decide
democratically within rights, not a question a recidivism model resolves.

This selection would require fresh reconciliation with the old criminal code and
procedure, family-care orders, security powers, local competence, probation and
prison law, civil compensation, victim services, budget and AI-authority clauses.
Do not retroactively create crimes or increase penalties. Pending reports, cases,
custody clocks, appeal periods, evidence duties, lawful orders and compensation
claims need named successors. New diversion eligibility must not silently dismiss
serious pending cases or compel victims to participate. Prior convictions materially
relying on newly excluded evidence need a lawful review route, not automatic erasure
or automatic continued confinement. Existing staff, records and facilities require
lawful transfer, individual misconduct review, training and overlapping funded
capacity before commencement.

The bounded next choice for the integrated design is to state D's hierarchy in C08:
**prevention and immediate safety on their own grounds; criminal guilt only under
published law and fair proof; the least restrictive adequate, proportionate
disposition; voluntary restoration; humane confinement when justified; and
delivered reintegration.** The register should preserve the code, capacity,
official-abuse and adoption gaps. A real jurisdiction would then compare actual
harm, case attrition, service reach, wrongful coercion, custody conditions,
reintegration and full costs by affected group before fixing offense and sentence
parameters. Revisit D if its alternatives widen state control without protection,
if serious victims remain exposed, or if a simpler lawful package demonstrably
delivers equal safety and liberty. Missing monitoring does not count as success;
urgent protection and existing legal duties continue.
