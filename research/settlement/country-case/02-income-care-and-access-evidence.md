# Original-study reports: cash, coverage, access and adverse care evidence

*Design 85; these are different treatments/outcomes, not an ownership tournament.*

| Study / inspected comparison | Result, costs and transfer boundary |
|---|---|
| [Egger et al., Econometrica 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/ECTA17945), §§2/4–7: randomized saturation of externally financed one-off rural Kenyan cash transfers | Positive consumption/assets/spillovers with little measured inflation and no significant overall labor-supply reduction. National taxation/monetary/FX responses are outside treatment; no recurrent national financing, income-taper or clinical-capacity estimate. |
| [Baicker et al., NEJM 2013](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/nejmsa1212321.pdf), pp1714–1721: Oregon lottery opportunity to apply for Medicaid, instrumental-variable coverage comparison among affected enrollees | Financial protection and depression-screening/access benefits accompany increased estimated medical spending; selected physical outcomes show no significant two-year improvement. Nonresponse, local complier scope and limited expansion constrain inference. Null is not zero; spending isn't complete social cost or proof services save money. |
| [Finkelstein–Notowidigdo, QJE 2019](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/aaFinkelstein_Noto_QJE_August_2019%20%281%29.pdf), §§IV–VII: information/application assistance randomized among older Pennsylvania Medicaid enrollees not on SNAP | Help raises enrollment more than information, but marginal responders differ in need and important needy nonresponders remain. Administrative contact frame and imperfect likely eligibility limit coverage; intervention costs omit applicant time/processing/benefits. No compelled enrollment or AI eligibility mandate follows. |
| [Baker–Gruber–Milligan, AEJ Policy 2019](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pol.20170603): Quebec-policy exposure across cohorts/provinces | Published abstract reports adverse later noncognitive, health/life-satisfaction/crime outcomes in exposed cohorts. Only abstract/original method excerpt inspected; full paper fetch failed. No numerical effects, cost or specific quality mechanism imported. Policy exposure does not isolate all providers/intensities; not grounds to withdraw optional childcare. |

These reports support complementary resources and actual services while
challenging automatic claims of health savings, successful targeting or child
benefit from attendance. They do not identify NP's optimal public/plural mix,
validate S10 hours or establish the cost of every legal entitlement. Repeated
versions/summaries are not independent families. Raw surveys/administrative
records and study code were not reanalyzed; no primary participants were involved
in this design. New current context and original affected accounts remain absent.

Canonical corrections cover safe practical help for nonclaimants/nonusers and
separate assessment of child/recipient health, development, autonomy, relationships
and worker conditions. Take-up, parental employment and accepted places remain
intermediate outcomes. Preserve cash autonomy, optional care, actual consent,
safe existing provision and stronger rights while testing rivals.
