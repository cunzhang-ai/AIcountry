# Two districts, a shared crew and a single receiving slot

This is a constructed heat and power failure exercise in minutes from
the common warning. River and Hill each have an affected group who can
use an accepted powered and staffed site. Their professional teams set
different safe-receipt times in this example. Harbor is the donor. It has
one transferable crew and one separate crew already committed to protected
home duties. The latter must remain. Each route is one package. A provider
is responsible for every person's consent, appropriate care and receipt.

| Path | Last safe start | Verified scenario and receipt |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| River transfer to regional centre | 30 | Shared crew starts at 20 and hands over by 90, before the deadline at 100. The centre has one suitable staffed slot for either group until minute 160. River takes it. |
| Hill transfer to regional centre | 65 | From the same base, 75 minutes from launch to receipt in isolation. Deadline is minute 140. The shared crew cannot start again until at least 165 after River, even with immediate successful handover. |
| Hill alternate local reception | 50 | Existing building with separately funded local staff and independent usable power. Verified and started at 35, it can receive by minute 125, before 140. This route uses neither Harbor's crew nor the regional centre. |

For the Hill transfer in isolation the suitable centre slot can accept
at the stated time. Assume the risk assessment establishes that River
needs earlier protective action under the applicable lawful priority.
With River already dispatched, no second slot at that centre can receive
Hill before minute 140. A second crew alone does not fix that. An
unmatched duplicate dispatch could occupy more work without helping
Hill. The exercise assumes the route to the local site and its actual
capability remain viable in the primary event.

| Both districts keep fully independent local routes | 12 | Both can be served in the case, with no Harbor release. Requires the stated local staff, site and upkeep throughout. |

The assumed readiness units include staffing, maintenance and reserve
preparation for the named routes only. For the full pooled option they
include four for the added crew and three for an additional staffed
receiving slot. The table compares readiness spending. It does not
rank all ownership and transition costs. For the selected arrangement,
activation needs two spendable units at minute 20 and one at minute 35,
from separately authorized reserves. Replenishment needs one further
unit at minute 165, from a dated local source. No future reimbursement
appears as spendable incident cash. If the first three are not usable at
those times, report the exposed route. If the fourth is missing, do not
count the restored mobile capacity for the next incident. Moving the
first crew and returning it to availability are different achievements.
No national price, emergency transfer or downstream health outcome is
inferred from these hypothetical amounts.

At minute 35 the ordinary dispatch channel fails and Hill sees the
previous listing for Harbor's crew. The dispatch owner still holds the
River commitment. Hill uses its prepared independent local route and a
compatible record, preserving the original claim and delivery deadlines.
If Hill's local staff or power also fail before launch, that alternate
cannot be credited. If the coordinator calls Harbor's home crew spare,
the donor's actual officer must retain its protected service until a
lawful and accepted substitute is in place. The coordinator's description
does not dispatch the crew. No other timely path is established here.
Keep the affected people in the response and protection account, seek
available lawful help and notify independent review and elected source
owners. A claim for failure remains separate from proof of bodily harm.
The result changes if geography, deadlines, costs, ordinary local
capacity or receiving constraints change.
