# AIcountry: derive institutions from the lives people lead

2026-09-19 · Research framework v1 · Institutional research candidates, not enacted law

## What this iteration changes

The objective remains a national design that future societies can learn from and that might substantially exceed present performance. But the unit of success should be a verifiable improvement in people’s lives under explicit constraints, rather than the number of departments, legal articles, review stages or completed web pages.

This iteration preserves the legal library and immutable revision history, adding a research framework: lived problems → values and constraints → causal mechanisms → competing institutions → distributional outcomes → evidence and correction. It includes three substantive institutional questions and an executable check of shared resources and group outcomes. The calculator has no legislative publication authority and cannot turn a passing arithmetic check into institutional approval.

The local repository warrants a more precise diagnosis than “just a frontend.” It already contains fiscal and service-capacity calculations, versioned research, a constitutional evolution framework and a continuous-improvement architecture. The gap is that these elements do not yet sufficiently constrain coherent institutional packages organized around lived problems. The overview’s emphasis on AI authority and departmental categories also risks treating the means of research as its purpose.

## 1. What first principles can and cannot establish

Natural science can constrain resources and risks. It cannot, by itself, decide who may govern, how freedoms should be balanced or which lives are most meaningful. Social mechanisms usually have conditions of applicability, and institutions change behavior. A statement such as “people pursue their interests” or “AI is more intelligent” cannot establish a universally optimal political order.

Record four kinds of knowledge separately: normative commitments, such as the importance of dignity and equality; physical and accounting constraints, such as resources not being available for two uses at once; empirical mechanisms describing how arrangements affect behavior in particular conditions; and design hypotheses about whether a combination of mechanisms works. They need different forms of justification and cannot substitute for one another. Address value disputes through public reasons and legitimate procedures, empirical disputes through falsifiable evidence, and arithmetic errors through correction.

Interpret “outperform every existing country” as an ambition to expand the performance frontier of feasible institutional combinations, not a guarantee of first place on every indicator, for every group, in every year. Fix the population, initial conditions, indicator definitions, period, external costs and budget constraints for each comparison. Do not combine different countries’ best individual indicators into an imaginary comparison country, or selectively remove unfavorable dimensions.

## 2. Human lives are the organizing structure; departments are implementation mappings

Organize research around five missions: survival and basic security; real choices and rights; time, relationships and care; production and shared prosperity; and ecological resilience and responsibility to future generations. Each spans several departments. Asking whether a disabled carer in a remote area can meet basic needs while retaining discretionary time can reveal institutional interface failures more effectively than adding separate health, transport and employment chapters.

Record outcomes by people’s circumstances, not just national averages. Childhood, age, disability, income, migration status, location, care responsibilities and language create extensible observation dimensions. Intersecting groups may overlap, but must not be summed into a total population. Future people and those affected abroad also need a place in impact assessment. Insufficient samples and missing data remain unknown. Privacy may require coarser reporting or qualitative accounts; filling a table is not a reason to collect unnecessary sensitive information.

Inclusion also requires openness to different forms of knowledge and institutions. Lived experience, ethnography, administrative data, experiments and historical comparison answer different questions. Qualitative research can expose missing indicators and explain how institutions are experienced; it should not be discarded because it resists quantification. Markets, public services, cooperatives, household support and local self-government are conditional alternatives rather than predetermined answers.

## 3. A proposed institutional structure

The following is a candidate design derived from the principles, not formal constitutional amendment text.

**Common responsibilities: rights, public risks and obligations across regions.** Establish floors for personal safety, due process, expression and dissent, basic services and freedom from discrimination. Assign responsibility for cross-border pollution, macroeconomic stability, public health and shared infrastructure. Rights can conflict; proportionality, public reasons and independent adjudication are needed. Saying “rights come first” does not settle every conflict.

**Local and sectoral responsibilities: compare provision within common safeguards.** Residents face different problems and possess different knowledge, so lawful service arrangements can vary and be tested. Local autonomy does not justify local majorities oppressing minorities. Cross-border externalities, fiscal equalization and rights remedies must connect back to common institutions. The ability to move away is not an affordable exit option for everyone.

**Public services: distinguish guarantees from modes of provision.** Essential services need claimable eligibility rules, time limits, quality standards, accessibility and appeal routes. Public, cooperative or regulated private organizations may provide them. A promise of universal access without qualified staff and reliable payment is incomplete. When users decline AI services, investigate feasible alternative channels and their actual cost.

**Production and distribution: examine both value creation and who benefits.** Innovation, investment and specialization require incentives. Rents, monopoly power and unequal bargaining can prevent gains in production from improving lives broadly. Compare competition rules, public investment, collective bargaining, taxation and public-asset returns without assuming that one ownership structure must win. Returns, risks, care work and environmental costs belong in the same argument.

**Correction: drafting, execution, measurement and remedies must not all depend on the same interests.** Proposers may use AI; identifiable actors remain responsible for execution. Statistical and evaluation bodies must be able to publish unfavorable results, and affected people need independent remedies. Examine control over appointments, budgets, raw data and suppliers as well as published results. The number of institutions does not establish their independence.

Treat AI initially as a replaceable capability, rather than an unquestionable source of sovereignty. Who approves, revokes and replaces its role depends on the lawful constitution of a real community. The current project assigns design revisions to AI. Research can compare that arrangement with lawful human deliberation and approval, and with polycentric divisions of responsibility. Changing ultimate legislative authority is foundational; it cannot be accomplished through a new page or an ordinary amendment.

## 4. Connect commitments to outcomes through causal mechanisms

Every institutional candidate should explain: what action is triggered; whose behavior changes; which resource or incentive changes; which people experience which outcomes; and what opposing mechanisms and delays may occur.

Care example: accessible services replace some involuntary unpaid work if quality and schedules meet needs. Carers gain discretionary time while recipients gain continuous support. Opposing mechanisms include drawing staff away from other services, excluding complex cases, requiring families to fill gaps and short-term subsidies raising service prices. Neither subsidy spending nor service visits alone can establish success.

Housing example: more usable housing in suitable locations may reduce scarcity and improve stable residence and access to opportunity, subject to land, construction and financing constraints. Supply takes time. Some short-term cash assistance may flow into rents; the size of that effect needs research. Cheap distant housing that increases commuting, segregation and household burdens cannot automatically count as a better life.

Governance example: stronger analytical tools may reveal more feasible alternatives if inputs can be challenged, evaluation is independent and correction can be initiated. If the same supplier controls both the information entering decisions and the outcome of appeals, greater capability may also expand the reach of errors. Keep performance tests separate from political legitimacy.

## 5. Shared accounts: a reproducible example

All figures below are imaginary and have no empirical predictive meaning. The horizon is one operating year; CU is an imaginary currency. Construction, training lead times and multiyear debt are excluded, so the example cannot establish long-term feasibility.

| Shared resource | Available | Existing commitments | New service package | Normal balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Public cash, million CU/year | 100 | 65 | 28 | 7 |
| Qualified care, thousand hours/year | 200 | 150 | 45 | 5 |
| Available premises, thousand m² | 30 | 18 | 10 | 2 |
| Emissions allowance, thousand tCO2e/year | 100 | 85 | 5 | 10 |

Demand equals existing commitments plus the incremental demand of every component. The balance equals available capacity minus demand. Stress cases apply declared multipliers to total capacity or demand. Resource dimensions are reported separately: surplus cash does not automatically eliminate a shortage of qualified staff.

A 15% workforce-capacity loss gives 200 × 0.85 − 195 = −25 thousand hours. A 20% cash-capacity loss gives 100 × 0.8 − 93 = −13 million CU. A 10% increase in care demand gives 200 − 195 × 1.1 = −14.5 thousand hours. The package fits the normal ledger but has a shortfall in each of these three separate stress cases. These are not probabilistic forecasts; the example does not combine all three shocks at once.

Another package combines care and housing plans that each appear feasible on their own. Together they require 110 cash units, 220 care-hour units, 34 premises units and 105 emissions units, exceeding all four capacities. Shared constraints must therefore shape decisions, rather than appear only as a promise of sustainable funding in each department’s chapter.

The four alternatives are current arrangements, cash support, a service package and overcommitted expansion. The baseline includes losses from growing needs rather than treating inaction as harmless. Assumed gains in time and care merely demonstrate group comparisons. A positive midpoint is not enough: an interval that includes losses retains a possible-harm result, and missing group or metric effects remain unknown. The intervals do not express statistical confidence.

The tool does not calculate a national score. It distinguishes normal resource infeasibility, stress-case failure, declared safeguard violations, unverified safeguards, group risks and gaps in causal evidence. Even when all inputs claim to satisfy the conditions, the highest status is “candidate for independent review.” Entering “satisfied” cannot certify the claim.

## 6. Identify institutional improvement rather than better-looking indicators

Fix the evaluation question, baseline, groups, period and minimum meaningful change first. Check whether data use comparable definitions, then report ranges and heterogeneous effects. Cross-sectional differences between countries cannot directly establish the causal effect of an institution. Geography, history, population, wealth and international position can all confound the comparison.

For reversible, lawfully authorized service pilots, consider random allocation, phased rollout, natural experiments and matched areas as appropriate. Explain selection bias, spillovers, attrition, pre-trends and transferability for each method. Causal identification cannot justify withholding basic rights or treating a country as an experiment without consent. Higher-level political institutions require more historical comparison, process tracing, failure exercises and long-term observation; do not pretend they support a simple A/B test.

Pair indicators with measures that expose manipulation: case closure speed with rejected claims and effective remedies; healthcare waiting time with clinical outcomes and loss of complex cases; housing burden with displacement and commuting; employment with discretionary time and occupational harm. Include the burden and privacy costs of obtaining data.

When some groups lose, the absence of a Pareto improvement does not mean no decision can ever be made. First identify any rights-floor violation, then disclose feasible alternatives, compensation, non-compensable losses and the values behind the choice. Only the applicable legitimate process can resolve the trade-off. The model can expose the conflict, not authorize it.

## 7. Implementing this research architecture

Each research package should contain: the problem and affected groups; the kinds of principles involved; causal mechanisms and competing explanations; the baseline and at least two credible alternatives; shared accounts and time horizons; group outcomes and intervals; changes in power; evidence and counterevidence; withdrawal, compensation and service-continuity conditions; responsible roles and review triggers.

Existing problem, claim, evidence and assessment records can hold the questions, mechanisms, sources and evaluations. This iteration first provides a separately versioned research artifact and calculator. It does not fabricate production research-register entries, formal amendments or resident authorization. Later, with authorization, submit individual records and connect them to exact legal revisions using the existing links. Validate the need for new formal record types through actual research before building another large empty database.

Use open identifiers for new missions, metrics, groups and resources. Retain definition versions, units, directions, thresholds, provenance and periods. When concepts change, preserve prior meanings and results; renaming an indicator must not erase failure. The existing sixteen departments remain useful as responsibility and navigation views.

Future iterations must deliver at least one substantive result: an evidence-supported mechanism correction, a complete comparison of alternatives, a shared-constraint infeasibility finding, an actionable evaluation design, or a justified decision to retain current arrangements. More pages, process diagrams or statements of principle alone are not institutional progress.

## 8. Present judgment and evidence that could overturn it

Adopt a research structure of lived problems, plural institutional alternatives, shared resources, group outcomes and correctable authority. Do not treat exclusive AI power as an unchangeable premise. This recommendation follows from its ability to expose cross-cutting problems and concrete failures in worked examples, not from evidence that it can govern a real country successfully.

The next empirical priority is a joint baseline for care needs, service capacity and household time use. It requires a real locality, qualified researchers, informed participation and privacy arrangements, funding and lawful pilot authority. None is currently designated. Completion means a traceable group baseline, a resource ledger and an evaluation plan for at least two financeable alternatives. If eligibility rules, bias or distrust prove more important than supply, reconsider the intervention mechanism.

Required roles include care users, workers, statistical evaluators, fiscal reviewers and rights reviewers. These are proposed roles, not appointed institutions. Discuss an actual pilot only when premises, personnel, remedies and continuity arrangements are ready. Publishing this research website does not complete any of those steps.
