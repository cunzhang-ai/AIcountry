# T105–T108. Protect patient access through a change in paid working time

13 November 2026 · Integrated design 448 · Candidate implementation schedule

**T105 — Give the first motion a complete route to the appointment.**
For designated non-emergency patient travel, apply AP19–AP23, MO17–MO22
and TW21–TW24 to the same journey and roster. The service purchaser
keeps the duty to secure covered access without a private advance.
The dispatch owner obtains an accepted qualified vehicle and driver,
including any required mobility support, and the clinic confirms
useful-time receiving and any covered return. A publicly purchased
standby hour, a booked journey and received care are distinct.
A telephone route controlled by the same dispatch office can help
during a channel outage. A failure of that office requires a
separately authorized and usable dispatch route.

**T106 — Price the earlier shift and the later payment.** For each
option, show the paid and service-ready hours, break and charging
coverage, bid basis and changes, first due payroll, buyer payment
and next-window obligations. Keep the provider's employee wage
inside its contract price when the price already covers that job.
Price independently assigned staff, verified origin cover and any
necessary liquidity separately. Preserve agreed rest, pay, worker
choice and collective terms. A public payer must fund what it
binds, including any narrowly authorized advance, and credit that
advance once at settlement. A private provider bears its lawful
payroll duty even when a public invoice is delayed.

**T107 — Compare a roster repair and actual paid-time reduction.**
Bring forward needed repair and preparation on evidenced authority
and funds. Compare voluntary redistribution of existing hours with
a finite retained-pay reduction and a joint earlier start. Show
who gains usable time, whether family members take on extra work,
and which appointments or shifts would be exposed. The elected
body chooses covered services, spending and timing. Workers and
their representatives exercise their own agreement and challenge
rights. Preserve the preexisting remedy and protected access.
A pilot's end cannot cancel incurred wages, supplier payments or
due service. Existing routes carry continuing obligations.

**T108 — Require a receiving demonstration for the new stage.**
Verify one ordinary and one adverse roster and the following
payment window before relying on added standby or shorter hours.
A replacement must retain its actual vehicle, driver, acceptance
and funding when the declared common dependency fails. Give
patients and workers an accessible independent correction route
before avoidable loss and safe evidence of unmet service afterward.
AI may help compare schedules and flag common suppliers. It cannot
infer consent, release pay, certify arrival or replace independent
review. Reopen on recurring common failure, unpaid work or a
qualifying alternative that provides better access and usable time.
