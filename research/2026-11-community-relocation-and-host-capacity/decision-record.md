# Design261 decision record

**Baseline and requested decision.** Design260 retains FR01–FR08,
with personal assistance, alternative places, host costs and cultural
ties. It lacks a distinct way to authorize a voluntary shared recovery
project while protecting people who prefer a separate move. Add
FR09–FR13 through the [working framework](../FRAMEWORK.md) and
[decision handoff](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md).

**Evidence and competing explanations.** The Hawke's Bay evidence
shows different pathways for property, home and shared institutions.
Completion statistics for one cannot answer whether the other was
received. Lack of safe available dwellings, refusal of unsuitable
placement and losing the place needed for care, work or culture are
different reasons for slow recovery. Shared siting may solve the last
two while failing to deliver water, accessible housing or a practical
option for a dissenting household. The Trust's account represents its
reported mandate. It cannot decide AIcountry's proposed rule or serve
as evidence of individual consent. [Source scope](sources.md)

**Alternatives and selection.** Retain safe return and mitigation as
live alternatives where timely and rights-compatible. Where movement
is necessary, retain direct individual placement and separate site
stewardship as strong options. Add a staged opt-in group route when
affected people seek it and independently qualified sites and recurring
funds exist. A pure property purchase leaves occupancy and social
infrastructure unresolved. A mandatory group move can use public
infrastructure efficiently while coercing families or overriding
collective rights. Reject both as defaults. In the authored case
prefer the mixed route with six confirmed group households and six
individual routes. The mixed route requires real new capacity and is
more expensive than twelve available dispersed places on the same
cost assumptions. [Matched comparison](../settlement/community-relocation/02-group-and-dispersed-alternatives.md)

**Authority and objections.** Keep three acts distinct: competent
public decisions on spending and receiving capacity, lawful collective
authority over its own common interests, and each person's informed
acceptance of their own housing and services. A housing authority
cannot certify cultural consent and an existing community leader
cannot choose for an individual resident. Independent advice and a
confidential objection route address coercion and unequal access.
Source terms must fund the host increment and pay for lawful
maintenance. Existing host users and future residents are not spare
capacity. Opposition members can challenge costs and receiving under
existing oversight. Courts and competent reviewers enforce applicable
rights and process without themselves approving a new project.

**Transition.** Existing safe assistance, housing status, titles,
pending claims, customary rights and separate insurance and debt terms
remain on their original routes. No transfer arises from a risk class,
a public commitment or an AI estimate. Respect applicable existing
contracts and require fresh qualification before changing a project
or its recipients. Keep the smallest independently payable alternative
through a late site, an unmet minimum phase or end of the recovery
office. Correct against source evidence with personal choices protected.

**Next bounded choice.** Price a safe adaptation rival, six actual
group opt-ins and matched individual placements for one lawful recovery
area. Ask competent originating and receiving authorities to document
owner, consent, usable tenure, water, school, care, stewardship,
recurrent source and remaining temporary support. Set the authority,
cap, deadline and safe bridge before releasing optional work. Reopen
on a changed hazard, actual refusal, changed collective mandate,
receiving delay, coercion or a better feasible dispersed alternative.
History and authored arithmetic are qualified only as described.
Legitimate adoption, local independent review, funded operation and
effects on human lives remain open.
