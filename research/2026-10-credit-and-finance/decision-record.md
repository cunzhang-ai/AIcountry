The bounded question is **how the future country allocates ordinary credit and
contains financial risk before a bank needs rescue**. C06 already distinguishes
currency, tax-payment finality, emergency liquidity, deposit coverage, bank
resolution and public borrowing. It leaves normal lending, nonbank leverage,
household over-indebtedness and the political power of controlling payments and
credit underdetermined. A state could enact a good bank-failure plan while
allowing its protected liabilities to subsidize a housing bubble, excluding
opponents from basic payments, or shifting the same risk to an affiliate.

**Values:** useful investment, safe payments, household autonomy, viable small
enterprise, saver/creditor rights, financial stability, equitable access and
democratic control over public risk. **Constraints:** existing property and
contract rights, independent courts, privacy, finite supervisory skill and
public money, and the C06 prohibition on hidden bailouts. **Empirical claims
not established here:** which structure would supply productive credit at
acceptable cost, whether a supervisor could see common exposures in time,
whether narrow banking would preserve access, or whether proposed consumer
rights would reduce over-indebtedness. **Hypothesis:** regulated plural risk
taking, separated from basic payments and explicit public investment, can
improve real access without leaving all losses to taxpayers or debtors. This
may fail when regulators, lenders, payment operators and political sponsors
share control or when credit restrictions simply migrate activity offshore.

## Coherent alternatives

| Package | Mechanism and attraction | Structural failure or cost |
|---|---|---|
| Retain design 21's resolution-focused reference with ordinary licensing left for later | Avoids prematurely choosing ratios or a new agency without a real economy; private contract and bank resolution remain. | It does not say who may create credit-like claims, how linked nonbanks are seen, or what happens to households before default. The gap is not closed by a receiver after losses crystallize. |
| Lightly regulated private credit and disclosure | Entry and lender choice may speed innovation and avoid political allocation. | Opaque leverage, common underwriting, deceptive terms and payment exclusion can make nominal choice unusable; depositor and taxpayer protection can subsidize risk taking. Disclosure alone may not correct dependency or unequal bargaining power. |
| Directed public credit and a dominant public bank | Elected priorities could channel funding to housing, industry and neglected places and retain gains publicly. | The governing coalition may choose borrowers, conceal loan losses, employ supporters and use payment access against dissent. Public deposits and monetary credit could become an unvoted industrial budget; professional independence alone may become its own unaccountable allocator. |
| Narrow public payments with risky lending mostly outside insured banks | Separating payment balances from risky credit could limit the public guarantee and give savers a clearer choice. | Existing deposits, bank funding, credit supply and fiscal costs would need a difficult conversion. A monopolistic payment utility might still exclude critics, while risk could accumulate in funds or foreign providers. This remains a serious rival if an adopter can build a plural, funded transition. |
| **Selected: regulated plural intermediation with a distinct public-risk account** | Banks, cooperatives and nonbanks may finance activities at their own risk; prior-law prudential and conduct duties follow economic function; a bounded systemic-risk council addresses cross-sector spillovers; basic payments and public investment have separate mandates. | Complex supervision costs money and can be captured. Limits may ration sound credit, favor large incumbents or move activity outside view. The rulemaking body's power over housing and enterprise requires democratic and judicial control. |

The selection does not treat more credit as welfare. A household may be better
off with affordable rent, wages and services than a larger mortgage. A firm
may need equity, market access, infrastructure or bankruptcy relief rather
than another loan. A public or narrow-payments architecture could displace
this choice after a real comparison. Neither financial stability nor nominal
credit inclusion compensates for practical political exclusion.

## Selected authority and claim chain

1. **Keep claims distinguishable.** Law specifies which licensed entities may
   take insured deposits, safeguard payment-client assets, offer investment
   funds, extend credit or sell insurance. The customer receives intelligible
   terms about claim, currency, loss exposure, fees, withdrawal and recourse.
   An e-money or platform balance does not acquire deposit protection by an
   interface label. Essential tax, wage, benefit and ordinary payment receipt
   needs a usable public or regulated route without borrowing or political
   profiling; the route still requires lawful identification and anti-fraud
   controls with review. No single payment provider or AI account becomes the
   sole gate to civic life.
2. **Make private credit bear private risk.** Licensed banks face proportionate
   capital, liquidity, governance, related-party and large-exposure duties.
   Bank owners and creditors bear losses in the C06 enacted order; the
   resolution fund and central-bank liquidity window do not grant unlimited
   protection. Nonbanks face activity- and entity-based oversight for actual
   leverage, maturity/liquidity mismatch, redemption promises, common funds,
   bank links and guarantees. A change of legal wrapper does not convert the
   same economic exposure into a risk-free asset. Supervisors need lawful
   access to original exposure, ownership and funding records, and must
   publish gaps when they cannot see an affiliate or foreign source.
3. **Bound systemic tools and regulators.** The Assembly enacts the purpose,
   eligible instruments, maximum reach, decision procedure, publication,
   expiry and review of system-wide capital, liquidity, concentration or
   borrower-risk tools. A statutory stability council with one vote each from
   the monetary authority, prudential supervisor, and financial markets and
   conduct supervisor can activate only those tools
   for evidenced systemic risk by recorded majority; the competent supervisor
   implements the sector rule. Prior law supplies conflict and vacancy
   substitutes; if the council cannot lawfully decide, sector supervisors
   retain only their own existing tools. Treasury supplies fiscal facts without
   a council vote and cannot veto a warning or turn the council into a fiscal
   lender. The council publishes uncertainty, expected geographic/class/age
   effects, credit
   supply, dissent and renewal grounds. Borrowers, small firms, renters and
   creditors have proportionate hearing and legality-review routes; an urgent
   short rule receives prompt later hearing. Neither a hearing nor a court
   substitutes its preferred named loan for a lawful general rule. Protected
   supervisory data remains accessible to lawful auditors and courts. A
   legislature can revise general powers but a minister cannot command a
   named loan or suppress a named
   borrower's complaint. The same controller must not own source data,
   licensing, a prudential exception, conduct appeal and case judgment.
4. **Protect borrowers without pretending every loan is safe.** A
   financial markets and conduct supervisor distinct from the prudential bank
   supervisor applies conduct law to banks, brokers and digital lenders:
   clear total cost, rates, currency, variable exposure and security; a
   proportionate repayment
   assessment; fair credit-data access, correction and limits on reuse;
   reviewable adverse decisions; and no deceptive refinancing or harassing
   collection. Legitimate lenders may decline unpayable credit or recover
   lawful debts. Individual decisions require objective, contestable grounds;
   an AI score is evidence to examine, not the responsible lender or final
   adjudicator. Statute sets a timely complaint route independent of the
   originating lender and appeal to an ombud or court outside that supervisor,
   with interim protection where loss of a home or
   livelihood would make later relief useless.
5. **Resolve distress with actual continuity.** Voluntary workouts and court
   procedures for households and small firms address unpayable debt,
   proportional discharge, security, creditor ranking, fraud, wages, tenancy
   and subsistence. Foreclosure needs notice, proof, a court-controlled route
   and C07 housing-continuity duties; due process does not erase a secured
   creditor's valid claim. Financial-firm failure follows C06's separate
   supervisor, receiver, deposit coverage, loss order and payment fallback.
   An extraordinary public guarantee, recapitalization or blanket debt relief
   needs the Assembly to vote its cost, beneficiaries, alternatives and
   distribution under C06; neither council nor receiver can create it.

This chain separates prudential safety, market conduct, public industrial
investment, monetary liquidity, firm resolution, personal insolvency and
civil adjudication. It needs pre-enacted coordination duties when one
exposure spans regulators; a coordination meeting is not a new fiscal or
legal power. The council's own appointments, removal, budget, votes, data,
exceptions and relationship with major financial firms require C03/C04
safeguards and independent review. Its votes belong to leaders chosen under
published qualification, staggered appointment and reviewable for-cause
removal rules. Conflicted members are replaced only by deputies named under
prior law, not by a cabinet choice after a bank is in trouble. A bounded
statutory operating budget and lawful source access should not depend on a
single regulated firm or minister; fees, contacts and exceptions are audited.
The Assembly can change the general tool mandate prospectively, not a named
loan. A narrow-looking exception for one favored bank can cumulatively become
a policy bailout. Competition law and
public procurement examine lenders' common control over employers, housing,
media and payment rails under C03/C05, including after mergers.

## Support and contested inferences

The [Basel Core Principles](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-framework/standard/bcp?allChapters=true)
provide a primary benchmark for bank supervision, not capital numbers for
this country. The [FSB's 2025 nonbank-leverage recommendations](https://www.fsb.org/2025/07/leverage-in-nonbank-financial-intermediation-final-report/)
show why bank-only oversight may miss connected leverage and why measures
must follow identified risk. The [joint IMF–FSB–BIS macroprudential account](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2016/08/30/pr16386-imf-fsb-bis-publish-elements-of-effective-macroprudential-policies)
describes mandates, coordination, transparency and accountability; it
does not select this council or prove a tool's benefit here. The [World Bank's consumer-protection guide](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492761513113437043/pdf/Good-practices-for-financial-consumer-protection-2017-edition.pdf)
informs credit disclosure, data and collection safeguards; its practices
need local legal and distributional evaluation. The
[World Bank insolvency principles](https://www.worldbank.org/en/brief/2026/02/24/the-world-bank-principles-for-effective-insolvency-and-creditor-rights)
inform debtor/creditor balance and small-firm continuity. The earlier
[monetary decision](../2026-10-monetary-architecture/decision-record.md)
supplies the distinct bank-resolution and public-risk boundary. None is an
original observation about an AIcountry population, market or funded agency.

Important objections remain. Strict limits can exclude low-income or new
borrowers and entrench large licensed firms. Credit data can penalize people
already disadvantaged or become a political surveillance layer. Protective
debt discharge can shift loss to small savers or raise future borrowing costs;
harsh enforcement can destroy homes and enterprise. A market crash can require
quick joint action while a legislature is divided, but a fast council can
become an unelected credit government. Nonbank activity can migrate beyond
legal reach. These are tradeoffs to test, not arguments that one institution
should secretly carry all powers. The selected architecture permits
proportionate rules, public reasons, individual appeal, time limits and
lawful financing; it does not prove equal access or resilience.

## Compatibility, transition and next test

This changes C05 productive financing, C06 monetary/fiscal/financial authority,
C07 access through payment and housing, C08 debt and court remedies, C11 credit
data/AI, C13 lived effects, C14 founding assets, proposed A5 and stress
exercise F7. It retains
design 18's conditional domestic currency, lawful deposit coverage, limited
central-bank liquidity and separate bank resolution. It retains design 19's
taxpayer rights and design 20's public-procurement limits; no old review
transfers approval to this new council. A foreign-currency or narrow-banking
package would need its own full transition, not selective borrowing of the
chosen safeguards.

An adopter must inventory banks, nonbanks, insurers, funds, payment rails,
depositors, client assets, household and firm debts, collateral, arrears,
guarantees, beneficial controllers, cross-border exposures and existing
supervisory/legal claims. It must map original data custodians, capable staff,
conflicted appointments, court and conduct capacity, alternate payments,
insolvency caseload, public-risk funds and creditors' lawful rights. Existing
contracts and deposits cannot be relabeled or discharged by this research.
Rules can be phased prospectively, but a grace period that lets a risky
affiliate accumulate unobserved leverage must be reported as exposure.

**Evidence qualification:** primary international institutional sources and
the predecessor design were compared conceptually; no original borrower,
balance-sheet, housing, lender or labor data, and no affected-party review.
**Research selection:** regulated plural intermediation with separate basic
payments, bounded cross-sector stability tools and debtor remedies.
**Legal authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** no law, licensed
market, independent supervisors, court, resolver, payment substitute or
funded consumer help has been demonstrated. **Observed outcomes:** none;
the authored F7 case is a logic probe, not a successful crisis exercise.

The next bounded adopting-public test is a **housing-price decline plus a
nonbank funding run and a major payment operator outage**. Using real lawful
contracts and opening balance sheets, trace one tenant, mortgaged household,
small firm, saver and essential-service payee through credit losses, payments,
workout, bank/nonbank intervention, public exposures and enforceable remedies.
Compare this package with narrow payments and directed public credit under
the same starting assets; include creditors, borrowers, workers, opposition,
regulators, housing providers and independent reviewers. The deliverable is
a due-date balance and rights account with failed paths and dissent, not a
model-generated stability score. This project has no adopter, field budget
or such evidence. Reopen design 22 if actual access, risk migration,
political capture, costs or a coherent rival undermines the choice. If
exposures cannot be observed, do not call the finance system safe.
