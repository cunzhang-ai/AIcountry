# Staff a new care service without shifting the missed visits

*Design490 · Constructed comparison on one workforce calendar.*

Start from the [repeated provider exit](../repeated-provider-exit/01-two-exits-and-an-ordinary-service-choice.md)
decision. An elected authority is considering two recurring specialist
home-support visits per window. Six equivalent visits are already due
at other providers in the same labor market. The places and residents
are distinct. Every counted visit needs a qualified paid worker,
records, acceptable travel and actual resident receiving. One remote
resident can use only an assisted intake route and can refuse a
proposed handoff. Workers can decline a shift, change jobs or leave.

| Window | Existing duties and genuinely available work |
|---|---|
| Preparation | Six qualified shift units cover six existing visits. One clinician can teach in a paid window only if independent backfill covers their visit. A named qualified substitute can cover one visit after a lawful source and actual acceptance. |
| First ordinary window | A returning worker accepts one qualified shift independent of the six existing commitments. One learner has completed independent assessment and accepts a second. In the transfer alternative, workers move from existing services. Their original visits remain due. |
| Next ordinary window | An experienced worker retires from the existing roster. The returning worker keeps their one accepted shift. A further qualified entrant could replace the vacated shift, but only after completed assessment, voluntary acceptance and safe receiving. |

Assume three units of startup cash are usable. Two pay for the
learner, mentor, assessment and payroll administration and one for
accepted mentor backfill. The dedicated new service has three
recurring units: two for independently qualified and accepted visits,
one for assisted receiving, lawful standby preparation and independent
review. Funding and roster assignments are separate. The recurring
source buys no unlisted replacement worker. In the positive later
branch the existing provider may reuse one released payroll unit
after retirement, if it lawfully remains available at the next
cutoff and the verified replacement costs no more. Otherwise their
own authority must fund the gap. A replacement never uses the
new team's allocation by default. Startup and continuing
costs are illustrative, with ordinary staff and provider pay carried
on their existing sources.

| Alternative | First and later results |
|---|---|
| Recruit two experienced staff from existing providers for the new team | The new team can report two visits, but without replacement the old sites deliver only four of six. Complete original and new care stays at six. Legitimate resignations cannot be blocked. The public proposal cannot claim improvement by moving unmet need. |
| Paid return plus protected training | With the full startup, the mentor is covered and current care continues. The returning worker and assessed learner can deliver two additional visits at the first ordinary window. At the next window the existing roster loses one to retirement, leaving seven of eight total visits unless a distinct replacement actually receives the work. |
| Maintain ordinary care and prepare phased entry | Protect existing visits first. If only two startup units arrive, do not bind the full optional placement on an unsecured cover promise. The returner can cover one new visit if separately authorized and funded. A larger start depends on later qualified cover and an exact elected decision. Preserve any binding wages, teaching and ordinary duties under their own law. |

For the second alternative, a later entrant does not automatically
fill the retirement gap. Verify the lawful scope, planned start,
actual completed qualifications, worker choice, acceptable route and
first delivered visit. If the first learner takes work elsewhere,
report the other accepted shift as one and test the actual fallback.
A shared supervisor or credential service can defeat both proposed
backup names. Test rostering with that dependency removed. Pay owed
work and keep residents' ordinary remedies while reporting the gap.

Where an existing provider could retain a worker through better terms,
compare a funded retention offer and a smaller new team with full
training on the same timeline. A retention offer matters only if the
worker accepts and it changes the future staffing path. Requiring
unpaid family care or compulsory overtime assumes away the loss.
Pay for cover and genuine preparatory work without purchasing an
exclusive claim on a worker's future labor.

Test a protected reserve call separately. In the transfer alternative,
an origin provider could spend its one reserve slot on ordinary work
if legally permitted and show one fewer missing visit today. If a
separate protected call then needs that same slot before replenishment,
it remains uncovered.
A paid mentor replacement counts as additional only if the promised
reserve and other duties remain available. Check whether returning
workers had essential unpaid care responsibilities and whether lawful,
voluntary support covers those needs. Seek only the facts needed for
the plan. If a resident declines, record the missing visit and offer
funded lawful alternatives without restarting their case.

Apply the same receiving rule to Design489's standing provider.
Two staffed slots can yield zero, one or two usable visits. Mutual
aid is blocked only where deploying it would leave a protected
sending duty uncovered. If finite authority expires with no other
lawful route, both threatened visits remain uncovered.

Select voluntary return plus protected training as the **first-window**
reference with these assumptions. Approve standing expansion only
against a separately verified next-window plan for attrition and
receiving. Where second-window cover remains open, narrow the optional
release and state which duties and people remain exposed. Permission
to prepare or a signed employment contract does not establish that a
resident received a visit. AI may compare dates, overlapping work and
demand using necessary records. It cannot decide worker availability,
qualification, care acceptance or whether an absence justifies denial.
