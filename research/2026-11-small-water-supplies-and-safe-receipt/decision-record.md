# Design390 decision. Close the drinking-water gap outside the public network

## Baseline and choice

[Design389](predecessor-design-389/COUNTRY-DESIGN.md) assigns water
safety, public residual supply and remedies across WS01–WS25 and TD07.
Small supplies need an identifiable operator, independent oversight and
a funded response to users who may have no utility account.

Select [WS26–WS32](../country/C09/16/06-small-water-supplies-and-safe-receipt.md)
under the [working research method](../FRAMEWORK.md). Require proportionate
registration and prevention where suppliers serve others. Offer confidential
help for solely household self-supply. Tie credible danger to independent
safety action and an accepting alternate service, financed through safe return.

## Alternatives and unresolved objections

Voluntary registration and incident-led investigation preserve operator
choice and cost less to administer. Their visibility depends on users
recognizing and reporting harm, and an incident can leave tenants or pupils
without a usable alternative. Universal network connection can pool quality
control where feasible. It can also exceed capital or source capacity,
displace useful community provision and leave users waiting through the
transition. Apply common safety and receipt requirements, and compare
repair, shared provision and connection using actual local conditions.

Small suppliers may lack time or money for oversight. Phase in registration
and fund proportionate assistance. Protect household privacy, lawful access
and operator review. Publish coverage gaps and recurring failures so that
cooperative management, public assistance and public failure remain open
to challenge. Urgent protection and eventual liability each need authority.

## Evidence, transition and next action

[Primary records](sources.md) identify practical surveillance needs and
document repeated failures within a defined monitored population.
The [constructed case](../settlement/small-water-supplies-and-safe-receipt/01-two-supplies-and-one-backup.md)
shows how a common vehicle, delayed reopening and late recovery change
receipt. It estimates neither prevalence nor the cost of implementation.

Prospective adoption needs elected funding, a qualified public-health
reviewer, the local Water Service Director and named national fallback,
lawful payment authority and actual operators. Preserve existing lawful
source rights, service duties and remedies. Use the original evidence and
independent review for restrictions and restoration. Fund the health
review separately from the operator whose service is under review.
Elected bodies remain answerable for budgets and service leadership,
and affected users retain an independent route to enforce delivery.
Affected participation,
territorial authority, safe capacity and better outcomes remain unverified.

Next compare a school and a shared supply over two days using documented
delivery and testing capacity, lawful initial operation and reopening
conditions, and input from affected users, including people unable to
collect water. Reopen on repeated failures, excluded households, shared
transport or laboratory failure, or a better qualified provision model.
