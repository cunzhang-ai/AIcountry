# Four hours free, but only one resident can attend

*Design296 · Constructed comparison of ordinary time and democratic access.*

Two residents each have four hours they can direct in the same day.
One has four separate one-hour periods. The other has two continuous
two-hour periods. They both want an independent review of a benefit
decision and to take part in an optional local deliberation. The
review is offered at 09:00 or 12:00 and takes one hour. Both can
receive it in the declared window. A common live meeting runs
from 17:00 to 19:00. Only the resident with a continuous evening
period can attend it.

The fragmented schedule belongs to a resident whose other periods
are occupied by paid work or chosen care. The council can fund
a way to submit a contribution at noon and reply during the first
evening hour. The review can occur in the morning. An independent
human facilitator transmits the views and replies before the
decision. The council keeps the live route. This adds a practical
choice in the example, with less opportunity for spontaneous group
exchange. If the facilitator fails or reports a view incorrectly,
the affected resident needs a real way to speak or correct the
record before their contribution can matter.

A different choice buys one qualified relief hour so the resident
can attend the full live meeting. This needs the care recipient's
consent and an accepting provider. A promise from a family member
does not qualify. Where the meeting is still useful without any
individual's attendance, a resident may freely decline. The
council cannot claim someone had a practical opportunity merely
because an invitation was sent. The benefit review and any due
remedy continue independently of participation.

The [comparison](../../../settlement/usable-time/README.md) holds
the same people and deadlines fixed. The schedules are assumptions,
not observed lives. The [national rules](../../C01/03/08-usable-time-and-public-participation.md)
put the time people can use and the price of widening access on
the same public decision.
