# One shared service and three independent public duties

*Design525 · Constructed comparison · Workload, money and outcomes are illustrative.*

Compare the same residents and due duties under a complete shared-service
withdrawal and a separate scenario in which the controller can keep its service
running but denies access to the affected institutions. The shared layer had
helped turn submissions into accessible docket and claim material. Use
identical lawful authority, original deadlines, sites, minimum qualifications
and affected populations for all three arrangements. Hold other voted work
constant. Earlier orders, accepted filings, payments and requests for access
remain in force on their own terms.

## Protected work on two binding dates

| Owner and destination | First call | Next call after finite cover ends |
|---|---|---|
| Court administration and independent order recipient | Receive and hear one urgent application. Deliver the authentic order and verify relief due at this call. | Hear a new urgent application and rule on a separately due challenge to a record created before withdrawal. Deliver and verify execution of relief due on either. |
| Election authority and voters | Receive one time-sensitive correction and get the verified instruction to the affected voters | Receive and resolve one new filing before its binding contest date |
| Benefits authority, independent paying route and residents | Deliver two due individual payments | Deliver one current payment and a separately due correction on an earlier underpayment |
| Total protected work units | 4 | 5 |

One unit here is one specified end-to-end assignment. It is not a generic
staff-hour measure or transferable across owners. Costs below use assumed
recurring units per budget period including pay and on-costs, sites, accessible
receiving, lawful evidence exchange, paid practice and future renewal. One-off
conversion is assumed already financed for the first two options. The cost
and exact starting date of any actual conversion require separate proof.

## Put all three on the same ordinary budget

The available recurring envelope for this comparison is 10 units. Common
needs outside the example are held fixed. Each ordinary independent floor
below costs 2 units per owner. Its qualified roster and separately usable
payment route can handle that owner's exact load on both dates. For the
protected acts, the court and election owners keep their respective records,
staffing and instructions. The court's authentic order reaches a separately
accepted executor. The benefits authority has a lawful second paying route
with its own available cash and original resident authorizations. An issuing
office merely marking a job done does not count. For C, assume shared operations
cost 4 and full independent operation of the three routes costs 6. A lawful
independent reserve covers the first call within those 6 units. Its designated
records specialist leaves before the next call. The separately due contested
record and the election filing then require the same remaining specialist on
overlapping deadlines. The appointment and independent receiving arrangement
for a replacement cost one additional recurring unit. The 4 shared units are
already committed through the next call and cannot finance the replacement on
that date. The ordinary coverage includes each available qualified specialist
once. A later repair of the contested-record hearing keeps its original date
and remains a missed deadline in this comparison.

The one uncommitted unit in A and B cannot also finance C. The missed hearing
in C is a consequence of the assumed staffing and deadlines. It gives no power
to choose which resident loses relief. If C can lawfully release shared spend,
hire and qualify the successor and accept the work before those dates, compare
that funded variant. A second service brand, a public acquisition or a duplicated
model with the same controlling operator does not meet the denial scenario.
An independent successor can use scoped shared source evidence via an accepted
route. Each owner must retain the lawful evidence and capability it needs.

Select B as the allocation of authority and ordinary capacity. Compare the
optional spend separately. At the same envelope, it must beat A's additional
staffed language access on actual comprehension, timely receiving and correction
for the same people, including those who decline AI. Until it does, fund A's
ordinary repair for this case. If B qualifies, give the shared operator a separate
budget and preserve both the independent floor and accessible direct route.
After withdrawal, consider redirecting the shared allocation only when it is
legally released and additional qualified staff can actually be paid and received.
Any protected need identified by the trial enters the responsible ordinary
owner's account with its original date. It cannot be relabeled as optional.

## Institutional reference and evidence limit

Estonia's Information System Authority describes a shared exchange layer in
which platform interoperability and agreements permit data sharing while the
data owner retains control. This illustrates a narrower distinction between
support and authority. The exchange description does not establish independent
court or election operation through loss of that layer. [Estonian Information System Authority on X-tee](https://www.ria.ee/en/state-information-system/data-exchange-platforms/data-exchange-layer-x-tee)

The UK National Audit Office's 2024 report links AI adoption to workforce
changes, funding, legacy systems and data. Its findings support pricing these
inputs together. They do not establish the cost ratios or service results
above. [UK National Audit Office on AI in government](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-government/)

The first real comparison needs the same original deadlines, signed duties,
independent receiving authorities and named paid roles across at least one
replacement cycle. Test controller denial in a protected exercise without
withholding a due act. Seek qualified direct evidence and voluntary accounts
from residents who need language and disability access and from staff doing
the work. No constructed count establishes performance in an actual country.
