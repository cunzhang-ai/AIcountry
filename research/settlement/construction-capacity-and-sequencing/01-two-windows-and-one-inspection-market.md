# Two public starts in one construction and inspection market

Design356 · Constructed comparison under
[IV33–IV38](../../country/C06/63/08-construction-capacity-and-sequencing.md).

A region has two work windows. Each has ten qualified crew slots and
four independently staffed inspection slots. The two capacities are
non-interchangeable. Protected public water and existing-school work
already require six crew and two inspections in window one, then
five and two in window two. Committed privately financed works need
two crew and one inspection in each window. Those uses are distinct
from public appropriations.

Two optional public stages, accessible housing adaptation and a
depot electrification stage, each need two crew and one inspection.
Both can start in window one if assessed against the base separately.
Together the requirement is twelve crew and five inspections. There
are enough public funds to pay for both stages, but neither the crew
nor inspection ceiling can be expanded by the vote alone.

| Choice | Window one crew and inspections | Window two crew and inspections | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bind both in window one | 12 / 5 | 7 / 3 | Two crew slots and one inspection slot are missing |
| Stage housing first, depot second | 10 / 4 | 9 / 4 | Both fit at the declared cash and staffing limits |
| Same staging after an additional private commitment in window two | 10 / 4 | 10 / 5 | Crew fits, one independent inspection is missing |
| Hold the unbound depot after that change | 10 / 4 | 8 / 4 | Due work and private commitments fit; depot waits |

Housing is chosen first in this case because usable adaptations
are needed before the wet season. The depot remains operational under
its previously funded safe schedule. This timing is an explicit
assumption, not a general priority for one sector.

Window one has 20 public cash units. Protected work costs 8, and
each new stage would cost 5 if opened then. The proposed simultaneous
18-unit programme is affordable in cash but undeliverable. Staging
uses 13 in window one. Window two has 14; continuing work costs 8 and
the postponed depot costs 6, including the extra unit for delay.
This gives no right to transfer window-one balances. After the new
private commitment, a separate qualified inspector from outside the
region could add one slot without displacing another duty, but would cost
2 additional public units in window two and has no available lawful
source there. The schedule therefore holds the unbound depot. It
neither postpones the existing private work nor counts its deposit
or inspectors as public resources.

A structural rival invests in a separately staffed public inspection
service. Compare its recurring cost, qualification lead time and
independence against staged work and a contracted alternate. The
case assumes no qualified public team is ready within these two
windows. Nothing in the comparison prices an actual region,
guarantees a construction date or estimates private crowding out.
