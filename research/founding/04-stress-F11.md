# Whole-country failure exercise F11

*Part of [Founding and whole-country stress](../FOUNDING-AND-STRESS.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

## F11. A territorial-status demand during a shared-water and service dispute

**Authored scenario.** Several local councils and community institutions call
for a new autonomous region after a disputed water allocation. Their proposed
map includes a city whose residents disagree, a small Indigenous community
with an unresolved land claim, a downstream service area, a hospital drawing
workers from both sides and the main payment connection. A national cabinet
labels the movement a security risk and threatens to pause equalization grants.
A regional leader announces that a local vote will immediately transfer the
water network, police command, public debt and citizenship. Neither assertion
has an enacted source of authority in this scenario.

**Continuing protection.** Peaceful advocates, opponents and nonparticipants
retain expression, association, safety, ordinary voting rights, courts and
essential services. Existing councils, water permits, ecological duties,
contracts, grants, pensions and court orders continue under current law.
The water steward and local providers must keep safe life-sustaining supply
within actual capacity; a disputed map cannot extinguish downstream claims.
The cabinet's threat and the leader's unilateral transfer require prompt
legal challenge and executable interim orders. Police cannot be placed under
a new command by announcement. If actual scarcity exists, C09's preadopted
reviewable priorities and the elected budget route apply to all areas.

**Process chain.** A competent receiver acknowledges the proposal without
deciding that the claimant institutions speak for everyone. Accessible
verification records petitions and council decisions, other maps and affected
rights-holders; individuals can challenge exclusion without compulsory ethnic
registration. Elected national and local delegates and independently chosen
rights-holder representatives compare nonterritorial safeguards, autonomy,
boundary options and no change. An interpreter, facilitator or AI map tool
cannot set the agenda or evidence standard alone. A court can require fair
notice, evidence preservation, lawful grants and protection of prior rights;
it cannot select the water allocation or order sovereignty into existence.

**Final authority and delivery.** A consultative vote has only its published
procedural effect. A final text would need the prior constitutional and
affected-area authorizations, rights review and any applicable external-law
route. Before it could commence, named successors and funding must cover
the network, hospital, workers, current patients, bank/payment access,
water and habitat obligations, debts, pensions, security records and pending
cases. Separate local and national minorities need practical opposition and
remedies after the change. If negotiation fails, the present law and duties
remain, with published reasons, interim service protection and a path for a
later lawful proposal.

**What remains unproved.** No territory, people, rights-holder institution,
water system, court, funding or independent mediator has been identified.
The constitutional amendment thresholds and affected-area definition might
permit central obstruction or regional exclusion; joint facilitation may be
captured; an order may go unfunded. Original local accounts, adverse voices,
then-current law and real service capacity would be needed to assess those
risks. This exercise does not establish that the selected process is just or
operational.
