# Separate a popular access reform from a different grant power

Design333 · Constructed comparison for [CRV01–CRV05](../../country/C14/01/04-separate-constitutional-choices.md)

A sponsor proposes a constitutional right to usable election access
and a change giving ministers a new appointment power over a public
grants board. The access reform can operate through existing qualified
and funded election bodies. The grants board can be considered under
its own text. Neither depends on the other. A shared label of public
participation gives no legal reason to bind their approval.

Assume each later exact question has independent lawful authorization
and review. These are authored counts, not inferences from a joint ballot.

| Question | Lawful route | Valid yes / no | Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A. Usable election access | Assembly route, after the required Assembly approval | 700 / 300 | Passes the public majority. Operative effect still needs the lawful prospective date and any applicable RR finding. |
| B. New ministerial grants-board appointment power | Direct petition, after its own valid support | 590 / 410 | Fails the three-fifths public threshold. |
| C. An additional review service for the new A procedure | Assembly route, separately authorized, with exact lawful dependence on A taking effect by the stated date | 550 / 450 | Passes the public majority. It can operate only if A becomes operative on time. |

C's separate proposal is unnecessary for A because A can operate using
its lawful ordinary review. C needs A because its assigned role is
specific to the procedure A creates. The question and exact proposed
text both disclose that dependency. If only A passes, A can operate
on its qualified schedule. If only C passes, C remains without effect
and its authority lapses at the declared date. If both pass, the
combination must still meet each applicable legal date and protection.
No single tally supplies consent for both.

Compare an omnibus vote, independent votes with exact dependencies,
and strict severance that forbids all dependencies. The omnibus vote
conceals whether electors accept either specific power. A ban on
dependencies can make a genuine implementation option impossible.
Select separate authorizations and narrow, explicit conditions. If a
bundle already has the only petition signatures, a court can reject
unlawful grouping, but cannot reuse that support to certify newly
separated questions. Independently authorized untouched text follows
its own timetable. This case assumes no judicial power to change
the threshold or the protected succession.
