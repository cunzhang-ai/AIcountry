# A correction plan must end in a remedy people receive

*Design197 · Evidence and cases for [SR01–SR12](../../country/C08/30-repeated-public-failure-and-remedy.md).*

## Separate service, legal and financial outcomes

In *Black Sash* in 2017, South Africa's Constitutional Court used its
own constitutional powers to protect social grants during a procurement
failure. Its order continued the existing payment arrangement for a
specified 12-month period, imposed privacy limits, required quarterly
reports and provided for independent evaluation and financial
verification. A 2021 order in the related litigation required missing
documents and updated verification for the financial part of the
earlier order. That later order also recounts how an earlier assurance
of a ready successor had led to discharge of supervision before the
claimed readiness proved false. These orders illustrate different
deliverables and the difficulty of verifying each. They do not show
the receipt of every grant or establish another country's court power.
The candidate keeps existing lawful procurement and source limits.
[2017 judgment and order](https://collections.concourt.org.za/items/cf9689de-71a3-41fb-82ab-9ba83ab81183)
· [2021 verification order](https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZACC/2021/5.pdf)

The U.K. National Audit Office's 2021 Windrush compensation investigation
is a separate administrative case. At the March 2021 boundary, it
reported 2,163 claims on the system, of which 414 had reached the
payment stage in its analysis. The department had paid 14.3 million
GBP to 633 people, including people who could receive further amounts.
An internal total of 824 final decisions could not be reconciled by
the auditors. These are different units and stages. None can be
subtracted from another to obtain the number of eligible unpaid people.
[NAO report, summary paragraphs 10–13](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Investigation-into-the-Windrush-compensation-scheme-.pdf)

Of 11.6 million GBP paid after the December 2020 changes, NAO estimated
2.6 million in preliminary payments and 4.24 million in increased
awards to previous payees. Their combined 6.84 million is about 59%
of that period's value. This explains why a faster rise in payments
did not measure the same thing as final case completion. The report
also contrasts a 30-staff-hour initial planning estimate with a
154-staff-hour average through payment approval, and examines the
subset reaching that stage. It is evidence against casually reusing
a queue's assumed effort. It is not a new staffing price or proof of
the effect of judicial supervision. [NAO findings and scope](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-repeated-public-failure-and-remedy/sources.md)

## One joint receipt test

Suppose 100 established and unstayed benefits fall due in the same
period. These are authored cases and their distinct payment obligations,
not forecasts. The initial channel records all as dispatched. Actual
receipt by the original deadline is confirmed for 90, and reliable
evidence shows the remaining ten missed it. A lawful alternate delivers
six of the ten late. The other four have no verified receipt by review.

| Category | Count | Duty at review |
|---|---:|---|
| Received on time | 90 | Verify source and retain the original date |
| Received late | 6 | Payment received. Preserve the lateness and any remaining remedy |
| Receipt unverified | 4 | Keep open and determine the exact barrier without paying twice |
| **Missed original deadline** | **10** | Includes six already paid late. It is not an additional group of obligations. |

A dashboard could report 100 dispatched or 96 eventually received.
Neither number establishes full receipt on time or proves the next
payment cycle will work. SR orders review of the shared cause and an
accepted next-cycle path where recurrence is plausible. Actual cash,
proportionate recipient confirmation and known excluded people remain
part of the evidence.

## Stress the authority as well as the queue

- **An able official withholds the settled list.** Keep the precise
  order and deadlines. The already authorized independent executor
  releases the record or exact due payment within B19/E15. If that
  alternate depends on the same sole credential, report the blocked
  remedy and seek lawful relief. Liability needs its own fair proof.
- **Money or qualified care is unavailable.** Pay or deliver what is
  lawfully feasible. Record who still waits and the reason, present
  lawful funding or receiving choices to the elected body, and keep
  earlier due rights and independent protection. A shortage is not
  deemed refusal. An alternate license cannot supply the care itself.
- **A contractor promises it will be ready.** Verify rights, actual
  funded staff, original records, opening cash and receiving before
  closing the particular oversight scope. Protect the earlier service
  path within lawful terms. A signed promise cannot renew an expired
  contract or establish an accepted successor.
- **The first claimants are served but the same practice continues.**
  Apply an operative correction to the common rule and provide direct
  notice and intake for others. Keep their individual facts and
  remedies separate. Partial closure or expiry of special oversight
  does not waive outstanding claims.

## Compare the remedy models

| Choice | What it offers | What can still fail |
|---|---|---|
| Individual orders and improved internal administration | Lower supervision and direct case relief | A common cause survives and requires repeated litigation |
| Agency repair under legislative and public audit | Elected resource control and ordinary professional discretion | A minister can defer a binding duty and a report may not yield relief |
| Court-enforced bounded plan, selected for proved repeat failure | Requires an accountable lawful means and verifies actual delivery without assigning policy to the court | Needs independent staff and real evidence. Repeated extensions can become costly or intrusive. |
| Court-appointed operational manager | Can bypass an entrenched failure in systems that authorize it | Concentrates executive power, creates funding and accountability risks and still needs an actual receiver |

The selected design keeps direct personal relief and audit in every
case. Exceptional oversight is proportionate, evidence-bound and finite.
Actual source, performance and public acceptance remain to be tested.
