# Observed task gains and local labour effects

[Technology transition](README.md)

Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond study a staggered rollout of a
generative assistant at a customer-support firm. In the final paper
they report 15% more issues resolved per hour on average with tool
access, with substantial differences among workers. The observation
concerns a task and a firm. It does not supply a national gain,
actual wage or fiscal effect, or a coefficient for qualified clinical
work. Use the final-paper estimate for that stated scope.
[Original paper in the Quarterly Journal of Economics](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044)

Acemoglu and Restrepo estimate local employment and wage effects
from industrial robot exposure across United States commuting zones
over 1990–2007. Their final paper reports negative estimated
employment and wage effects in more exposed local markets. This is
a different technology, period and research design. It supports
checking local loss and national aggregation separately. It does
not predict displacement from a particular generative model.
[Final paper, author's institutional copy](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Robots%20and%20Jobs%20-%20Evidence%20from%20US%20Labor%20Markets.p.pdf)

The studies give different, potentially coexisting mechanisms.
Their results do not determine the trigger, tax incidence or the
preferred country package. In a real comparison, check actual
task quality, local earnings, sales, wages, source law, paid
collections and alternative explanations before inferring a gain.
