# C13. How the whole design works in a person's life: Whole-country stress paths

*Part of [C13. How the whole design works in a person's life](../C13-lived-experience.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The first national planning comparison now uses one invented population and three-year
budget for retained capacity, service expansion and an unconditional cash supplement.
Under its assumptions, all three fit the reference financing limits, but retention
and the cash supplement leave growing service-workload gaps. The service expansion
also fails a revenue/need shock and a separate care-workforce shock. These findings
support explicit shortfall duties and qualified expansion within that earlier
scenario. They do not cost the later electoral, monetary, production, essential
systems, disaster, time/leave, housing/mobility or public-evidence commitments
together, so they do not certify the present design's affordability, staffing,
adequacy or superiority. Exact assumptions and results belong to the
[shared settlement](../../SOCIAL-FISCAL-SETTLEMENT.md); the consolidated current
account remains a required lawful-adoption and readiness test.

The same model assumes debt refinancing, bank payments and access to imported inputs
without demonstrating them. Losing a refinancing rollover can leave its service
package below the model's cash buffer even if the annual appropriation is unchanged.
The [political-economy review](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-10-political-economy-review/analysis.md) therefore
adds bank, external-payment, production and physical-resource paths to the reference
design; it does not certify that any real country can operate them.

The integrated [failure assessment](../../FOUNDING-AND-STRESS.md) then connects delayed
election certification, a day-91 budget lapse, simultaneous fiscal and workforce
loss, a local service/AI outage, an emergency used against opposition, official
violence with evidence obstruction, and joint housing-credit/payment failure. Each
case distinguishes lawful authority, actual capacity and the point where common
control defeats the safeguard. The cases reveal failure routes; they do not
demonstrate that operating institutions will withstand them.

The eighth case joins armed attack, a contested election and a payment outage.
Standing defense continues under prior law, but neither disputed succession nor
an ally's request authorizes a continuing foreign campaign. The Assembly's
alternate sitting, civilian shelter, fuel, classified evidence, independent
investigation and lawful transfer of command must work together; an AI target
profile and a reserve entry on paper supply neither legality nor capacity.

The ninth case joins a disputed child-identity record, movement across localities
and intimate violence. It tests whether the child and carer can reach safe housing,
school, health care and money before a registry or parentage dispute is settled,
while an independent court promptly reviews protection and any removal. The [cross-local household exercise](../../settlement/cross-local-continuity/02-household-across-a-boundary.md) assigns real receiving, source and competing backup. Preserving
the first claim date matters, but no database entry supplies a safe placement.

The tenth case joins a court vacancy, a budget lapse and executive refusal to
obey urgent relief. It asks whether an ordinary court still has lawful limited
competence, whether a claimant can obtain counsel and original records, and
whether treasury and a named civil officer perform the order. No missed
appointment deadline creates a cabinet judge, and no court can turn a genuine
shortage into staff or cash by calling its order self-executing.

The eleventh case follows a resident who supports regional autonomy, works at
a hospital across the proposed boundary and shares care for a child whose
school is on the other side. The resident needs a lawful voice in the status
process and continued wages, school access, care, payments and legal remedies
before and after any vote. A neighbor opposing autonomy and a community with
an older land claim need their own representation and protection, not an
assumption that the first territorial majority speaks for them. A clear
consultative ballot can open negotiation; it cannot deliver the next day's
hospital staff, water or court jurisdiction. F11 tests whether both levels
preserve services and peaceful dissent while the status question remains open.

Another cross-system failure is a dominant private controller that employs residents,
finances a governing party, distributes most local news and operates the public
payment interface. After an electoral defeat it withholds opposition reach and
threatens workers and service continuity. The design's response is cumulative
disclosure and contestable political-finance enforcement, protected organizing,
plural information routes, enforceable procurement exit and a funded public-service
continuity path. A court may order access or stop retaliation, but success requires
independent investigators, practical alternative channels, payment operators and
staff able to carry the order out. A public takeover without those capacities could
merely move the bottleneck to the incumbent. This is an authored stress case, not an
observed outcome or a warrant for emergency censorship or confiscation.

An older resident with intermittent paid work and years of family care then
faces a disputed earnings record, a price shock and closure of the local care
provider. The basic pension and interim needs floor must arrive while the
earnings credit is reviewed; the social-protection office preserves source
records and an independent appeal. The care authority supplies a safe
replacement rather than treating a relative's unpaid time as the reserve.
Treasury publishes the extra cash, staff and inflation exposure for young and
old cohorts; the Assembly, rather than an actuary or AI model, chooses any
prospective financing change under existing claims. The case fails if a
payment dashboard is green while the person has neither usable income nor
the promised care. This is an authored test, not an observed outcome.

A government then claims shorter service queues and higher average income
before an election. Original records show that denied applicants, people
forced to move and a closed clinic were removed from the denominator; an
imported input also shifted serious harm abroad. The C11 evidence service
preserves both definitions and missing cases, outside investigators challenge
the causal story, and the C14 public hearing records opposition and affected
people's alternatives. A better headline cannot extend the cabinet's mandate,
erase individual remedies or authorize new spending. This case tests
measurement and answerability, not a real country's observed performance.

In another authored case, one sponsor releases thousands of agents into a
care-allocation consultation while a resident submits a spoken objection
through an unassisted route. The sponsor's volume does not become thousands
of independent mandates; the office preserves its distinct arguments and
funding but must also hear the resident's overlooked access problem. During
a network outage, two local agent groups each try to reserve the same
available care worker, and one principal revokes an agent that cannot yet
receive notice. Only prior nonoverlapping authority can support local
execution; valid acts, notice, unmet claims and errors need reconciliation
and review. A synchronized record or AI summary cannot create the missing
worker or turn the resident's silence into consent.

A drought and grid failure then leave one generator and limited fuel for a
clinic, water pumping, a cooling shelter and food delivery while the usual
Assembly chamber is unavailable. C06's prior-law plan supplies only bounded
first-hour allocations and a common stock record; the alternate elected
sitting must decide a new material tradeoff. The supplying locality, patients,
people unable to queue, downstream users and future water needs cannot be
omitted from the decision. If the generator never reaches a site or the
reviewer cannot access the denial record, neither an enacted priority nor an
AI forecast establishes delivered protection. This is an authored test of
the [cross-system scarcity choice](../C06/05-cross-system-scarcity.md).

A further workload test puts the annual budget, several material delegated
rules, a contested shared-water plan and treaty authorization before the
same Assembly committee while opposition and affected residents need time
to inspect the underlying records. C03's public capacity account must expose
which due decision was displaced and who controls the queue. Ordinary local
work continues within prior law; delay neither approves the water plan nor
transfers a legislative vote to cabinet. This authored collision does not
prove that a new elected region would be better in an actual territory.

A water authority then proposes a fast housing and industry expansion using
an aquifer that may not recover within the next generation. C09 requires a
lawful scope decision, original water observations, repair and lower-use
alternatives, downstream testimony and a credible funded supply and recovery
path before an irreversible withdrawal. Current households' housing needs
remain in the comparison. A court can stop an unlawful permit but cannot
choose the development plan; a later sunset cannot refill the aquifer. This
authored case tests future options, not actual water availability.

In another authored stress, a major employer replaces tasks with AI while
profits accrue through an offshore provider. Local wages and pension
contributions fall, care demand persists and the public tax yield does not
match the advertised productivity gain. C05–C07 must keep due claims,
expose household and service capacity, and bring a funded choice to the
Assembly. A training certificate is not a job; a robot-tax forecast is not
cash. The case assumes neither permanent unemployment nor AI abundance.

These examples explain institutional connections, not observed outcomes. Success must
be assessed through security, freedom, usable services, discretionary time, distribution,
ecological conditions and effective public control, including harms and missing voices.
