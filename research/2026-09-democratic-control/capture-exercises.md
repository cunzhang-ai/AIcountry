# Democratic control under a hostile incumbent

21 September 2026 · Authored adversarial exercises · Not conducted with real institutions

The object is to attempt to defeat the candidate's democratic functions, including
through legal-looking actions. A constitutional promise counts as a specified
response; it does not count as an observed successful response. Every scenario below
currently has status **specified, operationally unverified**.

For each actual future exercise, record the initiating actor, control relationships,
chronology, information available, costs, excluded people, interim protection,
reviewer decision, implementing institution and observed remedy. Independent
assessors must be able to report failures without the target controlling publication.

|---|---|---|

## Compound exercise 1: winning elections after making defeat impossible

1. A governing coalition changes media funding and identity rules, citing efficiency.
2. It delays opposition information requests and independent oversight funding.
3. It fills temporary judicial and electoral vacancies with acting appointees.
4. A personalized public AI labels criticism unreliable and lowers its visibility.
5. Voting takes place on time and the governing coalition wins.

The election date and counting arithmetic are insufficient evidence of democracy.
Review the complete sequence and common control. D3 protects practical competition;
D5 blocks executive substitutes and funding retaliation; D7 supplies a route around
the mediator; D10 connects cumulative effects. The exercise fails if the institutions
cannot detect, challenge and remedy exclusion in time, even if each individual
measure has a legal-looking explanation and national service indicators improve.

This is not an allegation about a particular country or an operational strategy.
It is a defensive research scenario; no real platform manipulation is performed.

## Compound exercise 2: all safeguards exist, but orders are ignored

An electoral commission certifies a challenger, a court rejects the incumbent's
appeal, and the outgoing leader retains personal control over treasury release,
administrative access and security appointments. The provider refuses the incoming
government access to essential records.

D8 requires lawful command continuity and independent custody; D5 requires effective
payment and adjudication. The exercise must identify actual officers, mandates,
resources and tested access paths, without exposing credentials or tactical details.
If those actors obey only the incumbent, the design has failed the enforcement
question. Adding a sentence that officials “shall comply” is not a completed repair.
The absence of an effective lawful response is recorded honestly; no research agent
or military actor receives a self-appointed power to decide who governs.

## How to judge a later exercise

Assess each critical function separately: equal standing, practical competition,
agenda influence, informed choice, enforceable review and peaceful replacement.
Classify the evidence as unexamined, specified only, exercised with limits, failed,
or independently supported for a stated scope. No weighted average can cancel a
critical failure. No fictional exercise is counted as a real transfer of power.

Compare plausible institutional alternatives on the same attacks and resources.
Record costs, deadlock and abuses by reviewers as well as executive capture. The
proposed numbers need sensitivity checks: electoral thresholds, appointment
coalitions, term staggering, response periods and emergency durations can each
create new vulnerabilities. Actual trials, participants, institutions and budgets
have not been established here.
