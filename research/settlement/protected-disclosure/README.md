# A public institution must be able to hear that it is wrong

*Design202 · Comparison for [WB01–WB12](../../country/C08/31-protected-disclosure-and-evidence.md).*

A nurse, analyst, contractor or service user can be the only person
able to explain a recurring failure. A chart, official figure or AI
summary may look credible even if its original records are incomplete.
The design must preserve the report, make independent testing possible
and protect the person before a firing, lost shift or unsafe disclosure
makes that process unusable.

| Arrangement | Why it can work | Failure to test |
|---|---|---|
| Retain or repair existing internal and sector channels | Existing sector competence and constructive informal relationships may correct a problem with little burden | A report can be sent to its subject, a contract can hide control, or the only recorder can identify and retaliate against the source |
| Common civil protection with multiple qualified receivers, selected | Direct protection reaches workers and people dependent on a service, while existing qualified bodies test facts and courts decide contested relief | More handoffs and record handling can leak identities. Actual paid outside review and independent execution are necessary |
| One new national disclosure authority | A visible entry, specialist capacity and more uniform practice | One office and vendor can control all source identities and gatekeep corrections. Paid, independent replacement and external access would still be necessary |
| Public disclosure as the default | Can expose imminent harm and bypass capture when other routes are unsafe or ineffective | Risks innocent people's privacy, safety, fair process and access to future confidential evidence. Proportionate disclosure and a protected outside route are both needed |

Select a common civil floor with outside access, earlier effective
interim relief and a same-decision defense. Keep voluntary informal
repair where it works. Use ordinary qualified investigators, auditors,
courts, payment executors and independent media in their own roles.
A report can correct public evidence, while the authorized officials
and affected public retain their actual rights and choices.

The stronger objections are tactical complaints, overbroad evidence
retention, burden on small providers and interim orders against
lawful safety action. Use reasonable grounds, restricted records,
genuine causal proof, independent fair hearing, scope-limited remedies
and safe alternatives. A protection request does not make the report
true. Keep powers narrow enough that the protection system cannot
itself become an instrument of censorship or patronage.

No remedy source, independence, staffing or better lives are
established by the examples. An observed case can justify revising
the design even when it does not estimate prevalence or causal effect.
