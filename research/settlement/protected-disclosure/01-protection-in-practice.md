# Public disclosure, retaliation and interim relief in existing law

*Design202 · Original comparative sources and scope limits.*

## The European Union directive

Directive (EU) 2019/1937, within its defined material and work-related
scope, uses reasonable grounds at the time of reporting and allows a
direct external route. Article 15 states distinct conditions for
protected public disclosure. Article 16 limits disclosure of source
identity with necessary lawful exceptions. Article 21(5) shifts the
burden after a reporting person establishes the report or disclosure
and detriment, and Article 21(6) includes access to interim relief.
Article 22 protects the rights of the person concerned. Article 3
retains separate classified-information, privilege and other rules.
National implementation and actual protection are separate questions.
[Consolidated directive, 30 December 2024](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A02019L1937-20241230)

The candidate chooses a broader common domestic protection, including
some affected people outside a work relationship. It requires a proven
contributing factor before a stronger same-decision defense. That
burden design is a different choice from the Directive's presumption.
The seven-day acknowledgement, 30-day status and 60-day update are
candidate procedure values, with earlier actual duties preserved.
They do not reproduce a foreign investigation deadline.

## U.S. federal personnel law and one stay

In its specific federal personnel scope, 5 U.S.C. § 1221(e) uses a
contributing-factor test and bars corrective action for the measure if
the agency proves by clear and convincing evidence it would have
taken the same personnel action absent the protected disclosure.
The candidate tests the same timing and scope explicitly and extends
the approach through a proposed ordinary civil action.
[House statutory text](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title5-section1221)

In December 2024 the Office of Special Counsel reported a Merit
Systems Protection Board grant of a 45-day stay for a Veterans
Affairs employee whom it said the agency removed in March 2022.
OSC described allegations about agency-owned supplies and a Board
finding of reasonable grounds at the provisional stage. This is a
reported interim result. The dates measure elapsed time from
reported removal to the announcement, without showing the filing
date, response-time compliance, ultimate merits, or restored pay
or work. This is one institutional example, with neither a national
effect estimate nor a term imported into the candidate.
[Official OSC account](https://www.osc.gov/news/2024-12-17/mspb-grants-osc-s-stay-request-for-va-whistleblower-removed-from-her-job/)

## Protected public communication and serious competing interests

In *Halet v. Luxembourg*, the European Court of Human Rights Grand
Chamber found an Article 10 violation in a case concerning an
employee's public-interest disclosure of confidential tax documents
to a journalist. It weighed the public contribution against the
harmful effects and considered the criminal sanction. Its contextual
test addresses the route, evidence and disclosure effects. The case
gives no general permission to acquire or publish unrelated
confidential material. The proposed country's separate protected
routes, ordinary speech and narrow judicial restraints keep their
own legal scope. [Grand Chamber judgment, 14 February 2023](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/docx/pdf?filename=CASE+OF+HALET+v.+LUXEMBOURG.pdf&id=001-223259&library=ECHR)
