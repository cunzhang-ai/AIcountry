# Decision record: an ecological transition people can govern and survive

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 46 research selection; not enacted law

## Baseline and bounded country decision

The [design-45 predecessor](predecessor-design-45/README.md) preserves the
current design before this choice. C09 requires ecological limits, reliable
energy, land review and future-option protection; C06 has pollution charges
and one resource account. The older `energy.climate-energy` manuscript in the
[local source bundle](../2026-09-readiness/source-documents.json) proposes
emissions budgets, system-cost comparison, permits, restoration and worker
support, but gives budget-setting to an AI legislature. The current working
design neither accepts that authority nor yet supplies an integrated elected
transition path. A ministry could claim an emissions goal while utilities,
transport, housing, agriculture and industry each assume another will deliver
it; a carbon price could raise bills before a usable alternative exists.

The bounded question is how a future democratic country chooses, finances,
operates and corrects a climate-and-nature transition while preserving
physical reliability, equal political standing and enforceable distinct
ecological rights. The actual territory, baseline, population, treaty status,
energy system and affected public are unknown. No numeric national emissions,
habitat or water target can be inferred from a synthetic scenario. Workers,
households, farmers, localities, Indigenous and other collective rights-holders,
downstream people, foreign suppliers and future people bear different costs.
None has participated in this AIcountry selection.

## Competing explanations and packages

The principal failure might be an unpriced external cost, insufficient grid
and trained labor, incumbent political capture, weak ecological law, imported
harm, unequal household burdens or no durable cross-sector mandate. Original
emissions and ecosystem observations, system operation, bills, firms, labor,
permits, imports and testimony are needed to discriminate. Several AI
summaries of one inventory do not create independent evidence.

| Package | Causal route and possible advantage | Material objection or failure |
|---|---|---|
| Retain C09 floors and project-by-project decisions | Protects specific habitats and local rights without a new national plan | Cross-sector emissions and infrastructure timing remain unassigned; sequential permits can cumulatively defeat a long-run path |
| Price-led transition with household rebates | Can reveal some external costs and allow decentralized adjustment | A price alone cannot build a grid or guarantee alternatives; low-income people may pay first, revenue falls as pollution falls, and habitat loss is not fungible |
| Expert or AI-set hard caps with automatic sector closures | May be fast and numerically clear | Transfers burdens and rationing to technical actors, can cut essential supply, and may conceal errors or imported damage |
| **Selected: elected multiyear path with separate ecological floors, sector packages and an integrated resource account** | Makes shared obligations and distribution visible, preserves public correction and supports real replacement capacity | Legislative workload, uncertain measurement, incumbent delay and investment cost can cause a missed path or unfair burden |
| Single public-owner transition | Can coordinate investment and service mandates | Requires capable funding and management, may centralize employment, permits, information and political power; plural provision could work better |

The [C09 rule](../country/C09/08-climate-and-ecological-transition.md)
selects a statutory multiyear emissions budget with measured sector scope,
not a constitutional number or AI-controlled switch. Habitat, water, air and
restoration retain separate enforceable conditions. Cabinet proposes coherent
sector alternatives; the Assembly authorizes the path, tax, spending and
material changes; local elected bodies choose lawful siting. Sector
inspectors execute specified permits and safety rules, courts review law,
and source custodians plus C11 preserve contestable evidence. The
[C06 account](../country/C06/08-ecological-transition-finance.md) traces
cash, workers, grid, imports and household effects. The [C13
exercise](../country/C13/11-transition-with-grid-delay.md) tests a clean
power target that precedes reliable delivery.

## Support and its limits

The [IPCC AR6 synthesis for policymakers](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2023/03/Doc4_Approved_AR6_SYR_SPM.pdf)
assesses distributional effects, participation and just transitions; its
[mitigation policy chapter](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-13/)
examines packages across sectors and levels of government. The [IEA grid
assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-grids-and-secure-energy-transitions)
shows why generation additions and actual network delivery must be separated.
The [Convention on Biological Diversity framework](https://www.cbd.int/gbf/default.shtml)
uses distinct biodiversity goals, monitoring and national planning. The
[UNFCCC NDC explanation](https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/nationally-determined-contributions-ndcs)
describes Party commitments and transparent successive contributions. These
are primary scientific and institutional comparators, not a treaty-status
finding for AIcountry or proof that the selected institutions outperform
other countries. The predecessor's local manuscript is a design input,
not enacted law and not independent empirical support.

## Power, compatibility and continuation

The elected Assembly, rather than the former AI legislature, must authorize
any binding national emissions budget and its exact lawful revision. This
requires a founding constitution and source-law reconciliation, with no
research edition changing existing permits, contracts or taxes. Accrued
wages, pensions, land and collective rights, valid environmental duties,
service claims and restoration liabilities survive a transition. A
prospective closure requires legal basis, reliable replacement or an openly
reported shortfall, worker and local-income effects, and fair review.
Operators cannot grant themselves climate exceptions through dispatch.
Independent evidence and audit appointments, funding, source access and
execution must not share one sponsor with the biggest affected firm or
Cabinet. Opposition and affected people need practical alternate analyses.

A missed statutory path calls for publication and a fresh Assembly package;
it does not automatically switch off a hospital, cut household support or
license excess pollution. Equally, no minister may relabel a missed path as
success by revising the denominator or counting an unverified offset. Courts
can protect the law and require reasons, not supply an energy mix. Where a
valid floor and essential service physically conflict, officials disclose
both duties, use only prior-law interim powers and seek a lawful elected
resolution; no score averaging erases the breach. Cross-border sourcing and
any actual treaty obligation need separate legal and physical accounts.

Objections remain live. Binding budgets can invite symbolic targets, investor
uncertainty or a court-driven politics of infeasible numbers. Price-led
reform could sometimes be cheaper and less bureaucratic. Public ownership
might be necessary for a particular grid or mine, while central ownership
could make political dissent costly. Workers may reject delayed or
insufficient support; environmental rights-holders may reject trading a
habitat for national carbon progress. These values cannot be settled by a
single cost-per-tonne estimate. If an actual jurisdiction's measured options
favor a rival package with better rights, reliability and ecological effect,
this working choice should be replaced rather than adding another layer.

**Evidence:** external primary assessments and an older local proposal,
without AIcountry original inventories or affected participation.
**Research selection:** an elected multiyear path, distinct ecological
floors and coherent sector/finance package. **Legal authorization:** none.
**Operational readiness:** no territory, enacted targets, inspectors, grid,
workforce, supply chain or transition budget. **Observed outcomes:** none;
no superiority claim follows.

The next bounded test is one real jurisdiction's power-and-housing transition
with original emissions, habitat, grid, water, household, labor, local tax,
import and treaty data. Compare the selected package with a price-led and a
public-investment alternative under one feasible timetable, including a
delayed network and rights conflict. Reopen the choice if actual supply
cannot carry essential services, burden protection fails, accounting hides
external or noncarbon harm, or public control cannot correct a missed path.
