# Original sources for the conflict procedure

| Primary source | Verified procedural distinction | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [England and Wales Civil Procedure Rules, Part 3, rules 3.1 and 3.3](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part03) | The rules distinguish a joint trial, consolidation, trial of a separate issue, a stay and variation. They also provide a route for affected parties to challenge specified orders made on the court's own initiative. | Useful examples of separate procedural powers. They do not supply this proposed cross-order jurisdiction, time limit or authority to reroute public resources. |
| [United States Code, 2019 edition, 28 USC §2112(a)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2019-title28/html/USCODE-2019-title28-partV-chap133-sec2112.htm) | The statutory text assigns court selection in a defined set of multiple petitions concerning the same agency order to a judicial panel using random selection. | A reference for preventing party control of assignment. It does not address independent ordinary orders or their joint physical execution. |

The [matched exercise](../settlement/conflicting-public-orders/README.md)
uses authored clocks, acceptances and costs. The sources establish that
these procedural tools can be specified distinctly. They do not establish
coverage, safe actual transport, court staffing or received remedies.
