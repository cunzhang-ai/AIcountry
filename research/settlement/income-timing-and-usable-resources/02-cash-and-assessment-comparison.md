# Equal wages, two different award calendars

This example uses the project's authored I05 reference of 0.5 taper
and a personal disregard of 20 units, with recognized need N of 100
and no other counted resources. No tax, contribution or award rates
from the observed British case are used. All wages here are net and
safely received unless stated otherwise.

A worker normally receives a 100-unit salary in each of two months.
A banking closure moves the second payment into the first month.
Two identifiable salary payments still exist. The early receipt may
help cashflow if the person can retain it, but the assessment rule
affects when and how much support is paid. The dates below identify
ordinary advance awards as finally determined. Treasury must allow
for a forecast correction if the actual event was not known in time.

| Treatment | Month 1 earnings for assessment | Month 2 earnings for assessment | Ordinary awards | Total ordinary awards |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Normally timed wages | 100 | 100 | 60 then 60 | 120 |
| Raw receipt after the early payroll | 200 | 0 | 10 then 100 | 110 |
| One actual regular wage assigned to each scheduled period | 100 | 100 | 60 then 60 | 120 |

The raw treatment loses a chance to apply the disregard in the
second month. Merely waiting for the second award cannot restore all
of the difference. Across 100 identical affected cases, calendar
matching needs 5,000 extra units by the first award date and reduces
the later awards by 4,000 relative to the raw rule. The period total
is 1,000 units higher. The later reduction cannot finance the early
gap without a separately lawful usable source. The same cohort is
used on both sides. These are award and payment timing assumptions
rather than an estimate of national take-up or available cash.

Now test an actual missed first wage. The advance award of 60
has already been received on the 100-wage forecast. Assume no wage
or other resources become usable during month 1, and unchanged
monthly need. Correct the ordinary award to 100, leaving 40 due in
addition to the existing 60. A previously paid lawful emergency
bridge is reconciled only against the same due component. Its
amount and date are determined separately by actual short-window
need and the applicable rule. If the wage is later recovered, verify
the actual payment and apply its source rule in the receipt period.
The example does not recoup the corrected first award by treating
the missed wage as available when it was not.

An unconditional payment of 60 in each period would avoid routine
wage attribution and match the ordinary payment on stable wages in
this fixture. It would leave the missed-wage case needing a further
40 units or an actual equivalent for the same defined need. Compare
the full distribution of unconditional and needs-based payments,
tax sources, claimed and unclaimed protection, independent cash
receipt and paid administrative work before choosing the national
package. The two-month example supplies neither a national price
nor a comparison for different wage cycles, changing hours or
self-employment.
