# Two service areas and one paid cohort

This is an authored comparison of one care role at a common peak. All
options use the same quality, independence and other existing
duties. Unrelated staffing, funding and infrastructure are outside the
measured margin. The receiving area requires 18 accepted shifts and the
origin area 20. The continuation path has 20 receiving shifts in the
initial window and 18 in the later window, because two ordinary shifts
exit by then. The origin has 20 in both windows. The lawful illustrative
increment ceilings are six cash units at start and five recurring units
in the receiving period. Money cannot substitute for a missing person.

| Option | Early cash | Later recurring cost | Early shifts, receiving / origin | Later shifts, receiving / origin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Continue the current funded route | 0 | 0 | 20 / 20 | 18 / 20 |
| Repair working conditions and support voluntary paid return | 3 | 3 | 20 / 20 | 21 / 20 |
| Publicly planned transfer of four, with one origin replacement | 3 | 4 | 20 / 20 | 22 / 17 |
| One paid cohort, with an early paid replacement | 4 | 3 | 18 / 20 | 20 / 20 |
| Retention and the full paid cohort together | 7 | 6 | 18 / 20 | 23 / 20 |
| Retention and a smaller staggered cohort | 6 | 5 | 19 / 20 | 22 / 20 |

The retention option keeps two shifts that would otherwise exit and adds
one willing paid return shift. The example assumes the returner is not
currently providing paid or essential unpaid work that needs replacement.
The transfer moves four already staffed origin shifts later. It pays for
one newly available qualified origin shift. System-wide it adds one shift,
while leaving three due shifts uncovered at origin. Individual workers may
still move under ordinary law. The public plan must fund a real remedy for
the uncovered duty.

Training buys eight offers, six accepted entries, four qualifications,
three accepted paid deployments and two shifts still staffed in the
scored later window. For this exercise each retained deployment supplies
one compatible shift. This is a declared conversion for the fixture, not
an observed retention rate or a legal employment promise. Early training
uses three qualified mentor shifts, adds one funded backfill shift and
counts zero trainees as independent staff. If that replacement fails,
early receiving coverage drops to 17. Starting the same cohort without
a separately verified backup would breach the common protected floor.
Bound trainees' wages and teach-out remain due if the loss follows a
lawful commitment. The combined option would also exceed both cash
ceilings.

The smaller staggered intake costs three early and two recurring units
within the combined total. It buys four offers, three accepted entries,
two qualifications, two accepted deployments and one retained shift.
It needs two early mentor shifts rather than three and keeps one funded
backfill. The retention and the small cohort use distinct staff and money.
If the funded backfill fails and one otherwise retained shift is lost,
the joint option still provides 18 early and 21 later receiving shifts.
The authored costs include the contracted contingency and learner closeout
for that tested case. Additional loss or demand growth can defeat it.
The option uses all six early and all five recurring units.

**First motion under these assumptions.** Fund retention and voluntary
return. Include the smaller staggered cohort as a distinct conditional
release. Before admitting that cohort under the elected ceiling, confirm
qualified mentor schedules, actual cash, coverage under the declared
stress and paid closeout. Keep the larger cohort unstarted. Retention
alone is the lower-cost rival and keeps cash for other needs. If the two
incumbent shifts actually exit despite retention, the return alone gives
19 later shifts under its own declared conditions. Predictable exits
can strengthen the case for an actual replacement path, and a smaller
cohort means some learners must wait or receive another usable offer.

No optional use may treat the same three prospective later retention
shifts as permanently free or the two training shifts as available early.
None of the table's staff counts proves actual visits, safe acceptance,
timely remedies or human outcomes.
