# Higher pay, reduced income support and a care fee

This constructed comparison exercises
[I30–I35](../../country/C05/06/09-coordinated-benefit-withdrawal.md).
A parent uses a staffed care place and qualifies for ordinary income
support. Use after-tax and after-contribution earnings. Holding care
hours, need, other resources and the period fixed, earnings rise from
100 to 110. The separate schedules reduce income support from 30 to
23 and raise the care fee from 10 to 20. All amounts are illustrative.
The figures are matched scenarios, not I05 or a proposed tariff.

| Schedule or response | Net earnings | Income support | Care fee | Usable amount after care fee |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before the increase | 100 | 30 | 10 | 120 |
| Uncoordinated withdrawal | 110 | 23 | 20 | 113 |
| Enacted bridge of seven | 110 | 23 plus 7 | 20 | 120 |
| Coordinated fee schedule | 110 | 23 | 12 | 121 |

The bridge requires actual seven-unit funds for the current period.
With a five-unit cap, two units of the verified gap remain. The
coordinated fee schedule needs eight units in continuing provider
funding compared with the higher fee, assuming the rest of the
provider's costs are unchanged. Both measures require their own
enacted sources. The bridge does not recur beyond the authorized
periods. If it expires and the old fee remains, the same stipulated
earnings and support again leave 113. Count only one correction for
the same loss when the scheduled fee change takes effect.

If the higher wage is forecast and has not arrived, the stipulated
110 is unavailable. Use the separate missed-pay rule and protect the
original due dates. If the staffed place fails, a receipt of 120 or
121 alone cannot establish usable care. Arrange qualified receiving
under the care duty and report unmet need and new costs. The example
does not assume how wages, take-up, work hours, private costs or
availability change in practice.

The [arithmetic check](verify-benefit-withdrawal.py) compares the
different cash results and the missing-care branch. Actual income,
fee schedules, authorities, provider capacity and outcomes remain open.
