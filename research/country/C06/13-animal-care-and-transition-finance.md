# C06. Animal care and transition finance

*Part of [C06 public finance](../C06-public-finance.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The Assembly funds the prospective animal-protection floor in the same
commitment account as food, health, ecology and livelihood duties. It counts
veterinarians, independent inspectors, laboratories, travel, safe temporary
care, court time, disease response, fishery and farm changes, research
alternatives, disaster shelter, continuing animal care and public reporting.
An order to take an animal cannot precede an accepting safe place and a
lawful funding route. Inspector salaries and independence cannot depend on
penalties collected from the people inspected.

Before phasing in a production or research standard, the elected budget shows
the price, worker, small-producer, trade, nutrition and import consequences,
as well as animal outcomes. Assistance can finance feasible conversion,
training, alternate production and income protection under public conditions;
it is not a permanent subsidy for harmful practices or a promise that every
existing facility will remain profitable. A poorer household's essential
food claim and a live animal's minimum care are not made invisible by a
balanced sector ledger. Where a proposed rule lacks actual staff, safe
places or affordable substitutes, law must fund a feasible sequence and
protect animals already affected rather than declare paper compliance.

Emergency disease or ecological action separately records owner loss,
compensation or insurance under enacted law, treatment and care costs,
human health claims and receiving-place capacity. Public money cannot be
awarded solely on an AI welfare score or industry self-certification.
No country animal census, species mix, import chain, appropriations or
transition workforce exists; affordability and welfare improvement are
unverified. Reopen if the shared account hides a human service cut, leaves
animals without care or shifts harm to imports.
