# C09. Land, ecology, infrastructure and resilience: Land, water and shared planning

*Part of [C09. Land, ecology, infrastructure and resilience](../C09-land-and-ecology.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

Public planning connects housing, work, schools, care, transport and ecosystems.
Communities participate in siting and redevelopment; property and displacement remedies
remain enforceable. Existing owners cannot privately decide every national infrastructure
need, and national planners cannot treat affected communities as expendable.
Shared civic and cultural places are also land uses with real maintenance,
access and displacement effects. Their value cannot be inferred from an empty
map or a count of buildings; a contested removal compares usable alternatives
and existing claims before an elected choice under C11's access floor.
Burial, cremation and other lawful [body-care routes](../C08/08-end-of-life-decisions-and-survivors.md)
also need reachable sites, safe transport, land and water protections and
room for different faith and secular practices. A local plan cannot provide
one affordable rite only by displacing another community or hiding
groundwater and maintenance costs.

Plans distinguish land on a map from safe, serviced and reachable land; water
withdrawals from ecological flows and quality; electricity generation from grid
and backup capacity. They record titles, tenancies, customary or collective claims
where applicable, existing permits, prior displacement, flood risk and uncertainty.
Affected tenants, workers, farmers and neighboring communities can challenge
alternatives while siting and scale can still change. The planning body publishes
cumulative effects, gain and loss, maintenance, mitigation, dissent and the capacity
each option actually needs. If councils cannot agree on a material shared allocation,
the Assembly takes the reserved decision under C04 with reasons and rights review. A court can
stop an unlawful plan; it does not choose the country's preferred density or water
use. No approval follows merely from missed time limits.
The [scope rule](../C03/05-assembly-capacity-and-scope.md) keeps a minor
cross-boundary effect within ordinary local competence and counts cumulative,
irreversible shared burdens before a choice is called routine. A plan's
classification can be challenged without letting a court select the site.
If a plan removes a material option that later residents cannot recover,
the [future-options duty](04-future-options-and-irreversibility.md) requires
elected reasons, feasible alternatives and a funded repair or safe exit.

During a shared-area deadlock, existing lawful permits, property claims, service
and ecological duties remain with their named authorities. No new irreversible
allocation follows from inaction; an unmet housing or life-sustaining water duty
still requires lawful interim delivery, funding disclosure and an effective
challenge. The interlocal forum's map must include downstream and other materially
affected people beyond its member councils, with safe evidence access and dissent.
The Assembly's reserved decision cannot erase historic titles, tenancy, customary
claims or restoration liabilities without the applicable process. Repeated
national backlog or excluded communities is a reason to reconsider the territorial
model, including a properly founded elected region, rather than silently handing
the decision to a planner, landowner or first permit holder.

A territorial-status proposal does not freeze ecological flows at a political
line or convert disputed land into unowned national property. Existing and
claimed collective rights, downstream dependence, habitat obligations,
maintenance duties and restoration liabilities must be represented in every
competing map and transition account. A new authority needs lawful consent or
adjudication for rights it does not own, plus a funded shared-basin and network
arrangement before it can change extraction or disconnect essential service.

**Design145 property-power join.** [LA](13-public-acquisition-and-secure-relocation.md)
gives a positive route to acquire necessary public-project rights without letting
planning or a map itself transfer them. Actual collective/downstream/ecological
sources, nonreplaceable protections, title disputes and least-intrusive options
remain. An Assembly allocation decision is not a court's title/possession order.

[PT01–PT24 ordinary property law](../C08/26-property-transfers-and-secured-credit.md)
now distinguishes authorized recorded conveyance/land security, earlier valid
lodgment, actual completion and protected preexisting/customary/tenant rights.
Planning, tax and an apparently empty map confer no title. Permanent private
easements need their actual source/registration; LA acquisition and ecological/
collective duties remain separate. Actual parcels and independent proof are
unknown, not supplied by the new positive transfer rule.

[NR01–NR16 mineral rights](15-finite-resources-and-mineral-rights.md) now makes
separate positive in-situ ownership/grant/depletion/closure choices. Public or
actually ungranted rights need actual title; new discovery/incomplete registry
cannot erase private/collective interests. Land/water/operating permission and
required consent/paid LA acquisition remain distinct from the finite grant.

[WS ordinary water](16-water-allocation-and-service.md) now chooses actual allocation/service roles, connected title-neutral new permissions and old-right-preserving seasonal rules. A plan/permit does not transfer land/water/collective title or foreign authority; actual LA/TS/source/compensation and protected flow duties remain.

[LU01–LU20 ordinary development](17-development-permission-and-serviced-land.md)
now specifies elected plans, enforceable conforming-project permission, actual
serviced phases and attributable costs. Planning remains distinct from title,
water allocation, TD05 construction/occupation and actual paid supply.

[CLX01–CLX06](../C04/01/02-cross-local-effects-and-residual-decisions.md)
gives directly affected people a protected first receipt and an Assembly
disposition for a material shared choice beyond local powers. Separate
water, land, collective rights and safety decisions remain necessary.
