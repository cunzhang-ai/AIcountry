# Primary evidence for qualified and assisted public communication

- Martos and colleagues, JAMA Network Open, 2025, compared a neural
  translation system with professional translations in 148 sections of
  34 actual discharge instructions at a single pediatric hospital.
  The system met noninferiority in some dimensions for Spanish and
  performed worse than professional translation across the assessed
  dimensions in Simplified Chinese, Somali and Vietnamese. The authors
  distinguish meaning, fluency, adequacy and error severity. These
  observed comparisons qualify only the tested written work and system.
  [Original comparative study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2839035).
- Kong and colleagues, BMJ Quality & Safety, assessed ChatGPT-4 and Google
  Translate on 50 sets of written emergency discharge instructions,
  316 sentences, in three languages. The study reports different
  findings per sentence and per complete instruction set, including
  inaccuracies in a material share of complete sets. This comparison
  does not establish safety of an oral, signed, urgent or untested route.
  [Original comparative analysis](https://qualitysafety.bmj.com/content/qhc/35/3/150.full.pdf).
- NHS England's 2025 community-language framework identifies inconsistent
  interpreting access and describes commissioning and monitoring for
  spoken and written languages. Its Accessible Information Standard
  separately describes qualified support for disability-related access.
  These are institutional comparators, not estimates for AIcountry.
  [Community language framework](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/improvement-framework-community-language-translation-and-interpreting-services/)
  · [Accessible Information Standard](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/accessible-information-standard-requirements-dapb1605/).

The local worked case is constructed. None of these records validates
a particular tool, task, language or staffing plan in the proposed country.
