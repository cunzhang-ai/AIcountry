# Social provision as usable time and connected capability

2 October 2026 · Supporting analysis for [country design 5](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md),
especially C04–C07 and C13. This is a research recommendation, not adopted law or
evidence of operational readiness.

## Decision and baseline

The working design already selects national guarantees, local delivery, a named
authority for each service, a joint guarantee-and-delivery budget, an income floor,
and recognition that unpaid care cannot silently fill a staffing gap. Keep those
commitments. The missing national choice is whether a person has an **effective
access claim across services**, or only separate claims that may be impossible to
exercise together. The latter can fail even when every agency truthfully reports
that its own offer was made.

Consider an authored, not observed, case: a working parent and an older disabled
relative are offered a school place, home support, a medical appointment and
housing assistance. The school and appointment are far apart, the care worker's
visit overlaps the parent's shift, and the housing is inaccessible or far from
transport. A cash transfer or one more referral does not make all of these offers
usable. Conversely, a combined plan could become a new gatekeeper that pressures
the family to accept intrusive surveillance, compulsory unpaid care or a single
institution's preferred life arrangement. Both failures need design attention.

The design premise is that care, travel, waiting, administrative work and paid
work all consume finite human time. The empirical question is how often and for
whom incompatible offers, absent capacity, provider selection or lack of cash
cause actual non-use; no population-specific answer is available here. WHO's
[people-centred services framework](https://www.who.int/health-topics/integrated-people-centered-care)
supports coordination, continuity and attention to preferences within and beyond
health care, but does not validate this country's proposed institution. The UN
[Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 19](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-19-living-independently-and-being-included-in-the-community.html)
connects personal assistance and community participation; the [OHCHR housing
guidance](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/en/economic-social-rights/human-right-adequate-housing)
recognizes location as part of adequate housing. These are relevant normative
comparators, not proof of local capacity or an automatic source of AIcountry law.

## Competing diagnoses and institutional packages

| Package | Mechanism and attraction | Failure and power risk |
|---|---|---|
| Retain separate sector guarantees | Professional and local agencies retain clear duties; people can use only the services they want. | No authority has to resolve a conflict between appointment hours, transport, care and school. Each can count a nominal offer as delivery while the person's usable time is exhausted. |
| Add a voluntary navigator, leaving duties unchanged | A local human or replaceable tool helps people apply, schedule and appeal without changing law or professional authority. | It cannot command capacity, funding or compatible schedules. A person may be sent repeatedly among agencies; apparent case closure can conceal unmet need. |
| Create one consolidated national welfare agency | One budget and command chain could settle cross-sector conflicts and exploit shared infrastructure. | It concentrates eligibility, data and provider control; remote rules can erase local conditions and professional judgment. An agency failure affects every service, and monopoly intake can make exit impossible. |
| **Selected research candidate: connected-access duty with distributed provision** | National law defines effective access and continuity; local public authorities plan the actual service network; separate sector authorities keep their obligations. A person can request a joint resolution when the combination fails. | Needs skilled staff, funded coordination, independent review and real capacity. It can still devolve into coercive case management or a paper plan unless consent, privacy, provider incentives and remedies are enforced. |

The candidate addresses a coordination failure without assuming that every unmet
need is caused by coordination. Where qualified staff, safe housing, medicine or
funds are missing, the duty exposes that shortage; it cannot manufacture them.
Where a person rejects a combined plan, separate lawful entitlements remain.

## Proposed national design correction

1. **Define effective access in the enacted guarantee.** The elected Assembly
   specifies, by service, covered need, quality, response limits and the conditions
   of practical use: distance and accessible transport, compatible hours,
   communication, affordability, required support and safe continuity. The law
   does not promise that every preferred arrangement is immediately available.
   It forbids agencies to treat an unusable offer as completed delivery without
   reasons and challenge. A person's unpaid time is a resource in the public
   account, not a free residual that closes the budget.
2. **Join planning, not political or clinical authority.** Each local elected
   authority publishes a place-based access plan connecting housing supply,
   schools, clinics, care, transport and emergency intake, with actual workforce,
   capital, procurement and operating costs. The national authority funds its
   minimum guarantee and equalization, including geographic and high-need costs,
   and retains fallback responsibility if local delivery fails. Sector agencies
   retain clinical, educational, housing and benefit decisions under their laws.
   A designated local convenor must resolve cross-service scheduling and referral
   conflicts and escalate missing capacity, but cannot cancel another agency's
   entitlement or override independent professional judgment.
3. **Make help available without making it compulsory.** A person may ask for a
   joint access plan in a human, paper, telephone or digital channel. The plan
   records only the shared facts needed for the requested coordination, named
   responsible authorities, agreed tasks, deadlines, an interim arrangement and
   how to contest failure. Ordinary interagency disclosure requires a lawful basis
   and proportionate consent or other enacted safeguards; urgent protection
   exceptions must be narrow and reviewable. A person may refuse a joint plan
   without losing sector rights. AI can assist scheduling or explanation under a
   revocable mandate but cannot conclusively decide eligibility or consent.
4. **Protect two distinct sets of care rights.** The recipient chooses among
   lawful supports and retains autonomy and safe continuity. Relatives are not
   treated as a compulsory labor reserve, and their income, time, training, respite
   and own claims are assessed separately. Paid care workers need lawful pay,
   safe staffing, training and collective voice; otherwise a new entitlement
   can transfer cost to precarious labor. The [ILO's 2024 care resolution](https://www.ilo.org/resource/record-decisions/resolution-concerning-decent-work-and-care-economy)
   recognizes the linked questions of unpaid and paid care and decent work, but
   supplies no AIcountry staffing ratio or wage amount.
5. **Align provider incentives with the guarantee.** Public, nonprofit and
   private providers can participate, but contracts and public budgets must account
   for high-need people, continuity, reachable hours and non-users as well as
   treated volume. Risk adjustment and supervision must guard against choosing
   easy cases, over-treatment, hidden waiting lists and shifting care to families.
   Do not reduce professional quality to one numerical target or reward an agency
   merely for transferring a case. Procurement must preserve a funded replacement
   or interim service path before a critical provider exits. WHO's [strategic
   purchasing guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-UCH-HGF-PolicyBrief-19.6)
   describes the relationship between entitlements, population needs and payment;
   its [private-provider primer](https://www.who.int/europe/publications/i/item/WHO-EURO-2025-11654-51426-78470)
   identifies patient selection and equity risks. Neither selects an ownership
   model for this proposed country.
6. **Make joint failure reviewable without dissolving sector duties.** A named
   access-resolution office may require a reasoned joint answer from the relevant
   agencies, arrange lawful interim support and refer an unresolved conflict for
   independent review. The reviewer may identify which authority must act and by
   when under enacted law; a court or tribunal cannot create absent staff or cash.
   Actual non-delivery, backlog and remedy completion remain visible, even during
   shortage. The Assembly must address a systemic capacity deficit through a
   funded, rights-respecting adjustment rather than letting an administrator
   narrow eligibility by practice.

## Integration and limitations

The candidate changes the **meaning of a delivered guarantee**, not just the user
interface. C04 would assign local cross-service convening and national fallback
without merging all service authority. C06 would add travel, waiting,
administrative and unpaid-care time, plus joint access and replacement capacity, to
the guarantee-and-delivery schedule. C07 would establish the effective-access claim,
voluntary joint planning, independent review and separate carer/recipient rights.
C13's household example should test compatible actual hours and locations, not
merely the existence of each listed benefit. No current manuscript or API rule is
changed by this analysis. Existing sector claims, professional responsibilities and
contracts continue pending legitimate revision; a new duty would require a mapped
transition, trained staff, funding and data-protection rules.

There are unresolved value conflicts: how much choice can be guaranteed when
nearby capacity is scarce; how to balance the recipient's preference against a
carer's autonomy; and how much coordination can occur without an intrusive common
dossier. Those are not settled by aggregate service statistics. No affected-party
participation, local time-use data, provider accounts, workforce assessment,
costed geographic plan, current-law review or independent appraisal was obtained.
Evidence qualification is limited to the cited international guidance; research
selection is the candidate above; legal authorization, operational readiness and
observed outcomes are absent.

The next bounded question is whether the connected-access duty would change a real
jurisdiction's *unusable combination of otherwise valid offers* rather than merely
relabel a capacity shortage. A legitimate future public authority could commission
a consent-based, privacy-preserving review of anonymized service paths and
participatory time-use accounts, covering non-users and paid/unpaid carers, then
compare costed local access plans with the current sector route. Completion requires
identified gaps, responsible institutions, financing and a rights review, not a new
case-management platform. If evidence shows that coordination adds gatekeeping
without improving access, retain separate claims and fund capacity directly.
