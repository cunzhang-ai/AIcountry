# Hawke's Bay. Buyouts and community relocation are different decisions

New Zealand's Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet reported
151 settled voluntary buyout agreements in Hawke's Bay on 27 June 2025.
This was a property programme milestone. Hastings District Council's
published conditions tell landlords to arrange for tenants to leave
by settlement with the required notice. The settlement count cannot
establish which tenants obtained safe and affordable housing.
[June status, paragraph 17](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-community-relocation-and-host-capacity/original-sources/dpmc-status-recovery.pdf#page=4)
· [Hastings owner guidance](https://www.hastingsdc.govt.nz/hastings/cyclone-gabrielle/cat3buyout/)

The same June status reports two separate signed agreements for
Hawke's Bay marae, Māori communal meeting places, with new sites
secured. The trustees had authorized their respective governance
entities to negotiate. A residential pathway across the reported
regions included 16 properties approached directly and eight handled
through Maungaharuru-Tangitū Trust for a papakāinga, a community
housing arrangement. All 16 direct property offers had reached
payment under the reported agreements. The eight-property collective
agreement was nearing signature at the reporting date. These are
different populations and stages. The 151 buyouts, the two facilities,
the 16 direct properties and the eight-property agreement cannot be
added as counts of people rehoused.
[June status, paragraphs 27–35](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-community-relocation-and-host-capacity/original-sources/dpmc-status-recovery.pdf#page=5)

In its submission to the local development strategy, the Trust
described temporary arrangements outside its traditional area and
reported a mandate from the Tangoio Marae trustees and some affected
families to negotiate a safer site within that area. This is a
first-party statement of an affected institution and the scope it
reported. It is separate from consent by any person to a particular
housing offer. On 16 April 2026 the government announced approval of
NZD 11.54 million for Petāne Marae and NZD 16.06 million for Tangoio
Marae, a total of NZD 27.60 million for the two facilities. Those
approvals establish a public investment decision. Payment timing,
completed facilities, household occupancy and host services remain
outside the evidence used here.
[Trust submission, paragraphs 3–6](https://www.hastingsdc.govt.nz/assets/Document-Library/Hastings-and-Napier-Future-Development-Strategy/Submission-documents/Sub-094-Maungaharuru-Tangitu-Trust.pdf)
· [16 April approval announcement](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/investment-strengthens-resilience-two-t%C4%81kitimu-marae)

The design inference is that a recovery process can protect individual
placement while supporting a separately mandated common place. This
case does not transport New Zealand's Treaty institutions or show
that one relocation path had better outcomes than the other.
