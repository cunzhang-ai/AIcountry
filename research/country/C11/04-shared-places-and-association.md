# C11. Shared places, culture and voluntary association

*Part of [C11. Knowledge, culture and AI as public capability](../C11-knowledge-culture-and-ai.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

People choose whether, where and with whom to form relationships. The state can
protect time, freedom and usable places for meeting, learning, play, art,
organizing and quiet presence; it cannot promise friendship, assign belonging
or require participation. Families, cultural communities, religious groups,
unions, clubs, mutual aid and informal neighbors remain independent of a
government definition of community. Their freedom includes criticism of the
country's institutions and the choice to remain private.

## A public duty over conditions, not relationships

The Assembly sets a statutory floor for fair access to a maintained network of
shared civic and cultural places. The floor covers safe and accessible routes,
usable hours and charges, nondiscriminatory entry, lawful assembly and a route
to challenge exclusion or closure. It does not guarantee a chosen building,
particular association membership or a free venue for every event. Elected
local councils choose a mix of libraries, parks, streets, community rooms,
sport and cultural places that fits actual geography and local preferences.
They may own, lease or commission spaces from independent providers, but keep
a named authority answerable for maintenance, accessible alternatives and
remedies when an operator exits. National equalization supports the statutory
floor; a locality cannot meet it by counting an inaccessible or perpetually
closed venue.

Existing land, ecological, cultural and property claims remain binding. A plan
to remove or relocate a shared place records who uses it, who cannot enter it,
the maintenance and land costs, safe alternatives and expected effects on
surrounding residents. Affected people can propose a different site or use
before the choice is fixed. The elected body gives reasons; independent legal
review protects rights and required process without freezing every existing
facility. Shared space must not be financed by silently displacing housing,
care, ecological obligations or another locality's users.

## Plural access without an official community

Public-space rules address capacity, safety, accessibility and conflicting
uses through published, reviewable criteria. They may provide protected times
for children or people facing a specific safety risk, but cannot use safety,
membership or a dominant group's custom as an unreviewable exclusion of
newcomers, minorities, dissent or lawful opposition. Bookings and grants use
open criteria, disclosed conflicts and timely appeal. The public may support
plural independent cultural and civic groups; funding does not buy agreement
with the council or designate one organization as the voice of all affected
people. Private associations retain their lawful autonomy and duties to others.

People can find and use the public network through in-person, assisted and
non-digital routes. An AI guide may explain times, access and options, but
cannot become the compulsory booking gate or infer whom a person should meet.
Routine use requires no national relationship record. Necessary safety or
booking data have limited purpose, retention and correction. Staff, cleaning,
repairs, access support, public transport and energy enter the same C06
commitment account as housing, care and schools. C05's usable time and C04's
local service plans must match the places' real hours and travel paths.

The C14 public-conditions account may report reach, closures, prices, denied
uses, maintenance and voluntary accounts of inclusion or loneliness with
privacy protection and missing-group disclosure. These are separate signals,
not a state target for friendship or a single cohesion score. An average gain
cannot justify suppressing a lawful association or excluding a small group.
The [public-life decision record](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-10-public-life/decision-record.md)
compares rival approaches and states what would reopen this selection.

[CB01–CB24](../C09/14-shared-buildings-and-neighbor-rights.md) distinguishes
ordinary private/common residential use from this public civic-place duty.
Residents may choose funded everyday common use with actual equal resident
voice; managers/owners gain no authority over belief, friendships or lawful
opposition. Private building membership is no public allocation/booking licence,
compulsory club or permission to use a public-space budget as free building repair.

[CPV01–CPV05](04/01-open-access-and-usable-succession.md) specifies the
free ordinary floor, paid local sources, fair bookings and effective
alternatives through closure and operator change.
