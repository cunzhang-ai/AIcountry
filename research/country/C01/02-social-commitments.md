# C01. Social commitments and limits

*Part of [C01](../C01-first-principles.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The proposed settlement has five connected commitments:

1. Everyone has enforceable basic rights and access to essentials, including people
   without political membership or complete documents.
2. Members of the national electorate have equal political standing, effective
   opposition and peaceful replacement of rulers. Good service statistics cannot
   substitute for public control; the electorate's boundary must itself be contestable.
3. People may build businesses, cooperatives, associations, families and different ways
   of living. Neither state permission nor compulsory data collection governs ordinary
   lawful relationships.
4. Those exercising power or imposing costs must answer to affected people through
   law, democratic institutions and usable remedies.
5. The present generation must maintain ecological and institutional conditions that
   leave meaningful choices to future generations and people affected abroad.

The proposed country also treats avoidable serious suffering of plausibly
sentient animals as an independent public concern, not merely a proxy for
human disease or species loss. An elected [animal-welfare law](../C09/10-animal-welfare-and-one-health.md)
must define its scope and reconcile food, work, culture, research, nature and
humane treatment. This does not claim animal electoral membership or settle
all moral disputes about animal use.

Equal standing is not exhausted by a national majority vote. People may share a
language, culture, history or place without being compelled into a state-defined
group; some peoples also hold distinct rights and prior claims that a simple
resident count cannot extinguish. Territorial power must remain answerable both
to its residents and to people whose rights or essential systems cross its map.
Neither an official identity register nor an AI classification may decide who a
people is or whose political voice counts.

Conflicts between these commitments require public reasons and legitimate decisions.
No national happiness score, intelligence ranking or optimization system settles them.
For a hard-to-reverse public choice, a present majority must disclose what
options it removes from later people and who will bear restoration and debt.
This does not let an unelected advocate claim future consent or deny current
people essential protection; the [future-options rule](../C09/04-future-options-and-irreversibility.md)
keeps lawful floors, evidence and elected responsibility distinct.
