# Two requests, one interpreter and a mistranslated refusal

*Design395 · Constructed comparison using abstract staff blocks.*

A resident uses assisted speech to tell the housing office that
they cannot safely travel to an offered place. An AI draft labels
this as refusal. At the same time, a clinic needs qualified
interpretation for a person's material care decision. Both public
acts need one compatible qualified block before their respective
deadlines. Assume both appointments overlap a shared availability
window. No scheduling move can fit them in the same block. The
shared interpreting supplier has only one confirmed usable block
after a network failure. A separately staffed offline route can
accept one more block if already funded and available.

| Route | Ordinary navigation | Material acts in the affected window |
|---|---|---|
| Paid human navigation and one shared interpreter | Six routine navigation blocks used | Only one of two qualified interpreting needs can be served in time. |
| Qualified optional AI navigation and the same interpreter | Four routine navigation blocks used, including review and correction | Still only one qualified interpreting block. The two routine blocks released have the wrong qualification for this task. |
| Optional AI navigation and a separately funded alternate | Four routine blocks used | Both acts can receive qualified interpretation if the two routes actually accept the respective assignments. |
| Treat the draft and an automated reply as sufficient | Apparent completion at low cost | Neither machine output establishes qualified meaning or valid consent. The housing record still falsely treats an access barrier as a refusal. |

The housing desk retains the original filing time and asks the
responsible public owner to arrange any lawfully available interim
protection. The clinic keeps its own urgent care rules and decision.
The case assigns neither clinical nor housing priority from the
shared capacity count. If the alternate misses its deadline, keep
the particular act and any unresolved duty visible. A provisional
action under existing law is separate from final confirmation.

After qualified review, the housing office corrects the meaning
with the resident's confirmation and tells the lawful placement
receiver. If the corrected record reaches the office but the
placement receiver still applies the refusal, correction remains
open. Verify that the receiver removed the barrier and any due
accommodation actually became accessible. Correcting a translation
does not by itself determine entitlement or erase earlier harm.

The two routine blocks may improve public assistance if they
can be reassigned lawfully. They are neither interpreter capacity
nor a cash saving. The case does not estimate actual language
coverage, clinical effectiveness or costs.
