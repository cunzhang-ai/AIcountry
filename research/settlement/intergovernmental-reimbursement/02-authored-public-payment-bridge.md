# Pay a resident while the public bodies settle who owes whom

This is an authored fixture. A municipality owes two eligible
resident-payment batches of 5 and 3 at distinct deadlines. The
national payer has 5 undisputed programme units due before the first
batch, eligible for either batch or arrears within the same programme.
A separate claim of 3 is contested between public bodies.
The municipality can use 1 of eligible local funds without
displacing other protected work. The lawful optional bridge is
capped at 2 and can be used for either eligible batch if needed.
Use the same balance once. The batches and public sources have
different clocks. No case assumes a valid payment through a
failed rail.

| Route | Timely amount at first and second deadlines | Original amount exposed at those dates |
|---|---|---:|
| Withhold all national money until the dispute ends | At most 1 and 0 if the local unit is used first | 7 |
| Pay the undisputed amount, with no bridge | 5 and 1 | 2 |
| Pay the undisputed amount and add the qualified independent bridge before the second deadline | 5 and 3 | 0 |
| First national transfer arrives only at the second deadline, bridge can run independently at the first | 3 and 3 | 2 at the first deadline, even if cured later |

The last row uses 1 local and 2 bridge units early. At the later
deadline 5 arrives. The lawful order uses 2 for the first batch's
outstanding amount and 3 for the second batch, subject to actual
receiving. That late cure does not make the original
payment timely.

If the dispute later allows 2 and denies 1, verified settlement
of the allowed 2 repays the 2 bridge units. The already spent
local unit covers the difference. The resident receives no
second award from the later settlement. The example assumes
authorized zero-interest funding for this brief interval and
a separate, priced provision outside these payment batches
for any ultimate bridge loss. If only 1 is finally recovered
or usable, the bridge has a 1-unit uncovered balance before
applying that provision. Neither a favorable decision nor an
unreceived reimbursement restores the bridge.

Select early payment of the undisputed amount with the capped
contingent bridge for this comparison. Direct national payment
is a structural rival if an authorized national payer can
confirm eligibility, timely access, independent review and
safe transfer at its full price. Reimbursement without an
advance remains a rival where the locality has adequately
funded ordinary liquidity and bears expressly chosen risk.
Different outcomes on source, cost and access can displace
the selected route. Real appropriation and recipient receipt
remain unverified.
