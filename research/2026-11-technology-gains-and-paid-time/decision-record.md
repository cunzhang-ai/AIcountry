# Design328 decision on technology gains and paid time

## Baseline and choice

Pin Design327, AT01–AT17, TW, WL, DP, service law and the working
framework. Existing law candidates protect shorter ordinary hours and
worker bargaining. Specify paid worker bargaining and a reply before
a material allocation of technology gains, qualified receiving and
actual free time by roster, and a reversible first step before
permanent reliance. The project assistant authors the proposal.
Legitimate lawmakers and properly mandated parties make their
respective legal, budget and bargaining decisions.

## Alternatives and evidence

Compare ordinary process with no new reliance, assigning gains to
service output, shorter paid hours, and a mixed roster with funded cover.
A general release can protect leisure but leave uncovered work.
Assigning all released hours to service can fail on qualifications
and shift times, and leave no practical worker benefit. Prefer
bargained matched choices with actual retained pay, accepted cover
and protected review. The selected process may cost bargaining time,
training, replacement and measurement. It grants no automatic claim
to a fixed share of modelled gains.

The [primary studies](sources.md) measure productivity in a specific
deployment, wellbeing in a voluntary organization-level trial, and
observed worker representation practices. None establishes a common
national workweek, a care staffing ratio or this proposal's yield.

## Authority, migration and reopening

Employers and workers use ordinary agreement and bargaining within
nonwaivable law. Service owners retain safety, staffing and receipt
responsibility. Elected budget owners decide public costs and coverage.
Existing labor and sector review remain directly reachable. Define a
bounded paid first trial and the relevant full roster cycles. Carry
earned pay, work guarantees, original service deadlines, data rights
and accepted ordinary fallback through any change or failure.

Test one real affected workplace with paid worker and resident
participation, representative evidence and an independently staffed
comparison. Examine after-hours work, intensity, waiting, privacy,
paid time, service receipt, costs and uneven gain. Check a second
cycle and an unavailable tool or provider. Reopen on uncompensated
work, deteriorating service, lost autonomy, weak outside review
or a better qualified allocation. Actual adoption, staffing, money
and measured outcomes remain open.
