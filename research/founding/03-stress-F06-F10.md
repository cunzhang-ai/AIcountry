The cross-case tests below apply to all eleven F1–F11 exercises. Moving the
case text does not convert a written exercise into operational evidence.

Across all eleven cases, analyze both a lawful actor's obstruction and a genuine
shortage. Each can look like delay but calls for a different response. A standing
authority and remedy preserve legal claims; they do not demonstrate cash, workers
or compliance. No aggregate service outcome can substitute for a failed peaceful
handover or excluded opposition.

Also trace the complete resident or officer journey through each case: first
receipt, required handoffs, decisions that need independent judgment, shared
clerks or vendors, urgent protection, elapsed time and delivered remedy. Count
qualified people and resident effort across the eleven cases together rather than
assuming the same reviewer, interpreter or alternate operator is freely
available to every failing system. A shorter route is not a successful test if
it makes a single intake, credential or vendor the gate to opposition or review.

F2 also tests a cabinet's attempt to rewrite due benefits through a fiscal
regulation during budget lapse; F4 tests a silent AI eligibility threshold
change during service outage; F5 tests serial short emergency orders against
opposition. For each, identify the enabling statute, legal rank, public
version, Assembly scrutiny, direct challenge, executing officer and due
claims that continue when an unlawful or expired rule falls away. No authored
exercise proves that a real Assembly or court could stop the abuse in time.
