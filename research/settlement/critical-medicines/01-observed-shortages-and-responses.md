# What observed medicine shortages establish

## A disruption can travel from one product to another

The US Government Accountability Office's April 2025 report
identified 102 drugs in active shortage on 31 July 2024, including
71 sterile injectables. It also traced the 2023 oncology shortages.
A major manufacturer's plant closed to address quality issues,
disrupting cisplatin. The report found that the shift toward
carboplatin increased demand that other manufacturers could
not meet, contributing to a second shortage. This is evidence
for checking substitute stocks and existing patients together.
It does not determine which treatment is appropriate for any
individual or quantify the effect of a national buffer.
[GAO-25-107110](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107110/index.html).

## Temporary supply has a defined regulatory scope

A May 2023 manufacturer letter hosted by FDA described a
temporary cisplatin import route using a specified presentation,
non-US labeling and a named distribution route. The FDA's June
2024 account distinguished its temporary importation discretion
from later approval and reported that the national cisplatin
shortage had ended. Those two documents describe the same
episode as the GAO case. They are not independent replications.
A limited import measure and recovery of national supply do
not establish timely receipt for every patient.
[Temporary import notice](https://www.fda.gov/media/169001/download).
[FDA dated account](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-roundup-june-28-2024).

## Public lists and protocols can have separate purposes

The EMA's Union list combines the seriousness of the clinical
need and the availability of alternative medicines, along with
its own further criteria. This illustrates the design of a
prioritization schedule. It does not decide treatment or prove
that priority products are available in each place.
[EMA Union list and methodology](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/medicine-shortages-availability-issues/availability-medicines-during-crises/union-list-critical-medicines).

The UK Department of Health and Social Care's description of
serious shortage protocols gives an example of a defined route
for a community pharmacist to supply an alternative with patient
consent. The instrument has a defined legal scope. Its existence
does not establish general interchangeability or safety for
any patient or medicine.
[Department of Health and Social Care account](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/managing-a-robust-and-resilient-supply-of-medicines/managing-a-robust-and-resilient-supply-of-medicines).
