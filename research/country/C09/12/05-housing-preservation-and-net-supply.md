# Protect existing homes and add usable homes on distinct schedules

*Design332 · H21–H25 · Proposed public and community housing portfolio rules.*

**H21 — Follow the dwelling, its occupant and its legal use separately.**
For a material housing portfolio the responsible local owner records four
distinct results: preserved affordable tenancies at evidenced risk,
physical changes in the total dwelling stock, restoration of unusable
existing dwellings to safe service, and net suitable offers actually
available to entrants. Publish opening, change and closing counts by
scope, date and location. An occupied purchase may secure existing
tenure and change the ownership or affordable stock account without
creating an entrant offer or a physical addition. Distinguish known
loss, possible loss and observed retention. Deduct removals, prior
allocations, rights of return, displaced offers and incompatible reserve
commitments where relevant. Trace a dwelling across stages so the same
unit or household is not counted twice for the same result.

**H22 — Elect separate delivery schedules and expose their tradeoffs.**
The competent elected bodies set dated preservation and entrant objectives
against actual needs. Compare retention agreements or acquisition of
at-risk tenancies, repair or lawful conversion, and serviced new supply.
Give each material option its own gross capital, ongoing operating and
renewal cash, qualified crews, infrastructure, temporary housing and
ownership consequences. Use the same land, workforce, ecology and
receiving evidence across options. Choose the portfolio and its binding
stages under IV, DB and MF. If a proposal moves money or skilled work
from one adopted delivery objective to another, publish the affected
households and decide the change expressly before committing it. A
portfolio target cannot extinguish an existing right or repair duty.
Temporary help keeps its original owner and source.

**H23 — Verify preservation and successor duties before binding title.**
Show the actual threatened loss, alternatives to purchase, private and
public costs, title, deposits, security, repair and term of affordability.
Name the funded accepting operator, existing tenants' usable rights,
and the route if the buyer or lender fails. Compare a lawful retention
agreement where it can provide equivalent durability and accountability.
For new public finance, secure and enforce continuing affordability
through the governing instrument. Use existing title and compensation
law for any acquisition. Preserve actual tenants, lawful creditor
priority, worker claims, rights of return and independent remedies.
No promise of empty possession or forced relocation is a cost saving.

**H24 — Count an entrant offer only after real acceptance conditions.**
Track design, permission, funding, build or repair, qualified safety and
service acceptance, affordability, lawful allocation and occupied
receiving separately. Existing commitments to rehousing take priority in
their legal scope before counting entrant offers. Use H07, RT and HL14
for suitability, fair allocation, payment and actual affordability
through the next known funding change. Keep cases, claims and
compensation open after delay or a failed handover. Where part of a
phase fails inspection, retain separately usable accepted parts only
if their dependencies and safe services work. Report the number and
deadline that remain unmet and return any material change to the
competent elected choice.

**H25 — Audit both outcomes and carry them through renewal.** Keep
accessible evidence and timely independent review for residents,
applicants, workers, opposition and lawful providers. Compare actual
retention and new entry, including people missed by the housing
queue and changes in private stock. Do not publish protected addresses
or individual records. AI may trace duplicate units and construction
dependencies, without making an allocation, assessing title or
certifying safety. Adopt prospectively with paid casework, review,
operator succession and retained old duties. Reopen on unexpected loss,
shorter affordability, exclusion, unserved entrants or a better
costed portfolio.
