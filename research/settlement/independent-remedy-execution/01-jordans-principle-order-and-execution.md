# Canada. First Nations children's remedies have separate delivery stages

Jordan's Principle responds to discriminatory gaps in services for
First Nations children. It is rooted in the particular Canadian
legal and Indigenous rights context. The Canadian Human Rights
Tribunal's January 2025 reasons describe earlier discrimination
findings, the purpose of equal practical access and its oversight
of the backlog and urgent protection. Its order distinguishes
requests waiting for intake, requests in progress and reimbursements
still due. It requires contact with certain urgent applicants and
interim measures against reasonably foreseeable irremediable harm.
Those requirements are judicial acts, rather than observed
payment or completed service.
[Canadian Human Rights Tribunal, 2025 CHRT 6, paragraphs 1–3 and 558–560](https://fncaringsociety.com/publications/2025-chrt-6-full-reasons-jordans-principle-non-compliance-motion)

The department's May 2025 internal audit reports growth in recorded
individual requests from **38,273 in fiscal 2020–21** to **141,266
in fiscal 2023–24**. The counts come from the administrative system
and exclude specified categories, including Inuit children and
service coordination. They count requests, with no inference about
distinct children or unentered requests. The audit identified
intake entries that could occur only after a request was deemed
complete in some regions, and instances where entered timestamps
did not match supporting documents. Reporting only the formal
decision clock can conceal a separate wait at intake.
[Internal audit, Figure 1, sections 3.2 and 3.5](https://sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1753100031042/1753100058429)

The audit describes separate administrative systems for decisions and
payments, individual reimbursement, some direct vendor payment, and
group funding through contribution agreements. It also reports no
formal mechanism during the audited scope for settling overlapping
responsibilities after assistance. The financial table records CAD
3,744.69 million in expenditures for the four fiscal years through
March 2024, excluding programme administration. Recorded expenditure
does not show that a particular family was able to prepay or actually
received its service by the needed time.
[Internal audit, Table 2 and sections 3.2 and 3.4](https://sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1753100031042/1753100058429)

The proposed country's inference is limited. Keep a direct protected
route, original intake and service clocks, payment authority and actual
receipt separately traceable. Keep a later public payer dispute out
of the person's useful-time route where lawful protection can operate.
The Canadian record supplies no AIcountry funding authority or estimate
of the effects of its proposed mechanism.
