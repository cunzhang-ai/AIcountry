# A warm room and care at the same address

*Design287 · Constructed path through the first government's service duties.*

At hour 0 a tenant loses heat. The local assessment finds that safe
warmth is needed by hour 8 and a scheduled essential care visit by
hour 10. The tenant wants to return to the same home. The housing
officer speaks to the tenant by their preferred accessible route,
arranges interim protection and keeps the building repair open. The
care team keeps responsibility for the visit and for accepting a
change of place. The tenant's ordinary benefit continues on its own
clock. The times and needs are assumptions in this example.

The home route is preferable if safe independent heat can operate
there by hour 8, ordinary care still works and the arrangement lasts
until the next safe decision. Here the ordinary heat source and
portable fallback depend on the same failed control. A warm room
is offered before hour 8, with an accessible ride, but the existing
care shift still goes to the home. Both providers can report an
on-time task while the tenant still has no viable route.

The viable temporary route has an accepted ride and room by hour 7
and qualified care at that address by hour 9. The tenant accepts
the move. It needs five units of eligible cash by hour 6, before dispatch, and eight
in total for the priced contingency. A four-unit early envelope
can cover only part of it. One separately authorized unit actually
received in time can close that specific gap if the service is still
available. Without it, available safe components and urgent review
continue while the protection gap remains visible. A delayed grant
or a clinical slot offered at the old address cannot close it.

The temporary reservation ends at hour 24 and the extension must
be secured by hour 20. The repair slips to hour 26. With the
accepted extension the tenant stays warm and receives care through
verified return at hour 28. A booking renewed after the resident
loses access leaves a gap even if all the repairs are eventually
complete. Safe return requires usable heat, access and resumed
essential service. A secure permanent offer is worth considering
if the disruption will persist and the tenant wants it. An offer
available only later cannot satisfy the first two deadlines.
