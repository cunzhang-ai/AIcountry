# One retirement, two service clocks

Construct a public benefits desk and a local housing help desk.
They use distinct applications on the same upstream model.
The vendor gives notice on day 0. The old version is supported
only through day 10. The procurement contract lasts to day 30.
The proposed successor cannot be independently evaluated for
both uses until day 14. Assume no sector has authority to rely
on an unsupported build.

After retirement, the benefits desk needs 6 qualified ordinary
hours and housing needs 5 before their respective service
deadlines in a shared window. Independent review needs 2 hours
on a separate roster. The two offices originally claim 8
available hours each, but these are the same pool of 8. The
service shortfall is 3. The review roster has exactly 2 and
cannot backfill either service. A named distinct qualified
team can provide 3 hours under an authorized paid arrangement
if accepted before the last useful staffing date.

| Same duties and window | Qualified hours for protected service | Disposition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Treat the live contract as continued model support | 8 available outside the retired model | Three hours uncovered |
| Switch immediately to the vendor's unevaluated successor | 8 available outside the unqualified model | Three hours uncovered |
| Use ordinary service with the independently accepted cover | 11 | Both service duties and separate review fit |

In this constructed case select the funded ordinary route from
retirement until each sector accepts its successor or chooses
continued ordinary operation. Support continuing through day 10
may be useful for preparation within the verified lawful scope.
A forced substitute under the same label counts as a proposed
change even during that period. Existing rights and earlier due
dates survive. If only 2 of the 3 alternate hours are accepted
in time, one hour remains uncovered. Report the resulting duty
under applicable law without inventing a priority between desks.

The exercise checks shared capacity, authority and timing.
It gives no observed service improvement or actual cost.
