# Specific material use, donor identity and no automatic parenthood

*RP03–RP04; part of [C08/20](../20-reproductive-law-and-parentage.md).*

**RP03 — Reproductive consent is specific and revocable before use.** Obtain
actual informed authorization for collection, storage, named/scoped recipients,
reproductive use, research and any permitted transfer separately. Include
future derivation of gametes from a person's cells; consent to treatment,
research, a skin sample or digital analysis is not consent to reproduction.
No ownership of a gene sequence, relatives or a future person's body follows.

A donor must be 18 for donation to another's reproduction; young people's own
necessary fertility preservation remains under B22/S. No proxy donation or
sale of another's material. Before irreversible insemination or embryo
transfer, each material contributor's actual required reproductive consent
must still be valid within scope; withdrawal stops future use, including
stored jointly created embryos. A valid continuing scoped instruction does
not need a fresh signature for every clerical event. Prove revocation/receipt
and scope, not just a clinic login. After transfer, no donor or co-parent can
compel termination, removal, continued pregnancy or treatment.

A valid advance nonparent-donation record, independently evidenced before
use, excludes parentage/support merely from genetic contribution. This can
use safe signed, witnessed, assisted or equivalently reliable future media;
free legal help and protected filing are available without a mandatory clinic
or central account. A private donor arrangement is not intrinsically unlawful.
Fraudulent after-conception relabelling cannot erase an existing child's
support. Actual intent/care/adjudication, unauthorized material use and a
disputed record require B/RP court findings; a clinic never decides finally.

**RP04 — Knowable origins without public exposure or a contact command.**
New donations cannot promise permanent legal anonymity. Preserve a minimal
sealed link among actual material, scope, use, origin and child; do not
collect a population genomic database. At 18 the conceived person can directly
obtain identifying origin information without donor/parent permission. This
is verifiable identity, not automatic access to a present home address or
unrelated private/contact data; safe contact has a distinct scoped route. Before
then, relevant nonidentifying medical/origin information is accessible with
supported voice; an independent service/court can authorize earlier identity
for a proved health/identity need, with reasons, support and direct challenge.
The origin service offers a confidential direct notice of the record/access
route at majority, safely addressed without exposing it to an unsafe household;
no parent/clinic may make the right unusable by hiding or destroying its link.
Provide supported age-appropriate earlier explanation without compulsory
public disclosure. The person may decline information; identity access grants no compelled
contact, relationship, parenthood, inheritance or permission to expose others.

A specific serious safety risk may change secure delivery/contact measures,
not create an indefinite official/donor veto over the origin claim. Correct
uncertainty, mix-up and safe records rather than guessing ancestry. Essential
origin links follow the lifetime identity purpose with at least five-year
privacy/access review; unnecessary clinical detail/samples are minimized,
not permanently public or automatically training data. Provider closure
requires an accepted protected custodian; a new edition cannot erase origin.

Licensed distribution uses a prospective maximum ten independently planned
recipient parenting units per donor, counted at first use, not per marriage,
clinic, account or agent copy. Later household division does not reset it.
Reconcile actual known domestic/foreign uses; unknown provenance is not a
certificate of unused capacity. Do not compel DNA tracking or punish existing
children/families. Ten is an uncalibrated bound, not proof of genetic safety;
private-use consequences and any wider limit need specific prospective law.
