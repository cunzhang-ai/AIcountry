# One opening budget, four payers and two payment windows

This constructed case uses one opening set of balances and
obligations. It does not combine the different hypothetical
amounts or the observed accounts of Designs435–439. Window one
covers days 1–30 and window two covers days 31–90. In this case
all early funding must settle before the corresponding day-30
payments and all later funding before the day-90 payments.
The second-year balance remains open.

| Payer | Opening usable cash | Restricted cash excluded | First-window external duties |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Treasury | 20 | 0 | 5 principal, 3 election administration, 3 court and remedy execution |
| Local authority | 2 | 5 for separate capital use | 7 care payroll and supplies |
| Earned pension fund | 3 | 0 | 7 pension payments |
| Public operator | 2 | 0 | 5 rail operating payments |

Three Treasury transfers are due: 5 to the locality, 4 to the
fund and 3 to the operator. They belong to distinct instruments
L1, P1 and E1. They are debits of 12 for Treasury and credits
of 12 to the receiving payers. In a broad view that includes
all four payers, eliminate both sides of those three entries.
The 30 of external obligations remains, including the 5 of debt
principal, which is financing rather than operating expense.
In any narrower perimeter retain the flows crossing its boundary.
No amount is a dividend or guarantee.

Under normal conditions, 4 of external Treasury refinancing
settles in the first window. The three transfers and all four
payers' obligations fit. Treasury ends at 1, its stipulated
minimum. The three service payers end at zero and need the
next authorized funding before another obligation falls due.

In the combined stress, refinancing slips to the next window
and the ordinary transfer rail fails before the service due
dates. With no authorized alternative the receiving payers
are short by 5, 4 and 3 respectively. The resulting total
funding gap is 3 before any Treasury minimum or alternate fee.
The Treasury balance of 9 cannot reach them through the failed route. The alternative tested uses 4 of already
authorized standby financing and an independently staffed
transfer route that costs 1. It can carry all three transfers.
It needs one additional unit of genuine Treasury cover to
retain the 1-unit minimum. Specify that cover as an additional
authorized standby draw, for a total draw of 5. Assume both
draws and the three replacement transfers settle before their
cutoffs, with no prior transfer still in flight. This meets
the stated cash obligations with 1 remaining in Treasury.
Actual worker, resident and passenger receipt would still
require separate proof.

In window two, the stipulated Treasury receipts are 20,
including the late 4. External duties are 8, including the
stipulated financing cost, and the three new transfers total
7. The 5-unit standby principal can be repaid once under its
terms. Treasury ends at 1. The service payers' second-window
external duties match the new transfers. If the late 4 again
fails to arrive, this package is short by 4 including the
minimum, or by 3 against the due payments alone. Secure the
funded response before drawing standby on these terms. If a
later failure still occurs, seek competent correction before
the relevant deadlines. No unilateral cancellation of a binding claim
is assumed.
