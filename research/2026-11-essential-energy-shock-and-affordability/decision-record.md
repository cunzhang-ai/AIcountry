## Diagnoses and genuine alternatives

A high bill, an absent fuel delivery, an inaccessible credit and an unsafe
building can all leave a resident cold. A household or national price
average cannot resolve these explanations. A price intervention helps
fast where households can use billing and energy is available. Money
helps differently where income is the constraint. Physical interruption
requires an accepted replacement or a safe alternative that reaches the
resident. Mixed cases require both.

- **Retain and fund ordinary protection with a narrowly scoped addition.**
  Compare existing automatic claims, safe dispatch and direct casework.
  This path uses the narrower voted bridge and physical alternates if
  early cash cannot cover the larger choice. Repeated proof, hidden
  non-take-up and insufficient capacity can defeat casework.
- **Select a finite targeted bridge and separately paid supply.** Use
  reliable existing proof where possible and fund an assisted provisional
  route outside billing. Preserve ordinary rights and protect care, water
  and independent duties on their own sources. Price both dispatch and
  actual household use at the same useful time. Residents outside the
  selected need scope bear part of the higher cost. Budget staff and
  independent reviewers must test exclusion, staffing and earlier cash.
- **Use a broad temporary price subsidy with the same physical safeguards.**
  Faster use of ordinary billing and wider coverage can justify this rival
  when targeting is too slow and sources and supply are secure. Price
  the independent non-account route, compensation, wholesale risk, higher
  optional use and exit. If adopted it requires express elected authority
  and a cap or other lawful risk limit. Reopen this comparison where
  narrower routes cannot protect need on time or the shock is price-only.
