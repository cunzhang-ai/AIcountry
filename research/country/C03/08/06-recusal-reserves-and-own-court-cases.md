# Constitutional review: reserves and who judges the judges

*Canonical CJ11–CJ12; part of [C03/08](../08-constitutional-review-and-public-control.md).*

**CJ11 — Recusal and finite reserve.** Disclose material personal financial,
family, sponsor and prior-involvement conflicts or evidenced bias. Naming every
judge as a defendant, disagreement with a ruling or diffuse institutional interest
alone does not disqualify everyone. The challenged judge cannot decide a contested
recusal. Five unchallenged lawful judges may decide it; otherwise CJ12 supplies
the external route. Unsupported repeat motions do not automatically stay a case
or reset a rights clock; material new evidence remains hearable.

One named, consenting reserve per seat is appointed before a disputed case by
the same source, criteria and votes as the primary, for the same seat expiry.
Reserve activation follows the established inability/recusal, not the presider's
preferred outcome. Permanent vacancy activates an eligible reserve for the
remaining term; temporary inability lasts only while its ground exists.
Check availability, conflicts, training, paid standby and release from other
assigned work every six months and before activation. One person cannot count
as simultaneous reserve, ordinary trial judge and external appeal capacity.

If primary and reserve cannot serve, that seat is unavailable. Replenishment
uses the original source's full process for the remaining term; a person first
selected after a case arises cannot be its emergency substitute. Pending cases
await their lawful original/reserve bench or a general future appointment under
prospective ordinary law with adversarial assignment review, never a case-targeted
recruit. Below five lawful judges final constitutional adjudication stops.
There is no executive acting judge, term extension or lower quorum.

**CJ12 — External own-court jurisdiction.** Prior constitutional law assigns
seven lawfully appointed ordinary appellate judges final jurisdiction over
constitutional judges' appointments/qualifications, personal term/remuneration,
discipline/removal appeals and contested recusals not decidable under CJ11.
It also covers legality/retaliation challenges to the constitutional court's
own institutional funding where that court would otherwise judge its budget,
and decides whether a dispute falls in this narrow exceptional jurisdiction.
This is an express exception to CJ01/CJ09, including necessary constitutional
questions within these closed subjects. It may correct the contested appointment,
personal status or funding act and require lawful reconsideration, but cannot
convert an incidental wider issue into general constitutional jurisdiction.
Its scope ruling prevails over administrative routing, without enlarging this
constitutional exception. It is not a second general constitutional court.

Use the published annual ordinary-appellate roster and auditable predeclared
draw order, excluding selectors, investigators, underlying-case judges and
materially personally interested people. A first-stage disciplinary panel under
CJ13 is distinct from the seven and its reserves. The external chamber has
quorum five and needs four affirmative votes of the fixed seven for a disposition;
no majority means no disposition, not confirmed appointment or proven misconduct.
Constitutional judges cannot reverse its own-court judgment. Further correction
for concealed fraud/material procedural error goes to a different preassigned
seven-person formation, not the implicated constitutional court.

Diffuse interest in maintaining courts does not alone disqualify ordinary judges;
this narrow prior-law necessity rule requires disclosure and adversarial reasons.
It never cures direct personal pay/tenure, participation or sponsor conflicts.
If all available ordinary judges/reserves are actually conflicted, the case lacks
a lawful final decider. Record it; do not invent an impartial guardian. An external
judge's own status follows the unconflicted ordinary/constitutional route with
no self-review. Simultaneous capture or conflict of both routes can still defeat
control. Assignment rules cannot prove otherwise or make all-judiciary pay
litigation impartial merely by renaming its beneficiaries.
