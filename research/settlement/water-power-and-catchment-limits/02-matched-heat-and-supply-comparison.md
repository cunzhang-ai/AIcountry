# Compare electricity portfolios with the same catchment limits

This authored four-hour window schedules 10 MW at one receiving bus.
Nine MW supplies the protected loads. One MW can be deferred for
four hours under an existing, qualified and compensated nonessential
contract. Test that the later workload can actually run. Assume all
sources and loads are at the receiving bus and that power is constant
within each modeled hour. This is a planning fixture, with independently
assigned water constraints, rather than a river or grid simulation.

A source unaffected by the local catchment provides 6 MW. The river-
dependent plant can run at 2 MW within its ordinary operating band
and adds 1.5 modeled heat units per MWh. For the test, assume prior law
allows an operating adjustment, subject to independent verification.
The hard receptor limit leaves at most 5 heat units per hour available
to this plant. The remaining four-hour budget is 15 heat units,
inclusive of the plant's ordinary use. Hold upstream and other
contributions fixed only for this authored calculation. Independent
supply can provide 1 MW for all four hours in the first test, then
fails entirely in the correlated loss. The model holds all other
generation, transmission, water and duty constraints fixed.

| Path | Electricity delivered to protected loads | Catchment and service result |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary plant, independent supply and lawful deferral | 6 + 2 + 1 = 9 MW for four hours | 36 MWh received if qualified. The plant uses 12 heat units. |
| Ordinary plant after the independent source fails, with lawful deferral | 6 + 2 = 8 MW for four hours | 4 MWh of protected load is exposed. Plant use is 12 heat units. |
| Adjust to 3 MW for all four hours after the independent source fails | Would supply all 9 MW | Hourly use is 4.5 heat units, but the plant would use 18 over the period. This plan is infeasible. |
| Adjust to 3 MW for two hours, then return to 2 MW, with lawful deferral | 9 MW for two hours, 8 MW for two hours | Uses all 15 heat units and leaves 2 MWh of protected load exposed unless another qualified service or supply receives it. |
| Raise the plant to 4 MW and avoid deferral after the independent source fails | Would supply 10 MW | Hourly use of 6 exceeds the hard limit of 5. No period accounting can cure that violation. |

The combined loss needs one further independently qualified MW for
the last two hours in the limited-adjustment example, or a sector
alternative that covers the same protected people and time. If the
catchment limit tightens or approval is missing, the additional
generation may have to cease sooner. An alternate service must have a
safe source, lawful authority, operating staff, funded receipt and
sufficient duration. Neither the fixture nor a real budget should
credit a second source that relies on the failed corridor or a water
body with the same unresolved constraints.

The preference for ordinary operation with independent supply and
lawful flexibility holds under the stated receiving and environmental
bounds. Money, displaced demand, grid security and actual environmental
thresholds remain to be qualified. A cooler or better connected site,
paid local storage or direct service could change the preferred mix.
