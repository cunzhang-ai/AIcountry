# Eight sponsors and a full-Assembly majority

There are 100 statutory seats and three vacancies. A group of eight
independent members offers one exact alternative. Every package preserves
nine units of due services, election and court commitments. Three further
units and one qualified commissioning slot are actually available in the
fixture. The parties disagree about the discretionary margin.

| Exact text | Discretionary use of the three units and slot | Ordinary designations |
|---|---|---:|
| G | Reduce avoidable losses in the existing water system | 46 |
| O | Add staffing for elective clinical intake | 43 |
| I | Fund accessible review and outreach to prevent unjustified loss of already earned benefits | 8 |

G and O meet the ordinary gate. I is eligible to request recovery after
its timely filing. Of the 97 members, the eight independent members,
43 ordinary O sponsors and one G sponsor select I for the first final
vote. The 52 priority selections admit I once. In the binding vote one
of those 52 withholds assent. I passes on 51 separate affirmative votes.
Its discretionary units are then unavailable to G or O for the same
window. If the ordinary sequence alone were used in this fixture, G
would pass with 54 on its final vote. The hypothetical choice of I is
a political preference of the specified majority and is not a finding
that its outcomes are better.

| Stress | Required result |
|---|---|
| Two priority supporters withhold assent in the final vote | I has 50 and fails. G retains its ordinary path and passes with 54 in this case. |
| The 52 priority supporters split 27 for I and 25 for a materially different I2 | Neither reaches 51. The Clerk must keep the two versions distinct. |
| Twenty-five selections use a second label for the identical I and its same operative conditions | One entry has 52 distinct members, not two alternatives or any extra vote. |
| A party registers several variants using software assistants | A member may request one text and select one. Even if an organized bloc partitions its selections, no second majority slot is possible. |
| I is replaced by a different text or source after the priority ballot | The replacement cannot take I's slot. The unchanged ordinary sequence remains. |
| Only 50 members are present at the priority checkpoint | The ballot is unheld. The alternate or a lawful resumption can restore access without inventing votes. |
| A BLC payment is overdue while the floor is obstructed | The ordinary payer, substitute and direct remedy retain their exact prior authority, dates and source limits. |

Independents can still lose access where colleagues oppose priority or
where the group lacks usable scrutiny. The arithmetic does not supply
staffing or a source, show freedom from political retaliation, or measure
the number of people who receive a lawful benefit.
