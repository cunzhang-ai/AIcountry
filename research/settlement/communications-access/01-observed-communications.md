# What existing communications arrangements establish

## Wholesale access can be a funding condition

In Telecom Decision CRTC 2024-228, paragraph 44, the Canadian regulator
required a named funded transport project to provide access at eligible
funded points, at the approved capacity and price bounds. Other providers
received terms at least as favorable as affiliates and partners. The
condition applies to the funded project and its lawful operating carrier.
It establishes a concrete control over wholesale terms. It leaves retail
entry, affordable household use and physical redundancy to be checked
separately. [CRTC project decision](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2024/2024-228.htm).

## Installed access can coexist with household exclusion

The US Government Accountability Office examined the Affordable
Connectivity Program in its January 2023 audit. More than 14 million
subscribers had enrolled at the time reported. The audit found that the
program's performance goals and measures needed clearer targets and that
language outreach and fraud-risk management needed improvements.
Enrollment measures programme reach. The audit does not supply a matched
estimate of the share who could newly use a connection because of it.
Use a separate affordability and receipt test, with accessible
enrollment and correction. [GAO-23-105399](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105399).

## Wireless and wired routes can fail together

In July 2022, a configuration change overwhelmed Rogers' shared IP core
in Canada. The external assessment commissioned by the CRTC traced how
wired and wireless traffic depended on that core and how its failure
disrupted emergency calls and alerts. It also found gaps in alternative
access for network operations staff. The assessment drew heavily on
operator records and responses. Its public version contains redactions
and does not establish how many simultaneous emergency contacts a
different carrier could have received. Compare end-to-end failure
domains, including the people and tools needed to repair them.
[Xona Partners assessment published by the CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/publications/reports/xonarp2023.htm).

## Open carriage can distinguish equal treatment from safety exceptions

Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2120 distinguishes end-user access,
equal treatment of traffic, technical management and limited exceptions.
This supports the distinction as a legal design mechanism. It supplies
no local enforcement capacity, evidence of AIcountry outcomes or
authority to make restrictions. CN's concrete review and urgency routes
are domestic design choices. [Regulation text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32015R2120).
