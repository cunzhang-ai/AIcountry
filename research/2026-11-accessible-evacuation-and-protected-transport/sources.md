# Evidence for accessible evacuation and protected transport

| Primary source | Observed mechanism | Design use and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-06-826](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a250833.html) | In interviews concerning recent hurricanes, health-facility administrators reported contracts drawing on the same vehicle pool during simultaneous evacuations. One nursing home reported that its contracted transport lacked the power lifts many residents needed. GAO also found gaps in the first leg to mobilization centers and in receiving support for nursing home residents. | Test distinct vehicle and crew availability and the route through actual receiving. This evidence covers particular past facilities and arrangements. |
| [U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-07-44](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-07-44) | The historical evaluation documents evacuation barriers for people without private transport and gaps in preparedness and shared responsibilities. It examined state and local arrangements through selected jurisdictions and relevant federal agencies. | Preserve accessible intake, assign route ownership and check residents unable to evacuate by private means. The evaluation does not compare the proposed transport priority or voting arrangements. |

Both studies draw in part on the Katrina experience. Treat the findings
as overlapping evidence, with distinct scopes and no claim of independent
replication. The civic collision, timing, reserve costs, local payer and
second call in the comparison are authored. The sources establish no
project readiness, adopted law, political preference or expected effect.
