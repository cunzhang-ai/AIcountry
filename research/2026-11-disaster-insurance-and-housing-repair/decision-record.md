# Design349 decision on disaster insurance and usable housing repair

## Baseline and choice

Pin Design348, FR01–FR13, NB16, ordinary secured realization, housing
protection and the working framework. Existing law in the design
separates personal help from property claims. Specify the use of proceeds
after insurer payment and before actual repair, with a bounded interval
for a lawful possession decision.

Select direct occupant protection, a protected proceeds account and
timely release for verified repair, a narrow possession pause and
independent review. The project assistant authored the choice.
Elected bodies define authority and funds. Insurer, creditor, court
and housing office retain their distinct roles.

## Alternatives and objections

General lender discretion can protect security but can hold repairs
that preserve collateral and protect residents. Paying all proceeds
immediately to the owner is fast and can undermine repair or valid
rights. Select verified staged payment with useful independent review.
Retain a lawful voluntary early release and simpler direct procurement
where those work. Compare movement and safe repair without conditioning
temporary aid on either outcome.

The [primary comparisons](sources.md) describe actual disbursement,
disaster servicing and same-loss assistance rules. The five-working-day
and 30-calendar-day choices are proposed parameters. Verification can
delay work, and deferred debt or legal uncertainty can add later costs.
Preserve ordinary interest disclosure, proportionate review and safe
temporary receiving.

## Migration and reopening

Adopt prospectively with named payers, lawful insurance and security
terms, funded temporary housing and independent advice and inspection.
Keep old claims, priorities, stronger rights and due dates. Actual
adoption, affected participation, creditor response and repair outcomes
remain open.

Test a held but received payment, a disputed balance and a household
whose dwelling cannot be made safe. Measure actual occupancy, safe
repair, arrears, delay, displaced work and burden on lenders and residents.
Reopen on avoidable delay, duplicate recovery, rent or debt pressure,
missing safe places or a better funded alternative.
