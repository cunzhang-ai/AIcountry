# Keep a viable budget available while a separate council decides

*Design309 · Constructed comparison on the first-programme calendar.*

Use the [same budget and receiving scope](01-shared-sources-and-votes.md).
On day 0 the national Assembly separately approves the independently
viable energy bridge E and then prefers housing H to care preparation P
for the remaining optional choice. Each has an exact schedule. The case
uses mutually exclusive starts to preserve the smaller accepted work
portfolio under the declared constraints. The ordinary repair baseline,
the bridge's own local approval, and its foreign settlement by day 15
are held fixed. Existing care, income and tenancy duties are outside
the optional competition.

| Additional choice after E | National money by day 30 / 90 / recurring | Local own money on same dates | Care / review / logistics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H. Three secure tenancies and voluntary support | 2 / 3 / 2 | 2 / 4 / 3 | 2 / 1 / 0 |
| P. A paid care preparation cohort and backfill | 1 / 2 / 0 | 1 / 2 / 0 | 1 / 0 / 0 |

In the weaker reference path, H fits only with the separately competent
local council's exact funding and the matched national advance. H's
gross local outlay is 4 / 7 / 5. The local council has already authorized
P on the stated terms if the national legislature separately chooses it.
Assume there are no leases or public H liabilities before commencement.
The H reservation keeps the stated unused national amount available
through the end of day 10 and does not spend it. A separately funded,
accessible fallback sitting is reserved on day 12. Current evidence,
receiving, source and exact scope are rechecked. The fallback and review
dates are authored assumptions, not observed administrative performance.

| What happens | What residents receive or are still owed |
| --- | --- |
| Exact local funding effective on day 8, with the condition proved by day 10 | H can commence on the authorized scope. Release and actual tenancy receipt still require separate evidence. P is displaced. |
| Council rejects on day 7. Under a procedure closing the docket after the first affirmative vote | The H condition fails. Ordinary repair and E continue. P has no protected vote in the reserved window and no authority to start. |
| Same day-7 rejection under BCV16–BCV20 | The H reservation ends. P obtains the day-12 vote. With the stipulated 53 yes votes and current qualified sources it can start. National early use for E plus P is 3, compared with 4 for E plus H. |
| Council approval arrives on day 11, after expiry | The expired H schedule does not revive. P can still obtain its vote. A future H proposal needs new lawful authority. |
| The day-12 fallback vote lacks a quorum | P stays unauthorized. Retain due work, publish the unheld vote and reconvene where useful and lawful. |
| H lawfully starts and leases bind. The advance then fails | The three tenants keep their leases and accrued support claims. Preserve the actual liability and seek a funded lawful bridge. P cannot reuse the promised resources. |

Advance conditions in another public body's law can respect both
electorates, but pre-approval before the national vote may be simpler
when the calendars align. Compare that route first. A simultaneous vote
on a fully specified fallback could save sitting time, but would require
the elected bodies to authorize its exact trigger and future scope.
Automatic selection by Treasury or a fixed formula cannot settle an
unvoted choice between training and housing. Protect a real fallback
vote here and expose its time and standby costs.
