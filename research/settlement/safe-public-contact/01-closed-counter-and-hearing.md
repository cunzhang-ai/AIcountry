# A closed counter, an income decision and a civil hearing

This authored comparison exercises
[SCN01–SCN05](../../country/C04/06/05-safe-public-contact-and-notice.md).
In working-day units, a public office accepts a filing on day 0, issues a
proposed income reduction on day 2 and owes an ordinary determination by
day 30 unless a lawful exception applies. The resident chose a counter to
keep their temporary location confidential. Its relay scans the proposed
reduction on day 2. Its pickup site closes before the resident can safely
collect it. The resident has an existing payment of 300 due on day 6.

A separate civil claimant files on day 1 with a hearing scheduled for day 12.
A notice for the other party also waits at the closed site. The claimant and
the court can verify routing events without seeing the other party's address.
Each file has its own sender, contact authority, notice and deadline.

| Option | Income reply clock and payment | Civil path |
|---|---|---|
| Treat the relay scan as service | A nominal day 22 reply deadline starts before a usable opportunity. Day 6 interruption based on silence would be unlawful. | The scan supplies no ground to enter default. |
| Use a staffed independent counter from day 4 | Verified delivery on day 4 opens the ordinary full 20-working-day reply period through day 24. Pay the existing 300 on day 6. | Deliver the exact packet on day 4. The judge verifies time to answer or changes the hearing. |
| The alternate is also closed | The failed route starts no reply period. Day 30 remains the office's own deadline and the payment remains due on day 6. | Preserve day 1 filing and seek a judicial direction. Use only separately lawful interim protection before an effective opportunity. |

The independently powered counter and staff need four paid hours at 35,
safe delivery and access help need two at 40, and directions and review
need two at 50. The local administration's voted contact budget covers 220
and the court service's appropriation covers 100. The payment of 300 remains
a separate existing obligation. A backup using the same closed premises
does not count as receiving. Protected records identify the correct
packets without including the confidential location in notices to
opposing parties. The default comparison assumes no usable delivery
independently proved and no deliberate refusal.

If the intended recipient instead deliberately refuses lawful tender of
the usable civil packet, a human witness and packet record can support a
judge's decision on whether and when to proceed. The court still gives
reasons and sets a usable response interval. A relay's failed delivery and
a recipient's silence supply no such evidence. The
[arithmetic and decision check](verify-safe-contact.py) uses the two
closures, dated obligations and a reasoned judicial direction as inputs.
Actual security, delivery, costs and court practice remain open.
