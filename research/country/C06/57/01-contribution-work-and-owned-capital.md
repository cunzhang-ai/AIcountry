# PF01–PF04: saving follows ordinary source treatment

*Part of [private retirement tax](../57-private-retirement-tax-and-personal-capital.md); design 142 future-law candidate.*

**PF01 — Choose post-source private saving, preserve public insurance.** New
voluntary additional NB DC/funded DB and retirement annuity arrangements receive
no new personal contribution deduction/credit, employer pension exclusion,
annual asset-yield levy, tax-free-return promise or special early-withdrawal tax.
PA/TA rates, one personal allowance and actual source law remain. This does not
deduct private saving as business expense or privatize Y/S. A prudential licence
proves neither tax classification nor an actual available public source.

**PF02 — Establish actual own capital.** A person's actually paid private
contribution comes from lawfully owned funds after their applicable source
treatment, including a lawful zero liability/exemption. Record date, amount,
owner, prior relief and unrecovered basis; an asserted unpaid contribution or
untaxed employer promise is no capital. Tax recognition/authorized withholding
can establish basis despite payer remittance default; it does not prove G/S cash.
Do not demand positive income tax where a legitimate allowance made it zero.

An owner-preserving in-kind custody transfer carries original basis/dates;
actual sale/exchange uses TA06, with no market-value step-up by relabelling.
Already recognized personal income actually reinvested adds basis once. Company
tax paid by another legal owner is not the member's personal contribution.
Distinct consumed insurance riders, earlier deductions and cost reimbursements
are not added again. Missing history needs protected evidence/correction,
not automatic zero or full claimed market value.

**PF03 — New work-sponsored saving has an actual cash-remuneration route.**
Include new current-service private-plan funding in ordinary gross remuneration,
with applicable Y, PA withholding and a genuine safe net-cash choice before
voluntary saving. Pay cash to the person's accessible route or, under their
specific revocable instruction before payment, actually remit the after-source
net amount to their plan. An actual authorized net-wage payment to that plan
is paid remuneration; a payslip or unfunded credit alone is not.

State whether an offer is gross pay or a guaranteed net contribution, with its
actual tax/contribution/source cost. A net promise requires its funded gross
equivalent under the applicable one-person tax function; no silent wage/U/I05
deduction, loan or plan-created tax exclusion. New DB current-service premiums
need NB19's actual full cost and person/period allocation, the same remuneration
route and consent. Cash election affects only that new private accrual, not old
DB guarantees, minimum wages or public Y. No forced DB-to-DC conversion follows.

This is an explicit prospective funding choice. It does not deem an inaccessible
unfunded pension promise received or tax a new actuarial present value as cash.
Existing lawful compulsory/private plans retain PF15 transition and stronger
source rights; a new contract cannot postpone presently due wages by a label.

**PF04 — Separate remuneration from sponsor capital and old guarantees.**
NF04 includes genuine gross remuneration once, including withholding inside
gross pay; do not deduct it again as the member's private net contribution.
Sponsor equity/loans/collateral keep their actual capital/claim treatment.
Evidenced guarantee/recovery costs use NF04 once, not a second deduction for an
already recognized cost or an unlimited reserve for projected future payouts.

Restoring an already earned DB promise is not new member principal or a second
private contribution merely because the employer pays more. New service or
benefit enhancement cannot hide as recovery of old funding; trace the actual
promise/period and proper remuneration/source rule. No relief for missing cash
or another entity's tax is fabricated. Sponsor/manager profit and losses remain
their own; beneficiary assets are not unrestricted company or Treasury property.
