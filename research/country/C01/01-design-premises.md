# C01. Design premises

*Part of [C01](../C01-first-principles.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

People have different conceptions of a good life. Knowledge is dispersed and fallible;
power can protect or dominate; material resources and human attention are limited.
Institutions must therefore distribute authority, support real alternatives, expose
their mistakes and preserve the ability to change course. These are design premises,
not scientific proofs of a uniquely correct constitution.

The time and attention people spend proving a need, finding the right office and
correcting a state error are real social costs. Independence of judgment can protect
rights; a separate form, queue or institution for every concern can make those rights
unusable. The design therefore distinguishes decisions that must be independently
made from support functions that may safely be shared. Neither fewer offices nor
more formal review is an end in itself: compare delivered protection, burden,
informal cooperation and the ability to challenge a shared controller.
