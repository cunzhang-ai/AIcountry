Scope: the preserved September reference exercise below tests a limited
social/fiscal workload. It does not cost or validate the full current country
design. Its dated inputs and results remain unchanged; the [current design
priority](../DESIGN-STATUS.md) now uses the [expanded commitment inventory](commitments/README.md)
and [combined-shortfall results](commitments/06-combined-results.md). Both

## 5. What the common accounts reveal

| Reference scenario result at end of year 3 | B | S | U |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Funded debt, million CU | 631.525 | 740.500 | 780.000 |
| Liquid assets, million CU | 150.000 | 137.975 | 111.000 |
| Unfunded cash obligations | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Staffing gap against authored year-3 workload, FTE | 760 | 0 | 760 |

The staffing gap comprises 200 health, 100 education, 400 care and 60 network posts.
These professions are not interchangeable. B and U also allocate only 300 public-service
GWh against the assumed year-3 requirement of 340; S allocates 340 within capacity of 360.
This is not a national emissions or private-energy model. Land, material supply,
geographic access and other ecological limits are not yet quantified.

All packages pass the narrow reference financing limits; B/U do not meet the authored
service workload. That does not prove cash ineffective: this model gives it no estimated
effect on private supply, prices, demand or work. Nor does S's assigned workload prove
that its services actually meet people's needs. Three-decimal accounting is reproducibility,
not empirical precision.

Two separate adverse assumptions expose different failures:

- A year-2 revenue loss of 10% plus 20 million extra income-support need leaves year-2
  financing gaps of 12 million for B, 52 for S and 84.5 for U after assumed borrowing
  and available cash. S carries 64.5 million of unpaid obligations into the end of
  year 3. The gap is not extra debt that a lender has agreed to fund.
- A year-2 loss of 10% of available care staff leaves S short of 155 care FTE despite
  unchanged money. B/U still have their existing workload gaps. A national cash surplus
  cannot stand in for the missing professional capacity.

For S, postponing its additional 20 million of capital in shock year 2 alone would
still leave at least 32 million of that year's financing gap, before any consequences
for service capacity. Raising 52 million in additional receipts that year would address
the immediate arithmetic gap, not establish fair incidence or solve every future need.
Neither adjustment is adopted by the model; each requires a revised coherent package.

## 6. Selection and what changes in the country design

Retain services plus an income floor as the **conditional planning reference**. Reject
the inference that cash expansion or low spending alone completes the social guarantee.
Keep unconditional income open; it could be financed differently or meet goals not
captured here. The figures demonstrate accounting and capacity conflicts under assumptions,
not a causal or moral victory for S.

The concrete design now requires a joint guarantee/funding schedule, designated national
and local delivery duties, protected correction, one consolidated resource account and
a democratic shortfall response. Main-design C04–C07/C13–C14 incorporate those decisions.
The next main task is to reconcile legislative, treasury, local-delivery and judicial
authority into one proposed legal package. Further fiscal calibration remains a named
prerequisite to real operation, not an excuse to avoid that design work.

## Sources and limits

The [ILO's social protection floor explanation](https://www.ilo.org/universal-social-protection-department/areas-work-social-protection-department/policy-development-and-applied-research/social-protection-floor)
describes essential healthcare and basic income security across life stages. It is a
normative comparison, not endorsement of our amounts, allocation or institutions.
The [IMF fiscal-transparency overview](https://www.imf.org/en/topics/fiscal-policies/fiscal-transparency)
describes reporting, budgeting and fiscal-risk disclosure. It supports the transparency
comparison, not these invented financing limits or a finding of fiscal sustainability.
Both pages were inspected on 29 September 2026. No independent empirical estimates,
real population consultation, operational trial or comprehensive country calibration
were obtained. The linked IMF Code PDF could not be fetched; it is not claimed as read.
