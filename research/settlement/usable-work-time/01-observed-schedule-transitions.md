# A weekly number and the hours a worker can actually use

## Chile. A national reduction needs a distribution rule

Chile's Law 21.561 set a staged reduction of the ordinary workweek
from 45 to 44 hours in April 2024, to 42 in April 2026 and to 40 in
April 2028. It prohibits reducing remuneration on account of the
law's application. The Labour Directorate's 16 April 2026 ruling
describes agreement first, including with unions, and a fallback
under the law and its statutory clarification. For a five-day
44-hour week in the specified no-agreement case, the two hours come
off the end of two different days, one hour on each. For a six-day
week the ruling distributes the reduction in two 50-minute parts
and a separate 20-minute part. The employer determines the relevant
days within the legal and contractual limits. This is evidence of
an enacted implementation path. It does not measure compliance,
actual leisure, a gain in national productivity or the cost of a
different hours standard.
[Law 21.561](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/N?f=2023-04-26&i=1191554)
· [Labour Directorate ruling 253/21](https://dt.gob.cl/legislacion/1624/w3-article-129189.html)

## France. Continuous public work has its own constraints

The French public service portal describes a standard of 35 effective
hours per week and 1,607 effective annual hours outside overtime. It
describes reductions for some demanding missions and limited
derogations under specific rules for continuous services or exceptional
circumstances, with compensatory rest. These rules demonstrate that
the weekly norm, effective-hours definition and actual continuity
mechanism can differ. They supply no observed same-service result.
AIcountry's retained paid-break and rest rules have their own scope.
[French public service working-time guide](https://www.fonction-publique.gouv.fr/etre-agent-public/mon-quotidien-au-travail/temps-de-travail-et-conges/la-duree-du-travail-dans-la-fonction-publique)

The existing [matched study record](../../2026-11-paid-time-and-joint-source/sources.md)
already qualifies evidence of recovery and wellbeing from reduced
hours. The legal examples add allocation and timing mechanisms.
They do not provide additional observations from the same worker
interventions or establish a funded 36-hour or 32-hour national stage.
