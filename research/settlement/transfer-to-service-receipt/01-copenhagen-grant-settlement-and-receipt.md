# Copenhagen's midyear grant adjustment and the last payment date

On 17 September 2026 Copenhagen approved its midyear budget
adjustment. The municipality shows a DKK 111.5 million positive
component relating to costs for people displaced from Ukraine.
The whole block-grant adjustment is negative DKK 97.7 million.
Changes to the municipal budget frames total negative DKK 63.7
million. Their difference is negative DKK 34.0 million in the
budget-management cash calculation. The separate local reserve
changes of 355.8 and 110.4 bring the combined assessed draw to
500.2 million. [Council decision](https://www.kk.dk/dagsordener-og-referater/Borgerrepr%C3%A6sentationen/m%C3%B8de-17092026/referat/punkt-4).

Under section 15(2) of Denmark's 2026 settlement order, the
midyear adjustment is applied in thirds to the Danish Tax Agency's
monthly payments in October, November and December. Dividing the
published net adjustment gives roughly DKK 32.57 million less
per month than the unadjusted schedule. The positive component is
roughly DKK 37.17 million per month in that decomposition. These
are amounts inferred from rounded published rows and the legal
schedule. They are not standalone transactions, the whole monthly
remittance or confirmations of settlement. [Settlement order](https://www.retsinformation.dk/api/pdf/250763).

## Revisit the predecessor's displayed difference

[Design436](../local-cash-and-national-budget/01-copenhagen-cash-and-local-payment.md)
used the August report's 1,135.5 million as the starting amount
and found a negative 1.0 million residual. The separate September
decision reports 1,134.5 million for the earlier forecast. Using
that starting amount, the September draw of 500.2, and August's
displayed additions of 66.1, 8.4 and 2.0 reproduces the August
closing figure of 710.8 exactly. This locates the numerical
difference in the two published opening amounts. The reason for
the different openings and a common transaction ledger remain
unknown. The historical August calculation is preserved.

## Constructed release test

A city expected its ordinary October payment of 15 units. A new
programme adds a 4-unit component, while unrelated adjustments
reduce the same remittance by 6. It receives 13. It owes 14 by
the service deadline and has no other eligible cash. Even though
the programme's positive component is 4, the shortfall is 1.
A lawfully preauthorized 1-unit advance can protect payment if
settled before the deadline. If it settles the next day, retain
the missed date and verify the later receipt. If a provider is
paid but delivery has yet to be verified, the service result
remains open. These amounts and outcomes are constructed.
