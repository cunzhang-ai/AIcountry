# IV09–IV12. Budget timing and the date people can use a service

**IV09 — Classify timing changes before approving them.** For a material move
between fiscal periods, the budget office and service owner identify the
affected programme, past authorization, original and revised payment profiles,
current usable service dates and actual dependencies. Distinguish a dated
cash forecast change, a change in lawful payment authority or commitment, and
an expected change in service. State the reason and preserve the previous
approved baseline and later forecasts. A bookkeeping change that leaves
contracts, lawful dues and service unchanged can follow ordinary budget
procedures. Where usable dates, access or burdens may change materially, apply
IV05 and explain who waits, whose work moves, what interim provision will be
available and who pays. An unverified effect remains unresolved. It cannot
be described as a saving or as unchanged access.

**IV10 — Prepare alternatives within limits and release a feasible set.** An
elected body may fund bounded project preparation for several alternatives
while releasing only the set that can run concurrently under its payment,
staff, work-window and receiving constraints. State which stages are
conditional, who can release them and when the option expires. Count actual
preparation costs, option fees, commitments that can be called together and
termination obligations. Test plausible concurrent acceleration and common
delay, not just independent average slippage. No expected underspending
authorizes new commitments or postpones a valid due payment. If several
projects become ready together, the delegated officer acts only within the
recorded limits. A change to protected services, authorized priority or gross
exposure returns to the competent elected body. An independently safe and
useful stage may proceed with its own funding and authority.

**IV11 — Preserve useful access through any material reordering.** Identify
the people who would lack a practical route, including those who cannot use
an apparent substitute. Use ordinary sector standards and evidence of actual
access to distinguish improvements from an immediate existing deficiency.
A count of facilities, applications or appointments is evidence of scale and
cannot decide accessibility, capacity or safety by itself. Before a material
reordering, identify a paid interim route, its accepting owner, useful dates
and the final disposition. Where no safe lawful alternative exists, use the
existing protection and escalation route and report each unmet obligation.
The Assembly or competent local body can decide which unadopted improvement
waits. It cannot silently cancel enforceable duties by changing a cash year.
Keep limited funds and jurisdictional obligations separate, including school,
care, disability, energy and democratic or legal access.

**IV12 — Keep review useful without duplicate government.** Use existing
budget publications and review channels for the shared fiscal and service
account. Give affected people and opposition a way to challenge an
unrealistic service date or an unavailable substitute before the relevant
season, closure, payment or loss of access. Preserve safe source access and
scope for oversight. Courts enforce existing law, and auditors may examine
the original and revised results. Neither sets the investment ranking.
AI may compare schedules and disclose assumptions, with accountable staff
checking disputed cases against original evidence. Use this rule prospectively
under authorized thresholds and paid review. Keep prior claims and contracts
through migration. Reopen if the added step delays service, leaves the same
people unreached or fails to detect cumulative exposure.
