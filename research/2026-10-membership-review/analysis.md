# Democratic membership: who can replace the government?

2 October 2026 · Assistant-authored supporting analysis · Research proposal

## The design gap

[Design 4](../2026-10-integrated-review/COUNTRY-DESIGN-design-4.md) gives national
votes to adult citizens and leaves local participation for long-term residents
under an unspecified rule. It promises an accessible citizenship path but no
effective boundary against an indefinitely resident, taxed and governed population
with no national vote. A government could control naturalization or immigration
status, thereby choosing part of its own electorate. This is a textual possibility,
not evidence of an actual excluded population. It matters across C01–C03, C10 and
C14 and the older [democratic candidate](../2026-09-democratic-control/democratic-constitution.md).

Values are equal political standing, self-determination, durable community, the
interests of those who move or are displaced, and security of genuine elections.
Constraints include identity and residence evidence, lawful migration and treaty
rules, safe records, privacy and the legitimacy of the founding electorate. The
founding people cannot be created by this research choice; the proposal below is
for an ordinary operating franchise if legitimately adopted.

## Alternatives and recommendation

| Reference arrangement | Democratic case | Failure or contested value |
|---|---|---|
| Retain citizenship-only national vote with a discretionary naturalization route | Nationality marks a durable political bond; ordinary electoral law can remain simple. | The cabinet can make naturalization inaccessible or employer-dependent while long-term residents remain governed without national control. |
| Citizenship-only national vote with a guaranteed, time-bounded naturalization right | Residents can reach the electorate while retaining citizenship as the formal boundary. | Applications, language tests, documentation and appeals may still delay or exclude; changing nationality may be unwanted or impossible for people with other ties. |
| Select a settled-resident franchise alongside citizens | Adult residents who have lived under national institutions across an ordinary electoral cycle can vote and stand regardless of nationality; citizenship has other lawful functions. | One-citizen/one-resident voting across jurisdictions, evidence of residence, short-term movement, campaign finance and nonresident-citizen voting need careful rules. This may be controversial and cannot be imposed at founding by its proponents. |
| Immediate vote for every person physically present | Minimizes exclusion of newly arrived people. | A visitor with no durable stake could affect government before people who remain; identity, duplicate voting and administrability become harder. Basic rights remain universal regardless of franchise. |

Select the third as the **working national design**, conditional on a legitimate
founding settlement. An adult citizen keeps one national vote, with usable
registration from abroad under adopted anti-duplication rules. An adult noncitizen
becomes eligible for the same national vote and candidacy after **one ordinary
four-year Assembly term of habitual residence**. This period is a provisional
normative choice tied to the existing election cycle, not a proven optimum or a
rule already binding anyone. Time spent as an asylum seeker, refugee or under a
disputed administrative status counts when habitual residence can be shown;
eligibility is not controlled by an employer, landlord, model account or party.
Local voting and candidacy begin under a shorter, published habitual-residence
rule set by valid law. Temporary visitors retain rights and standing to contest
harm but not an immediate claim to govern a polity they are visiting. Citizenship
acquisition remains available under transparent, reviewable rules and must not
decide essential protection.

The same substantive eligibility rules apply to government and opposition voters.
Registration uses accessible evidence, independent correction and a timely election
remedy. Missing documents call for a safe evidentiary procedure, not automatic
disenfranchisement or unverified voting. Stateless people, displaced people and
people with disability or limited language access require workable routes. Children
retain rights, supported voice and representation of their interests without an
invented child ballot; the age 18 remains an explicit reference hypothesis. Any
restriction on candidacy for a particular sensitive office must be narrow, lawful,
reasoned and reviewable, rather than a blanket way to nullify settled residents'
vote.

## Sources, objections and migration

The [UN Human Rights Committee's General Comment 25](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=NDHbUvPo0H0e6ReM%2BeoASfvbY3aH6JttLpsRvkmL87wD80W4xnAiRpmKA6ZLwTp9xzCLNUYwTBP35uH1Fw6yqQ%3D%3D)
explains international protections for every *citizen's* political rights and
requires objective, reasonable conditions. It does **not** require the selected
noncitizen national franchise. The [Council of Europe explanatory report on foreign
residents' local participation](https://rm.coe.int/16800cb5e1)
offers a comparison for local voting, not national adoption. [International IDEA's
study of out-of-country voting](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/html/global-state-democracy-2025-democracy-move)
identifies practical and value questions for citizens abroad. These are primary
institutional sources, not evidence that this AIcountry rule works. No real
population, migrant association, election body or independent reviewer took part.

The strongest objections are that national voting without nationality may weaken a
shared political bond, permit foreign influence or give temporary populations
disproportionate power. The selected duration and one-person-one-vote record address
some mechanisms, while ordinary campaign-finance and coercion rules address others;
they cannot prove legitimacy in a particular polity. The opposing objection is that
even four years leaves governed people without a vote and that citizens abroad may
have more influence than long-term residents. Earlier local participation, universal
rights and safe naturalization mitigate but do not erase this exclusion. These are
partly value disagreements, not facts to average away.

Adoption would require changing C01–C03 and the older citizen-only candidate D1,
plus election registers, candidacy, local voting, naturalization, data protection,
appeals, migration and anti-duplicate rules. Pending residence and nationality
claims, existing rights, and valid citizenship cannot disappear during migration.
The legitimate founding electorate must be settled **before** any proposed new
franchise approves itself; no design author or AI can claim a ratifying population.
There is no legal authorization, operating register, verified cost or observed
electoral outcome here. Reopen the choice if a real affected public rejects this
boundary, evidence shows an unjustified exclusion or capture route, or a different
democratic membership arrangement better protects equal standing.
