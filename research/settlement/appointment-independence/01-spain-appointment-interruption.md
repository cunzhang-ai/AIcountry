# Spain. A renewal impasse reached downstream judicial posts

The European Commission records Spain's Council for the Judiciary
serving in an interim capacity from December 2018. The original Organic
Law 4/2021, published on 30 March 2021, limited what the interim
council could do. Article 570 bis retained named ordinary functions,
such as participation in judicial selection under applicable law and
regulated career decisions, while removing the ability to make the
senior appointments identified by the Commission. The restriction
was specific to the acting council and did not dissolve the courts.
[Original law, pp. 35948–35951](../../2026-11-appointment-independence-and-concurrent-vacancies/original-sources/boe-2021-4907.pdf)
· [Commission country chapter, pp. 3–5](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/2bd09a6f-ef56-494a-8303-e0de808ee981_en?filename=23_1_58063_coun_chap_spain_en_0.pdf)

The Commission's July 2024 report records **27 Supreme Court
vacancies** within a broader **99 judicial posts pending appointment**.
They are nested counts, rather than additive categories. The same
report describes extra lawyers at the court's technical office as a
partial operational response. An administrative support measure
cannot fill the judicial positions. Decrees dated 24 July record ten
appointments to the council on each parliamentary chamber's proposal.
These are two groups of council members, a different population from
the 99 pending judicial posts.
[Commission country chapter, p. 5](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/2bd09a6f-ef56-494a-8303-e0de808ee981_en?filename=23_1_58063_coun_chap_spain_en_0.pdf)

The record supports tracing downstream positions and ordinary service
under an appointment interruption. It gives neither the number of cases
that would have been decided under another constitution nor a test of
AIcountry's particular selection rules. Earlier and later Spanish law
remain outside this specific comparison.
