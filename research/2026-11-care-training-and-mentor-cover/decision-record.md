# Design283. Care training and mentor cover

10 November 2026 · Research selection · No actual enactment

## Baseline and decision

[Design282](baseline.json) carries a priced care preparation option
within a constructed first budget. WF85–WF89 already distinguish
training and delivery. This decision specifies the frontline source
and useful-time protection required before opening a real cohort.
Apply the [working framework](../FRAMEWORK.md) and
[decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md). Codex assisted the
research and drafting. No affected party, independent reviewer,
delivery authority or elected body took part in this selection.

## Diagnoses and alternatives

The [Copenhagen evidence](sources.md) identifies funding for training
work at the service sites. It also reports higher annual completions
alongside a lower completion rate. The evidence does not supply the
causes of individual departures or a roster of received care. Test
whether the constraint is missing applicants, unsuitable terms,
scarce supervisors, shortfalls in ordinary cover or a lack of actual
receiving work for qualified people. A larger class addresses only
some of these problems.

- **Retain and repair.** Fund ordinary care, support current workers
  and help qualified returners where feasible. This can address an
  earlier service loss with no placement burden. It may leave an
  enduring shortage of particular qualifications.
- **Select a separately funded cohort with protected teaching sites.**
  Bind only places the sites can actually teach and backfill. Prefer
  a parallel start when all sites and relevant periods qualify.
  Choose an exact staged intake in the declared weaker case when
  it is authorized and both windows are covered. Retain repair and
  hold the cohort when the needed cover remains unqualified.
- **Create a standing regional teaching and relief team.** An
  independent paid team can make placements reliable across employers,
  and can reduce dependence on a single contractor. It has recurring
  costs, shared-call limits, travel and specific qualifications.
  It could move a shortage to the sending district or displace a more
  effective retention policy. Reopen if repeated separate procurement
  is slower or more costly on the same protected workload.

## Authority and continuity

The competent elected bodies vote sources. The school owns lawful
instruction and assessment, the placement site owns mentorship, and
the current service owner owns actual receiving and the duties at risk.
Each accepts only its work within valid law and budget. Independent
review can inspect the original records and correct a missed service or
unfair application through the existing routes. Separate labor choice
from the operator's staffing forecast. Safety complaints and refusal of
an optional model remain available without undue consequences.

The [constructed comparison](../settlement/care-training-and-mentor-cover/02-authored-placement-window.md)
verifies two windows, current care, backfill and the effect of a delayed
source. Its numbers are independent of Copenhagen and the earlier
integrated budget. The evidence qualifies paid preparation design,
with actual funded starts and care capacity still open. Preserve wages,
training already promised, independent assessment, original care dates,
worker mobility and the funded closeout of a failed route. No automatic
renewal, withdrawal of rights or national capacity estimate follows.

## Next bounded action

Ask the school and one competent service owner for an exact placement
calendar, payroll and funded backup for a single cohort. Compare a
later staggered start and funded retention on the same due tasks.
Verify available cover at both origin and receiving sites, acceptance
and useful-time independent challenge. Treat later placement or hiring
as a separate fact. Reopen on a missed shift, material learner or user
harm, a binding change to pay, or a better supported whole alternative.
