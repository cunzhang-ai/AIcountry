# Power during disagreement, delay and institutional failure

22 September 2026 · Scoped successor research candidate · Not enacted

Read with the [democratic candidate](../2026-09-democratic-control/democratic-constitution.md).
The corrections below concern D2–D5 and D8. Other provisions remain research
hypotheses with their existing limits. Dates and thresholds below are authored
comparison parameters, not empirical optima. No current national edition was verified.

## 1. The deeper problem: doing nothing distributes power

Democratic control requires both limits on unauthorized power and usable ways to
exercise authorized power. A rule may achieve the first while frustrating the
second. A veto can protect a minority, entrench an incumbent, or do both. Continuing
the status quo also distributes benefits and harms; it is not politically neutral.

Three obligations must be evaluated separately:

- **Authority:** no timeout, silence, emergency or model prediction creates consent,
  a winning candidate, a new legislative mandate or an unlimited guardian.
- **Progress:** under stated conditions, a valid claim can reach a decision and an
  effective remedy despite specified actors' refusal to cooperate.
- **Continuity:** people retain essential services and existing claims while authority
  is contested. Service delivery does not legitimate whoever happens to deliver it.

These are design commitments, not a theorem deriving a unique constitution. There
is no demonstrated mechanism here that guarantees all three under simultaneous
capture of courts, treasury, electoral administration and enforcement. Claims must
identify the independent capability they still assume. A failure state describes
lost protection; declaring it neither excuses officials nor creates extra powers.

In particular, distinguish disagreement over policy, refusal to carry out an
already valid order, uncertainty about who has authority, and actual inability to
deliver. Each needs a different response. Peaceful refusal to approve a proposal
is not misconduct merely because it causes delay.

## 2. Compare the defaults before choosing a fallback

| Package | Mechanism and possible benefit | Who gains from delay; principal failure |
|---|---|---|
| A. Retain incumbents until agreement | Continuity and pressure to negotiate; few extra institutions | Existing officeholders may benefit from preventing agreement; disputed power can become indefinite |
| B. After a deadline, permit a simple majority to decide | Produces appointments or government formation when a majority exists | A majority can deliberately exhaust the clock to evade the original safeguard; does not solve missing evidence or payment capacity |
| C. Separate policy authority, service continuity and case protection | Time limits restrict discretionary power; preauthorized services and independent case remedies can continue | Requires real staff, funds and independent adjudication; may preserve services while the political dispute remains unresolved |

Select C for further research because it does not make every unresolved dispute a
choice between unchecked rulers and a service shutdown. Do not claim that C always
resolves deadlock or is cheaper. Retain A as a serious comparator for short, ordinary
coalition negotiations. B can be a defensible choice for ordinary government formation
in a separately designed democratic system; it is not automatically appropriate for
appointments intended to constrain that majority. The purposes differ.

The Venice Commission's Montenegro opinion discusses how fallback incentives can
undermine negotiated appointments and why mechanisms need context-specific design
(paragraphs 10–19). It does not validate C or these parameters.
[Original opinion, CDL-AD(2018)015](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD(2018)015-e).

As a different constitutional choice, Germany's Basic Law Article 63 provides a
timed parliamentary selection process with a presidential appointment/dissolution
choice in its final minority outcome. Article 69 separately addresses continued
conduct of government business. AIcountry has not established that presidency;
borrowing the timing without its allocation of authority would be incomplete.
[Official text, Articles 63 and 69](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html).

## 3. C1 — Start the clock before the actor can block it

**Proposed replacement for an unqualified reading of D8:** final certification and
successor election remain necessary legal events, but cannot be the only triggers
for oversight and restrictions on the outgoing administration.

An election schedule adopted before the contest must identify the scheduled end
of the ordinary mandate, polling dates, publication obligations, complaint channels,
institutional convening duties and service-continuity authority. Scheduled dates
remain observable even if the commission, speaker or platform refuses to publish.
A missed duty creates a challengeable omission; it does not create a winner.

Reference schedule to compare, with day 0 as the preannounced close of ordinary
polling:

| Trigger | Required response | What it does not authorize |
|---|---|---|
| Day 7 without a published count and certification status | Electoral authority publishes missing records, affected contests and reasons; candidates and voters can seek an order from the independent electoral court | Inferring a result from polls, AI estimates or the deadline |
| Day 14 | Independent court reviews unresolved omissions or outcome-relevant disputes and gives an evidence-based, scoped remedial timetable; serious cases may require longer | Automatic dismissal of complaints or approval of an incumbent's account |
| Scheduled end of the ordinary mandate, even without final certification | Ordinary outgoing policy authority expires; only the separately preauthorized caretaker remit continues | A renewed ordinary mandate, arbitrary service cancellation or an unelected substitute government |
| Final lawful certification | D8's 14-day convening duty applies; a preauthorized parliamentary clerk can convene without the outgoing executive's signature | Seating disputed representatives solely because time elapsed |
| 30 days after the new Assembly first meets without electing a cabinet | A mandatory public formation debate and recorded ballot; Assembly considers continuation, a minority-government alternative requiring its legitimate adoption, or D2's two-thirds early-election route | Lowering the majority threshold by this research note or executive dissolution |
| Successor legally elected | D8's 7-day transfer duty, with independently authorized custody recovery and enforceable orders | Treating signed paperwork as completed transfer |

The 30-day review is an escalation deadline, **not a bound on successful formation**.
D2's unspecified automatic formation-failure election trigger remains unqualified
for adoption until its conditions, repeat-election costs and caretaker consequences
are settled. No automatic new election is introduced here. Repeated elections can
reproduce the same impasse and impose costs on voters and services.

Complaints stay only what a reasoned independent order finds necessary. A local
complaint does not automatically suspend a national result. Conversely, apparently
local errors in a compensatory proportional system may affect national seat allocation;
scope follows the demonstrated dependency, not administrative geography. Do not seat
an outcome-determinative majority under the label of an uncontested partial result.
Absence of a functioning court is an unresolved protection failure, not deemed consent.

Caretakers may execute existing lawful service obligations and narrowly necessary
reversible administration. Exceptional acts require the applicable independent
authorization, reasons and a funded account; neither an executive nor a reviewer
may use necessity to change election rules, allocate permanent oversight appointments
or determine its successor. Material decisions remain challengeable, with weekly
public reporting of the continuing impasse and outstanding remedies. Reporting
alone is not a remedy. All caretaker service counts toward D2's eight-year limit.
The lawful personnel succession at that limit must be specified before adoption;
this candidate does not invent a neutral replacement when the whole chain fails.

## 4. C2 — A reserve is a dependent institution, not a promise

D4/D5's preselected alternates remain a possible continuity device. Withdraw the
implication that their mention resolves appointment deadlock.

For every seat, expose the complete chain: qualification, nomination, selection,
challenge, acceptance, availability, succession and payment. Include who selects
the selectors and can withhold access or disqualify a candidate. Map a vacancy
against actual voting and quorum rules. D4/D5 have not yet specified those rules;
therefore their claimed tolerance of multiple vacancies is unknown.

Proposed ordinary appointment schedule: open a scheduled vacancy process 180 days
before expiry; publish an initial selection and reserve-availability assessment by
90 days before expiry; publish unresolved deficiencies and hold a hearing by 30
days before expiry. Unexpected vacancies trigger an immediate availability check.
Failed preparation does not lower selection thresholds, prolong a nonrenewable
term or create executive acting powers. Reservists must consent and remain eligible;
a stale name on a list is not staffed capacity.

A lawful reservist serves only the existing remaining term, with the seat's original
source and restrictions. Deliberately repeated vacancies do not restart terms or
authorize a new source. Reserve replenishment is independently reviewed under its
applicable procedure; actors cannot nominate their own emergency replacement after
the selection failure they induced.

If lawful appointments cannot supply a functioning court, compare two materially
different continuities before adoption: a preselected reserve bench, or previously
authorized ordinary courts retaining limited rights-remedy jurisdiction while final
constitutional questions await a competent court. The second distributes case
protection instead of handing someone the vacant office. It also risks inconsistent
orders and professional capture. Neither option is selected as an automatic
country-wide fallback here: jurisdiction, review conflicts, resources, quorum and
reconciliation are unresolved. Existing ordinary remedies cannot simply be erased
by creating a new constitutional court.

Maintain duties to seek lawful appointments and remedy existing harms even when
the reserve fails. Do not punish a representative for a good-faith vote against a
nominee. Sanctions require independently established specific unlawful conduct;
an AI assessment of obstruction is insufficient.

## 5. C3 — Formal opposition support is not independent authorization

Retain D5's three-fifths plus one-third-of-non-government-members rule only as a
**formal bargaining hypothesis**. Withdraw any inference that passing it proves
independent review or effective opposition. See the numerical counterexample in
the [failure traces](failure-traces.md).

Before an appointment process begins, publish its governing composition date,
denominator, treatment of vacancies, abstentions and coalition changes. Members
retain political freedom to change affiliation; affiliation changes do not silently
rewrite an ongoing appointment's denominator. A demonstrably changed governing
coalition requires a reasoned procedure for reassessment and fresh consideration,
not retrospective transfer of votes. This anti-manipulation rule needs legitimate
adoption before use; no denominator convention is silently supplied to current law.

Review financial dependence, nomination control, employment pressure, shared campaign
infrastructure and actual freedom to challenge or replace leaders. Shared policy
views or voting together are not proof of common control. Missing evidence is not
proof of independence. Protect sensitive witnesses and give an accused group access
to the case and a fair challenge route.

Crucially, an independence assessment is not a new power for government, judges,
platforms or this project to declare an opponent inauthentic and remove its votes.
Formal eligibility, research confidence and adjudicated misconduct are distinct.
Any binding consequence requires an existing lawful, proportionate remedy with
independent review. The same scrutiny applies to citizen panels, professional
selection bodies, media owners and the investigators themselves.

Plural nomination routes should be compared with meaningful competitive entry,
minority access to information and resources, and the distribution of underlying
ownership. Creating another commission cannot substitute for those conditions.

## 6. C4 — Protect services without entrenching the previous budget

Replace D5's unspecified continuity-rate claim with the following bounded research
candidate. Its funding and appropriation route must be established before use.

A preadopted bridge appropriation can cover up to 90 days of essential existing
obligations after an ordinary budget expires. Eligible lines, payment dates, responsible
staff, accounts and appeal routes are specified in advance. The cash demand for a
line is its documented obligations falling due in that period under the last lawful
schedule, subject to its preadopted bridge ceiling; do not assume annual costs arrive
in equal monthly portions. Election-period costs, due judgments and care obligations
must be included explicitly. Unfunded amounts remain visible obligations.

This does not establish that the previous allocation was fair or constitutionally
adequate. A rights-related insufficiency remains open to independent remedy; the
ceiling is not authority to deny a right. Material or revenue loss requires a
shared account of competing essential claims, harm and feasible alternatives.
An equal percentage cut is a policy choice with unequal consequences, not a neutral
constitutional answer. Reviewers cannot create missing cash or reserve it all for
themselves. Disputed own-budget claims require a separately constituted review route
under the applicable prior law, with the conflict and the route's capacity disclosed.

Treasury officers implement the preauthorized schedule without a new discretionary
executive release. Independent audit reconciles authorization, available cash,
payment, recipient receipt and continued service. Separate payment authorization
from custody of the only usable banking credential; qualify actual recovery and
manual alternatives. Lack of a usable payment channel is a delivery failure even
when a ledger shows full appropriation.

By day 30, publish shortfalls and the successor budget proposal. By day 60, the
Assembly must deliberate and vote on an ordinary budget or a separately authorized,
time-limited continuation, with reasons and rights review. The executive and treasury
cannot renew the bridge on their own. At day 90 unused bridge authority expires;
accrued claims remain and any distinct, valid standing service authority continues.
If no funded lawful continuation exists, report the impending interruption before
expiry and the responsible duties breached. Expiry does not authorize an invented
payment power. **This proposal has not yet demonstrated a safe day-91 service path**;
that is an adoption blocker, not an acceptable way to discipline voters or staff.

## 7. C5 — Apply the same distinctions to future community collaboration

In research collaboration, a lack of agreement may justify further inquiry or no
change. A question must not acquire an answer because the most active agents kept
submitting it. A preauthorized steward may keep research records and ordinary
services available; it may not infer adoption, discard dissent or amend its mandate.

For consequential shared decisions, identify when a response period begins, who
can observe it, accessible notice, the cost of delay and the authorized default.
Pause or extend affected response opportunities when notice or access fails, under
the applicable procedure. Do not let an operator silently start a clock that only
its agents can meet. A pause in publication is different from extending public
office or suspending someone's existing remedy.

Immersive meetings, demonstrations, asynchronous personal agents and future media
may express proposals or disagreement. Preserve their relevant meaning and known
loss, with a human-usable way to challenge interpretation. Unreceived messages and
disconnected agents are not affirmative consent. A signed message can prove origin
without proving comprehension, a current mandate or agreement about its meaning.
Replication and faster computation cannot multiply standing or response obligations.

Alternative groups may continue inquiry under their own lawful scopes. Forking
research does not divide national sovereignty, duplicate a budget, move private
testimony without permission or discharge duties to affected people. When common
authority is unresolved, preserve existing protections and explain the conflict;
do not make a central AI the default constitutional arbiter.

## 8. Scope of the improvement

These provisions expose four previously hidden failure paths and specify narrower
responses. They do not demonstrate a working democracy. Independence, staffing,
effective enforcement, legitimate founding membership and actual public participation
remain unestablished. Maintaining these limits is part of correcting the proposal,
not evidence that the remaining gaps are harmless.

The next bounded comparison is the court-vacancy case: reserve bench versus retained
ordinary-court remedies, under the same missing appointments, common-controller
threat, staff and cash limit. Compare claimant access, delay, conflicting orders,
political capture and cost. A result favoring a different judicial architecture is
allowed. Do not add a new guardian until that comparison changes the choice.
