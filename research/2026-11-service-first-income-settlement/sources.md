# Original comparator, source meanings and current-event check

Inspected 3 November 2026 · English research notes, not foreign benefit advice

[Pareliussen, Hwang and Viitamäki (2018), OECD Economics Department Working
Paper 1464](https://doi.org/10.1787/d8c0fbc4-en): original
[31-page PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/04/basic-income-or-a-single-tapering-rule-incentives-inclusiveness-and-affordability-compared-for-the-case-of-finland_bba33b82/d8c0fbc4-en.pdf).
Read its model design, tables 5/A3.2, distributional section and source caveats;
visually checked tables and fiscal section. Source family FI-2018-TaxBen/TUJA,
distinct from the randomized experiment reused in design 135. The two modelling
frameworks and different hosted versions are not independent replications.

**Bounded source summary:** rules date 1 July 2015. Published household cases
compare existing benefits, BI and coordinated tapering, including childcare
fees; they are not observed individual outcomes. TaxBen BI changes allowances
and replaces benefits including early retirement. TUJA additionally changes
pension/disability amounts and other benefits; its targeted variant uses family
means where individual insurance cannot be modelled. The two layers therefore
have different specifications. TaxBen's 65% taper is not TUJA's fiscally neutral 38%.
The fiscal scenarios assume entitlement take-up. Their distributional results
cannot qualify an unchanged-rights individual guarantee by themselves.

**Our calculation:** transcribe four table rows with explicit insured status,
children, wage/time and table/page locators in [published cases](published-cases.json).
For each, `100 − AETR` is net-income change per 100 extra gross earnings.
It is not absolute income, an hourly marginal slope, a work response, service
quality, a pension outcome or a new national currency/median. No rates were
digitized from plotted curves or estimated for missing households. Source table
rounding remains; no average or population reweighting of these four cases.

**Boundary audit:** source substitutions violate this project's retained-rights
comparison, and family means conflict with individual actual access. Reject
transferred fiscal/poverty/cost/superiority claims for either candidate. Complete
source-law mapping, protected income after new taxes, actual old-age coverage,
care/service quality/time, lawful transition and behavior remain unmatched.
These are constraints on inference, not evidence that every U or A must fail.
The limited table arithmetic remains usable within its own dated context.

**Current-event check:** [Kela's current general social security
benefit page](https://www.kela.fi/unemployment-general-social-security-benefit)
identifies a means-tested unemployment benefit from 1 May 2026, with registration
requirements. Thus the 2015 comparator is expressly historical, not today's
Finnish benefit system or a universal payment. The ministry press-release
retrieval returned 403; no claim to have read that page. The source-law case
is not silently updated using 2026 amounts or transplanted eligibility.

Design135's [later original experiment review](../2026-11-income-security-and-credit-continuity/sources.md)
also remains scoped: it is no national financing or unconditionality-only
identification. No new private records, testimony, recruitment, data permits,
affected participation or qualified independent review were obtained.

The second comparison layer loads unchanged authored country130/131 case
artifacts: one named power/provider context, not observed Finnish households
or a newly calibrated country. Full model/purchase/workforce assumptions are
not independently requalified here; use its scoped accounting and preserved
unknowns only. Consumer/source pins record exactly what was inspected and
what remains outside this decision. No price/year/currency conversion connects
the two layers, and no Finnish law or political authority is inherited.
