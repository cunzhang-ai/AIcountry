# Observed enforcement, prosecutorial rules and alternatives

## A candidate can remain on the ballot and lose political opportunity

The European Court of Human Rights Grand Chamber judgment of 22
December 2020 in *Selahattin Demirtaş v. Turkey (No. 2)* found, among
other violations, that the applicant's pretrial detention pursued
an ulterior purpose of suppressing pluralism and political debate,
particularly during two campaigns. It also found a violation of
the right to free elections under Protocol No. 1 because prolonged
unjustified detention prevented him from taking part in parliamentary
proceedings. The Court required measures to secure immediate release.
The judgment establishes the findings and remedy in its case. It does
not establish the time of actual release, a universal custody rule
or the later disposition of distinct proceedings.
[Grand Chamber judgment, paragraphs 383–398, 437–442 and operative part](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/docx/pdf?filename=CASE+OF+SELAHATT%C4%B0N+DEM%C4%B0RTA%C5%9E+v.+TURKEY+%28No.+2%29.pdf&id=001-207173&library=ECHR).

## Written directions can coexist with ministerial authority

Canada's federal Director of Public Prosecutions Act sets written and
publication requirements for Attorney General directives under its
scope, with specified delay rules. It also provides a notice route
for assuming conduct of a prosecution after consultation. The statute
shows how a system can keep a trace while reserving intervention.
It is a substantive rival to AIcountry's selected OJ separation.
Publication does not independently verify neutrality, useful-time
remedy or actual protection in an individual case.
[Canadian statute, sections 10–11 and 15](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/d-2.5/page-1.html?wbdisable=true).

## Protecting a democratic function and insulating a person differ

The Venice Commission's 2014 report distinguishes non-liability
for parliamentary functions from procedural inviolability. It
argues against absolute and unconditional inviolability and
discusses why preliminary investigations need to remain possible.
The joint Venice Commission and OSCE/ODIHR guidelines address
misuse of administrative resources during electoral processes.
These are institutional standards, not evidence of local execution
or a reason to transfer a particular immunity into AIcountry.
[Venice Commission report, paragraphs 89–96 and 157–163](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD%282014%29011-e).
[Joint administrative-resource guidelines](https://odihr.osce.org/odihr/elections/227506).
