# Does one national form fit an unknown founding country?

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 41 research selection; not enacted law

Prepared by an AI assistant without a territory, population, founding public,
service map, electoral administrator, court, budget or affected-party
participation. The [design-40 predecessor](predecessor-design-40/README.md)
is preserved. This record qualifies the working country design; it does not
create a region, change a ballot or authorize a founding election.

## Bounded problem and competing explanations

The C03 reference specifies a national Assembly with N seats, D territorial
seats and a two-vote proportional count. C04 assigns local government and
interlocal preparation, retaining a final Assembly vote for unresolved
material common allocations. C03 counts potential legislative overload and
names a regional rival. Those protections do not establish that the same
arrangement is practical at any scale. A remote place can lack a usable
opposition and remedy even with one formally equal vote; a vast national
docket can transfer practical control to cabinet; an extra regional tier
can also duplicate costs and create a new local patron. None of these
failures is observed in AIcountry because no actual country exists.

Possible causes of failure include geography, population distribution,
languages, historical rights, scarce staff, national-party capture,
interlocal spillovers or poorly drawn electoral rules. More efficient
communication could reduce some hearing and travel cost while leaving
material conflicts and voting authority intact. Values in tension include
equal national standing, local control, protection of distinct peoples,
efficient common provision, legislative attention and affordable oversight.
A distance or population threshold alone cannot choose among them.

[International IDEA's electoral-system handbook](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/electoral-system-design-new-international-idea-handbook)
treats electoral choice as interdependent with the whole institutional
framework. Its [boundary guidance](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/international-electoral-standards-guidelines-reviewing-legal-framework)
identifies equal vote weight, public participation and legal delimitation.
The [OECD multi-level governance review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/regional-governance-in-oecd-countries_4d7c6483-en.html)
describes different regional forms and the need to clarify shared duties;
[International IDEA's federalism primer](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/federalism)
explains constitutionally protected regional self-government. These are
comparators, not a universal population rule, affected-public consent or
proof of AIcountry's preferred form.

## Coherent options for a founding public

| Package | Possible gain | Material failure or value conflict |
|---|---|---|
| **A. National Assembly, elected local councils and interlocal preparation; retain as conditional reference** | Clear national rights/budget chain and no permanent region whose area lacks a shared political life | Many recurrent interlocal choices can overload the Assembly; remote places can lose real access |
| B. Directly elected regional tier with limited legislative/fiscal competence and national rights floor | Durable shared-area choices can be made by a reachable electorate with own staff and budget | New patronage, minority exclusion, uneven tax bases and duplicated institutions; a single regional map may fit neither catchments nor identities |
| C. Issue-scoped interlocal/service compacts with national residual law but no general regional legislature | Flexible fit for different watersheds, transport or health networks | Indirect control and fragmented claims; compacts cannot legitimately tax nonmembers or resolve material disputes by silence |
| Retain the jurisdiction's existing lawful form while preparing a decision | Protects due rights and avoids an invented state start | Existing institutions may themselves be exclusionary or unable to deliver; retention requires review and remedy |

The selected [C14 rule](../country/C14/05-scale-and-national-form.md)
retains package A as a **conditional reference**, with B and C genuinely
available to the legitimate founding public. Before the first election,
that public must choose N, D, the map and full count law from original
territorial and population evidence, and examine a whole package of
legislative work, service reach, equalization and remedies. The C03
one-third D ceiling and no legal threshold remain candidate rules, not
portable numbers. If A fails practical representation or Assembly-capacity
tests, it is not silently repaired by cabinet authority, a self-appointed
region, D=0, or a model-drawn map. B, C or lawful retention must be compared
and specifically authorized. Distinct Indigenous and other collective
claims are not treated as an administrative region-sizing input.

## Democratic power, capacity, transition and objections

Trace who sets the founding electorate, obtains original geographic data,
proposes maps, hears remote and excluded people, appoints the election
body, funds opposition and courts, reviews boundaries, staffs local and
national bodies, executes remedies and transfers power after defeat.
A pre-founding cabinet, colonial predecessor, dominant party or AI mediator
cannot use its own count of affected people to certify its mandate. A
binding first ballot cannot depend on post-election completion of N, D,
registration, list eligibility or recount rules. Confidential claims and
unsafe testimony need protected participation and a legal objection route.

C06 must cost each complete package in cash, qualified staff, hearing and
resident time, tax base, equalization, travel, communication, court and
source-record capacity. C04 assigns each water, housing, care and shared
network duty to an accountable level with a funded successor; a new region
cannot inherit only attractive assets while another level keeps accrued
liabilities. C02's person-level rights and distinct collective claims,
C03's opposition and actual transfer, C11 evidence and C12 plural
communication continue across any branch. C14's opening inventory and
commencement gates must preserve current law and claims until lawful
successors work. A map is not an executed judgment or a delivered clinic.

The strongest objection to A is that a large dispersed polity could be
permanently governed through distant national bargains. The conditional
test gives that objection authority to displace A, but no real data yet
establish which alternative wins. The strongest objection to B is that a
new regional majority might dominate its minorities or waste scarce
professionals. The protected national floor, real cost and territorial-
status route constrain it but cannot eliminate the risk. C can follow
functional geography yet diffuse accountability; direct claims and elected
finality remain necessary. These conflicts cannot be averaged into one
score. No affected public has considered or accepted this selection.

## Status and bounded real-world test

Evidence qualification: the sources above describe institutional options;
the gap is a logical dependency in current C03/C04/C14 design. Research
selection: A remains conditional, and B/C/retention receive a mandatory
pre-founding comparison when scale and reach matter. Legal authorization:
none. Operational readiness: no population, map, staff, money, data or
court. Observed outcomes: none. No comparative superiority claim follows.

For a real possible jurisdiction, a legitimate public sponsor would select
one actual national/local interface and one contested constituency. It
would publish original population and claims maps, travel/communication
routes, an Assembly docket and service/court capacity; rehearse a contested
ballot, shared-resource dispute and remote person's remedy under A, B and C.
An independent review and affected people must test both outcomes and who
controlled the framing. The lawful sponsor, scope, time and budget remain
unassigned. Reopen the conditional reference if actual representation,
backlog, cost, rights-holder claims or delivered remedies favor another
form, or if new communication changes what participation people can use.
A failed A test does not itself ratify B or C.
