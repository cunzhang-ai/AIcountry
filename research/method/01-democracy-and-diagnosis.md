# Democratic standing and diagnosis

## Democratic control is a design requirement

The project's intended country is a constitutional democracy. People must have
equal political standing and practical means to organize alternatives, influence
decisions, select and peacefully replace government, and obtain enforceable rights
remedies. A democratic label, public feedback or good service performance cannot
substitute for those powers. This commitment follows the user's clarified direction;
it is not a claim that any real country or ratifying population already exists.

The [democratic constitutional candidate](../2026-09-democratic-control/democratic-constitution.md)
specifies a parliamentary reference design, competitive elections, opposition rights,
appointment and funding safeguards, actual handover, emergency limits and accountable
AI assistance. Its institutional form and numeric parameters remain hypotheses to
compare with other democratic designs. Its [capture exercises](../2026-09-democratic-control/capture-exercises.md)
require evidence that protections work against a noncooperating incumbent.
The [continuity correction](../2026-09-democratic-continuity/README.md) qualifies that
candidate's appointment, opposition, funding and handover claims. Conditional
deadlines do not bound a process whose trigger can be withheld; named reserves and
formal opposition votes do not establish independent, available capacity.

AI-exclusive lawmaking is not the desired sovereign endpoint. Citizens and their
authorized representatives must have an effective legislative route independent of
AI approval and be able to revoke its delegated role. Current research-publication
permissions are unchanged; replacing the old constitutional allocation requires
the applicable fundamental-transition process. Research authorship is not sovereignty.

For consequential power changes, trace who can initiate a challenge, who decides it,
who funds and supplies the reviewer, who executes the remedy, and whether those
actors share control with the target. An incumbent's effective veto over opposition,
legal review or lawful replacement is a critical failure. No aggregate welfare or
democracy score can compensate for it. Review cumulative changes and the risk that
the safeguard itself becomes an instrument of repression.

Assess power during delay as well as power after a decision. Distinguish legitimate
disagreement, refusal to execute a valid order, disputed authority and lack of
capacity. Identify who benefits from inaction, who controls the clock's trigger,
which powers expire, what services and claims continue, and what happens if the
fallback also fails. A timeout cannot establish consent or a winner. A fallback
must not reward deliberate obstruction with powers the actor could not otherwise
obtain. Nor does every disagreement have to end in a new decision: evaluate limits
on power, conditional progress and continuity separately. A stated failure or a
missed deadline does not discharge existing duties or supply emergency authority.

## 1. Separate the research method, social models and decisions

| Layer | Function | What it must not silently decide |
|---|---|---|
| Common research method | Preserve reasons, sources, uncertainty, affected-party standing, contestability and coherent commitments | Which social model wins or who acquires governing power |
| Competing models of society | Explain causes and propose institutions, resource arrangements and ways of living | That their definitions and values are universal or empirically established |
| Contextual decisions | Choose a bounded investigation or qualified option under an identified procedure | That a local finding transfers everywhere or becomes binding law |

The common method itself has normative commitments, including standing to challenge
power, democratic public control and protection against concealing harms. They are explicit and open to
reasoned criticism, not claimed to be politically neutral consequences of science.
Revising applicable protected commitments requires their proper revision route.

The coordination-centered 3.0/3.1 architecture is one social-design hypothesis.
Material provision, relationships, ownership and bargaining power, ecological
conditions and institutional discovery offer other starting points. These are
examples, not an exhaustive taxonomy or a quota of perspectives to mention.
A future contributor may introduce a genuinely different account.
Competing economic, social and administrative arrangements must meet the democratic
design requirement to be recommended as compatible with this project's intended
country. Comparative study and peaceful criticism remain open; calling a proposal
an alternative does not qualify it to remove political equality or future choice.

## 2. Start with a situation and competing causal explanations

Record whose lives are at issue, the actual or hypothetical context, current
arrangements, experienced harms and missing perspectives. Distinguish what was
observed from what someone inferred or values. Preserve the contributor's meaning;
an AI-assisted interpretation has its own attribution and can be disputed.

Separate four kinds of statements:

- **Values:** what matters and which tradeoffs remain contested.
- **Constraints:** rights, authority and physical/accounting limits, with their bases.
- **Empirical claims:** what happens or causes what, with evidence and applicability.
- **Design hypotheses:** how a proposed arrangement might change behavior and outcomes.

Do not turn an uncertain empirical estimate into an unquestionable constraint.
Do not treat a moral conflict as something a larger model will necessarily solve.
A changed definition can change entitlement, measurement or power and therefore
requires substantive examination.

For consequential design, compare plausible diagnoses before choosing the instrument.
Would the problem persist with perfect communication but unchanged resources and
ownership? Would identical service statistics hide a loss of relationships or
agency? Could a different boundary expose costs shifted to outsiders or the future?
Use only the questions that can change the diagnosis. Do not make another ritual form.

State what observation would distinguish the leading explanations and what remains
unresolvable by observation alone. The original problem definition may change.
