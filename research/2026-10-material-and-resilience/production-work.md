# Production, investment and worker power in the proposed country

3 October 2026 · Supporting C05/C14 decision analysis · Research only

## A missing operating choice, not another sector promise

The [country design](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md), integrated design 8, selects a plural mixed economy, collective bargaining, an essential-capability strategy and gains from automation shared through wages, taxes, services and returns on public assets. The [status register](../DESIGN-STATUS.md) keeps production, imports, distribution and ecological accounts unverified. The [social-fiscal settlement](../SOCIAL-FISCAL-SETTLEMENT.md) models public spending, staff and limited energy; the [political-economy review](../2026-10-political-economy-review/analysis.md) and [external-currency operating analysis](../2026-10-operating-settlement/economic-control.md) show why neither appropriations nor bank credit alone supply inputs. The bounded question here is: **who makes and finances productive investment, with what worker voice and public obligations, when no owner may dominate politics or quietly bill losses to the social floor?**

The underlying values are material security, freedom to start or leave a firm, workers' bargaining power, usable opposition and care for people and ecosystems outside the transaction. These values can conflict: strict public direction may suppress entry and invite patronage; unrestricted private discretion may leave essential capacity and worker adjustment underprovided. Whether a particular bargaining form, ownership mix or investment incentive raises productivity is an empirical question, not established by these sources or AIcountry's invented ledger. The institutional package below is a **design hypothesis**. No electorate, firms, workforce, tax base or lawful jurisdiction has authorized it.

## Coherent rivals and provisional selection

| Model | How investment, work and essentials operate | Main attraction and failure route |
|---|---|---|
| **R0. Retain design 8's broad mixed-economy wording** | Firms, cooperatives and public bodies decide case by case; existing competition, bargaining, tax and social-floor principles constrain them. | Leaves flexibility and avoids a central allocation mechanism. But it does not say how a public investment promise is funded, who may direct a public enterprise, or how the same workers and foreign currency serve several missions. Retention is defensible as an outline, not an operating settlement. |
| **R1. Market-led capacity with a residual state** | Private firms and lenders bear investment risk; state enforces competition, safety, contracts and a cash floor, purchasing essential output when available. Training is mainly purchased by workers/firms. | Decentralized discovery and lower risk of cabinet-directed credit. But providers may rationally avoid unprofitable locations and long-payback capacity; cash support cannot hire a missing clinician or manufacture a grid part. Workers with low savings or care duties may be unable to bargain or retrain. |
| **R2. Public production and directed credit** | Assembly sets sector production targets; public banks and enterprises finance and provide essential outputs; worker participation is arranged inside public ownership. | Can coordinate sites, inputs and long projects if public institutions have competence and usable currency. But an external-currency state cannot create the needed settlement balances by directing credit, and a single political controller can allocate jobs, credit, contracts and information to supporters. Public ownership alone does not secure independent worker voice or entry. |
| **R3. Plural investment with bounded public missions and independent bargaining — selected conditionally** | Private firms and cooperatives choose investments and can fail under general law. Assembly separately funds defined public infrastructure, service capacity, training and essential supply continuity; accountable agencies procure or provide them. Workers organize and negotiate terms, automation transitions and paid learning, while providers must meet statutory safety and access duties. Public enterprises have explicit mandates, separately costed noncommercial duties and reviewable management. | Links distributed risk-taking to a funded rights floor without requiring one owner or planner. It requires capable procurement, union freedom, competition enforcement, public-enterprise governance and real fiscal/foreign-currency capacity. Their common capture or scarcity could make the package fail. |

Choose R3 for the *reference design*, subject to actual participation and capacity. It is an integrated choice rather than a claim that the benefits of R1 and R2 can simply be added. Public missions consume capital and skilled labor that private or cooperative investment might otherwise use; bargaining can raise fair compensation while a poorly designed transition may exclude entrants. A public network's equal-access duty may require transparent compensation that a commercial rival does not receive. These conflicts must be shown in the joint account, not averaged into a growth score. Cooperative predominance, a larger public sector or market-led provision remain legitimate public alternatives. No numeric ownership share or automation dividend is justified here.

## Operating decision chain

| Decision | Competent actor and proposed rule | Constraint, contest and continuity |
|---|---|---|
| **National priorities and public capital** | The elected Assembly sets rights, labor and competition law, binding ecological limits, tax and debt authority, and a *separately appropriated* public-capability portfolio for networks, education/training, essential supplier diversity and maintenance. Cabinet proposes costs and alternatives; responsible ministries deliver named projects. | Publish capital, recurring operations, land/water/energy, imported inputs, expected payment dates, liabilities and who loses access to shared capacity. An agency cannot turn an optimistic return forecast or private guarantee into a new appropriation. Assembly may stop new projects while accrued worker, creditor and service claims continue under law. |
| **Commercial investment and entry** | Private firms and cooperatives invest retained earnings, equity or lawfully supervised loans at their own risk; they choose products and methods within labor, consumer, ecological and competition rules. Start-up, exit and bankruptcy rules allow a new entrant to challenge an incumbent. | A license, public contract or platform cannot reserve the market for the governing party's firm. Ownership and control across employers, land, finance, communications and public contracts are reviewed cumulatively, with reasons, appeal, property due process and a workable transition. No firm receives an implied state guarantee because its failure would be inconvenient. |
| **Public services and enterprises** | Where the essential floor needs provision the market does not supply, the responsible public authority commissions an accessible service or uses publicly owned capacity. The Assembly states each enterprise's public-service mandate and separately funds its noncommercial duties; qualified management makes operational choices. | Separate service costs from commercial accounts; disclose subsidies, public guarantees, contracts, appointments and performance without exposing private case data. Independent audit, courts and democratic oversight examine capture; workers and service users retain distinct challenge routes. A public monopoly does not get to set eligibility or silence competitors. The [OECD's 2024 state-enterprise guidelines](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-on-corporate-governance-of-state-owned-enterprises-2024_18a24f43-en/full-report.html) are a comparison for clear ownership rationale, professional boards and disclosure, not proof this design works. |
| **Finance and foreign inputs** | Regulated lenders price and bear commercial credit risk under the selected [external-currency settlement](../2026-10-operating-settlement/economic-control.md). Any public concessional loan, equity stake, export credit or procurement guarantee needs its own Assembly mandate, cap, loss account and independent review; ordinary bank-resolution funds cannot finance industrial policy. | Identify where the currency, import contract, transport, materials and skills actually come from. Debt and guarantees cannot be booked as tax revenue or useful output. A domestic-currency bank rescue is not silently available. A foreign supplier's refusal is a service and external-relations failure to disclose, not proof that the state's forecast was binding abroad. |
| **Worker voice and transition** | Independent worker organizations bargain over wages, hours and conditions; workers receive timely information and consultation on consequential automation or closure, and a real route to negotiate paid learning, redeployment and safety. Firms retain lawful investment decisions; statutory safety and anti-retaliation duties are enforceable. Public education and transition support require explicit budgets, including replacement care and time away from work. | Collective voice cannot be chosen by management, a public owner or an AI for the workers. Protection needs interim relief against retaliation, usable representation for small, temporary and care-constrained workers, and fair process for employers. No blanket worker veto over every investment or guarantee of permanent employment is assumed. [ILO Convention No. 154](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A12100%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP12100_INSTRUMENT_ID%2CP12100_LANG_CODE%3A312299%2Cen) supplies a normative comparison for voluntary collective bargaining; it does not settle AIcountry's coverage or effectiveness. |
| **Tax, returns and democratic independence** | Firms pay enacted taxes; workers receive negotiated compensation and portable protection; returns on *actual* public equity or assets enter public accounts after costs. Assembly decides any additional distribution through services or income support. Election, competition, labor, procurement and audit authorities inspect linked corporate and public control under separate, reviewable mandates. | No presumed robot tax, productivity windfall, instant retraining effect or future profit may finance a current obligation. Commercial public firms and private contractors face published access and procurement rules, with explicit exceptions where a funded public-service duty requires them. The [OECD's competitive-neutrality recommendation](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0462) is a comparison for avoiding ownership-based advantage, not a rule that cancels universal-service obligations. |

The public investment portfolio and private production share one **resource and incidence ledger** with the C06 budget: existing assets, cash, debt, bank exposures, projects by payment date, maintenance, training lead times, workers, care time, sites, energy, water, ecological repair, imports, and impacts on taxes, prices and access. The same nurse or construction crew cannot appear as spare capacity in a new public service and a private project. Costs shifted to suppliers abroad or future residents remain visible. The [IMF Fiscal Transparency Code](https://www.imf.org/external/np/fad/trans/Code2019.pdf) is a methodological comparison for disclosed public guarantees and contingent fiscal risks; it cannot validate an invented return or show that the selected package fits an actual budget.

## Failure, exit and democratic correction

If a dominant employer automates rapidly, the firm must comply with safety, notice, anti-retaliation and negotiated commitments. Workers can bargain and seek timely independent relief; job loss triggers portable income and service rights, not a forced return to an unsafe employer. If it then buys the only local supplier or payment gateway, competition and civic-power reviewers assess the *combined* dependency, with disclosure, lawful remedies and due process. Its private investment authority does not include power to punish opposition; neither a minister nor a public buyer may favor a party's employer. If the firm fails, ordinary bankruptcy distributes lawful claims, the state preserves accrued wages and benefits through the applicable legal route, and the service authority activates a funded alternative where an essential guarantee is involved. The government does not acquire general ownership or cancel creditors merely by declaring the firm important.

If a public enterprise or mission contract fails, the responsible authority preserves claimants' existing service route, records incomplete delivery, and seeks a qualified successor within previously authorized funds. Workers, users, suppliers and competitors may challenge discriminatory transfer or political appointments. No procurement exit is real without another operator, staff, records, material inputs and foreign settlement. If those are absent, Cabinet must report the failure and alternatives to the Assembly; a minister cannot expand a guarantee or commandeer a firm by administrative order. Under a wider downturn, automatic stabilizers operate only as enacted and funded; a new rescue, tax or credit guarantee requires democratic authorization. Courts enforce legal claims and expose breach but cannot make growth, profits or foreign currency appear.

This is not a promise of full employment, a specific growth rate or post-scarcity. It also does not assume that ownership, unionization or competition alone prevents capture. Trace appointments, funding, data access, enforcement and political donations across public and private firms; protect practical entry, exit and opposition. The OECD state-enterprise guidance and ILO bargaining standard are normative reference points, not an independent endorsement or an affected population's consent.

## Two bounded changes for the main design

1. **C05:** Add the explicit investment chain: firms and cooperatives choose and bear ordinary commercial risk; Assembly appropriates a limited public-capability portfolio; ministries and public enterprises execute disclosed mandates; workers have enforceable bargaining and automation-transition rights; competition and audit bodies police both public and private capture. Public credit and guarantees require a separate cap and loss account under C06.
2. **C14/status:** Add a pre-commencement and review condition for this production settlement: inventory incumbent ownership, worker obligations, skills, essential suppliers, public-enterprise and contractor liabilities, foreign-currency and ecological constraints; fund transition and provider overlap; test at least one essential-capability failure and worker exit without assuming a bailout. Preserve existing property, labor, pension, contract and service claims during lawful migration.

**Status and next bounded question:** Evidence here is qualified reasoning from the cited primary institutional standards and the linked AIcountry documents inspected on 3 October 2026; no original market data, worker or employer participation, independent review, legal adoption, operational readiness or observed outcome exists. The conditional R3 selection is a research choice only. A real jurisdiction's next decision is whether the proposed public-capability portfolio plus private/cooperative investment and worker bargaining can cover an identified essential input *without double-counting labor, finance, imports or ecological capacity*, while leaving a usable rival provider and remedy. The deliverable would be a joint account, affected-party alternatives and an enforceable transition map; no such account can be populated honestly for the fictitious country now. Reopen the selection if a working market-led, cooperative or public-directed alternative better protects people and democracy under the same constraints, or if actual workers, firms, residents or outsiders show material harm.
