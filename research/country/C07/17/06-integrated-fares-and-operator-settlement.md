# MO23–MO29. One usable public fare across participating operators

12 November 2026 · Integrated design 432 · Candidate ordinary law

Select a common fare on participating public-network routes, with a
published transfer allowance and spending cap for covered paid travel.
Keep multiple delivery operators. MO09's protected journeys remain
free at use and funded on their own authority. A fare integration plan
must work for riders who pay in cash and operators who need timely cash
to maintain the network.

**MO23 — Define the fare and the authority to change it.** Competent
elected transport bodies adopt the common schedule, covered routes,
transfer time, service week, price cap, concessions and lawful sources.
Use an interlocal agreement where journeys cross elected boundaries
and apply [CLX](../../C04/01/02-cross-local-effects-and-residual-decisions.md)
to unresolved effects. Give affected riders, workers,
localities and operators an opportunity to challenge the price and
coverage before effect. Publish exclusions, prospective changes and
accessible worked examples. Calculate a single authorized fare for a
covered transfer journey and apply the lowest lawful cumulative amount
under the applicable cap. Preserve stronger source-law terms and never
use a free MO journey to consume a paid cap or make free access depend
on a purchase. A common fare alone does not confirm a connection under
[MO17](05-joined-journeys-and-service-access.md).

**MO24 — Make the same fare obtainable with cash and limited data.**
Provide an accessible staffed and cash-funded route with equivalent
prices, transfer credit and caps, including a low-cost physical bearer
medium or an independently usable receipt route. Do not require a
smartphone, bank account, permanent registration or purchase of a full
week in advance. Validate only what is needed for the transfer, cap,
settlement or remedy. Where several instruments are used, allow a
claimant to combine eligible receipts voluntarily under a published
once-only rule, without enrolling in continuous tracking. Protect
location, restricted concession proofs and original dispute evidence.
No shared passenger identity dossier or sale of travel histories.
Any authorized AI assistance may identify discrepancies in limited
records. The authority must verify the fare and provide human
correction.

**MO25 — Keep a usable boarding and correction route when systems
fail.** Before launch fund an accessible offline or manual boarding
arrangement for covered service. Use proportionate, published evidence
of travel or eligibility and issue a dated receipt. Where the system
fails, do not impose a duplicate fare, maximum fare, penalty or unsafe
refusal for the missing system validation. Any later lawful charge is
limited to the correct fare under the original period and counts once.
For disputed price or proof, the first competent desk keeps the case
until a named receiver accepts it. Provide timely usable boarding
relief where due and direct independent review. Credit or return money
through a method the rider can actually receive, including a cash
alternative where appropriate. Preserve the transport and destination
remedies when a paid journey is also missed.

**MO26 — Separate the rider fare from the operator's payment.** The
lawful authority or designated interlocal body contracts for defined
service and availability, with dated authorized payments and independent
verification. Attribute fare receipts to the public network fare account
on the agreed legal terms. Pay operators for verified work or lawful
availability, with disclosed terms and any authorized incentives.
Do not base payment solely on taps or let an operator control a
competitor's source records or settlement. Distinguish paid travel,
free protected journeys, authorized standby and corrections. Allocate
shared costs by published contract and interlocal rule. Count each
journey credit and operator claim once. Allow independent review of
exclusion and contested claims. Operator disputes do not shift a
second fare or an advance payment onto riders.

**MO27 — Require gross cash when the next service payment falls due.**
Keep cleared usable fare receipts, unspent rider balances, unsettled
payments, refunds, contracted liabilities and voted support separately
visible under [TPE](../../C06/60/11-public-cash-and-independent-execution.md).
Safeguard unspent rider balances under their own legal terms.
Do not spend an unsettled collection, a stored balance belonging to a
rider or the same subsidy twice. For both initial and adverse windows,
price the fare reduction, ordinary operations, eligible refunds,
maintenance, accessibility, standby and an alternate settlement route.
Use a dated bounded appropriation or other previously authorized
bridge when net receipts fall short. A delayed collection only restores
the bridge after actual settlement and lawful reconciliation. Keep
current obligations and applicable continuity powers, hold unsupported
optional expansion and bring a recurring gap to elected budget choice.

**MO28 — Preserve a replaceable payment and transport network.** Publish
the fare rules and the minimum common contract and record formats.
Fund a separately controlled review and settlement fallback with
lawful custody, qualified staff and safe access to original records.
A single supplier must not be able to stop both boarding and
independent review. When operators or technology change, accept
valid fare and cap credits on their existing terms, preserve claims
and refunds, reconcile unsettled transactions, and obtain a paid
accepting successor before withdrawing the old path. Apply MO17–MO22
for actual journey continuity. Competition and worker protections
retain their own authorities.

**MO29 — Test affordability and the service it buys.** Compare
independent fares with protected concessions, common transfer pricing
with a cap, and funded wider fare-free service on the same coverage,
cost and calendar. Examine paid household travel, unsuccessful
attempts, cash access, total household time, displacements, operator
cash and protected journey receipt. Observe whether the cap serves
riders who could not afford frequent travel, as well as frequent
riders who already travel. Publish protected results by place and
relevant access needs. Consider cheaper local service when fare reform
leaves travel or service unavailable. Reopen on exclusion, repeated
underfunding, unpaid operators, privacy failure or a better complete
route. No cap amount or network-wide appropriation follows from the
worked comparison.
