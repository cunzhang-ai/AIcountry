# WB05–WB08. Test disclosure and retaliation under separate rules

*Part of [protected disclosure](../31-protected-disclosure-and-evidence.md).*

**WB05 — Protect proportionate disclosure when other routes will fail.**
The common civil protection applies to a proportionate public or
journalistic disclosure if the person reasonably believes timely safe
external action will fail because of imminent serious harm, material
retaliation risk, likely concealment, implicated controllers or an
unaddressed material report after a useful opportunity for action.
There is no mandatory delay to use that route if the conditions
already hold. Disclose only what is reasonably necessary for the
public interest at stake. Weigh actual harm to unrelated people,
privacy, safety and legitimate confidence, and feasible safer ways
to disclose. This protection is distinct from ordinary freedom of
speech and independent editorial decisions. It requires no licensing
or prior government permission to speak.

A confidentiality, settlement or platform term cannot prohibit a
covered disclosure or protected advice. In a civil claim based on
the disclosure, allow the defense at the earliest fair stage and
give proportionate disclosure and cost protection. Separately unlawful
intrusion, acquisition, coercion or unnecessary exposure of protected
information remains subject to its own law and defenses. Privileged,
medical, safety-sensitive and classified material has existing
protected pathways, with independent counsel, judicial scrutiny and
review of claimed secrecy where law permits. This common floor
does not grant blanket public immunity or authority to disclose
someone else's protected information. Apply the existing narrow
court standard to any prior restraint.

**WB06 — Identify retaliation by its actual effects and controller.**
A materially adverse act or omission, threat or attempted
disadvantage can include pay or shift loss, dismissal, refusal
to renew, credential removal, exclusion from a service or bid,
blacklisting, threats to a relative or abuse of litigation.
Consider cumulative acts and indirect direction through another
employer, contractor or AI operator. An entity answers within its
lawful responsibility for its own conduct or a measure it actually
directs or controls. A report does not immunize unrelated conduct
or require continuation of a role, contract or access unsafe on
specific lawful grounds. Preserve stronger labour, equality and
political protections and their existing claim rules.

**WB07 — Preserve a real life and a fair hearing during review.**
A reporter or person facing related retaliation may seek direct
B17 relief before losing pay, safe access, records, a home or
another remedy in practice. Apply existing hearing, evidence
and interim expiry rules. A competent judge may preserve specific
records, stop a threatened act, restore eligible due pay or order
other proportionate provisional protection within lawful means.
A protected person need not return to unsafe work or disclose
identity publicly. Where actual danger requires separation or
restricted access, use a safe lawful alternative and preserve
its independent pay and welfare consequences. Notice and useful
reply must reach the respondent under B17. A preservation or
provisional order decides neither underlying misconduct nor final
retaliation. No court order supplies money or staff without a
legal and available executor.

**WB08 — Make the same-decision defense testable.** In this civil
action the claimant establishes the protected act or perceived
association, the material adverse measure, and facts showing on
the balance of probabilities that the protected factor contributed
to the measure. Knowledge, chronology, inconsistencies, comparison
and records may establish the connection. A date or model score
alone does not automatically settle it. The respondent can avoid
retaliation liability for that measure by clear and convincing
evidence that the same lawful action would have occurred at the
same time and scope without the protected factor.

Where the respondent used an automated or outsourced process,
examine the actual data, controller, applicable version, settings,
human instructions and comparators. Give protected access as
necessary. Distinguish proven inability to supply records,
ordinary omission and deliberate suppression under existing
law. The civil standard neither alters criminal proof nor shifts
the respondent's burden on the truth of the underlying report.
