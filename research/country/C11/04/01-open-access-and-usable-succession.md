# CPV01–CPV05. Keep a usable network of shared places open to everyone

*Design317 · Proposed delivery of the [public shared-place floor](../04-shared-places-and-association.md).*

**CPV01 — Fund a free ordinary place to be.** Each elected local authority
provides a reasonably reachable, maintained network with free ordinary
drop-in access and some free bookable time for eligible noncommercial
community use. Specify usable hours, supported access, occupancy, safe
routes and actual open sites in a public service schedule. Ordinary
drop-in access does not require a purchase, association membership,
digital account or a reason for visiting. Apply proportionate
safety and disability accommodation under their own law. Specialized
activities and exclusive uses may have separately authorized charges.
A free place may be quiet, informal or shared. No compulsory activity,
belief, relationship or guaranteed booking follows. National
equalization and local budgets fund the floor under MF.

**CPV02 — Match people, opening hours and resources.** The local
service owner checks actual use, unmet demand, travel cost, access
barriers, time people can use, and experiences of people who avoid
official venues. Give workers, small groups, children and other
affected residents a usable route to contribute. Compare public,
community and contracted provision without assuming volunteer shifts
or household travel are free. Identify qualified staff, leases,
cleaning, accessibility, maintenance and qualified alternatives.
Count overlapping uses and simultaneous staffing once. A library
collection, route, workshop or legal booking may need different
resources. One room may serve them at compatible times.

**CPV03 — Allocate scarce bookings under rules people can challenge.**
Publish capacity and priority rules before requests. Preserve open
unbooked use and a reachable assisted booking option. Use actual
group and person needs and lawful safety limits without favoring the
incumbent, sponsor or dominant organization. Protect meaningful access
for lawful dissent and minority groups on the same terms, under AX
and applicable electoral law. A use restriction must serve its actual
legal purpose. Keep fair access for activities that need privacy
or accommodation where lawful. Give a timely reason, correction
and useful alternative where available. Paid independent review and
competent relief must be reachable before the event loses its purpose.
A reported threat or duplicate booking requires actual assessment.
AI may compare schedules and explain options without ranking beliefs
or deciding whose event can proceed.

**CPV04 — Verify the replacement before a planned material cut.**
Before planned closure, sale or reduction that affects the floor,
the elected body considers retention, repair, co-location and a funded
alternative on the same user scope. Publish the exact hours, reachable
sites, accepted staff and capacity, price, transition and known gaps
in time for public scrutiny. Keep the floor available through the
change and require a witnessed usable successor before withdrawing
the affected service. Record operator exit and keep existing lawful
leases, wages, bookings and remedies on their governing terms.
Where immediate safety requires closure, use its competent authority
and seek the earliest paid lawful alternative. Record any missed
access as a failure. A court can protect a right or enforce a specific
duty without selecting a permanent facility or creating an appropriation.

**CPV05 — Verify continuing access without tracking social life.**
Name the local authority responsible through any operator change.
Report safely aggregated actual access, closures, denied requests,
displaced uses and received remedies. Keep private use and testimony
under purpose-limited custody. Do not infer friendship, cohesion
or agreement from admissions or bookings. Reopen on recurring
exclusion, loss of free access, unserved users, operator failure
or a better lawful arrangement with less public burden.
