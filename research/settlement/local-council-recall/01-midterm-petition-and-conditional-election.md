# One midterm petition, two questions and a fixed local term

Design335 · Constructed comparison for [LRC01–LRC05](../../country/C03/13/08-local-council-recall.md)

A nine-seat EL council has a corrected opening electorate of 10,000.
The single 90-day window opens 18 months after the term begins.
Of 1,570 submitted entries, 70 are duplicates, ineligible or revoked.
The 1,500 valid supporters meet the 15 percent threshold. With 1,499
there would be no automatic poll. The petition itself removes nobody.
On lawful certification, final voting is 90 days later, with first
voting after 76 days. The ordinary election and original term end
are fixed independently.

| Recall choices | Result |
|---|---|
| 2,600 yes and 2,400 no | The yes side has a majority and meets the 2,500 affirmative floor. |
| 2,450 yes and 550 no | The yes side has a majority but falls short of the affirmative floor. |
| 2,500 yes and 2,500 no | The tie fails the majority test. |

On the first result, assume the independently valid replacement
ballot elects five members from one list and four from another under
the ordinary EL allocation. No list receives a winner-take-all mandate.
The new council serves the original remainder. On either failed result,
the replacement ballot elects nobody. If the recall is valid and the
replacement count is materially lost, the court checks severability
and orders only the required repeat within prior law. Bounded
caretaker and career duties carry due services, payments and records
without continuing the recalled legislature. If the recall tally
itself is lost, a possible replacement tally cannot prove a recall.

Compare ordinary elections and council confidence alone, individual
recall, and the selected whole-council option. The status quo avoids
interruption and leaves fewer tools when a council majority protects
itself. Individual recall may target a real grievance, but a
single-seat procedure can defeat the original proportional choice.
The selected option reopens all seats and therefore risks displacement
of effective opposition as well as a failed majority. One window,
an affirmative floor, paid equal entry and a full proportional
replacement are candidate safeguards with costs and tradeoffs.
