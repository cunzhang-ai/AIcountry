# Primary price evidence · differences in household exposure

1. UK Office for National Statistics, *Household Costs Indices for UK
   household groups, January 2022 to September 2023*, section 3,
   Figure 3. Annual Household Costs Index inflation in October 2022
   was 13.5% for income decile 2 and 11.5% for decile 9. Household
   energy and mortgage interest contributed differently to the
   two profiles. [Official bulletin](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/householdcostsindicesforukhouseholdgroups/january2022toseptember2023).
2. UK ONS, *Household Costs Indices for UK household groups, October
   to December 2024*, main points and income section, original
   release. Annual costs rose 2.6% for decile 2 and 3.2% for decile 9
   in December 2024. The direction reverses across these examples.
   The 2024 values here refer to this edition, without merging later
   revised estimates. [Official bulletin](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/householdcostsindicesforukhouseholdgroups/octobertodecember2024).
3. UK ONS, *Tracking the price of the lowest-cost grocery items, UK,
   experimental analysis: April 2021 to September 2022*. Online
   quotations for 30 selected everyday groceries yielded about 17%
   annual growth to September 2022. Estimates are sensitive to
   substitution and availability. They do not establish in-store
   stock, a complete diet, distributional incidence or a replacement
   for an official price series. [Official study](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/articles/trackingthelowestcostgroceryitemsukexperimentalanalysis/april2021toseptember2022).

The first two releases are from one statistical family, without
independent replication. These measures describe historical UK price
experience, not local AIcountry prices, necessary basket levels, the
size of a bridge or evidence of the proposed protection's effects.
