# A retain-and-repair comparator that already adapts care

## What the observed institutions do

Denmark's original 2024 Elderly Act provides coherent care arrangements that
can adapt to changing needs through dialogue and professional judgment.
Sections 9–11 describe the arrangement and continuity. Sections 18–22
address provider choice, specified payment rules, selected helpers and private
provider failure. Sections 28–31 provide municipal responsibility, written
awards and review of specified decisions, with a transition limitation on
review. Sections 34–35 provide elected municipal elder councils with advisory
roles and municipal resources. The original Act took effect on 1 July 2025. Sections 45–46
provide a transition for specified earlier decisions through 1 July 2027,
with exceptions including a change in need that makes an earlier award
inadequate. Its age-related scope and provider architecture differ from HS.
This describes the original enactment as a historical comparator.
[Official Elderly Act 1651](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2024/1651/pdf)

Copenhagen's April 2025 care committee record describes a planned citywide
change for municipal home care and assessment from 1 May 2025, with around
80 teams and continuing implementation work through 2027. It reports a
DKK 27.5 million transition envelope for 2024–2027, including DKK 20,461,936
earmarked nationally for fixed teams from June 2024 through December 2027.
The committee took the status report into account. The item records no new
financial consequence. The envelope already includes the earmark.
[Copenhagen's April 2025 committee record](https://www.kk.dk/dagsordener-og-referater/Sundheds-%20og%20Omsorgsudvalget/m%C3%B8de-24042025/referat/punkt-2)

The city's separate Budget 2025 correction schedules a return of previously
allocated budget room based on anticipated partial replacement from national
funding. Its 2025 price-and-wage basis records DKK 0.367 million in 2025,
2.112 million in 2026 and 0.729 million in 2027, a total of 3.208 million
over the three years. The correction is a funding substitution. The
published scopes do not justify subtracting it again from the 27.5 million,
adding it to the national earmark or extrapolating it beyond 2027. The local
figures describe budgets and planned coverage. Actual paid staff, cash dates,
the recurring cost after 2027 and a full reconciled programme outturn remain
unobserved here.
[Copenhagen Budget 2025 correction, section 2.26 and table 28](https://www.kk.dk/sites/default/files/agenda/724d8328-822a-4f47-83e5-56a5b94b28f3/e1ff200a-c609-4994-b8d4-0e522e171f44-bilag-2_0.pdf)

## What recipient evidence can support

VIVE's 2024 recipient evaluation used a 2023 telephone survey of 150
people recruited by 25 participating municipalities. It found that many
respondents valued continuity with workers who knew their needs. Respondents
could not tell whether the team structure changed the quality of help. The
analysis did not establish that different municipal approaches explained
satisfaction. Selection required that the recipient or a relative could take
part by telephone, among other criteria. This is earlier pilot evidence, not
an evaluation of Copenhagen's later citywide rollout. It supports tracking
the relationships people value, while leaving selection, continuity under
pressure and causal effects open.
[VIVE recipient results](https://www.vive.dk/media/pure/yxdre7lz/25027984)
