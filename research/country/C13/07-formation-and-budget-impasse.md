# C13. A government-formation and budget impasse

*Part of [C13 lived experience](../C13-lived-experience.md); integrated design 55 authored stress, not an observed event.*

Suppose an election produces four Assembly groups with 32%, 27%, 23% and
18% of seats. The old cabinet has already entered its limited caretaker
role during the election period.
No group can claim government merely because it is largest. Elected members
can negotiate, nominate and vote on a majority prime minister; their votes,
programmes and objections are public. If no candidate receives a majority
within 21 days, the highest-supported nominee can be tested for tolerated
minority government under [C03's fallback](../C03/06-government-formation-and-caretaker.md).
If the groups support only their own nominees, two reach the quarter
threshold. If one is rejected and the other test is obstructed, the
alternate sitting and urgent review remain available. If there is no
lawful investiture by day 30, the first repeat follows the prospective
C03 rule. An unheld vote is recorded as obstruction.

At the same time, a care worker's wage, a resident's disability payment,
an election appeal and a water-pump contract become due while the annual
budget has not passed. The existing essential-obligations schedule covers
only its specified duties with real cash; the separate temporary bridge
covers only lawful existing operations for its term. Treasury must expose
any arrears. The caretaker may organize delivery under old law but cannot
invent a new service cut, levy, borrowing line or procurement franchise.
The Assembly can hear opposition budget alternatives and pass a lawful
appropriation despite the absence of a majority cabinet. A court protects
claims and procedure within available law; it cannot create the missing
carer, tax or political agreement.

Now a flood damages the pump while the second election is being prepared.
The competent local and national safety officers use only preauthorized
response powers; the caretaker cannot defer the vote indefinitely or turn
emergency command into a new term. The election body tests reachable polling
and a lawful alternate site; courts review any postponement under the rule
fixed before the election. Displaced residents, care users and local
opposition need actual communication, transport, evidence access and
remedies. If the repeat election also yields no government, the failure is
reported as a constitutional crisis with the limited caretaker and public
change route still visible. A written clock does not prove that services,
ballots or peaceful transfer can be delivered.

[DB ordinary budget settlement](../C06/60-democratic-budget-and-execution.md)
adds member alternatives and execution/year-end permission boundaries; actual
standing/90-day bridge, day30/day60/day91, confidence/handover and accrued claims
remain. A balanced total or late vote proves neither authority nor actual source.

In the [day-91 worked case](../../settlement/budget-lapse/01-due-money-and-independent-receipt.md),
the election appeal loses its ordinary payer to selective withholding.
A court-authorised substitute can use its own accessible rail and the
existing restricted cash. If both routes share a failed rail, the funded
judgment still misses its due date. Water faces a different failure
because the lawful balance is short. The supplier that completed bridge
work retains the debt and can receive a timely payment only from valid
settlement authority. Record each missed deadline even after a new budget.

[BCV competing budgets](../C06/60/05-competing-budgets-and-floor-choice.md)
makes a timely supported member budget eligible for a real vote. In
the worked comparison an elected majority can approve a replacement
even when the Cabinet budget lacks support. Each exact alternative
needs its own votes, and a budget outcome does not change who holds office.

In the [eight-sponsor case](../../settlement/budget-floor-recovery/01-eight-sponsors-and-a-full-assembly-majority.md),
a small group can miss the ordinary threshold but still obtain a separate
majority for putting its text to a real vote. A separately counted majority must
affirm it through the actual appropriation process. Before that vote
no statutory payment or remedy changes.
