| Arrangement | What it can solve | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary equalization, direct correction and voluntary debt agreement | Simpler, respects existing obligations and market terms | A holdout can block a feasible workout. A separately underfunded service still needs its source. |
| Finite local bridge, separately funded common duties and narrow adjustment of eligible future financing, selected | Distinguishes liquidity, minimum service and debt. Preserves elected power. | Additional legal and administrative cost. Narrow eligibility leaves some distress unresolved. |
| Broader national guarantee or ministerial control | Can reassure lenders or intervene in a broken payment operation | Transfers losses or control, including to unrelated residents and elections. |
| Wider municipal bankruptcy or permission to use capital for operating costs | Can release financing pressure on different terms | Can shift costs to future budgets or reach protected claims and public choices. |

Choose a voluntary ordinary agreement first where it works. Keep the
local elected choice, protected national service funding and independent
court limits. Compare a recurring repair with actual revenue and access
beside any debt plan. Treat municipal borrowing and national financial
support as separate authorities and exposures. A temporary nominal balance
cannot prove usable care, teaching or a remedy.
