GC37 discusses noncitizen/online/spontaneous assemblies, tolerable disruption,
individual versus collective violence, facilitation, counterassemblies, privacy,
notice versus permission, timely review and exceptional dispersal. It permits
justified notification systems rather than prescribing this project's voluntary
notice choice. Its guidance on particular force measures does not itself enact
an underlying national police task or replace LF's stronger actual candidate
limits. The source also recognizes public/other users' rights and the context
of serious sustained disruption; inconvenience is not automatically violence.

GC34 distinguishes opinion/expression and discusses precise law, necessity,
public-figure criticism, privacy/reputation, media/internet limits and actual
article20 duties. It supplies no state truth licence or generic power based
on offense/official embarrassment. Its interpretation is not a complete ordinary
civil/penal code or a claim that this proposal fully implements every treaty
duty. Actual applicable law/treaty status and mandatory protections must be
qualified by legitimate competent review; this hypothetical project cannot
ratify treaties or invent a foreign state's consent.

where material. This is not a complete jurisprudence/foreign-law survey. No

## Project choices beyond these sources

No-permit/fully voluntary notice, prior ordinary court for material planned
assembly-specific restrictions, existing local career/PO roles, explicit limited
physical task, six-hour unilateral maximum, 24-hour AX ex-parte maximum and
seven-day order limit are this project's uncalibrated choices. They are not UN
mandates, observed optimal timings or evidence of enough judges/police/cash.
AX's narrower provisional political/public-interest restraint test and distinction
from final post-merits remedies need qualified local rights/treaty reconciliation.
Actual broader specific law keeps its own source until valid coordinated change.
