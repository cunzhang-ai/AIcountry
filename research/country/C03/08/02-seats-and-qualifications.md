# Constitutional review: seats and open qualifications

*Canonical CJ03–CJ04; part of [C03/08](../08-constitutional-review-and-public-control.md).*

**CJ03 — Nine seats, finite terms.** Nine full-time judges hold twelve-year,
nonrenewable seat terms. Three cohorts expire four years apart; each contains
one seat from each of the three CJ05–CJ07 sources. At founding, each source's
three seats receive four-, eight- and twelve-year initial terms, allocated by a
published draw before nominations. Initial short-term holders are also ineligible
for another primary constitutional seat. A permanent replacement serves only the
seat's remaining term, never a fresh twelve years. No incumbent holds over and
Cabinet cannot appoint an acting constitutional judge.

Every seat has one separately selected, named reserve under CJ11, with the same
source and seat expiry. Reserve service consumes that seat's term rather than creating a further term.
After exercising constitutional judicial authority, a person cannot receive
another primary or reserve seat term; succession within their designated seat's
remaining term is permitted. No former primary returns as another seat's reserve.
An unserved reserve has no priority or entitlement to a future appointment.
A person can hold only one primary or reserve seat role at a time, even if they
applied through several sources; accepting one withdraws incompatible candidacies.

There can never be two votes from one seat. Actual availability, remuneration,
ordinary work released and qualification are separate from a name on a list.

The nine judges elect a presiding administrator by five votes for a two-year,
nonconsecutive term; failure leaves the next judge in published seat rotation
performing necessary administration only. The presider has no casting vote,
private assignment power or authority to change the bench or jurisdiction.
Administrative continuity cannot create final merits authority.

**CJ04 — No universal shortlist owner.** Any consenting applicant may apply
directly to any or all sources. Twelve years of relevant legal work, demonstrated
constitutional/evidential/fair-process competence, lawful language access through
appropriate interpretation, material conflict disclosure and practical availability
are common statutory criteria. Relevant work may include judging, legal practice,
public defense, teaching, legislative or customary dispute work of demonstrated
relevance. Wealth, proprietary credentials, AI rankings, political agreement,
ordinary party membership and unpopular lawful interpretation are not criteria.

A current minister, legislator, senior party executive, campaign manager or paid
political lobbyist cannot serve; four years must elapse after those leadership
or lobbying roles. Ordinary civic advocacy is not such a role. Judges cannot
retain incompatible paid representation, operational party office or interests
in a litigant; recusal, divestment and disclosed lawful accommodation apply.
Each source publishes its own reasoned application of the common criteria.

The application register timestamps, preserves and supplies material; it cannot
own an exclusive eligibility pool or suppress a candidate from every source.
Unlawful exclusion is reviewable through CJ08/CJ12. Review can require lawful
reconsideration or correct a qualification finding, not install a judge chosen
by the reviewing court. Multiple sources can still share schools, patrons,
funders and prejudices; disclose these dependencies without claiming that
nominal plurality or an AI independence score establishes independence.
