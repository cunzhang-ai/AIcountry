The question is how people can **revise an inadequate charter** while a
current majority cannot use the amendment machinery to extend itself,
disable opposition, or turn a cabinet-run plebiscite into constituent
consent. A legal label cannot prove either democracy or amendment legitimacy.
The proposal concerns a future legitimately established country; it does
not adopt itself, change current software/publication permissions or choose
the founding electorate.

Equal political standing, peaceful replacement, practical opposition,
rights remedies and the ability to contest the system are explicit values
of this project. Whether a signature threshold is practically usable,
whether media and employer power corrupt a campaign, and whether a
double-approval process is too slow are empirical/contextual questions.
The chosen numbers are hypotheses, not discovered universal optima.

## Alternatives that cannot be silently combined

| Package | Democratic gain | Failure it risks |
|---|---|---|
| Retain D10's Assembly-only two-thirds plus referendum | Requires broad parliamentary agreement and a direct public final choice; limits transient amendment. | Incumbents or a blocking minority can prevent citizens from even testing a proposal to repair a captured Assembly or franchise. |
| Executive-called referendum on a government text | Allows fast direct approval and a clear yes/no choice. | Cabinet controls timing, wording, information and public resources; a favorable vote under manipulated conditions can legitimate tenure extension or weakened oversight. |
| Constituent convention for every amendment | Dedicated deliberation may expose systemic alternatives and keep day-to-day lawmakers from writing their own rules. | Repeated conventions are expensive, slow and can themselves be captured by delegates, funders or a restrictive selection rule; small repairs become impractical. |
| **Selected: Assembly and citizen initiation, a protected public vote, and prospective effect** | Citizens can place exact text before voters without an Assembly veto; Parliament retains a deliberative role; constitutional changes receive public ratification. | A wealthy sponsor or cabinet proxy can buy signatures and attention; a higher direct-route approval threshold can entrench defects; courts and verification officials can become new gatekeepers; the process needs real funding. |

International IDEA's [amendment primer](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/constitutional-amendment-procedures?lang=en)
describes the tension between adaptation and protection from short-sighted
partisan amendments. Its [direct-democracy handbook](https://www.idea.int/publications/catalogue/direct-democracy-international-idea-handbook)
examines constitutional initiatives and their campaign/access rules. The
Venice Commission's [revised referendum code](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD%282022%29015-e)
calls for clear questions, balanced information, transparent campaign
funding and generally cautions against turnout quorums. These are primary
comparative sources, not proof that AIcountry's electorate or thresholds
would be legitimate or effective.

## Selected proposed amendment route

1. **Initiation.** At least one-third of the full Assembly may place an exact
   amendment text into the legislative route. Any person, including someone
   outside the current franchise, may petition and receive a reasoned response.
   A petition by 1% of the existing C02 electorate triggers publicly funded,
   accessible drafting assistance and an Assembly hearing, without giving
   officials ownership of the sponsors' meaning. A final exact text supported
   by 5% of that electorate within a law-fixed collection period reaches the
   referendum route even if the Assembly rejects it. The 1% and 5% thresholds
   are provisional; the charter must protect the initiative, its threshold,
   a collection period of at least one year, objective verification and
   independent correction. Implementing election law supplies usable safe
   paper/assisted signing, duplicate checks and notice without narrowing
   the constitutional right or changing the rules during a live petition.
   Petition capacity must not depend on an employer, party, landlord,
   biometric ID or one vendor.
2. **Text and scrutiny.** The proposal publishes exact current and proposed
   clauses, legal rank, effective dates, affected people and localities,
   continuing rights/claims, fiscal and physical consequences, and known
   uncertainties. Unrelated changes receive separate votes; necessary linked
   provisions are disclosed and reviewed as a package. A competent court
   gives a prompt public, appealable ruling on procedure, subject unity,
   constitutional competence and whether the proposed effect would destroy
   the protected democratic functions below. It cannot reject a lawful
   proposal for its ideology or budget preference. An impartial election
   body reviews question clarity and publishes balanced, accessible
   explanations and opposing reasons; proposal sponsors retain an accurate
   account of their text. Restricted personal evidence remains protected.
3. **Assembly route.** Passage requires two-thirds of the *full lawful
   Assembly membership* in a recorded vote on the exact text. A cabinet
   cannot call a constitutional referendum by decree. The ratification
   ballot occurs only after at least 90 days of public consideration from
   the final published text and, in the reference, no later than 180 days
   after lawful certification unless an independent court orders a narrow
   postponement for an unresolved legal challenge or genuinely unusable vote.
4. **Citizen route.** Once the final petition is lawfully verified, the
   Assembly must publish its reasoned position within 60 days of certification
   and may present a clearly labelled alternative for deliberation; it
   cannot rewrite the petitioners' text without their consent. A competing
   Assembly amendment needs its own route and does not displace or share
   this ballot, so two incompatible texts cannot both win the same vote.
   Missing the opinion deadline does not veto the citizen ballot. The
   election authority schedules the vote under pre-enacted dates, and a
   competent court can order it to perform a withheld duty. No missed
   official deadline counts signatures as valid, adopts text or names a
   referendum winner by default.
5. **Public decision.** The C02 national electorate casts an equal, secret
   ballot on the exact text, with the status quo available and the legal
   effect of yes/no/blank explained. On the Assembly's two-thirds route,
   yes must exceed half of valid yes/no votes. On the citizen-initiative
   route without that Assembly supermajority, yes must reach three fifths
   of valid yes/no votes. The different hurdle prevents a cabinet from
   bypassing the Assembly safeguard through a sponsored petition while
   preserving a route around an Assembly veto. It can also obstruct a
   meritorious minority-backed change; three fifths is a provisional
   value choice requiring affected-public comparison, not an optimum.
   The reference has no turnout quorum: organized abstention must not count
   as a second negative vote. Actual participation, exclusions and
   geographic/group effects are reported, and proven disenfranchisement
   can lead to a lawful recount or repeat poll. A low turnout alone does
   not automatically enact or annul an amendment.
6. **Effective dates and self-dealing.** Ordinary valid changes take effect
   only on their published date with lawful transition. Changes to the
   franchise, electoral formula/map, office tenure, constitutional review,
   appointment/removal, emergency powers, AI/public sovereignty or the
   amendment route itself receive an additional constitutional-function
   assessment and prospective protection. A change that could materially
   benefit present officeholders may not govern their current tenure or the
   contest selecting their immediate successors. Election-rule changes
   therefore begin no earlier than the *following* general contest after
   one contest under the old rule; office and oversight changes cannot be
   used by current holders to appoint, remove or extend themselves. The
   incumbent procedure governs any attempt to amend that procedure.
   No text validates itself retroactively.

The voting route cannot turn the removal of **equal political standing,
effective opposition, genuine periodic competition, executable rights
remedies or peaceful replacement** into an amendment compatible with this
constitution. Institutional *forms* may change if those functions remain
practical; the charter is not a ban on rival democratic architectures.
Judicial review of that boundary must give specific reasons, hear supporters
and opponents, permit lawful appeal and expose shared appointment/funding/
information control. A court may not turn dislike of a tax, identity or
new party into a democratic-core violation. Peaceful advocacy of a
different or even anti-democratic order remains protected expression;
the project need not present its abolition as a valid internal amendment.
A genuine constituent replacement outside this routine route is a
separate legitimacy question for affected people under applicable law,
not a power an incumbent, court, AI or research author may declare.

The campaign must preserve effective opposition: neutral public
administration, meaningful pro/con access to public and independent media,
disclosure of large funders and coordinated third-party spending,
protection of vulnerable small supporters, accessible translation and
offline/assisted voting. A state employer, service provider, payment
operator or communications platform cannot make lawful political support
cost someone their work, home, essential service or ballot. The statutory
standing account funds petition verification, impartial information,
the election, urgent courts, legal aid, source records and transfer if
the amendment changes offices. A government cannot starve the process
and then claim there was no popular demand. AI may compare versions or
translate under challengeable human mandates; model summaries and agent
copies do not become signatures or votes.

## Delays, objections and compatibility

A declaration of emergency cannot change the charter or produce new
tenure. Petition filing and lawful debate continue if safely possible;
a referendum cannot be conducted while coercive restrictions materially
prevent free organization or voting. A necessary postponement has public
grounds, narrow duration, independent review and continuing court access.
It does not extend elected terms or let the cabinet choose a favorable
new rule. If the election body or court is captured or unable to act,
the existing law's substitute jurisdiction, source custody, protected
funds and executable remedies are tested; if those also fail, this is
a constitutional failure, not automatic consent.

The citizen route's 5% signatures and three-fifths approval may put reform
beyond a dispersed minority's reach and favor concentrated money. A referendum
can aggregate yes/no while hiding losses borne by a small group. Without a
quorum, a low-turnout change can appear broadly popular. The Assembly route's
two-thirds rule gives large parties influence, and judicial core review can
become a veto over legitimate reform. D10's simple route may be preferable
where Parliament is genuinely open; a convention may be needed for a
whole-order crisis. These objections remain live. No positive
turnout or signature count alone proves free consent. Actual public
deliberation, fair media, employer independence and an enforceable challenge
chain must be evaluated before adoption.

This selection changes C03/C14 and proposed A9 and affects C02 franchise,
election/registration law, C06 standing funds, C08 courts and legal aid,
C11 evidence, C12 communication and the C14 founding sequence. The older
D10 90-day, two-thirds and referendum elements are retained as a starting
legislative route, while its Assembly-only initiative gate is displaced
for this working design. No earlier review automatically approves that
change. Existing claims, judgments, offices and service duties continue
unless the exact amendment lawfully transfers them. The first constitution
must arise from the **valid predecessor or founding process**, not from
this proposed future amendment rule.

**Evidence:** project documents and the cited primary comparative
sources; no original electorate or implementation observation. **Research
selection:** dual initiation, exact-text public ratification, protected
democratic function and prospective anti-self-dealing rule. **Legal
authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** no jurisdiction, voter
roll, staffed election body/court, campaign independence or funds.
**Observed outcomes:** none; this route is not proven superior to current
countries.

The next bounded test is an affected-public comparison of three mock
cases: a popular rights-expanding proposal rejected by the Assembly, an
incumbent tenure/appointment package pushed through a referendum, and a
wealthy sponsor's citizen initiative. Use the *same* electoral register,
media/finance conditions, legal-aid capacity and timelines for this route,
D10 and a convention route. Publish who could actually enter, stop,
amend, review and execute each result, the delay and cost, and objections
from excluded residents, minority groups, opposition and election staff.
This research project has no public mandate or budget to run that field
test. Reopen the selection if the petition route is only nominal, if
rights review captures policy, if delay entrenches incumbents, or if a
different route better preserves equal standing and reversible power.
