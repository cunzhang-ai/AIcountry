# C01. Why this country might improve lives—and what could defeat it

*10 November 2026 · Design141 core and Design296 usable time · CS13–CS23 current country disposition;
CS01–CS12 retain their scoped design85 evidence. Proposed research, not enacted law.*

The proposed country combines equal political standing, personal independence,
essential access, plural production and paid delivery with public correction.
Many ingredients already exist in present states. Their combination must beat
strong actual alternatives in a defined context; novelty and longer law are no
advantage by themselves. The central hypothesis is that people gain practical
freedom when essentials and remedies work without an employer, patron, provider
or AI intermediary controlling every route. Added burdens can defeat that gain.

The [evidence register](../../settlement/country-case/README.md) distinguishes
original-study reports, values, accounting constraints and untested design
hypotheses. Ten studies are not ten independent validations of this package;
shared settings/versions and uninspected raw data remain explicit. The written
comparative argument is now supplied. Full-duty material estimates, actual
participation, legal authorization, safe operation and observed superiority
remain unfinished/unverified. The strongest complete rival may displace this
reference; institutional elaboration is not the default response to failure.

CS18 retains the current democratic/service-first/plural bargain and genuine
whole rivals. CS16–CS17 adds an explicit justification for consequential new
compulsory process steps and protects paid usable alternatives from forecast-only
savings. This is a future-country legal/design proposal, not a new project
approval method, all-sector law, numerical winner or renewed old-study review.
