# Three households, one room and two budgets

[Housing loss and secure receiving](README.md) · Authored comparison

Three households need an operating decision before the first unsafe
night on day 0. The budget covers days 0 to 28. All intervals are
start inclusive and end exclusive. The houses and funded routes
below are declared, with no actual market quotes or costs implied.

| Household | Need, acceptance and available option |
|---|---|
| A | Wants to keep a safe current home. A payment of 5 units and a binding lawful agreement by day 0 prevent the threatened loss. The continuing tenancy is affordable under existing verified income and support. The payment is incremental relative to the stated account. No unpaid public due or owner concession is counted twice. |
| B | No safe home, with accessible power and an accepting support worker needed. Accepts either an ordinary accessible lease ready on day 0 or an accepted four-night bridge and a different ordinary unit on day 4. Both permanent options use the same independent paid support scope and need 6 recurring units per 28-day period. |
| C | Cannot safely stay home during emergency repairs. Needs the same compatible room type as B for four nights. Its repair and safe return on day 4 are already funded, with actual receiving assumed. |

The local interim operator has one suitable staffed room available
for days 0–4. It cannot house B and C together. C's only suitable
interim path is this room, at 4 units. B can use an independently
controlled alternate room at 5 units. Both interim prices include
their declared transport and support costs. B's ready lease costs
8 in the first period, including 2 for the move. The later lease
costs 6 in the first period under the declared contract. Lease terms,
power, support and access are independently accepted by the applicable
deadline. Neither ready unit takes an already protected allocation in
this fixture. Price unavailable or displaced uses in a real case.

| Whole package for A, B and C | First-period cash | First-period outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| A stays, B takes the ready lease, C uses the local room | 17 | All three covered. One interim room used. |
| A stays, B uses independent interim space then later lease, C uses local room | 20 | All three covered. Two independent rooms used. |
| A stays, B and C each book the local room, B takes later lease | 19 on paper | Two concurrent bookings for one room. No complete schedule. |

With 17 first-period and 6 recurring units, select the direct option
on the declared cost, authority and suitability. With 20 and 6 the
independently bridged package is also a viable repair if the ready
lease is unavailable. With 16 first-period units no listed package
covers all three. With 17 or 20 first-period units and only 5 later
units, lawful access in the first period still leaves a 1-unit
recurring gap for B. An authorized lease, once made, retains its
actual tenant and owner rights. A cash gap exposes a funding and
continuity duty. It gives no automatic basis for eviction or for
calling the month-one package durable.

The case assumes prevention actually succeeds for A. If its
agreement fails or a return is unsafe for C, reopen receiving and
reprice rather than placing them in a fictitious reserve. If two
providers share the failed controller or power source, separate
bookings do not prove independent capacity. The schedules compare
resources, not legal priorities among the three people.
