# A common fare, a cash rider and two settlement dates

Construct a rider with five comparable paid transfer journeys over
one service week. The journeys are additional to any protected
free MO journeys. Two participating operators each charge 4 per
separate leg under the baseline. A common fare is 6 for both legs
with a test cap of 24 for the five journeys. The numbers are
illustrative and carry no local budget authority.

| Same five journeys | Separate fares | Common fares and test cap |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Amount before the cap | 40 | 30 |
| Amount due after the cap | 40 | 24 |
| Amount due after a separate eligible 2-unit refund | 38 | 22 |

The 2-unit refund is a stipulated compensation after fares have been
calculated for completed travel. It has no effect on cap progress in
this case. Correction of an erroneous fare would use the original
fare rules. A free protected return journey neither pays nor consumes
any part of the 24. A cash rider can obtain the common price without
advancing the whole week's fare. If the fare reader fails on the fifth
journey, staff use the accepted offline path and keep the capped price.
A replayed boarding record or correction must not duplicate a debit
or a refund.

The two verified operator payments total 30. Before the first due
payment, 18 of the 24 charged has cleared, 6 remains unsettled,
and 2 is due immediately in rider refunds. A recurring voted source
of 8 is available. Unspent rider credit of 4 belongs to its riders
and is unavailable for operations.

| Network cash through the first payment window | Units |
| --- | ---: |
| Cleared usable fare receipts | 18 |
| Authorized recurring public money | 8 |
| Rider refunds due | −2 |
| Cash available for the 30 of operator liabilities | 24 |
| Shortfall at first payment | 6 |

A separately funded and authorized bridge of 6 makes full timely
payment possible in this case. Without that source the payment is
unfunded at the due time. The second window confirms whether the
remaining 6 actually clears and is legally usable to replenish the
bridge. If it does, the final financing of operator payments is 22
of net fares plus 8 of recurring support. Otherwise the bridge
remains depleted and any next payment needs its own actual source.
A settlement report is not confirmation of public service delivery.

This isolates one tariff, a refund and two payment windows. It does
not represent demand, revenue sufficiency, operator cost or measured
welfare. Compare the tariff with funded fare-free service and a
stronger retained-fare option in an actual common scope. Include
riders who could not afford even the first paid journey, rural
stops where no suitable trip runs and protected free journeys
whose operators use the same resources.
