# One neighbourhood through a hot evening and an outage

*Authored counterfactual. Money, load and household counts are assumptions.
No countrywide quantity, clinical limit or actual readiness follows.*

## Use the same protected dwellings and costs

Forty occupied dwellings are in the same priority group. Assume that
qualified assessment identified each as unsafe without protection from
hour 1 of a particular evening through hour 6. Each unmodified dwelling
can be kept safe with 1.5 kW delivered continuously to suitable equipment.
There is no assumed safe rotation among dwellings. Under the mixed option
a distinct 16 can be brought into safe conditions through hour 6 by
verified exposure reduction and repairs alone. The other 24 require
the stated equipment and power. Use the same set of people and weather
for each option. This single modelled event does not settle winter loads,
future weather or the effect of different buildings.

After existing essential demands, the neighbourhood can use 30 kW of
ordinary grid capacity, sufficient for 20 of the active units. A firm
upgrade to 60 kW can run all 40 under ordinary supply. Both configurations
lose this feeder in the stated outage. Independently islanded backup,
paid in the common baseline, delivers 18 kW and 72 kWh net for this
purpose, after losses and concurrent protected reservations. It can
supply 12 receiving dwellings at once for four hours. A separately
powered and staffed temporary place can accept eight different households
through hour 6. Their safe assisted journeys have been matched and
paid in the common baseline. Ordinary debt, maintenance and protected
services in the baseline continue.

The lawful hypothetical extra window is 16 one-time units and six
annual units. The 16 comprises four committed owner units and twelve
voted public units. The annual units are separately available from a
continuing public source. Compare lifecycle obligations and other
owner and household costs before any real appropriation. These
incremental units buy the same eligible scope under each option.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Retain the building condition and supply suitable equipment | 8 / 4 | 20 | 20 |
| Equipment and a larger ordinary grid connection | 16 / 6 | 40 | 20 |
| Reduce exposure in 16 homes and supply equipment to the remaining 24 | 16 / 5 | 36 | 36 |
| Mixed option and independently accepted mobile supply for four additional homes | 20 / 7 | 36 on the ordinary feeder | 40 |

For the mixed option the capital split is 10 for building work and six
for equipment. Three recurring units fund essential use and two fund
ongoing maintenance and verification. The further mobile source costs
four more one-time and two more annual units and delivers a separately
tested 6 kW and 24 kWh for this event. It can protect the remaining
four during the initial four-hour outage window. That bundle exceeds
the stated window by four capital units and one annual unit. The
remaining annual unit in the mixed option cannot alone pay for it.

The mixed option has a substantial advantage in the stated common
failure. The grid upgrade reaches four more dwellings at home
under ordinary supply and may enable other lawful services not counted
here. If it were independently reinforced against this particular
outage, the outcome could change. The case does not convert electricity
or transfers into repair or justify choosing a package without accounting
for the four unserved households. Existing legal duties continue
under every option.

## Start early and follow the next exhaustion

At hour 0 the feeder fails. The first safe receiving deadline is hour
1. The last safe start for the eight matched journeys is hour 0.25.
The backup must be activated by hour 0.5 to pass the receiving test by
hour 1. The duty officer starts both in time even if the operator
expects repair at hour 2. If safe supply is still absent at hour 5,
the 12 backup dwellings lose their demonstrated route. Under the mixed
option only the 16 verified passive dwellings and eight placements
remain covered until hour 6 unless another route arrives. The four
previously uncovered and these 12 total 16 with no demonstrated
protection after hour 5. The mobile unit in the fourth option would
also exhaust at hour 5. It closes the initial shortfall but does not
extend protection for those dwellings.

Check the next source and latest safe handoff before hour 5. Document
the actual uncovered cases and alert the competent existing officers.
Opening the temporary place adds no receiving credit until the agreed
people can arrive and be protected there. A resident may challenge an
inaccessible transfer or an unsafe home repair. The lawful payer
and case owner remain named through repair and return.

## Repeat the test for winter before carrying over the result

A cold spell uses its own exposure window, seasonal supply and
building acceptance. The relevant service may depend on a fuel,
pump, controller, flue or external heat network as well as
electricity. Check actual delivered heat, ventilation and safe
operation with each failed dependency removed from the test.
Check that temporary equipment can be safely installed and operated
by the person receiving it. A household eligible for support has
no receipt until it can keep the protected room safe or reach an
accepted alternative. No summer load, equipment or travel result
transfers without a matched winter test.
