# Design432. Make a public fare work across operator boundaries

## Decision and baseline

Design431 is the predecessor. MO09 provides a protected free floor.
MO17–MO22 allocate responsibility for a joined journey and recovery.
They leave the price and settlement of additional paid trips across
operators open. Select MO23–MO29 for participating ordinary public
routes under competent law. This revision used AI assistance.

## Alternatives and objections

Independent operator fares with stronger concessions can preserve
local flexibility and simplify settlement. They can leave a transfer
penalty and make capped travel depend on the operator. Wider fare-free
service could remove the cost of the first journey and simplify
collection. It needs a durable funded replacement and an acceptable
capacity response. A common fare with a transfer allowance and cap
shares some pricing across the network while preserving plural delivery.
It creates settlement and confidentiality duties and can reward high
use more than it improves access for people with little cash.

Retain direct protected access and accessible concessions under their
own law. Compare the price of first travel, missed journeys and actual
local services as well as savings for frequent users. Select common
pricing for the first comparison, subject to equivalent cash access,
lawful voted support and a paid fallback. No universal cap amount
is selected. Treat the fare and any contracted payment separately so
cash collection delays can be detected before operators' payroll,
accessibility or the next service window is affected.

## Evidence, authority and migration

[Sources](sources.md) document existing common-ticket and capping
arrangements. They give no local causal or capacity estimate.
The constructed comparison tests one cap and the effect of delayed
settlement. No affected-party participation, independent qualified
review, adoption or appropriation has been supplied.

Elected bodies decide coverage, fare and public source. Use existing
mobility authorities and interlocal agreements, independent appeals
and ordinary procurement and competition duties. Verify operator work
independently of the dominant provider and payment vendor. Use
separately controlled boarding and review capacity and a legally
available route for payment. Publish safe contract, fare, cost and
service facts. Riders and operators can challenge actual exclusion
without disclosing a public travel history or relying on the disputed
vendor.

Apply prospectively. Keep the protected free floor, fare entitlements
already held, open disputes, refund obligations, worker rights, existing
contract claims and journey responsibilities. At any cutover, reconcile
each credited and unsettled item, name the accepting successor and
keep the lawful offline path available.

## Bounded next decision

Use one actual linked service corridor, its surrounding access needs
and a full weekly cycle. Compare retained fares with concessions,
common pricing and wider fare-free service under the same coverage
and paid capacity. Verify household affordability, including cash and
people unable to travel, actual journeys and lawful settlement at each
due date. Test weak collections, outage, accessible refund and a new
operator while maintaining the next protected service. Reopen on
missed first journeys, poor coverage, weak settlement, concentrated
control or a better funded alternative. Real readiness and life
outcomes remain unverified.
