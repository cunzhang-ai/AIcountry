# Design210. Screening before the formal decision

## Baseline and decision

Design209 is the pinned baseline. The existing framework and decision
template guide this research choice. DP already retains sector control,
qualified review, shared dependency limits and paid alternatives. AP already
protects first receipt and usable challenge. EV already addresses downstream
reliance on corrected evidence. Choose how to apply these duties to routing
that changes the service received before or beside an official decision.

## Evidence and rival diagnoses

The [source register](sources.md) gives the observation scope. One explanation
is that targeted referrals buy better detection with less total scrutiny.
Another is that the same queue transfers time and proof burdens to people
outside the measured referral sample. Both can be true. Signed decisions
cannot distinguish them. The agency analysis provides a positive signal about
the use of review time relative to random sampling. The paid-case medians
show why referral outcomes and time to payment need separate measures.
Good ordinary routing is a more demanding comparison. Affected claimants,
workers, disability and privacy representatives, local receivers and
independent adjudicators have not participated in this choice.

## Disposition and objections

Select effect-based responsibility, a protected ordinary route, and separately
funded constrained screening. When no independent evidence supports additional
proof or delay, retain original priority and the ordinary decision basis.
The alternative that only guards the final decision is insufficient because
the queue has already allocated a scarce public resource. A blanket prohibition
would give up some plausible targeting benefits. It remains appropriate for a
particular use when screening has no qualified benefit, interferes with a
protected duty or cannot be safely corrected. The ordinary route can itself
be unfair. Compare observed baselines and provide challenges in both routes.

There is a real cost in time and money. Keeping qualified staff available for
two routes can reduce or erase a tool's claimed savings. Random checks can
add burden and still miss excluded people. Use lawful minimal observation and
safe controls, with no new powers of search or proof. Keeping exact signals
away from a deciding officer can limit anchoring, but concealing material
grounds from a challenger would defeat due process. Record the routing cause,
protect actual sources under ordinary law and preserve review of those limits.

## Democratic control and transition

Enacted sector law and voted appropriations govern real priorities, personnel
and outlays. The existing task owner is accountable to sector leadership,
affected people and independent review. DP coordinates shared warnings and
has only its existing role. Auditors and courts retain separate appointment,
records, funding and execution boundaries. An appropriation or contract
cannot supply a new power to hold due benefits. Protected testimony stays
under existing evidence and privacy duties. DP16 and sector law govern the
start date, legacy queues and accepted succession. No new authority, funded
staffing, migration or delivered outcome is asserted.

## Bounded next action

The next useful test is a time-and-workload comparison on one essential
payment or access route with a matched ordinary comparator and a safely
observable cross-sector dependency. The sector owner would supply the live
process, an independent reviewer would examine measures and remedies, and
affected people would test access and correction. Any study would need
permission and a named source covering qualified working time and independent
review. Without that capacity, keep the existing evidence qualification.
A material change in source or route, observed crowding out, proof burden
or inaccessible remedy requires fresh review. A competent ordinary route
that performs as well within the same capacity displaces the screening choice.
