# C06. Mental-health and crisis finance

*Part of [C06 public finance](../C06-public-finance.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The Assembly funds the C07 voluntary mental-health and substance-use
network in one visible commitment account: primary-care training,
community and peer teams, specialist consultation, medicine, psychological
care, youth and older-person access, crisis response, mobile transport,
after-hours cover, safe short-stay places, overdose response, outreach,
protected records, housing and return after discharge. Health, care,
housing, income and justice authorities keep their distinct claims and
budgets; the same worker or bed is counted once.

A move away from institutional or police-led crisis response needs funded
overlap, not an assumed instant saving. Existing patients, workers, orders,
facilities, treatment and property claims require lawful continuity while
qualified community staff, accessible premises and independent reviewers
are recruited. Paid peer work, translation, travel, night cover, relief
for existing carers and alternate providers are real costs. Community
groups cannot be booked as a free substitute for trained care. Scarcity
does not create authority for a clinician, police commander or AI score to
change eligibility or impose treatment; unmet service remains visible to
the Assembly and claimant.

Legal aid, external inspection, complaint protection and investigation of
serious coercion draw on a separately traceable rights budget. Fine or
police budgets do not finance clinical eligibility, and treatment providers
cannot be paid for confinement as a default output. Report money, staff,
actual requests, accepted care, missed crisis response, medication access,
liberty intrusions, housing and recovery outcomes separately. No real
population need, workforce, infrastructure, law or costs are known.
Reopen if institutional closures abandon people, community capacity is
paper-only or the new account hides displaced services and coercion.
