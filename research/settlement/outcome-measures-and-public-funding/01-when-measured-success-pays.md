# When measured success pays

*Design542 · Constructed budget choice, with a historical failure mechanism.*

Compare two offers for the same 100 eligible residents receiving
employment support. Forty need more staff time or accessibility support.
The public budget can use 12 units after common duties. Under both offers
ten units pay ordinary service and wages. Independent review and existing
remedies have their own funded source. Two eligible units can pay a
performance supplement or buy verified additional access work.
Follow the same original population for the same 90-day period. A completed
support contact and a sustained placement are different events. Safe
voluntary work and usable net income also need separate evidence.
All cohorts, prices, results and contract terms below are assumed.

| Choice under review | What its evidence shows | Next budget decision |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic supplement for 70% reported placement among activated cases | The provider reports 46 sustained placements among 60 activated files, or 76.7%. Independent safe review finds four further sustained placements among the 40 omitted by this denominator. The common result is 50 of 100. Among residents needing more support four of 40 reach the outcome. | A self-reported rate crosses the contract threshold. If an existing contract lawfully makes the sum payable on those facts, follow that contract and its remedies. The reported rate supplies no basis for renewing this threshold. |
| Ordinary service, proportionate review and separately funded access repair | On the same cohort and dates, 55 of 100 reach the outcome, including 12 of 40 needing more support. The two-unit access work buys qualified help without taking ordinary wages or due care. | Select this branch if comparable independent evidence, accepted ordinary work and the funded next call qualify. That judgment concerns the observed people, not a prediction of the national effect. |
| New capped incentive on an independently confirmed result | An example prospective rule uses the original cohort and a separately specified protection for those needing additional support. On the first offer's record, an overall threshold of 70 of 100 cannot be met, and a hypothetical group threshold of 10 of 40 also fails. | Hold the optional supplement under the stated contract. Pay verified ordinary work. Price the protection and independent verification before considering this rival in a future budget. The illustrative thresholds set no national targets. |

If the review directly verifies 46 outcomes and has no defensible result
for the omitted 40, the population result is bounded by 46 to 86 of 100
if the other 14 activated outcomes are verified non-outcomes. A reported
76.7% among activated cases still cannot establish 70 of the original
100. Where loss of evidence blocks the optional release under the exact
new terms, keep the money and ordinary service separate, and act before
the next release. A missing outcome does not count as a known failure.
If two AI tools infer 24 additional successes from the same portal
closure field, the verified result remains 46 and the range stays 46–86.
The reviewer can request original evidence. Agreement between the two tools
cannot turn closure into proof of employment. Residents whose actual wait
or rights were impaired keep their remedies.

If the operator relies on the optional two units for the next ordinary
shift, the claimed separation of funding fails. Price the real base roster
without the reward. Bring a funded source or provider correction to the
competent body in time. Paying an unearned reward creates no lasting
protection, and stopping it creates no permission to miss an existing duty.

Test a contrary case in which independently checked and comparable evidence
shows 72 of 100, including 16 of 40, sustained placements. If the published
contract's exact release conditions are met, the stipulated supplement
can be payable. Check whether spending the two units on a different
ordinary improvement could do as well. A contractual obligation and
renewal on comparative merit are distinct. Where the group worsens or
staff are drawn away from lawful duties, report the harm and handle it
under the applicable service and review rules even if the payment has
validly accrued.

England's National Audit Office reported in 2014 that the Work Programme
set higher outcome payments for harder-to-help groups and that prime
contractors' planned spending per person for those groups fell on average
54% from the initial bids. The latter measures providers' planned
allocation. It does not measure each person's support or identify the causal
effect of the incentive. The evidence tests the assumption that a higher
payment automatically protects harder-to-serve people.
[National Audit Office report, paragraphs 3.7–3.9](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/The-work-programme.pdf)

Before a real decision, establish a relevant baseline, paid independent
verification, directly affected people, an exact lawful instrument and
accepted next-window capacity. Compare original rights and actual receipt
separately from the outcome measure. [Canonical rule](../../country/C14/04/10-outcome-measures-and-public-funding.md)
