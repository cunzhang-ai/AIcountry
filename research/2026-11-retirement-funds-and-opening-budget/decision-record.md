# Design438. Protect both pension receipt and care in the opening budget

Design437 is the pinned predecessor. Existing RF and Y provisions
preserve earned rights and specify the proposed earned-scheme
cash reserve. T73–T78 bring the payers, reserve and underlying
care obligations together on the opening calendar without pooling
restricted balances. This revision used AI assistance and parallel
source and design review.

The [two Danish annual reports](sources.md) distinguish the state
and municipal financing shares in a social-pension accounting line,
separate the administrator's cash perimeters, and supply ATP group
cash flows. They establish neither due-day usable cash nor a complete
legal payer census. The constructed test shows why a late authorized
transfer can impair the reserve even when today's pension can still
be paid. Actual local care, other schemes and opening pensions
require their own receipts and obligations. No affected-party
participation, independent qualified review or legal adoption occurred.

Compare retimed ordinary backing, a separately funded standby and
a lawful alternate. Earlier backing can reduce bridge charges but
moves pressure to the national payer's existing calendar. Standby
preserves an alternative at a carrying and repayment cost. A different
paying route can protect continuity if its legal authority, staff and
receiving work. Select lawful advance cover where needed and verify
repayment against the next due period. Retain due benefits and ordinary
independent review. Do not use a care source to repair a pension buffer.

Carry existing earned and survivor rights, lawful foreign claims,
restricted funds, review and worker obligations through handover.
Elected bodies choose appropriations and the liability-bearing rules.
Each payer proves availability and receipt. Independently reconcile
one actual basic and one earned payment from source to receipt,
including the next care deadline and a delayed transfer. Reopen if
the source perimeter, due dates, available bridge or remedy changes.
Full national readiness and comparative outcomes remain open.
