# Shared capital requires real acquisition and accountable control

*Part of [C05/08](../08-income-ownership-and-country-choice.md); live rival C.*

C keeps I05 and existing taxes but changes the ownership channel: a resident
mutual acquires the household-owned equity of market firms voluntarily.
Public equity and the insurance pool remain separate. This is a structural
rival to redistribution only through tax/benefit accounts; it is not an
instruction to compel a sale or establish one national investment controller.

The first-year fixture buys equity for 1,308.75 million, its **authored book
value and assumed tax basis**, through a seller loan at 5% with thirty equal
annual principal installments. Sellers receive 65.4375 interest and 43.625
principal in year one. Actual valuation, tax basis, willingness to sell,
capital-gains/transaction tax, covenants and legal authority remain unknown.
Book value is not evidence of the price at which owners would exchange
their future returns and control.

With private dividends D, purchase price P, interest r and years T, resident
cash after the fixture's pass-through tax is **0.6 × (D − rP) − P/T**.
Principal is paid from after-tax cash; it is not a deductible operating
cost or new income. A negative amount is an acquisition-finance failure,
not permission to borrow for distributions or raid an income floor.
Interest, redemption, reserves and investment need a viable future path;
a favorable first year cannot establish that path.

At the book price, old owners exchange firm equity for a mutual loan;
members initially have an acquired asset matched by the debt. Internal
household claims cancel in the aggregate. Principal builds members' net
position by reducing the sellers' loan, not by creating national resources.
Only actually received resident cash counts for I05; an illiquid unit or a
gross dividend already used for debt service does not pay living costs.

Shared financial benefit does not establish democratic control. A viable
rival must specify member voice and replacement, worker and user rights,
independent custody/valuation, disclosed manager pay, creditor voting and
default powers, conflicts, usable exit and continuity for dependants.
Equal units must not let managers, software vendors or creditors control
jobs, housing and political reach together. Replicated AI agents gain no
extra mandate. State regulators, tax collectors and independent courts
retain their respective powers; a mutual board is not the electorate.

Compare federated or competing funds, worker/user ownership and gradual
prospective capital sharing before any national pooling choice. Practical
alternatives require affordable entry, portability and surviving obligations,
not a nominal right to leave without assets or service access. No governance
or acquisition variant is selected here; its additional staffing, reserve,
transaction and transition costs are unknown. No public/pension guarantee
or unpriced compulsory transfer finances this reference.
