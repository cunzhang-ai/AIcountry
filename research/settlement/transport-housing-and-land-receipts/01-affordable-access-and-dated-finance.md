# What a transport project buys for households that live there now

*Design316 · Original finance comparators and separate constructed choice.*

## Two public financing routes

In its January 2026 decision, the Greater London Authority reports
a £4.1 billion Crossrail contribution for 2010–11 through 2015–16.
It distinguishes around £3.3 billion of borrowing from £0.8 billion
of direct contribution. Later contributions and financing use
the Business Rate Supplement and Mayoral Community Infrastructure
Levy under their separate legal terms. The decision forecasts
£3.6 billion in residual debt at 31 March 2026. That figure is
a forecast in the decision, without claiming a subsequent outturn.
A business-rate supplement is a statutory tax on a specified base,
with proceeds and timing distinct from a particular parcel's rise
in value. [London decision MD3465, sections 1.1–1.8](https://www.london.gov.uk/md3465-crossrail-business-rates-supplement-approval-policies-2026-27)

Copenhagen's By & Havn reports public ownership by the municipality
and state, property development and sale, and income that supports
the metro and other infrastructure. Its corporate description says
the owners are jointly and severally liable without limit for the
company's obligations. This gives a different institutional source
and risk allocation to compare. It does not establish the net cost,
affordability or resident displacement effects of a particular route.
[By & Havn strategy](https://byoghavn.dk/forretningsstrategi/)
· [Corporate conditions](https://byoghavn.dk/selskabsm%C3%A6ssige-forhold/)

## Compare the same residents and the same due window

Ten original households are in the protected study scope. Four
can currently afford to stay and reliably reach the relevant
destinations. Track an actual safe and affordable stay or accepted
equivalent through the work, a usable journey and real receiving.
The fictional budget has 14 available units by the work deadline.
Protected ordinary repair alone costs 8. Three more units buy a
feeder connection, and three buy funded housing retention and
alternative receiving. The full extension replaces the feeder
package and includes the ordinary repair and renewal scope. It
costs 18 without housing protection and 21 with it. Recurring
operation and legal approvals are assumed separately verified for
the exercise. No actual new tenure or travel is inferred.

| Exact project | Cash due in the first window | Original households with affordable housing and useful access |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Retain and repair | 8 | 4 |
| Repair, feeder and funded housing protection | 14 | 7 |
| Extension without the added housing protection | 18 | 5 in the comparison after four of nine reachable original households are priced out |
| Extension with funded housing protection | 21 | 9 under the stated receiving assumptions |

The extension's map suggests access for nine original households if
they all remain. Those who can only reach from a home they can no
longer afford do not count toward received access. Track the effect
on new households separately. The extension anticipates seven units
in public land receipts after the deadline. The adverse receipt is
four. Even in the stronger case the early shortfall for the protected
extension is seven. Borrowing seven on the later receipt without
independent backing would leave three of principal unfunded under
the adverse outcome, before financing costs.

Select the 14-unit package in the example. Keep the larger protected
extension open with seven additional lawful early units and an
actual ongoing source. Retaining public land under an affordable
lease can protect homes and a recurring source while bringing less
early cash. An earlier sale or a new general tax has its own
distributional and financing effects. Compare those choices
openly without making projected land income a condition of
existing housing protection.

All household counts, budgets, prices and effects in this exercise
are authored. The real financing sources supply no displacement
estimate or effect size for AIcountry.
