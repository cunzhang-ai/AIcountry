# A service can depend on AI that its purchase list does not reveal

*Design314 · Original audit evidence and separate constructed comparison.*

## An observed omission

The US Government Accountability Office's March 2026 report reviewed
the Internal Revenue Service's June 2025 internal AI inventory.
It contained 126 active use cases, including 77 in development.
The audit identified 62 entries with at least one information-quality
issue. It also identified contracted AI used to help build criminal
cases that was missing from the inventory. A prior identified omission
concerned contracted taxpayer identity proofing. The audit records the
IRS's subsequent work to add two identity-proofing uses separately.
Do not treat an entry as proof of deployed work, a quality issue as
a finding of unlawful use, or the known omissions as a census.

Officials reported that review capacity fell and that quality assurance
reviews of entries were suspended in early 2025. The audit supports
checking procurement, actual practices and staffing as well as annual
self-reporting. It does not estimate a nationwide omission rate,
show harm from each omitted use or test the proposed institution.
[GAO-26-107522, inventory findings and recommendations 2–5](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107522/index.html)

## A separate operating example

Two statutory services share a vendor-controlled proof function. The
income-support office lists its intake use. An education-access office
uses an embedded function in an ordinary casework contract. The service
owner establishes that the feature affects whether evidence reaches
a decision-maker. A reviewed ordinary contract and a small lawful
sample of actual service paths reveal that second dependence. Both
services' protected duties continue, with six and five due evidence
checks in the same time window. A separately tested ordinary staff
route can complete eight compatible checks in that window. All
workloads, paid reserve quantities and legal assumptions are authored.

| Operating choice under common loss | Qualified independent capacity | Due work left uncovered |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Count the formally listed income-support use only | 8 for the actual combined 11 | 3, although the list-based forecast shows zero |
| Turn off both proof functions and use the same staff as before | 8 for 11 | 3 |
| Add one qualified independent staff group with three accepted slots | 11 for 11 | 0 in this fixture |
| Discover the second use and postpone an unrelated optional launch, with no qualified new staff yet | 8 for 11 | 3. Publish the open duties and seek funded correction. |

Treat the earlier case dates as operative throughout. One reserved
worker group counted in both offices does not supply the three
additional slots. Staff first follow the applicable prior priority
rules. The example does not assign an unprovided clinical or legal
priority between residents. Protect the available eight checks and
record every missed check. Verify actual useful receipt separately.

Select a workflow-based check with funded independent confirmation.
A contract-only inventory is cheaper and misses uses outside its
classification. A centrally imposed supplier list can reduce initial
uncertainty while concealing common dependence and optional uses
that become consequential. Blanket monitoring would impose larger
privacy and employment costs. Trace material effects with existing
audit, worker and resident routes and deliberately limited evidence.
