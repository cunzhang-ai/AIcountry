# Decision record: animals as a distinct public responsibility

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 51 research selection; not enacted law

## Baseline, question and affected interests

The [design-50 predecessor](predecessor-design-50/README.md) preserves the
prior country. Its C09 food and ecology rules mention farms, fishers,
habitat and public health, but no current chapter assigns the experiences
of animals under human control an independent legal concern, inspector,
budget or remedy. The [function audit](../COVERAGE-AUDIT.md) omitted animal
welfare entirely. A food-safe product or viable species population can
coexist with severe individual suffering. The bounded country-design
question is whether, and under whose authority, avoidable animal suffering
constrains production, research, disease response and daily care while
people retain food access, rights, work and democratic control.

This is project-authored research, not participation by residents, farmers,
fishers, animal carers, workers, Indigenous peoples, researchers, consumers,
religious communities or people dependent on affordable food. Nor can
animals consent to a governance model. Their interests require careful
evidence and an accountable human representation route rather than
invented political votes. Values include reduction of suffering, human
wellbeing, cultural freedom, food security, scientific knowledge, nature,
livelihoods and public control. Science can investigate animal experience
and interventions; it cannot settle the weight of those values by itself.

## Rival diagnoses and whole choices

Unaddressed animal harm could arise from a moral exclusion, rules with no
inspector, poor veterinary access, unsafe production incentives, disease
response, consumer ignorance or a shift of harmful practices to imports.
Original site observations, species-specific health and behavior evidence,
worker and community accounts, disease and food records, inspections and
import-chain findings could distinguish these mechanisms. No AIcountry
animal or production baseline has been observed.

| Package | Mechanism and possible benefit | Serious objection or failure |
|---|---|---|
| Retain human-health, food-safety and ecological rules only | Uses current sector duties and avoids a contested new moral status | Healthy consumers and viable habitats do not ensure animals avoid severe suffering; no one owns that omission |
| Private certification and consumer choice | Diverse producers and purchasers may exceed a public floor | Price, hidden supply chains and unverifiable labels leave animals and low-income people without an effective remedy |
| Ban nearly all human uses of animals or grant broad equal personhood now | Strongly resists instrumental harm and may accelerate alternatives | Scope, conflicts with food and care, legal representation, transition and democratic consent are unresolved; an abrupt ban can abandon living animals |
| **Selected: statutory serious-harm floor plus species-sensitive welfare duties** | Separates animal welfare from human-health and ecology accounts; elected law, independent inspection, safe care, review and costed transitions make duties contestable | Standards can be weak, captured or selective; public standing and inspection can also become arbitrary coercion |
| Single integrated One Health ministry | Shares disease, human, animal and ecological information | A coordinator could subordinate animals to production or use animal risk to centralize coercive power |

The selected [C09 topic](../country/C09/10-animal-welfare-and-one-health.md)
states the independent welfare concern, cross-sector boundaries and life-cycle
contexts. [C08](../country/C08/07-animal-protection-and-remedies.md) assigns
enforceable public-law duties and review, [C06](../country/C06/13-animal-care-and-transition-finance.md)
counts capacity and transition, and [C13](../country/C13/16-animal-harm-food-and-disease-response.md)
tests a combined coastal case. The rival broad animal-personhood position
is preserved, not relabeled as this selection. Whether law should prohibit
particular uses regardless of measured welfare remains a democratic value
choice, not a veterinary parameter.

## Primary evidence and limits

The [World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH) animal-welfare
account](https://www.woah.org/en/what-we-do/animal-health-and-welfare/animal-welfare/)
describes welfare as physical and mental state and treats its five freedoms
as guidance, including behavior, fear and distress. WOAH's [standards
development page](https://www.woah.org/en/what-we-do/animal-health-and-welfare/animal-welfare/development-of-animal-welfare-standards/)
identifies transport, slaughter, research and production-system chapters;
its [aquatic-animals page](https://www.woah.org/en/what-we-do/animal-health-and-welfare/aquatic-animals/)
recognizes farmed-fish welfare. Its [research and education
chapter](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_research_education.htm)
sets out replacement, reduction and refinement. The [WHO One Health joint
plan](https://www.who.int/publications/b/66239) is a primary comparator for
human, animal, plant and environmental interdependence; WHO explicitly
describes it as nonbinding guidance in the [initiative account](https://www.who.int/teams/one-health-initiative/quadripartite-secretariat-for-one-health/one-health-joint-plan-of-action).
These sources do not establish legal authority in AIcountry, universal
sentience thresholds, a single moral ranking or that this package will
improve outcomes. WOAH standards cover species and settings unevenly;
national law would need its own scope, evidence and affected-public choice.

## Authority, dependency and dissent

The Assembly chooses the floor, defined exceptions, inspection powers,
appointment and funding, research-use authorizations, trade response and
transition. C09 veterinary and welfare expertise informs but does not
legislate; C08 inspection enforces; courts hear human challenges and
public-law failure claims; C06 appropriates; C07, food safety and ecology
retain their separate duties. Appointment, budget, source observations,
inspection, enforcement and court execution must be tested under a hostile
minister or dominant producer. A public advocate cannot become an
unaccountable substitute legislature. AI evidence processing cannot create
a mandate or multiply one by replicating agents.

An adopter must inventory existing animal, wildlife, farm, fishery,
veterinary, research, food, environmental, trade, cultural and emergency
law and actual treaty obligations. Existing lawful care, research, work,
contracts, permits and animals alive at commencement require prospective
rules, notice, support and safe successors. Valid existing law continues
until a competent process changes it. No research selection authorizes
new entry, seizure, culling or criminal prosecution. Before claiming
readiness, the state must show actual veterinarians, inspectors, shelters,
laboratories, fair hearings, affordable food and funded transition.

The limited welfare floor may be morally inadequate to people who reject
animal use; even well-run slaughter still ends a life. Conversely, animal
protection powers may burden poor keepers, minority food traditions and
remote producers more than wealthy operators. A uniform rule could fail
fish or other less-studied species; a broad precautionary rule could become
unworkable without transparent criteria. Disease controls can prevent harm
while themselves causing mass suffering. Imports can hide displacement;
trade limits face lawful constraints and foreign people affected. These
objections remain live and cannot be averaged into a welfare score.

## Result and next discriminating test

Evidence qualification: international guidance and project reasoning,
not local observations or independent review. Research selection: add a
distinct, species-sensitive and enforceable animal-welfare responsibility
to the reference country. Legal authorization: absent. Operational
readiness: absent. Observed human, animal and ecological outcomes: absent.
No current constitutional clause, criminal offense or agency has been
adopted by a real public.

The next bounded test for a real jurisdiction is an opening inventory of
the animals and activities covered, current legal protections, care and
inspection capacity, food and livelihood dependencies, and import exposure,
with affected people heard and animal outcomes observed. Compare this
package against human-health/ecology-only and stronger nonuse packages
for one specific production or research setting before choosing statutory
scope. The legitimate legislative authority, not the research project,
would decide. Reopen this selection if that evidence shows serious harms
remain unremedied, transition cannot protect food and existing animals,
or a rival package better meets the publicly chosen values.
