# Models, inspected primary texts and the limits of cooperation

*Part of [jurisdiction journeys](README.md); consulted 15 October 2026.*

**Treaty-first recognition** offers negotiated shared standards, reciprocity
and potentially lower uncertainty, but can leave people without a remedy
where no usable agreement exists. **Broader defendant/actor-based reach**
may help external victims hold mobile controllers accountable, with competing
commands, access and sovereignty risks. **Connected statutory cooperation**,
selected here, permits ordinary connected claims without compulsory treaty
reciprocity, while requiring specific routes for public/penal/special matters.
It requires links, scoped law, independent review and actual received remedies.
Its foreign-law proof and diverse-case cost may exceed the treaty-first model;
that remains a real rival if an actual usable network serves people better.

The HCCH [2019 Judgments Convention](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/full-text/?cid=137),
Articles 1–2/4–10, distinguishes scope, original effect, jurisdiction bases,
refusal/severability and noncompensatory damages. It excludes family/status,
estates/insolvency, specified public matters and arbitration. Our separate
domestic statutory route is not treaty membership or a claim that the treaty
covers those exclusions. Foreign recognition is not physical execution.

The HCCH [2015 Choice of Law Principles](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/full-text/?cid=135)
addresses commercial party autonomy and mandatory/public-policy limits;
consumer/employment contracts are outside its scope. It does not prescribe
our weaker-party defaults or supply a binding universal conflict rule.

The UN legal library's [official CAT introductory note](https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/catcidtp/catcidtp.html)
describes Article 3's prohibition of return/extradition to a substantial torture
risk and the convention's related jurisdiction/prosecution duties. The treaty
PDF endpoints were unavailable in this check; this is not a new full text/
party-status audit. P23's broader death/ill-treatment/child protections and
thirty-day filing rule are independent proposed choices, not CAT wording.

The inspected UN [Rome Statute Part 7 text](https://legal.un.org/icc/statute/99_corr/7.htm)
(1999 corrected text, Article 80) leaves national penalty application outside
that Part's prescription. This rebuts an automatic inference from ICC's own
penalties to a mandatory national 30/life rule. It does not establish present
full international-crime conformity, immunity, admissibility or treaty status;
the full specific domestic schedule and fresh complete source review remain.

All new connections, weaker-party defaults, foreign-finality choice and limits
are normative hypotheses. No actual cases, original observations, affected
participants, qualified independent lawyers/judges or partner states are
provided. Shared legacy sources/AI transformations are not independent evidence.
This bounded source/objection check is not a comprehensive 2026 law/events review.

Material objections: forum shopping, inaccessible distant proof, captured
recognition, foreign sham proceedings, immunity gaps, surveillance through
cooperation, unpredictable applicable law and expensive capacity. The draft
proposes narrow links, no automatic merits retrial, independent source/risk
review, real alternative forums, finite clocks and explicit unmet claims.
It has not demonstrated those protections effective; credible better models
can displace the choice. Ordinary foreign finality leaves a declared possible
impunity conflict for the separate international-crime schedule.
