# A published emergency-to-recovery cutover in 2016

Following the 14 November 2016 earthquake, New Zealand's Acting
Minister of Civil Defence terminated the state of local emergency
declared on 6 December for Kaikoura District. Termination took
effect at 1 pm on 9 December 2016. A distinct notice under section
94A began a national transition period at the same local time
covering Kaikoura, Hurunui and Marlborough districts. The two
instruments have different scopes. Gazette issue 118 dated
15 December prints both instruments on page 139. The issue date
is not the stated operative time. [New Zealand Gazette,
notices 2016-go7087 and 2016-go7088](https://gazette.govt.nz/issues/31765)

The 2016 amendment had inserted Parts 5A and 5B into the Civil
Defence Emergency Management Act 2002. In that historical law
section 94C set a default 90-day national transition period
subject to extension or earlier termination. The new regime
provided recovery-specific powers. For example, section 94L
specified grounds for entry and section 94M provided for road
and public-place access restrictions. The transition notice
therefore did more than assign staff or change a reporting label.
[2016 amendment, section 27](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2016/88/en/2016-11-29.pdf)

An extension signed on 7 March 2017 continued the national
transition period for Kaikoura and Hurunui districts and the
Wairau-Awatere ward of Marlborough. The notice states a June
7 endpoint at 1 pm unless changed earlier or extended. The
agency's retrospective table reports 180 days overall and
the geographic narrowing at 90 days. The original 90-day
default was therefore only one segment in this instance.
[Extension notice 2017-go1408](https://gazette.govt.nz/issues/31876)
· [Agency transition table](https://www.civildefence.govt.nz/emergency-events/previous-emergencies/transition-periods)

The Gazette verifies the legal cutover and its stated scope.
It does not show when each field operator received instructions,
which recovery powers were used, actual receipt of aid or the
effects on residents. This is a historical comparison of a
separately authorized recovery regime. It is not a description of
current New Zealand law or an authority proposed for AIcountry.
