# United Kingdom. A fiscal announcement and its independent forecast separated

On 7 September 2022 the Treasury told the Office for Budget
Responsibility that it would not commission a forecast for the
23 September announcement. The Office had begun preparing a
forecast round and provided preliminary analysis. This distinguishes
capacity and preparatory work from an independently published
forecast for the actual policy package.
[OBR November outlook, foreword, pp. 1–3](https://obr.uk/docs/dlm_uploads/CCS0822661240-002_CCS001_SECURE_OBR_EFO_November_2022_BOOKMARK.pdf)

The Treasury did publish provisional costings with its Growth Plan.
Its Table 4.1 estimated two 2022–23 energy interventions at
GBP 31 billion and GBP 29 billion, or GBP 60 billion combined.
These are reported direct cost estimates for that scope. The table
is not a cash execution record. Table 4.2 generally used the prior
Spring Statement economic forecast and identified measures without
a sufficiently specified costing. The distinction is between
proponent costings and a contemporaneous independently published
forecast for the exact set of policies.
[HM Treasury Growth Plan, Tables 4.1–4.2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-growth-plan-2022-documents/the-growth-plan-2022-html#policy-decisions)

A new forecast was commissioned on 26 September, initially for
23 November. The actual OBR outlook was published on 17 November,
55 elapsed days after the Growth Plan. It included changes through
the Autumn Statement, following later policy decisions. The
November estimate cannot serve as a missing contemporaneous estimate
of the unchanged September package.
[OBR chronology and November publication](https://obr.uk/efo/economic-and-fiscal-outlook-november-2022/)

In 2024 Parliament enacted a requirement for the Treasury to request
an OBR report for specified significant announcements. If an applicable
request is omitted and the OBR judges the measure significant, the
Act requires committee notification and a costing. It covers
combinations of measures and excepts temporary emergency measures.
This is a real commission-and-fallback alternative to AIcountry's
standing parliamentary work. Neither the historical sequence nor
the later Act proves how a forecast would have altered the vote,
financing costs or household outcomes in 2022.
[Budget Responsibility Act 2024, section 1 as enacted](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/24/section/1/enacted)
