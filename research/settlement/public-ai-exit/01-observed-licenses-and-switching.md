# What observed cloud procurement says about the cost of leaving

In its November 2024 report, the U.S. Government Accountability Office
examined six federal agencies and eleven selected cloud investments.
Officials at five of the six agencies described restrictive practices.
Four agencies reported increased costs and three reported limits on
provider or architecture choice. The categories can overlap. One
reported example involved a contractor requiring the agency to pay to
regain ownership of its data at contract end. GAO examined guidance and
implementation as well as reported impacts. None of the six had fully
established guidance covering both identified risk-management activities
at the time of the report. These results describe the studied arrangements,
rather than the prevalence of lock-in across all public IT or AI.
[GAO report GAO-25-107114](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107114)

The UK Competition and Markets Authority's 2025 final cloud market
decision distinguishes technical differentiation, skills and the fees
charged when data leaves a provider's cloud. Chapter 6 finds that
technical barriers and egress fees can restrict choice. It reports
limits to free switching programmes, which excluded ongoing multi-cloud
egress, and distinguishes technical barriers from inherently different
capabilities. Paragraphs 8.97–8.98 relate switching to costs, operational
risk and diverted IT work. The report is a market investigation across
its defined UK cloud scope. It does not measure the cost of a particular
public AI transfer, or prove that multi-cloud is always the best design.
[CMA final decision, chapters 6 and 8](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688b8891fdde2b8f73469544/final_decision_report.pdf)

The UK National Audit Office's 2025 study of technology suppliers
describes gaps in the public buyer's combined technical and commercial
capability. It notes that government did not have a consistent overall
view of digital spending across organizations and that procurements need
enough understanding of the existing service and how a new system
connects to it. Its lessons draw partly on earlier programme audits.
These findings qualify the proposed need for paid public competence
and comparable scope. They supply no current national price for exit.
[NAO technology suppliers report, paragraphs 1.2–1.8](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/governments-approach-to-technology-suppliers-addressing-the-challenges.pdf)

A lower exit fee can help a
buyer while leaving required licences, technical dependencies and staff
time unresolved. The contract and safe receiving test address those
distinct conditions. These reports do not measure the proposed country's
service performance or implies a public right to a vendor's model.
