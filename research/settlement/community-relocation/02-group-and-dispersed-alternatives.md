# Compare individual and group moves under the same recovery budget

This authored case concerns twelve households whose former sites cannot
safely receive them at the tested hazard level. Eight are interested
in living near an existing shared facility and four initially choose
independent placement. Earlier temporary housing and case support remain
lawfully funded to day 90. One separately authorized unit of reversible
preparation is available and carries no project consent. The permanent
placement budget is 13 capital units and five recurring units per period.
Units are case assumptions with no currency conversion or estimate of
Cyclone Gabrielle costs.

The individual route can accept at most nine households by day 90,
at one capital unit and 0.25 recurring units per placement. This
maximum is physical and qualified, so unused capital cannot create a
tenth available place by that date. The group site can accept five
households on its existing verified net capacity. The group phase
requires six actual opt-ins and can serve up to eight if qualified
upgrades add as many as three places without displacing other users.
Its cost is four fixed capital units, including the host increment,
plus 0.5 per participating household. Recurring cost is two fixed units,
including shared facility operation, plus 0.25 per household.
Independently verified water, school and care arrangements are required
before the selected sites or slots count toward coverage. This case
assumes they work on schedule where the budget and capacity pass.

| Alternative | Capital and recurring uses | Coverage by day 90 on the stated assumptions |
|---|---|---|
| Dispersed moves only | Up to 9 capital and 2.25 recurring spent on available placements | Nine placed and three still needing a financed safe bridge |
| Group phase with eight actual opt-ins and four independent moves | 12 capital and 5 recurring | All twelve if eight group places and four independent places are accepted |
| Group phase after two of eight decline, with six independent moves | 13 capital and 5 recurring | All twelve if six group places and six independent places are accepted |
| A third household declines before irreversible commitment | Stop the group phase because five is below its workable minimum. The same dispersed route covers up to nine. | Three still need a financed safe bridge and new accepting options |

A 12-unit capital cap would pass the original estimate yet fail after
two households choose the separate route. A qualified reduced phase or
a lawful one-unit addition would be needed to sustain the modeled
twelve placements. If the host increment is late, the five existing
group places cannot support the agreed six-household phase. That
reopens timing, holding costs and the independently funded bridge.
The planned dates for care, assistance and ordinary rights still apply.
The case supplies no verified bridge price or tenure beyond day 90.
A favorable placement calculation alone therefore cannot qualify a release.

The joint option makes more placements in this fixture because it
creates additional timely capacity and meets a stated preference. A
dispersed route with twelve actually available places would use less
money on these assumptions, at 12 capital and three recurring units,
and may better suit those who want to move elsewhere. It would still
need a separate decision on the common place. Relationships and
collective land authority cannot be measured by seat counts or settled
by an assumed willingness to move.
