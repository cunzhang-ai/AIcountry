# United Kingdom. An unchanged wage could change an annual award

The June 2020 Court of Appeal judgment in Johnson and others describes
four workers whose ordinary monthly pay dates interacted with their
monthly Universal Credit assessment boundaries. A non-banking day
could shift one of two regular wages into the same assessment period.
The court records a sharp award change and loss of an opportunity to
use a work allowance, rather than a cashflow difference that always
cancelled out. It records claimant evidence on budgeting, debt and
work choices. One claimant described a yearly loss of around £500.
That is claimant evidence in litigation, not a population estimate
or independently reproduced household account.
[Judgment, paragraphs 1–3, 58–62 and 97–102](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2020/778)

The court upheld the relevant interpretation of the existing receipts
rule while finding the failure to address this calendar effect irrational
in the stated circumstances. The 2020 amendment replaced regulation 61
with a rule including reallocation of employed earnings to assessment
periods. Its explanatory memorandum describes that scope as a response
to the judgment. This is evidence of a particular repair, without
independent payment receipt or measured improvement for the whole group.
[Judgment, paragraphs 45–47 and 111–119](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2020/778)
· [Amendment and explanatory memorandum](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/1138/pdfs/uksiem_20201138_en.pdf)

The department's separately published guidance says an ordinary monthly
pay date shift is usually adjusted automatically. It also identifies
calendar bunching for weekly, fortnightly and four-weekly payment.
Its four-weekly example has two actual paydays in one monthly
assessment period once in a year. That is guidance about a different
earnings cadence, rather than evidence of a second payment of the same
monthly wage. The source establishes neither the rate of corrections
nor their effects in practice.
[Department guidance, short and changed pay cycles](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/universal-credit-and-earnings)

AIcountry uses an individual reference and pays the I07 ordinary award
in advance. Those choices differ from the benefit described in the
judgment. The evidence supports testing the interaction of timing and
nonlinear awards and separating it from unavailable wages. It supplies
no national price, adoption or proof of improvement for this proposal.
