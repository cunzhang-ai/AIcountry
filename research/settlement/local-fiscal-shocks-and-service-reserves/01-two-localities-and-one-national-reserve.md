# Two localities and one national service reserve

*Design372 · Historical transfer records and a separate constructed comparison.*

## Historical timing and forecast changes

Finance Canada reports actual fiscal stabilization advances of CAD
251.4 million for Alberta in February 2016 and CAD 31.7 million for
Newfoundland and Labrador in March 2016, both for fiscal year 2015–16.
The rounded figures total CAD 283.1 million. The records establish
reported transfers and timing. They contain no matched service receipt
or evidence about which local duties would have failed without the money.

For Alberta's separate 2020–21 claim, the Alberta 2022 budget forecast
CAD 750 million in 2022–23. The Alberta 2023 budget forecast CAD 707
million in 2023–24. Finance Canada's payment table lists CAD 576,511,311
on April 6, 2023, 736 days after the end of the 2020–21 claim year.
That listed amount is about CAD 173.5 million below the earlier
forecast and CAD 130.5 million below the later forecast. These are
differences from the listed payment, not estimates of a service
shortfall, an audited total for the claim or proof of late payment
under Canadian law. The cited table covers payments starting in
2023–24. It cannot rule out other earlier transfers. None of the
amounts below is scaled from Canada.

## One early cut-off and one later call

Two councils face involuntary losses and verify uncovered protected
work. Each can buy a complete safe service package for four units of
cash by day 2. Neither operator can use less. A's work has the higher
preenacted protection priority on independent sector evidence. A
separate voluntary local tax cut does not create a third reserve
claim. Standing payments, rights and independent court and election
funding are outside the comparison and already reserved. Early
usable national cash is six units. A potential two-unit supplement
has separate authority and clears by day 2 in one path and day 3
in another. A separately reserved later service needs three units
by day 14. It receives three in the ordinary later path or two
when national revenue also weakens.

| Arrangement or event | Result on original dates |
|---|---|
| Wait for the ordinary transfer settlement | If cash arrives after day 2, neither early package can rely on it. A qualified earlier local bridge or shared operator could change the result. |
| Split the six units equally | Each gets three. Neither accepted early package delivers. |
| Reserve six under the published complete-package rule | Pay four to A and keep two available until B's lawful alternative or cutoff is resolved. B's original duty stays exposed. |
| Settle the authorized supplement by day 2 | With eight, both packages fit if both operators accept and can deliver. The same money on day 3 cannot satisfy day 2. A common supplier failure can still leave B exposed. |
| Test the later call after a successful early release | Three separately settled units buy the next accepted service. Two do not. The earlier payment cannot be counted again. |

When safe service can be split, usable smaller protection remains a
real option. The two early packages here are indivisible by assumption.
Selection is a bounded reserve with paid priority verification and a
funded supplement where it actually qualifies. No locality's claim,
court access or elected powers disappear when the reserve runs out.
A longer-run alternative is a more stable assigned source or funded
shared provision. Price its control, capacity and loss of flexibility
alongside its protection before replacing an arrangement.
