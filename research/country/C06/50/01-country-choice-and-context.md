# Let physical limits change the production choice

The old annual mixed-food/desal example is not a safe country blueprint. Updated
private demand, explicit institutional recipes, monthly irrigation/catchment,
treatment yield, reuse compatibility and compound exposures change its scope.
Passing an older, narrower arithmetic test supplies no current readiness.

Compare five physical profiles and five power arrangements. Cross mixed/import-
heavy food with desal/bulk water rather than confound food policy with one water
technology. Add a bounded-production rival: keep the mixed cereal/pulse shares,
but use one quarter of its proposed root/vegetable/fruit domestic shares, importing
the remaining same reference quantities. This is a voluntary new-production case,
not an order to existing growers or a claim that imported food has no foreign harm.

The declared five-train source envelope gives a crop-share frontier of about
0.28318 under the monthly stress. The selected quarter is below that numerical
frontier; neither figure is agronomic evidence, a safety certificate or an optimal
national share. Existing producers' title, contracts, employees, skills and losses
need paid lawful transition. No additional trade volume is presumed contracted.

This comparison favors the bounded crop reference for the next contextual design
because the fixed mixed expansion requires substantial peak-source construction.
It retains more domestic grain/pulse production than the import-heavy case while
exposing increased foreign dependence. It establishes no welfare, environmental
or fiscal dominance. Cheap bulk imports can fail source/route independence;
desal can fail siting, brine, energy and ecological conditions.

The two twenty-four-hour power arrangements remain unresolved choices. Their annual
fuel schedule is identical; a battery label proves no lower emissions. Real
geography, upstream harms, diet, public input or source terms can reject this
whole reference. Retaining existing effective arrangements remains a real option.
