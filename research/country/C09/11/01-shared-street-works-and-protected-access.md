# SW01–SW06. Keep neighborhoods reachable during shared street works

*Design383 · Proposed ordinary law under [safe infrastructure and mobility](../11-safe-infrastructure-and-mobility.md).*

**SW01 — Give the street authority one forward works calendar.** The
existing competent street authority maintains a joined schedule for
material planned public and utility works, including its own.
Promoters provide lawful scope, location, window, route and service
effects, duration, resource and restoration requirements. Coordinate
with neighboring authorities where closures or diversions interact.
Publish usable notices, changes and a safe offline challenge route
without publishing sensitive technical or personal records.
Minor works with no material shared effect keep a proportionate
route. A calendar entry creates no consent, excavation permission,
land right, payment authority or favorable claim to a scarce slot.

**SW02 — Protect actual access before permitting planned overlap.**
For a material restriction, the street authority checks the works
together for their actual windows and affected people. Require safe
usable access for walking and wheeling, emergency response, essential
transport, premises and time-sensitive school, care and other due
services. Verify accessible alternate routes or equivalent lawful
receiving with the responsible service, including any covered return.
Account for notice, temporary crossings, delivery, noise and burdens
shifted to another street. Include people unable to use a digital map,
a longer detour or an inaccessible vehicle. A planned overlap that
would remove a required route stays held until an accepted alternative
or safer sequence qualifies. Sector duties keep their original dates.

**SW03 — Combine work where the whole route improves.** Compare separate
renewal, a shared work window, safely phased work and any relevant
non-excavation alternative. Favor a common window where avoidable
closures and costs fall and all necessary independent inspection,
protected access and continuing network operation qualify. Keep
operators, technical approvals and asset duties distinct. A common
window does not require a common trench. Test leakage, maintenance
isolation, access rights and common interruption before physical
co-location. Keep independent routes where one failure would remove
essential service without a qualified backup. No promoter can delay
due maintenance indefinitely by waiting for a partner or reserve
future street access to exclude competing operators.

**SW04 — Assign each cost and control once.** The street authority
and promoters agree exact sequence, accepting crew, lawful permit
and conditions, public and operator contributions, safe access,
independent inspection, reinstatement and contingency. Attribute
incremental and shared costs under prior law to their actual causes,
responsible owners and common public purposes. Keep planned network
renewal, charges, private liability and user remedies distinct. Count
shared excavation and mitigation once, without assuming future
savings, fines or reimbursements pay current work. A street authority
as promoter faces the same access, evidence and outside review rules.
Share only the records necessary for scheduling and safety. No
monopoly, universal lane charge or fee exemption follows.

**SW05 — Handle an emergency without making it a planning exception.**
A competent operator may take only the immediate safety or restoration
steps authorized under existing emergency and sector law. Record
original times, grounds and minimum necessary occupation. Notify the
street authority and affected services as soon as practicable, then
update the common schedule and put available safe access into effect.
Pause or retime incompatible discretionary work within actual powers.
Preserve unmet access, missed duties and independent review. A late
planned job does not gain emergency authority. No new compulsory
worker shift, seizure, tariff or extension of emergency power follows.

**SW06 — Reopen the street on evidence, then check the next cycle.**
Verify the usable route and actual service at each stage and have
a competent independent verifier accept reinstatement where required.
Record temporary patches, paid follow-up, defects, outstanding
claims and the actual reopening time. Keep original liability and
guarantee obligations on handover or insolvency. Review avoided and
added closures, access by affected group, paid work, noise, costs and
repeat disruption. AI may reconcile schedules and identify overlaps
or map uncertainty. Qualified authorities decide permits, safety and
acceptance, and affected people keep direct remedies. Adopt with an
enacted local remit, funding and lawful succession. Reopen on repeated
missed access, deferred maintenance, unsafe co-location, distorted
incentives or a simpler qualified sequence.
