# BC01–BC05: a real connection and a serious-harm duty

*Part of [C10/14](../14-cross-border-buying-and-responsibility.md); design187 candidate.*

**BC01 — Commercial responsibility follows actual decisions.** The proposed
Act covers commercial buyers, importers and lead firms actually domiciled,
centrally administered or materially directing the relevant buying here,
and substantial local commercial establishments for their connected sourcing.
National/local public and publicly controlled SC buyers are covered too.
Ordinary household consumption, gifts and informal mutual help are excluded
from this new commercial duty; other actual law remains. A nominal shell,
intermediary or fragmented order cannot hide actual decision/control facts.
A foreign seller's merely accessible website, passing shipment or common AI
vendor supplies no domestic defendant or worldwide enforcement jurisdiction.

Goods, works and services qualify where an actual business relationship links
the protected harm to the buyer's operations or supply, including a known
material upstream tier. Tier labels do not settle responsibility; neither does
mere investment/diversified fund membership create a purchasing relationship.
Actual operator, employer, control and finance-specific duties remain distinct.
B41 forum, B44 conflicts, actual immunities and lawful foreign cooperation
still apply. The BC Act expressly makes the covered buyer's own relevant buying subject
to this mandatory duty despite a foreign contractual choice. For a covered
home buyer, its buying and controlled delegation remain covered wherever
performed: sending procurement abroad is no escape.
A foreign affiliate still needs its own nexus/attribution; group membership
alone creates neither its liability nor a domestic command over its assets.

**BC02 — A specified serious floor, not export of every domestic rule.** Cover
slavery, trafficking and work exacted through threat without genuine voluntary
consent, including coercion imposed after recruitment; economic need alone
is not proof of forced labor. For this serious floor a child is under18. Cover
commercial sexual exploitation of children; coerced child work or
work gravely endangering a child's health, safety or development; foreseeable
death/serious injury or disease from unsafe work; systematic deprivation of
actually owed pay on which subsistence depends; and violent/coercive retaliation
for peaceful organizing, complaint or testimony, even if a foreign prohibition
denies that activity. This does not immunize independently proved violence or
export every domestic bargaining procedure. Also cover grave physical harm
to community health, drinking water, subsistence land/livelihood or ecosystems,
including forced displacement without lawful protection and usable remedy.
Seriousness considers scale, severity and practical irreversibility, not the
buyer's reputation. Ordinary price competition or a minor isolated mistake
is not automatically one of these predicates. Existing stronger rights survive.

Actual applicable wage, child-employment, land and environmental law remains
independently binding. BC's serious floor does not export this country's nominal
minimum wage, every planning permit, age rule or carbon budget abroad. A foreign
permit cannot excuse the specified grave harm; lawful foreign policy differences
alone are insufficient. Courts interpret contested predicates with original
facts, expertise, reply and appeal; an AI list cannot enlarge them. No new
criminal predicate follows from the civil floor.

**BC03 — Reasonable inquiry must affect a real decision.** Before material new
sourcing and during its continuation, identify reasonably foreseeable serious
risks and reasonable measures to prevent/respond, proportionate to severity,
likelihood, actual influence, enterprise capacity and the transaction. Use
credible warnings, actual sector/process/location risks and changes in orders,
not nationality as guilt. No fixed annual report, mandatory consultant or
all-tier census for every purchase. Known grave harm needs prompt attention
regardless of buyer size. Deliberate ignorance is no defense; inability to know
a hidden fact despite proportionate inquiry is different from ignoring it.

**BC04 — Evidence belongs to people, not the badge.** Hear workers and affected
communities safely, with actual freely chosen representatives where possible;
management-selected interviews alone are inadequate where coercion is credible.
Inability to consult requires safer alternative evidence and disclosed limits,
not compelled exposure or a fictitious consent. Evaluate contrary observations,
shared original sources and reviewer conflicts. Audits, traceability tools,
AI inference and certificates may help inquiry but do not establish absence
of harm, independent consent or discharge of the buyer's own conduct duty.

**BC05 — Keep proportionate reasons and protected records.** The responsible
buying manager keeps material warning, own decision, response and follow-up
records sufficient for actual challenge, proportionate to BC03. Actual relevant
cases preserve evidence through supplier/platform changes under B/PD lawful
retention, security and access; no indefinite public worker/relationship graph.
Give an affected complainant a usable response, safe contact and reasons without
compulsory internal exhaustion or sensitive testimony publication. Public SC
accounts disclose material risks/costs within actual confidentiality law.
Private records are available through lawful targeted evidence powers, not a
general public business-surveillance portal or a vendor's exclusive credential.
