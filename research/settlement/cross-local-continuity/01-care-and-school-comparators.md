# Existing ways to keep care and learning during a move

*Design201 · Original evidence and institutional limits.*

## Adult care in England

Sections 37 and 38 of the Care Act and its statutory guidance assign
responsibilities for an adult's intended move between local authorities
within the relevant English adult-care system. In covered cases the
destination must arrange continuity when its assessment or arrangements
are incomplete on the move date. The previous support plan informs
interim provision, and actual needs, preferences and new conditions
still matter. Regulation 2 of the 2014 interauthority dispute regulations
says disputes must not interrupt support. It ordinarily keeps the authority
already meeting need responsible while the dispute is unresolved. If none
is meeting need, it specifies a fallback by where the adult is living, or
by presence where the adult is not living in an authority area, with its
own awareness and notification provisions. This division of delivery and
dispute is a real institutional rival. Its legal triggers and authority
cannot be imported into other sectors. [Care Act sections 37–40](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/23/part/1/enacted)
· [Statutory guidance chapter 20](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-act-2014-supporting-implementation/care-and-support-statutory-guidance)
· [Dispute regulations, regulation 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2829/pdfs/uksi_20142829_en.pdf)

In a May 2024 decision about a move to Devon, the Local Government
and Social Care Ombudsman found both councils at fault. The family
moved on 3 December 2022. The receiving council failed to ensure the
adult's needs were met until 17 May 2023, around five and a half months
after the move. The originating council reported funding through
16 January. The agreed remedy distinguishes the later insufficient-
support period from the broader continuity fault. The record also
reports some support purchased by the family, weekly visits from
March, respite from May and a direct payment received in July,
backdated to 17 May. These are different events. Do not interpret
the whole period as zero support, or an agreed corrective payment as
verified receipt. This case shows a real timing and accountability
failure, not a prevalence estimate. [Ombudsman decision, findings and actions](https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/adult-care-services/assessment-and-care-plan/23-012-482)

## School stability in the United States

The federal statute for homeless children and youths provides a
scoped school-of-origin or local enrollment choice guided by the
child's interests. Its immediate-enrollment rule covers missing usual
records and extends to a pending enrollment dispute under its own
conditions. The enrolling school seeks records. The statute also
requires covered school-of-origin transport on request, with cost
and responsibility shared equally if the relevant local educational
agencies cannot agree after a move across their areas. This is an
actual statutory default for specified travel. It does not prove a
driver is present or supply a default for adult care, medical
payments or national grants. [42 U.S.C. § 11432(g)(1)(J)(iii) and (g)(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t42/s11432/)

The country design uses the functional separation. Immediate
eligible receiving and protected school continuity run while fiscal
and jurisdiction disputes are decided. Source law specifies which
advance can pay and how to settle liability. The case reports
observable harm under an existing duty. These sources do not
compare overall local autonomy, national affordability or the
effects of the proposed alternative.
