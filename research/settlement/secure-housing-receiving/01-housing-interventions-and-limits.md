# Observed housing interventions and limits

[Housing loss and secure receiving](README.md)

The US Department of Housing and Urban Development Family Options
Study randomly assigned 2,282 families recruited from emergency
shelters in 12 communities to different offers. In Exhibit 6-12 at
about 37 months, 15.7% of surveyed families assigned priority access
to a permanent subsidy had experienced homelessness or involuntary
doubling up in the previous six months, compared with 37.8% of those
assigned priority access to project-based transitional housing.
These are outcomes by assignment within that comparison. The total
programme costs for the subsidy assignment were about 9% above
usual care over the follow-up in a separate comparison. Programme
costs do not represent complete social costs or this country's cash
requirements. The study supports comparing actual durable offers
with temporary provision on multiple outcomes and common costs.
[HUD final report, Exhibits 6-12 and ES-8](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/family-options-study-full-report.pdf)

Canada's At Home/Chez Soi project followed people experiencing
homelessness or precarious housing with mental illness in five
cities. Its final report gives 62% of Housing First participants
housed all the time in the last six months, versus 31% in usual
care. It studied housing with support and specified participant
contacts. It supplies no direct test of this proposal's independent
support terms, universal same-night duties or ongoing costs.
[National cross-site final report, summary](https://www.mentalhealthcommission.ca/wp-content/uploads/drupal/mhcc_at_home_report_national_cross-site_eng_2_0.pdf)

The two sources test different populations, outcomes and offers.
They cannot be pooled into a single effectiveness or savings number.
The chosen prevention and urgent duty are normative and institutional
decisions. Unit fit, ownership, worker cover, actual affordability,
community access and independent consent need original local evidence.
