# Auckland. Who funds recovery and who takes the open cases?

## Compare the same commitments

In October 2023 Auckland Council accepted a storm recovery and resilience
package of more than NZD 2 billion. Named work included NZD 820 million
for Making Space for Water, NZD 390 million for transport and an initial
NZD 774 million estimate for voluntary buyouts. The NZD 1,076 million
government-related headline included NZD 199 million of expected National
Land Transport Fund support. The remaining NZD 877 million was the direct
Crown envelope. These are programme and financing figures at the time,
with different scopes. [Council decision](https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/news/2023/10/auckland-council-and-crown-agree-to-cost-sharing-agreement-for-storm-recovery-and-resilience-work/)

A March 2025 briefing set out the effect of shifting direct Crown funding.
Figures below are NZD millions. The last column describes the proposal
in the briefing. [Briefing DPMC-2024/25-348, table 1 and paragraphs 18–30](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2025-07/pr-cyclone-recovery-unit-briefings.pdf)

| Direct Crown commitment | Original | After July 2024 shift | March 2025 proposal |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Category 3 buyouts | 387.000 | 447.350 | 624.093 |
| Category 2 mitigation | 380.000 | 319.650 | 142.907 |
| Direct transport | 110.000 | 110.000 | 110.000 |
| Total | 877.000 | 877.000 | 877.000 |

The proposed second shift was NZD 176.743 million on top of NZD 60.350
million already shifted. The briefing identifies additional local funding
needs because the council had a higher cost share for buyouts than for
the mitigation being shifted. It proposes removing four other mitigation
projects and Wairau stage two from the Crown funding schedule. The council
expected to fund most of this remaining work. At the time Kumeu had no
feasible solution for the known risks. The total Crown cap gives no proof
that local costs or the lost protection have been covered. [Crown briefing](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2025-07/pr-cyclone-recovery-unit-briefings.pdf)

The audited 2024/25 statements confirm a March amendment transferring
funding from mitigation to buyouts within the NZD 877 million cap.
At 30 June 2025 the estimated number eligible rose from about 900 a year
earlier to about 1,200. The council reported cumulative purchases of 792
properties and payments of NZD 863 million. It separately reported net
settlement payments of NZD 731 million for 667 properties during the year,
and a remaining discounted provision of NZD 356 million for Category 3
buyouts. Grant receivables had their own recognition rule. These columns
answer different questions about exposure, annual payments and obligations.
They cannot be added as one recovery cost. [Audited statements, notes D4 and F4](https://new.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/content/dam/ac/docs/reports/annual24-25/volume-3-financial-statements-2024-2025.pdf)

## Follow people and open work

Council communications in 2024 described a navigation service for owners
and tenants covering assistance such as accommodation advice and referrals.
The property categorisation programme worked directly with owners. The
council identified routes to disclose intolerable risk to tenants in some
circumstances. The publication also distinguished immediate building
restrictions from longer-run risk. It leaves open how many tenants were
reached in time and received a safe, affordable place.
[Council tenant support account](https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/news/2024/04/supporting-tenants-through-storm-recovery/)

The recovery office closed on 30 June 2026. The council reported 1,164
buyout offers and 138 grants in its June handover account. Remaining
complex buyouts and mitigation passed to a recovery team within Auckland
Emergency Management, and the property team took the acquired land.
Work on co-funded infrastructure continued. Offers, settlements, resident
moves and completed mitigation are separate milestones. These accounts
identify organizational successors. They provide no household-level proof
that each successor accepted, funded and carried out each open duty.
[Office handover](https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/news/2026/06/from-crisis-to-resilience-storm-recovery-office-wraps-up/), [official recovery entry](https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/recovery/)

The case supports explicit accounting, direct occupant contact and durable
case ownership in the proposed design. It does not identify a causal effect
or establish that the proposed arrangement would outperform Auckland.
