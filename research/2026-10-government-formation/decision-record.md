# Decision record: government formation without an incumbent veto

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 42 research selection; not enacted law

## Baseline, affected people and competing diagnoses

The [design-41 predecessor](predecessor-design-41/README.md) is preserved.
Its C03 Assembly elects a prime minister and can replace one by selecting a
successor. The no-threshold election can return a fragmented Assembly, yet
the current chapters do not assign nomination, formation failure, minority
cabinet legitimacy, a no-confidence vote without a successor, or the cabinet's
exact powers during a repeat poll. The C06 budget bridge and C14 handover gate
address related but different questions. This gap affects all residents
relying on law and services, challengers seeking office, Assembly groups,
career workers, courts, creditors and security services. No such people have
participated in AIcountry; this is one AI-assisted research selection.

Possible diagnoses differ. A formation impasse may reflect a genuinely
divided electorate that ought to choose again, a defective ballot or party
entry system, a Speaker or incumbent manipulating the agenda, or legitimate
refusal of unacceptable programmes. An automatic winner would misread these
causes. Stable services, a responsive executive, opposition freedom and
majority removal pull in different directions. Original election and
negotiation evidence would be needed to distinguish obstruction from
substantive disagreement; no present AIcountry observation does so.

## Coherent institutional alternatives

| Package | Democratic gain | Failure or cost |
|---|---|---|
| Retain majority-only investiture and constructive replacement | Every cabinet begins with an affirmative majority; a successor is known before dismissal | Repeated coalition failure can leave the old cabinet administering indefinitely; a rejected minority cabinet may be impossible to remove without agreement on its successor |
| Immediate negative investiture | A candidate able to avoid majority rejection can form a cabinet quickly | Nomination control can make the Speaker a kingmaker; the cabinet may lack affirmative backing and may govern by inertia |
| **Selected: majority negotiation, then a limited negative-investiture fallback, with majority dismissal and repeat poll** | First seeks a majority, allows transparent tolerated minority rule, lets an actual majority dismiss it, and returns four vetoed candidates to voters | Extra ballots and a quarter-support test need explanation; repetition can fail again, and a caretaker still operates through an unresolved crisis |
| Directly elected executive independent of Assembly | Voters choose the executive directly | Competing mandates can immobilize budget and law; changing the whole constitutional form requires a separate founding comparison |

The official [German Basic Law](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/index.html)
provides majority election, a later plurality path and constructive no
confidence. Sweden's [Instrument of Government](https://www.riksdagen.se/globalassets/03.-dokument-och-lagar/bestall-och-ladda-ner/other-languages/grundlagar-2023-engelsk-web.pdf)
uses a proposal rejected only by more than half of all members and a new
election after four rejections. The [Spanish Constitution](https://www.congreso.es/constitucion/ficheros/c78/cons_ingl.pdf)
allows a later lower-threshold investiture and a repeat election if no
candidate wins within two months after the first vote. These are primary
descriptions of different tradeoffs, not evidence that any imported clock
works for AIcountry. The selected combination and its numbers are untested.

## Selected package and authority chain

The [C03 canonical rule](../country/C03/06-government-formation-and-caretaker.md)
selects a 21-day affirmative-majority opportunity after the new Assembly
convenes or a later cabinet vacancy is recorded. Then a candidate with at
least one-quarter full-chamber support can form a government if no full
majority votes against; after four vetoes
or no qualified nominee by day 30, prior law calls a repeat election within
90 days. The clock starts from the preannounced new sitting or recorded
vacancy, so a Speaker cannot delay it by avoiding a first vote. A named
deputy can conduct a required ballot if the Speaker refuses. An
independent court can compel access and correct illegality; it cannot
nominate, invest or refuse a political candidate on merits. The Speaker
hears all groups and publishes all nominations, support and reasons; member
sponsorship, vote order, ties, candidate withdrawal, incapacity and disputed
seats need complete prior law. Agents can summarize options but have no
formal votes, nomination right or certification power.

A constructive full-majority replacement transfers immediately. A separate
full-majority dismissal without a named successor is retained because public
control would be weak if most representatives could reject a minority
government but not remove it. It starts a new formation route and a strictly
limited caretaker. A cabinet cannot create its own dissolution or emergency
authority. The elected Assembly can still enact lawful budgets and ordinary
statutes while negotiations continue; opposition and public evidence access
remain protected. If a repeat poll is called, the old Assembly exercises
its lawful duties until a successor sits, without changing the imminent
poll's rules for its own advantage. The last lawful cabinet carries only
prior-law continuing administration. A majority cabinet might dislike this
simple dismissal right; the cost is potential turnover and repeated polls.

The caretaker restriction begins at the prior-law election-period start;
the new Assembly sitting starts the formation clock. The C06 standing
essential-obligations schedule, separate 90-day bridge and
actual cash account remain distinct from the formation timetable. The
caretaker cannot turn a formation failure into a new appropriation, tax,
senior appointment, treaty or force mandate. Treasury, courts, electoral
workers, local service authorities and command custodians need separate
appointment, funding, information and execution paths. The entire chain
must survive a Speaker who blocks candidates, a cabinet that withholds
records, an unpaid electoral office and a court whose order is ignored.
The [C13 impasse exercise](../country/C13/07-formation-and-budget-impasse.md)
shows the combined failure but is not field evidence.

## Objections, limits, migration and next test

The strongest objection is that the fallback legitimizes a quarter-supported
cabinet while most voters did not affirm it. The answer is not that minority
rule is popular: new law and money still require elected votes, and a full
majority can dismiss it. This does not erase the possibility of administrative
advantage, especially if opposition cannot control information or security.
The quarter floor may be too low in a large Assembly or too high in a very
fragmented one. Four vetoes and 30 days may be rushed for a dispersed polity;
90 days may be too slow for urgent services. A repeat election can reproduce
the same division. Minority rights and collective claims cannot be traded
for a deal merely to end caretaker status. Dissent favoring affirmative
majority or direct executive election remains live.

This changes C03's older majority-only formation assumption and the D2
constructive-only no-confidence candidate. Current constitutional and
electoral law, cabinet terms, civil-service succession, annual and standing
appropriations, treaty commitments, appointments, pending claims and secured
records would need authorized reconciliation. Inherited lawful obligations
continue; no formation vote modifies the project website's current API or
adopts a constitution. The founding authority must enact the complete rule
before the first contest, with funded nomination, translation, parliamentary,
election and review capacity. Existing reviews of the older proposal cannot
be silently carried over.

**Evidence:** official comparator texts and logical stress, without an
AIcountry electorate or observed negotiation. **Research selection:** the
two-stage formation, full-majority removal and bounded caretaker above.
**Legal authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** no Assembly,
Speaker, election body, Treasury, court, budget, map or staff. **Observed
outcomes:** none; superiority over any existing country is unproven.

The next bounded test would use one real possible jurisdiction's seat results,
formation records, public service obligations and lawful authority to run
the same failed-coalition and budget sequence under this package and the
majority-only, immediate-negative and direct-election alternatives. Affected
voters, parties, independents, workers and service users would judge whether
the incumbent and Speaker can be replaced in practice. Reopen this selection
if the quarter floor distorts representation, if repeat polls entrench the
caretaker, if an opposition majority cannot effect dismissal, or if a rival
delivers more practical public control with no hidden loss of rights.
