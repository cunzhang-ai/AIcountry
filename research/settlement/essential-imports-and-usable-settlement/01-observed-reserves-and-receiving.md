# Sri Lanka 2022. A reserve asset, a credit line and delivered goods

## Start with the same reporting date and vintage

The IMF's 2025 third-review report shows the following for end-2022.
Its historical rows use the same vintage and express values in U.S.
dollar equivalents.

| Official reserve measure | USD millions | Prospective import months as published |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross official reserves | 1,898 | 1.2 |
| Usable gross official reserves | 462 | 0.3 |
| Difference of published amounts | 1,436 | Keep the two published coverage measures |

The usable definition excludes the conditional People's Bank of China
swap, which the report describes as about USD 1.4 billion in 2022.
Its cited usability condition is gross reserves above three months of
the preceding year's imports. The coverage column uses prospective
imports and is a different measure. The difference is not an extra
claim, a currency-by-currency Treasury balance or proof that the
remaining gross amount could pay a specified supplier. The Central
Bank of Sri Lanka's annual report gives the same rounded gross
boundary and notes the usability condition. These are overlapping
official evidence, not independent estimates of separate reserves.
[IMF Country Report 25/56, Table 1 and note 3](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1lkaea2025001-print-pdf.pdf)
· [Central Bank of Sri Lanka, annual report](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/en/publications/economic-and-financial-reports/annual-reports/annual-report-2022)

## Keep credit, dispatch and receiving at their own stages

The Central Bank's energy account describes a short-term USD 500
million petroleum credit facility from the Export-Import Bank of
India. It separately reports using USD 200 million for petroleum
from a USD 1 billion State Bank of India facility. A secured facility,
a particular documented use and an outstanding payable exposure
have different meanings. The annual observations do not give the
exact balance, currency availability or safe shipment for a given
day. Do not add the two credit ceilings and the 200 as three receipts.
[Central Bank of Sri Lanka, 2022 annual report, chapter 3, p. 100](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/sites/default/files/cbslweb_documents/publications/annual_report/2022/en/7_Chapter_03.pdf)

The World Bank's emergency brief reports financing a purchase of
85,000 metric tons of LPG and distribution of over six million
cylinders to urban and semi-urban households and businesses. It
separately reports USD 22.3 million in essential medicines delivered
to the Ministry of Health. A ministry delivery has a different
endpoint from a cylinder distributed, and does not identify a
patient receiving treatment. The brief was updated during the
response. Its purchase, distribution and shipment quantities
have different scopes. There is no verified common parcel count,
per-cylinder cost or complete household coverage in these records.
[World Bank emergency response brief](https://www.worldbank.org/en/brief/2022/09/21/world-bank-emergency-response-in-sri-lanka)

The original IMF staff report and the authorities' attached letter
describe shortages of fuel and medicines and power and water cuts
during the broader crisis. These records do not identify the
causal share of a particular facility or the timing of a shared
port failure. The country design uses them to require the joined
financing and receiving checks. It does not import an actual
foreign credit line, exchange rule or service price into the
Danish national comparator or the candidate.
[IMF 2023 staff report and authorities' letter](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2023/116/article-A001-en.xml)
