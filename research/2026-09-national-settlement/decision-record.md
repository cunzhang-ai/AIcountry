## Problem, principles and alternatives

Design 1 selected services and income security but did not yet state a common national
guarantee/funding schedule or examine their resource demands together. That is a local
textual gap. It is not evidence that a real country's services have failed.

Equal standing, usable essentials, democratic choice and protection from arbitrary
denial are values. Accounting identities and scarce qualified staff constrain provision.
All population, receipts, costs, workload, liquidity and finance limits in this iteration
are invented. Their assumed adequacy is not evidence, and no empirical causal effect
of cash versus services is estimated.

Compare retained capacity B, services plus existing income protection S, and retained
capacity plus an unconditional cash supplement U. The same population, revenue, opening
balance, financing limits and essential-workload assumptions apply. U is additive to
existing protection; replacing benefits or changing taxes would be a different package.
Tax-funded universal provision, more private supply and different ownership patterns
remain alternatives beyond the model. Better coordination is not presumed sufficient.

## Selection and objections

Select a joint guarantee-and-delivery schedule; explicit national/local funding and
correction duties; coordinated tax/benefit withdrawal; one consolidated resource account;
and a democratic response to impending shortfall. Retain S as the conditional reference
consistent with the prior social commitment, not because invented figures prove it best.

The shared model has nine trajectories, each with three years. All three packages fit
the narrow reference financing limits; B/U leave authored service-workload gaps. S's
year-2 fiscal-shock gap is 52 million CU and its separate care-shock gap is 155 FTE.
It therefore receives no readiness qualification. Closing arrears retain unmet obligations
instead of making the books balance with invented borrowing. Annual accounting does not
prove payments arrive by the day needed.

Material objections retained:

- S may embed bureaucratic paternalism or inadequate service quality. User agency,
  local choice and independent remedies remain necessary; no measured satisfaction
  or welfare effect is available. Institutional ownership is not settled by this model.
- U may reduce exclusion and support valuable unpaid activity. Those effects are not
  modeled. Its extra gross transfer cost is not a net social loss, and the comparison
  cannot rule out a differently financed universal-income design.
- Means tests can impose surveillance and benefit cliffs. Safe provisional access,
  coordinated schedules and accessible review constrain them but are unproven. No
  ideal taper or adequate basket amount is invented.
- National fallback can centralize power or reward local mismanagement. Published
  need/capacity equalization, audit and local challenge are required; actual formulas
  and capacity remain open. An entitlement cannot disappear to discipline an agency.
- The cash-buffer floor could protect lenders at residents' expense. It is a declared
  comparison parameter, not a constitutional priority. Changing it requires a reasoned
  financing and resilience assessment; the model does not authorize deprivation.
- Three years, annual cash and six broad sectors hide long-term pensions, project
  lead times, geographic exclusion and ecological limits. No national feasibility
  or sustainability claim is made. Additional grants cannot replace missing staff.

The retained design remains revisable on real incidence, exclusion, capacity and
distribution evidence. Dissent was articulated by the author, not independently resolved.

## Authority, transition and affected consumers

Assembly chooses entitlement, taxes and appropriations; cabinet proposes packages;
delivery authorities specify need and implement; treasury pays under law; independent
reviewers determine lawful claims; opposition and the public contest priorities. The
paying office cannot control the only review route or privately redefine eligibility.
Actual appointments, record access, funding and enforcement are not established.

The settlement affects budget and treasury schedules, tax/benefit interaction, local
equalization, health, education, care, housing and income-support schedules. Existing
awards, pensions, public contracts, staff rights, arrears and pending remedies must be
mapped to continuing authorities before actual change. No implied benefit offset,
private-contract cancellation, tax collection or replacement of the legitimate amendment
route occurs here. An experimental reference population has no power to ratify a state.

Compare transition capacity as well as steady-state promises. The scenario assumes
available staff and capital-delivery limits, with costs inside service/capital envelopes;
training pipelines and supply contracts remain unverified. Real phased expansion needs
both legal authority and delivery capacity. Scarcity cannot silently extinguish an
existing claim. Record late delivery and unmet obligations, with lawful correction.
