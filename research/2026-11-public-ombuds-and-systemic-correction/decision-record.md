# Design392 decision. Make recurring administrative failures independently visible and answerable

## Baseline and selected mechanism

[Design391](predecessor-design-391/COUNTRY-DESIGN.md) gives people direct
administrative and judicial challenge, independent resource audit and
a route to proactive redress after a common implementation error is
established. Several chapters mention an ombud without a general
appointment, inquiry, response or follow-through mandate. A person
missing from official intake may be unable to use individual review
or supply the evidence for collective repair.

Select [OM01–OM12](../country/C04/13-public-ombuds-and-systemic-correction.md)
under the [working research method](../FRAMEWORK.md). A free direct
report can support inquiry, alongside independent initiation. Separate
urgent relief, a reasoned response required by law, actual execution
and an elected decision on policy or funds. This office can examine
lawful but unfair practice and propose change. It cannot declare the
practice illegal or choose the public budget on that basis.

## Alternatives, independence and limits

Retain internal complaints and court cases, expanding advice and access.
This avoids overlap and should continue wherever effective. It gives
people strong legal remedies but leaves recurring omissions hard to
detect before an affected person can bring a suitable claim. Transfer
general complaints to the Auditor General instead. That could share
investigative capacity, but concentrates confidential records and
combines financial audit with different fairness and remedy questions.
Give a new ombuds office binding substantive corrective powers as
another rival. That could speed some cases while weakening independent
adjudication and creating a second source of administrative commands.
Prefer bounded records and response authority, competent execution and
ordinary courts for binding relief.

The appointment and parliamentary budget remain vulnerable to shared
party control. Use separate appointments, public source and vote records,
a directly voted budget, finite succession and case access beyond any
one vendor or investigated body. A qualified committee minority can
require a hearing. That procedure still depends on usable records,
funded clerk time and an actual sitting. Publish a missed hearing
without treating it as agreement. Courts review abusive demands and
the office's procedural failures. Audit examines its funds without
choosing its cases. No investigator receives automatic access to
privileged or unrelated private material. Exact terms and clocks are
proposal choices requiring independent review.

## Evidence, migration and next action

[Primary comparators](sources.md) describe real ombuds powers and
normative safeguards within different institutional settings. The
[constructed comparison](../settlement/public-ombuds-and-systemic-correction/01-hidden-intake-and-a-missed-care-deadline.md)
separates omitted intake, a care deadline, ring-fenced funds, required
answers and remedy. It establishes neither actual participation nor the
cost, independence or outcomes of this proposed office.

Enact prospectively with a qualified appointee, costed intake and
investigative work, independently controlled records and actual access
to existing service, audit, elected and judicial channels. Preserve
original rights, agency duties, direct appeals, pending investigations
and stronger sector protections. Do not merge testimony into an
unrestricted claimant file. The Assembly can revise the mandate by
law and replace leadership on lawful expiry. This research exercises
no governing power.

Next trace one observed administrative intake failure across agency
and service records. Compare time and cost to prompt individual
protection under internal review, independent inquiry and a directly
supported court route. Include people omitted from the agency's
initial count and their evidence, with consent and safe access.
Reopen on repeated nonresponse, blocked resources, overlapping
investigations, safety harm or superior timely alternatives.
