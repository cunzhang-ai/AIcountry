# From a workable trip to a service the person can actually use

A combined fare simplifies ordinary travel. A later refund may repair an
expense. Neither proves that a person reaches a service before it becomes
unavailable. Compare three actual arrangements rather than giving all
journeys the same public obligation.

| Alternative | Where it helps | Where it can fail |
|---|---|---|
| Retain integrated fare, operator replacement and refund, plus better public routes | Low complexity where alternatives are plentiful and accessible | A rider may need to advance cash or coordinate with a second operator. A late journey remains too late for some duties. |
| Confirmed journey with a funded owner and a separate service duty, selected | Keeps practical responsibility through a handoff. Lets ordinary improvements stay with the existing transport network. | Standby and trained receiving cost real money. Common vendors or inaccessible backups can still defeat the plan. |
| Transfer all dispatch, carriage and destination responsibility to one public body | A common budget and dispatch might reduce disputes | It concentrates service and political control and cannot replace clinical, judicial, local or election jurisdiction. |
| Move the service closer or provide a valid equivalent in place | Can remove a fragile journey and improve daily access | Some clinical, social, civic or personal purposes need a particular site, person or physical presence. The equivalent must be lawful, accepted and staffed. |

Retain the observed Copenhagen authorizations as the comparison baseline.
Select the bounded end-to-end duty for protected and expressly confirmed
journeys. Keep independently arranged trips and ordinary commercial access
under their own terms and law. Decide the case by actual transport and
service receipt, with separate authorization, staffing and funding findings.
