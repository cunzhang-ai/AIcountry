figures in **billion DKK at 2026 prices**, with a technical preliminary
2030 basis. They describe a defined central budget room before and after
specified choices. They do not represent general-government net lending,
an appropriation, dated cash, verified receipt or the full cost of maintaining
public services. They cannot be joined arithmetically with the 2023 WF

## Why constant real expenditure is a different counterfactual

The same plan's appendix estimates the aggregate increase in the
mechanical demographic pressure at about 11 billion DKK for 2027–2030
and 24 billion for 2027–2035. Those are national public consumption
estimates, distinct from the local and regional provisions above.
Over the longer period the published spending pressure for health
is about +18.25 billion and for education −2.5 billion, among other
changes. These are modeled differences in spending pressure with
demographic change, not appropriations, staffing estimates or a
claim that a school place can be withdrawn without harm. The model
uses 2024 expenditure patterns adjusted to the 2026 level and
population projections. Actual future need, unmet care, geography
and relative costs may differ. Tables and text come from one
ministry publication and do not independently confirm each other.
[DK2035 appendix, tables A.1.1 and A.1.2](https://fm.dk/media/ztvlsc22/dk2035-et-staerkt-danmark-i-en-usikker-verden_web-a.pdf#page=93)

The design use is to preserve the continuation and new-priority layers
and test the work and rights behind them. Reusing the apparent fiscal
margin alone would obscure which existing services and future
priorities already claim it. The Danish plan is a strong institutional
comparator because it already publishes that distinction.
