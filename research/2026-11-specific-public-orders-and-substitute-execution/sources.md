# Evidence for specific public orders and substitute execution

| Primary source | Observation and scope | Design use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [European Court of Human Rights, Hornsby v. Greece, 19 March 1997](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=002-9018) | The Court's summary describes administrative refusal followed by non-response after judgments set aside earlier licensing decisions. The Court treated execution as part of the protection afforded by Article 6 in the case. | Distinguish a judgment from actual public compliance. The judgment does not supply the proposed appointment, fiscal or service mechanism. |
| [U.S. Courts, Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, December 2023 edition, Rule 70(a)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/civil_federal_rules_pamphlet_dec_1_2023.pdf) | The published rule permits a court to appoint another person to perform a specific act after noncompliance with a judgment within the time specified. | A specific task and a whole institution can have different executors. This is a dated procedural example. It does not authorize government substitution, allocation of public money or activation before a final service deadline under the proposed rules. |
| [Design268 evidence on independent remedy execution](../settlement/independent-remedy-execution/01-jordans-principle-order-and-execution.md) | The previous source comparison distinguishes an issued order, accepted processing and actual delivery. | Retain the independent execution reserve and its previous evidentiary scope. The new case tests a distinct legal act beyond record and payment access. |

The [worked comparison](../settlement/specific-public-orders/README.md)
adds authored hours, cases, staff and cash. It does not establish a
new power, actual readiness or the effectiveness of any remedy.
No party to either case participated in this design.
