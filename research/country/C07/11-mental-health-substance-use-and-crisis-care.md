# C07. Mental health, substance use and crisis care

*Part of [C07 social floor](../C07-social-floor.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The reference country treats mental distress and harmful substance use as
health and social concerns, not evidence of inferior citizenship. Neither
diagnosis nor service contact removes legal capacity, a vote, housing, work,
parental status or ordinary due process. The Assembly enacts an essential
care floor with affected-person participation, including people with
psychosocial disabilities and people who use drugs. C07's health purchaser
funds a reachable network of primary care, voluntary community support,
trained peer services, psychological and medical treatment, specialist
care, crisis response and continuing recovery support. People can seek
help directly, through a trusted person or by assisted non-digital contact
without a police referral or formal diagnosis as the first gate.

Care has different pathways for children, adolescents, adults and older
people, and for acute distress, long-term conditions and substance-use
needs. A first receiver records a request, offers an immediate safe response
where indicated and remains responsible until an accepting service takes
over. The person receives understandable options and supported consent;
a local team cannot count a voicemail, risk score or closed referral as
delivered care. An interpreter, accessible communication, travel and a
safe location are part of reach. Person-chosen peer or community support
may complement clinical care; it is not a compulsory substitute for an
unavailable qualified professional or unpaid family labor.

Health professionals offer evidence-reviewed psychological, social and
medical care with attention to benefit, adverse effects and alternatives.
Drug-use treatment and harm reduction, where enacted, need an accepting
clinical and community route, safe medicines and a way to return after
relapse without losing housing or legal standing. People likely to witness
an opioid overdose can receive lawful access and training for emergency
naloxone, backed by emergency clinical care. An overdose report or request
for treatment is not itself a clinical basis for punishment. The specific
law on possession or supply remains a distinct elected criminal-policy
choice; it cannot be decided by a health-service database or provider.

Distress may be driven by violence, exclusion, unsafe housing, debt,
workplace harm, isolation or physical illness. The health network can
arrange voluntary links to C05 work and income protection, C07 housing
and care, C08 safety and legal assistance, and schools where relevant.
It cannot condition the person's lawful benefit, tenancy or release from
custody on agreeing with a treatment plan. A confidential record stays
with the lawful provider; any sharing for immediate safety or continuity
needs a specific legal ground, minimum necessary scope, notice where safe,
retention limit and correction route. C11 may preserve de-identified
methods and outcomes without building a police-searchable distress list.

For a crisis, the first option is reachable voluntary de-escalation,
clinical assessment, peer support if wanted, a safe place and an agreed
follow-up. A mobile civilian clinical team and an accepting short-stay
service are funded; police respond to a separate concrete safety duty,
not merely a diagnosis or a refused appointment. [C08's liberty
rule](../C08/09-mental-health-crisis-liberty-and-consent.md) separates
immediate rescue, medical emergency care, ordinary crime, and any coercive
act. A crisis contact cannot become an indefinite civil commitment or
automatic forced medication. Discharge requires a real receiver, housing,
medicines and a way to return; an empty bed does not justify sending a
person into unsafe isolation.

The public account reports help requested and received, waits, voluntary
choice, relapse and re-entry, adverse treatment effects, crisis harm,
restraint and police involvement, housing/work outcomes and actual
complaint repair by place and group. An apparent fall in demand may mean
fear or exclusion. AI may help with translation, scheduling or contestable
pattern analysis under consent and data limits; it does not label a person
dangerous, authorize force or make an unattended denial. No actual public,
service map, evidence of local needs, law, staff, funding or outcome exists.
Reopen if voluntary care is unreachable, disclosure causes punishment,
coercion hides in provider contracts or people reject the proposed support
model in legitimate participation.
