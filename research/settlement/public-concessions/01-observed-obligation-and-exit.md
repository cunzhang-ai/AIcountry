# What contract, default and fiscal reports establish

## Annual charges can continue for several governments

The UK National Audit Office's January 2018 PFI and PF2 briefing
reported over 700 operating contracts, about £60 billion in
capital value, £10.3 billion in annual charges for 2016–17 and
£199 billion in future charges extending into the 2040s.
These are the audit's dated programme measures. The future
charges are a payment stream including services and financing.
They cannot be compared with capital value as if the difference
were an overpayment. The briefing gives no project-level
counterfactual and draws no verdict on the whole model.
[NAO PFI and PF2 briefing](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/pfi-and-pf2/).

## Investors can lose money while the public loses useful time

After Carillion went into liquidation in January 2018, work
stopped on two UK hospital projects under private finance.
The NAO's 2020 investigation reported losses of at least
£603 million to private parties across the two construction
projects and substantial delays. At Royal Liverpool the
department paid £42 million to terminate the agreement.
The NAO found that a better estimate of remaining construction
cost might have reduced the lender payment to zero under
the same formula. The compensation was a payment. The
later completion-cost estimates and the zero counterfactual
are different evidence. This is one failure episode and
does not establish what all projects would cost under
direct public delivery.
[NAO hospital investigation](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/investigation-into-the-rescue-of-carillions-pfi-hospital-contracts/).
[Investigation findings](https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/investigation-into-the-rescue-of-carillions-pfi-hospital-contracts/).

The separate 2020 NAO expiry review found that roughly
35% of responding authorities reported insufficient access
rights to monitor maintenance adequately. Its survey is
evidence of weaknesses among respondents, not a census of
all contracts or an estimate of every asset's condition.
These UK publications share an institutional source and
do not count as independent replications.
[NAO expiry review, summary paragraphs 10–12](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Managing-PFI-assets-and-services-as-contracts-end-Summary.pdf).

## A contingent obligation can have a concrete payment date

Chile's Budget Directorate 2025 contingent liability report
describes minimum-income guarantees in certain public works
concessions. For its earlier contract group, an annual
shortfall below the contracted minimum could be paid in
the following calendar year, with revenue-sharing above
a separate upper band. The report distinguishes the terms
of different groups. It illustrates why the exact trigger,
recipient, cap and next payment date matter. It neither
transfers the terms into this country nor shows that the
same guarantee is desirable.
[DIPRES 2025 report, section III.2](https://www.dipres.gob.cl/598/articles-400549_doc_pdf.pdf).

[IMF and World Bank PFRAM](https://infrastructuregovern.imf.org/content/PIMA/Home/PPPs-and-PFRAM.html)
provides a structured way to analyze fiscal risks. Its output
is an estimate conditional on inputs. PL keeps lawful
exposure, due-date cash, political authorization and actual
service acceptance independently visible.
