# Design341 decision on funding noncommercial duties of public enterprises

## Baseline and choice

Pin Design340, OE20, democratic appropriations, local equalization,
ordinary enterprise distress, PL and the working framework. Public
enterprises already account separately for commercial operations and
funded noncommercial duties. The gap is the response when an elected
service promise, a compensation entitlement, cash and actual provision
diverge.

Select a recorded service mandate with a named sponsor and payer,
efficient attributable net cost, published treatment of cross-subsidies,
separate commercial exposure and a useful-time continuity route. Elected
bodies retain service and budget choices. Managers retain professional
authority. Users and workers have standing through existing routes.

## Alternatives and objections

Commercial service with individual assistance preserves ordinary price
signals. It may leave thin routes with too little revenue to operate.
A disclosed tariff cross-subsidy can support access with less budget
administration. It can burden other low-income users or make quality
decline hard to trace. Direct public provision makes the budget link
clear, with different operational, competition and transition costs.
Keep each rival live where it delivers the same usable service.

Separate sponsorship and ownership, paid cost verification and an
explicit response to shortfalls. The [primary comparators](sources.md)
inform these choices. They leave local service cost, lawful fiscal powers
and political discipline open. The two-working-day notice and 20-day
elected disposition are proposed standards. Verification can be costly,
benchmarks can entrench incumbents and a bridge can encourage serial
underfunding. Report these effects and keep genuine alternatives available.

## Adoption, transition and reopening

Use prospective law and funded migration for existing service mandates.
Keep current legal claims, service continuity, independent sector
authority and accrued obligations. The design is assistant-authored.
Independent qualified review, affected participation, adoption and actual
results remain open.

Test a shared-budget window with users of a sparse route and a second
essential service, workers, an independently audited payer and the
competent elected bodies. Compare actual receipt, burden on other users,
unpaid invoices, commercial solvency and maintenance. Reopen on excessive
administrative cost, systematic underpayment or overpayment, fiscal
concealment, failed service or a simpler funded arrangement.
