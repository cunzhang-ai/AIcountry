# Two load windows and an exit

*Design374 · Published demand connection evidence and a separate illustrative comparison.*

## Observed contracted offers

Ofgem reports that total contracted offers in Great Britain's demand
connection queue rose from 41 GW in November 2024 to 125 GW in June
2025. The difference is 84 GW, about 205% of the initial measure.
These figures concern contracted offers, not metered peak consumption,
unique viable projects, generation capacity or proof of necessary
investment. Ofgem separately proposed commitment fees and readiness
milestones for data centres. An Ohio tariff adopted in 2025 provides
a distinct comparison using minimum demand charges, collateral and
early exit terms. Neither example establishes an optimal tariff or
demonstrated cost or reliability effects in this country.

## Staged acceptance on common constraints

In the constructed network, ordinary capacity is 14 units and
capacity under the studied joint stress is ten. Existing protected
services need six in each window. One developer requests eight units
of firm supply and lists two mutually exclusive sites. The example
compares one site with an actual four-unit first phase. The two
applications do not establish two funded simultaneous projects.
All units below are illustrative and the three resource accounts
are distinct.

| Path | Result on the same needs and dates |
|---|---|
| Full eight-unit firm offer | In the stressed window the protected six plus the optional eight require 14 against ten available. A promise of investment cannot close the four-unit physical gap. |
| Four-unit first phase | The protected six and the firm four fit in both windows. The phase needs two units of water where three remain after protected uses. |
| Four firm plus four voluntary flexible units | Ordinary power fits. Initial combined water demand of four exceeds the residual three. A qualified lower-water plan using three fixes that window only. With two extra units of recovery load, the next stressed window needs 12, above its ten. A separately verified twelve-unit recovery window can fit. |

The full build would commit twelve cost units. Three of alternative
use are collectible by the exposure date, with eight units of
qualified security. One remains uncovered. A distinct first-stage
plan commits seven, with one of timely funded reuse and six of
enforceable security available by that date. Its attributable exposure
is covered. If the reuse never qualifies, the same security leaves
one uncovered. If the security is only an unfunded group promise,
it leaves six uncovered. Neither expected later use nor an unsigned
offer fills the current cash gap.

Under the stated evidence, the first phase is the eligible investment
choice. A later flexible stage can qualify only when water, recovery,
actual acceptance and the corresponding risk source qualify together.
The test assumes prior household duties continue. It supplies no
actual engineering rating, tariff, funded project or approval.
