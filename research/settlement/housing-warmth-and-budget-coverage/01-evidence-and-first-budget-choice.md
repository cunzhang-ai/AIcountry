# Housing and warmth under the same first-budget constraint

*Design371 · EU-SILC 2024 cross-sections and a constructed capacity comparison.*

| 2024 observation or derived interval | Denmark | EU-27 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Population below 60% of median equivalised disposable income | 11.6% | 16.2% |
| Housing-cost overburden in the population | 14.6% | 8.2% |
| Unable to keep the home adequately warm in the population | 4.4% | 9.2% |
| Share of people with housing-cost overburden above the income threshold | 46.95–47.73% | 38.06–39.57% |
| Share of people unable to keep the home warm above the income threshold | 78.42–81.63% | 64.19–65.53% |
| Population share with either observed problem, allowing unknown overlap | 14.55–19.04% | 10.92–17.45% |

The reported rates are official published point estimates. The intervals
are arithmetic bounds from the published rates assuming common
population scope, allowing for rounding and any feasible overlap.
They are not sampling confidence intervals. Housing-cost overburden
uses household costs relative to disposable household income, both net
of housing allowances. The warmth indicator is self-reported inability
to afford adequate warmth. The poverty measure uses the applicable
relative income threshold. Survey reference periods are as published.
The tables cover private households and do not tell whether each resident
controls the measured income or has a home suitable for their needs.

For each income group, the share with either problem is at least the
larger of its two rates and at most their sum or 100%, whichever is
smaller. Weight the two groups and intersect with feasible published
totals. The number above the poverty threshold is reconstructed by
weighting the relevant conditional rate. The calculation allows
rounding to one decimal percentage point and rounds reported bounds
outward. It never multiplies the two risk rates as if independent.

**What this changes.** A poverty-only entry rule would miss about
47% of people in Denmark with the housing-cost indicator and roughly
four fifths of people with the warmth indicator, even if everyone
below the line could use the service. This is a mechanical comparison
with the statistical threshold. It does not estimate exclusions from
any actual programme or identify residents whose housing is unsafe.

**One common constraint.** Take an illustrative first intake capacity
of 1,600 people per 10,000, assuming each person with either indicator
would use one intake slot. Denmark's compatible range corresponds to
1,455–1,904 people. The low case fits, while the high case leaves 304
people beyond capacity. This is a person-based stress comparison,
not a count of households, payments, repairs or places. It assumes
neither observed take-up nor that serving one person resolves both
problems. Reproduce it with the same documented limits before
claiming full coverage.

Retaining an income-only intake is easy to staff and misses evidenced
exposure. A joined intake with separate decisions expands reach but
needs paid assessment and real capacity for each service. Long-run
retrofit, secure tenure and net affordable supply address different
sources of pressure and require separate capital and staffing. In
the illustrative cap, retain due duties, use a paid qualified bounded
route or contingency and hold claims of full new coverage. The
published evidence selects wider entry on actual need and rules out
assuming one common caseload. It cannot choose a benefit rate, repair
portfolio, national source or real launch.
