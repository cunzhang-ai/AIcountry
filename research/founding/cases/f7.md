# F7. Housing debt and nonbank funding fail with payments

*Part of [Whole-country stress](../../FOUNDING-AND-STRESS.md); integrated design 55 research, not a real-world result.*

**Events:** Property values fall, a leveraged nonbank loses short-term funding,
a connected bank faces withdrawals, and a major payment operator fails while
rent, wages and benefits are due. A lender proposes immediate foreclosure;
another asks for central-bank support against securities of uncertain value.

**Candidate response:** The monetary authority does not equate missing cash
with solvency. Prudential and nonbank supervisors preserve original exposure
records and use only enacted tools; a nonviable bank enters the separate
resolution and depositor-protection route. Alternate payment channels preserve
instructions and evidence: a legally settled tax is not collected twice,
while unsettled wages, rent and benefits retain their due dates and lawful
defenses rather than being marked paid by the outage record. Borrowers and
creditors can seek lawful restructuring, court review and housing continuity,
without an automatic debt cancellation or mass repossession. Treasury reports
any proposed guarantee or relief as a
costed Assembly choice, not a council or receiver's emergency power.
**What remains unproved:** actual loss distribution, protected balances,
cross-border funding, court and workout capacity, alternate operator and
cash/foreign reserves must be tested together. The same public fund cannot
cover deposits, asset prices, housing relief and essential imports by being
entered four times in separate plans.
