# National decisions can reach residents before the franchise does

*Design318 · Comparative institutions and a constructed hearing.*

New Zealand's Electoral Commission describes national enrolment
for citizens and residents for electoral purposes aged 18 or older,
with at least 12 continuous months of residence at some time.
Its resident category has separate legal requirements, including
lawful presence and generally no requirement to leave at a fixed
time. This is a substantive earlier national-franchise comparator
for qualifying people, with a different affected population.
It does not establish a short waiting rule for all residents.
[New Zealand Electoral Commission eligibility](https://vote.nz/enrolling/get-ready-to-enrol/are-you-eligible-to-enrol-and-vote/)

The Scottish Parliament takes qualifying petitions from people
without residence or age requirements. A qualifying petition
can reach a committee without collecting any minimum number of
signatures. It has its own subject, information and committee
rules. This illustrates voice beyond the electorate. It does
not require a hearing before every bill or confer a vote.
[Scottish Parliament petitions](https://www.parliament.scot/get-involved/petitions/about-petitions)

## Same bill, same ordinary decision date

An authored national bill changes the notice period before a
covered work-status transition from 30 to 7 days. It is an
ordinary bill and the scheduled final vote remains 12 days away.
Two affected adults are noncitizens. One has eight months of
habitual residence and a local electoral home. The other has
recently arrived and has disputed status. Neither qualifies to vote nationally under
the stipulated circumstances and the Design318 research reference.
Neither adult's case is decided in this hearing.

They raise distinct issues. One needs enough notice to arrange
a lawful transition. The other says the notice may reach an
intermediary without reaching the person. Both need interpreting
support in the same protected window, and the same interpreter
cannot cover both sessions simultaneously. One separate committee
response slot is funded. Available capacity and dates are authored.

| Approach | Before the elected decision |
| --- | --- |
| Require national electors to qualify an ordinary initiative or rely on a sponsor | These two people cannot themselves supply qualifying national-elector support. A member could still act. Timely consideration is unproved. |
| Provide one simultaneous interpreting slot and report two invitees | One materially distinct account can be heard through the planned channel. Do not report the other as considered. Seek a paid compatible alternate or expose the gap. |
| Provide two independent slots and the existing funded response | Both reasons can be considered. Representatives can retain the bill, change the notice trigger or period, or reject it, with public reasons. |
| Publish the second account only after the vote | Preserve the record and review. The late response does not recreate influence on the original decision. |

A supported written account or a genuinely earlier separate
session can cover the same material reason if the person can
use it and correct its meaning. Formal evidence of attendance
is insufficient. Retain safe ordinary and urgent legal routes
and affected people outside the planned sessions. The test
makes no claim about the merits of the bill. The national
residence threshold remains a contestable choice. A shorter
franchise route would change electoral power and needs a separate
legitimate constitutional comparison. A paid hearing offers
affected input within the present proposed franchise.
