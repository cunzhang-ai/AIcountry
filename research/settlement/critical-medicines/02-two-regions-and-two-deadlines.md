# Two regions, two medicines and two deadlines

*Authored counterfactual. Products A and B and treatment units are
abstract placeholders. The case sets no clinical dose, substitution
rule, safety window or actual readiness claim.*

## Match demand to lawful usable units and reachable places

At hour 0 two regions identify a common supply interruption. A
delivery of 18 units of A has not arrived and is unavailable
for the next two windows. Ten units already in the North are
quarantined pending separate quality review. North has 20 other
units of A lawfully usable. South has 30. The independent central
reserve holds 24 usable units, of which four are protected until
the end of the first response window for a separate possible call.
Assume the only route can get eight of the available 20 units
to North and 12 to South by hour 24. Each stated treatment uses one abstract unit of its product. All
counts are net of other existing commitments and safety and expiry limits.

| Place | Due treatment using A by hour 24 | Usable A before reserve | Reserve arriving in time | Reachable A by hour 24 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North | 32 | 20 | 8 | 28 |
| South | 38 | 30 | 12 | 42 |
| Total | 70 | 50 | 20 | 70 |

The aggregate 70 matches aggregate demand, yet North has
four gaps. The South's surplus cannot reach North in time.
Increasing a central reserve alone does not solve the stated
route cap. If four of its existing units could instead be
lawfully placed in North before the incident, and their
quality, rotation and cost were verified, the same amount
would cover this first window. South would still receive
eight from the centre and meet its 38. This alternative
makes no claim about replenishment after the first window.

## Use substitutes and transfers only where the patient can receive them

North has 14 usable units of B before the same deadline.
Twelve already belong to due care for other people. Under the
stated professional decisions, three of North's four remaining
patients could use B. Two of them choose it and safely receive
it, using all the spare units of B. The third prefers to retain
A. That patient agrees to a separately paid and safely received
transfer to South. South treats 39 people using A, including
the transferred patient. The fourth patient has no safe route
by hour 24. The original team retains the case and seeks
independent useful-time correction. A proposed substitute does
not cancel an existing claim.

The first window therefore has 67 people treated with A,
two with B and one uncovered in the original group of 70.
The 12 existing users of B are protected. No quarantined or
unreceived units earn credit. An allocation of B to all four
would exceed its residual capacity even if each person could
safely use and accept it.

## Recheck the second window

At hour 24, three unused units of A remain in South. Assume
the earlier four protected units become available then because
their separate contingency has cleared and release is verified.
There are 12 newly due uses of A by hour 48, excluding the
earlier uncovered case. No other new usable stock has a
confirmed receiving route before that deadline. Even with
ideal onward transport, there is a new gap of at least five
units. The earlier patient's claim is additional and still
requires individualized follow-up. Published counts must not
turn it into an interchangeable order.

The next dispatch decision precedes the earliest safe start
for each actual route. A longer outage calls for the separately
funded alternate, received replenishment or lawful scarcity
correction. A warehouse confirmation or national alert ending
does not establish local recovery.
