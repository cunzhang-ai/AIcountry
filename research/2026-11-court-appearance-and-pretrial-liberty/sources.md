# Court appearance and pretrial liberty · primary evidence

1. Fishbane, Ouss and Shah, “Behavioral nudges reduce failure to appear
   for court,” *Science* 370 (2020), sections on the two field studies
   and supplementary Table S5. The New York City low-level summons
   study estimated a 6.2 percentage point reduction after a redesigned
   form against 47% in its estimation bandwidth. In a separate randomized
   comparison restricted to people who supplied a phone number, texting
   reduced nonappearance by about eight points against 37.9% in the
   control arm. These denominators differ and the effects cannot be added.
   [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abb6591)
   · [Full primary paper](https://www.ideas42.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Behavioral-nudges-reduce-failure-to-appear-for-court_Science.full_.pdf).
2. Chohlas-Wood et al., “Automated reminders reduce incarceration for
   missed court dates: Evidence from a text message experiment,”
   *Science Advances* 11 (2025), Table 2 and methods. Among 5,709 Santa
   Clara public defender clients with a phone number recorded, the
   any-date warrant rate was 20.7% in control and 17.6% in treatment.
   Remand on a missed-date bench warrant was 6.6% and 5.2% respectively,
   a 1.4 point reduction (reported 95% interval 0.2–2.6 points).
   Fifty-seven treatment recipients identified a wrong number when
   opting out. These are assignment effects for the observed sample,
   without evidence about people excluded for lack of a number.
   [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adx7483)
   · [Author-hosted primary paper](https://5harad.com/papers/court-reminders.pdf).

The studies distinguish failures of practical communication from an
explanation based entirely on intent. Neither identifies the reasons
for any individual absence, estimates the effect of the proposed
warrant rule, or establishes safe transport, funded assistance or
effects on witnesses. The constructed docket uses separate inputs.
