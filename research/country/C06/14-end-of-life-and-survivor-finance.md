# C06. End-of-life and survivor finance

*Part of [C06 public finance](../C06-public-finance.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The Assembly funds a serious-illness and palliative-care package in the
ordinary health commitment account: community and specialist teams,
after-hours cover, home visits, interpreters, transport, essential symptom
medicines, pharmacy stock, protected records and timely independent review.
It counts paid care support and respite separately from clinical labor.
No projected saving from shorter treatment or a person's early death is
booked as a reason to deny care or as the package's claimed social benefit.
The account shows unequal rural, child and disability-related costs and
actual service received, not merely accepted referrals.

Death confirmation, civil registration, investigation of suspicious deaths,
safe body storage and transport, inclusive public body-care sites, legal
aid, estate administration and interim survivor support have separate
budgets and accountable offices. A hospital cannot fund its treatment
system by selling access to a body or a donor decision; a funeral provider
cannot be the sole route to a public certificate. Statutory survivor and
dependant entitlements continue while a death record, relationship or
estate is disputed, with reconciliation after lawful judgment. The same
social worker, clinician, vehicle and reviewer cannot be pledged twice.

Emergency and disaster plans count surge death-verification and body-care
capacity, record recovery and family notice alongside rescue, without
turning a missing supply into an unreviewable mass-disposal order. Actual
patients, mortality, medicine supply, staff, land, benefit claims and
costs are unknown. Reopen if funded care is unreachable, a certificate
delay blocks survival income, or cost incentives distort patient choice.
