# Two services and a first independent act

Design367 · Constructed comparison under
[JC33–JC38](../../country/C06/65/08-joint-restoration-and-independent-start.md).

One district loses a utility and its normal logistics support.
In the ordinary recovery plan each must first receive the other's
restored service. Neither is restored at hour 0. The example gives
no technical instructions for operating either system. It stipulates
that qualified operators have accepted a lawful alternate arrangement.

Four early cash units are assigned to two independent temporary
deliveries of water and clinic support by hour 6. Two more pay
for an independently accessible hearing and execution by hour 8.
These six have separate existing authorities and qualified resources.
The elective starting arrangement needs two additional usable
units by hour 4. Its accepting team is independent of both failed
services. It enables utility receiving at hour 7, then logistics
receiving at hour 9. The latter is due by hour 10 in this case.
Any replacement of lawful sources is a separate decision.

| Matched route | Early usable cash | Temporary deliveries and hearing | Utility / logistics receipt |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Independently listed ordinary backups, each awaiting the other | 8 | Both deliveries and the hearing in time | No verified start |
| Independent starting arrangement | 8 | Both deliveries and the hearing in time | Hours 7 / 9 |
| Same arrangement, one unit late | 7 | Both deliveries and the hearing in time | Starting arrangement unavailable by hour 4 |
| Same arrangement, but its team is not independent in fact | 8 | Both deliveries and the hearing in time | No qualified start |

The case selects the second row on its stated evidence. All rows
preserve the six already authorized units. They do not authorize
taking a protected hearing or a temporary delivery to fund restart.
If the logistics deadline is missed, report that actual exposure
and seek lawful protection. Do not count the remaining one unit
as an executable starting arrangement.

The next essential delivery is due by hour 16. It requires
qualified receiving and a separate one-unit replenishment funded
by hour 12. If that unit arrives at hour 13, the first utility and
logistics receipt remains on record but the example has no
qualified next delivery. Its separate obligation remains due.

A structural rival replaces one standing dependency with a
continuously independent service. A qualified version could
recover without the special start, but it needs a real operating
source, staff and separately resilient receiving. Neither its
cost nor a real system's resilience follows from this exercise.
