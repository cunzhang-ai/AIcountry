# Two localities face population change before the next grant is due

*Design302 · Constructed service and transfer comparison. Amounts are units.*

Ridge and Port serve the same enacted accessibility floor under
different local conditions. Each previously had 100 eligible
residents in this service scope. The new estimate has 75 in Ridge
and 140 in Port. The case stipulates accepted need, fixed networks
and representative own-source changes where the estimate is
qualified. Ridge still needs an accessible local route. Port needs
more staffed receiving. Ordinary annual allocations are assessed
on gross efficient cost less legally assigned representative
own-source capacity and other previously attributed recurring receipts.

| Place and assessment | Fixed cost | Variable cost | Representative own-source share | Other attributed receipts | Residual national allocation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Ridge, earlier | 40 | 40 | 30 | 10 | 40 |
| Port, earlier | 30 | 50 | 35 | 5 | 40 |
| Ridge, qualified update | 40 | 30 | 28 | 10 | 32 |
| Port, qualified update | 30 | 70 | 39 | 5 | 56 |

A pure headcount rule applied to the old grants would set Ridge at
30 and Port at 56. Ridge would remain two short over the year.
Port's match on the annual number is incidental. The combined
costed residual rises from 80 to 88. A national source of only 87
leaves a one-unit annual gap requiring an actual source decision.
Cash timing must also fit. The national authority cannot spend
Ridge's restricted local balance on Port's services.

Day 30 gross due service costs 20 in Ridge and 26 in Port. The
already received local and other sources for those costs are 12
and 11. Under the prior lawful schedule, each receives a 10-unit
national installment before day 30. Port is five short. Ridge has
two left for its own authorized work.

| Day 30 policy | Ridge / Port national cash in time | Shortfall for Ridge / Port |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freeze the prior grant installment, without an advance | 10 / 10 | 0 / 5 |
| Scale each old grant by headcount, pay one quarter | 7.5 / 14 | 0.5 / 1 |
| Enact the qualified annual schedule, pay one quarter | 8 / 14 | 0 / 1 |
| Enact the qualified schedule and lawfully retime Port's own installment by one unit | 8 / 15 | 0 / 0 |
| Keep prior undisputed installments while the new series is contested and pay an authorized Port advance | 10 / 15 | 0 / 0 |
| Same contested case with only three usable reserve units | 10 / 13 | 0 / 2 |

The qualified path assumes separately voted annual sources and
an actual retiming within Port's 56-unit annual amount, with the
later duties still funded. Its four installments are 15, 13, 14
and 14. The day 90 second call needs 24 units, with 11 separately
received local and other units, so 13 suffices. If the second
call instead needs 25 and has the same other cash, moving one
unit forward would shift the shortfall to day 90. That version
requires a lawful funded alternative. The contested path pays five before
day 30 under a distinct temporary national authority based on
verified service costs and current cash, even though the new
annual population series cannot yet determine the allocation.
It pays no new amount to Ridge. If the advance arrives on day 31,
Port still has a five-unit gap on its original date. The national
shortfall and actual local receipt remain separate.

If Port later qualifies for the revised quarterly installment of
14, record the 15 already received and the one-unit difference.
A future credit is conditional on actual law and subsequent
protected cash. It is not new money or a correction of lawful
payments already received by residents. If Ridge's shared-service
successor actually takes over five of its fixed cost units in a
later lawful period, its residual then falls by five, assuming
the same other inputs. A proposed handover gives no early saving.

All costs, dates, inputs and cash are assumptions. The check
tests the financing arithmetic and its branches. A funded route
still needs lawful staffing and actual resident receipt.
