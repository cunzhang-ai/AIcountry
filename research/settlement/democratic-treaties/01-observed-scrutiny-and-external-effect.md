# Prior scrutiny and external legal effect are different decisions

The 1969 Vienna Convention distinguishes signature and pre-entry
duties, expression of consent, entry into force and provisional
application. Article 25 sets a default termination mechanism for
provisional application, subject to other terms or agreement. Article
27 addresses internal law and performance, without prejudice to the
narrow issue in article 46. Articles 54, 56, 65 and 70 distinguish
exit grounds, procedure and effects. These are legal distinctions
where the Convention or applicable international law governs. They
do not identify AIcountry's actual future treaty parties or confer
any domestic implementing power.
[United Nations text of the Vienna Convention](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf)

The United Kingdom's CRAG Act 2010 normally requires the relevant
treaty to be laid for 21 joint sitting days. The Commons can oppose
ratification and repeat its opposition if the government invokes
a further statutory period. The Lords' opposition has a different
effect. Section 22 allows an exceptional ministerial route subject
to the Act. The Lords International Agreements Committee reported
that the UK–France Agreement on the Prevention of Dangerous Journeys
was laid on 5 August 2025 after ratification. The committee also
recorded disputes about extension, debate time and the limits of
scrutinizing only implementing legislation. This shows actual use
and contested accountability, rather than a general effectiveness
estimate for the UK's external agreements.
[CRAG Act, Part 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/25/part/2)
· [Committee report, chapter 3](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld5901/ldselect/ldintagr/168/16807.htm)

New Zealand's Cabinet Manual describes parliamentary examination
and a national interest analysis for the instruments it covers. Its
usual practice is to wait until the committee reports or 15 sitting
days have elapsed, whichever is sooner, with a stated urgent exception.
Parliament's explanation distinguishes external binding effect from
domestic implementing law. The committee
examination is a real lighter arrangement. It gives no evidence
for an optimal scrutiny interval or equal control across cases.
[Cabinet Manual](https://www.dpmc.govt.nz/our-business-units/cabinet-office/supporting-work-cabinet/cabinet-manual/7-executive-legislation-and-house/parliamentary-treaty-examination)
· [New Zealand Parliament explanation](https://www3.parliament.nz/mi/visit-and-learn/how-parliament-works/fact-sheets/parliament-s-role-in-international-treaties/)

These examples motivate separate material approval, actual debate,
protected evidence and a lawful fast route. AIcountry's 30-day
ordinary and seven-day expedited limits are candidate elapsed
calendar times, with actual hearing and affirmative votes. They
are not numerical translations of either country's sitting days.
