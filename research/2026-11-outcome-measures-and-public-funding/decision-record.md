# Design542 decision record

*14 November 2026 · Keep consequential funding decisions answerable to actual outcomes*

Pin [Design541](baseline.json), the [working framework](../FRAMEWORK.md),
[PCO22–PCO26](../country/C14/04/07-better-lives-and-country-choices.md),
[PCO32–PCO35](../country/C14/04/09-compare-average-and-worst-served.md)
and [EV](../country/C11/11-evidence-corrections-and-public-reliance.md).
Select [PCO36–PCO40](../country/C14/04/10-outcome-measures-and-public-funding.md)
for measures used in material payments, reductions and prospective releases.
Ordinarily fund indispensable public service on its protected source.
Limit outcome incentives to qualified terms with independent proof and
explicit fallback. Keep existing public evaluation and elected renewal.

The [matched case](../settlement/outcome-measures-and-public-funding/01-when-measured-success-pays.md)
contains assumed costs, cohorts and outcomes. The original audit documents
planned spending patterns under an actual programme. The example tests a
reporting incentive. It offers no causal result or national valuation.
Actual affected-party participation, lawful adoption, review staffing,
and observed benefit remain open.

The main objection is that more verification consumes money and staff that
could improve work directly. An incentive with a clear observable additional
outcome could outperform routine funding. Require proportionate review,
ordinary verified work payments and a separate vote on the next scope.
Keep earned obligations, evidence challenges and original remedies on their
applicable terms. An elected body or contracting authority would need to
set exact conditions and provide the source before prospective effect.
Existing law and project procedure retain their ordinary revision routes.

Use one real programme to compare ordinary payment and correction with
an independently testable incentive on the same cohort and resources.
Reopen on a verified exclusion, unreliable proof, lost delivery, or a
qualified incentive or simpler ordinary rule with better lived outcomes.
