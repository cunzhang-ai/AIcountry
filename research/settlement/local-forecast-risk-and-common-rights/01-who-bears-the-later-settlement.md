# Who bears an elective local budget variance?

*Design533 · Observed budget decision and a constructed comparison.*

## A real decision with effects beyond the deciding council

For the 2027 budget, Copenhagen chose the state-guaranteed basis for local
income tax, grants and equalization. The Finance Committee's September
report compared it with using the municipality's own estimate. The city
approved the recommendation on 1 October 2026. The record says that choosing
the latter basis for 2027 would generate an adjustment in 2030 based on the
realized local inputs. The revised arrangement holds national inputs at the
guaranteed levels for that exercise. A positive aggregate reconciliation
from self-budgeting would also reduce the population-distributed balance
grant across municipalities, including councils choosing the guarantee.
The statute starts the aggregate gain cap at DKK 1.5 billion for 2026
and indexes it from 2027. If the cap binds, the statutory correction is
allocated among participating municipalities with positive amounts. The
wider balance-grant effect is separate. The council report states no
equivalent lower limit on municipal losses from self-budgeting.

The income base sensitivity gives alternatives of −166, −95, −25, +46 and
+117 million under the report's scenarios. These are estimates on the
declared terms. They are neither observed decisions by all municipalities
nor 2030 settlement amounts. The council's guaranteed basis is a defined
financing option. It supplies no evidence that every local public service
is funded or that there is no exposure from the wider grant. The national
design uses the legal and fiscal distinction. It does not transplant the
Danish parameters.

## Compare the legal assignment and the cash date

Assume two equal-population councils, A and B. Each has 10 units of usable
funding and due common services of 10 at the reconciliation cutoff, including
independent payment and review. A can elect a provisional financing basis.
B chooses the common basis. At final settlement the elective difference is
either +4 or −4 for A relative to the common basis, after the same ordinary
equalization. Treat the difference as one settlement between Treasury and A,
with no additional tax receipt assumed. Ordinary changes in assessed need
and capacity are held constant. The examples assume four units of additional
public cash can be set aside under a separately voted source, at a real cost
in foregone work. The location and permitted user of those reserves differ.

| Arrangement | If A is due +4 | If A owes 4 | Who bears the residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep the common basis for both councils | A and B each retain 10. No elective payment arises. | A and B each retain 10. No elective recovery arises. | No elective risk in this fixture. The ordinary fiscal regime still bears actual common-service and tax-base shocks. |
| Elective local result with separately paid cover | A receives 4 from Treasury and B retains 10. Treasury must provide the four on time under the offered settlement terms. | A uses a four-unit local reserve if lawfully funded, and Treasury receives four. B retains 10. | One side or the other pays in each branch. Price and verify the paying side's separate eligible reserves. No cross-council grant deduction. |
| Share the adjustment through an equal grant change | A receives 4 and both grants fall by 2. A has 12 and B has 8 before correction. | A pays 4 and both grants rise by 2. A has 8 and B has 12 before correction. | Treasury's net settlement is zero in each branch. Timely protection at either exposed council needs a separately authorized, actually payable source. A national average conceals the unpaid work. |

For the elective local option, the separate ability to settle four units
must be established for *both possible payers*. A single undifferentiated
four-unit balance does not qualify for both. Where only four public units can
be set aside, a prior law could authorize a single neutral reserve with
mutually exclusive releases and actual legal access by either payer. If the
two payers need separate sources and only one qualifies, retain the common
basis. If two elective budgets can give rise to simultaneous four-unit
payments, the reserve cannot support both. Count the legally permitted gross
debits and payment times, including lawful netting only after its verified
effect. Keep the reserve's displaced work in the vote.

On the stated comparison, retain the common basis unless the optional bid
shows a valuable distinct local choice and a funded two-sided settlement.
Prefer assigning optional variance to the choosing council and the expressly
authorized counterparty over an implicit deduction from B. A jointly funded
pool or an expressly financed national guarantee can win if it distributes
risk at a lower full cost and preserves timely independent service under
correlated losses. Any positive provisional result is a fiscal scenario
until qualified settlement. If a larger loss or delayed transfer threatens
a standing duty, use its existing remedy and applicable paid backstop,
record who bears any remaining loss and keep the original resident claim.
