# Temporary housing, a held insurance payment and the first repair

This constructed comparison exercises
[FR14–FR19](../../country/C09/07/04-insurance-proceeds-and-safe-housing-repair.md).
A household loses safe use of its primary home in a covered disaster.
Its mortgage principal is 80 units. A temporary accessible dwelling
costing six is paid from an enacted public protection source by day 2.
Assume usable notice of supported loss reaches the mortgage creditor
on day 1. The proposed possession pause starts then and expires at
the start of day 31 absent a competent extension.

The qualified repairer has two stages. Preventing further damage
requires 35 by day 8. Making the home usable then requires another
25 by day 20. The insurer pays an established 40 into the lawful
servicer's segregated proceeds account on day 3. Another 20 is
disputed. The accepted safe first-stage plan is independently verified
on day 4, with day 8 the earlier applicable preservation deadline
in this case. The ordinary five-working-day release limit cannot
displace that earlier date.

| Response | First stage | Second stage | Temporary housing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Servicer holds all proceeds pending a complete settlement | 35 short at day 8 | No verified safe return | Six paid under its own duty |
| Release 35 and retain the earmarked balance | Funded at day 8 | Five received and 20 short if the dispute is unresolved | Six paid under its own duty |
| Release the first stage and receive the remaining insurance by day 20 | Funded at day 8 | Twenty-five funded and separately inspected | Continue until verified safe receiving under applicable law |

The qualified first stage reduces further damage. It does not establish
habitability. If the second-stage insurance remains unpaid, an authorized
reconstruction source could separately cover 20 and retain any lawful
same-loss recovery on its own books. No such source is presumed here.
If an advance is actually paid and insurance later settles the same
repair loss, reconcile actual payment once with reasons and review.
The six units spent on temporary housing remain separate from building
repair and the mortgage balance. Missing reconstruction money leaves
a housing duty and a repair gap. A household can still pursue a lawful
move or another funded solution.

The [arithmetic check](verify-disaster-repair.py) verifies stage costs,
paid versus disputed amounts, the initial pause and distinct public aid.
Actual contract terms, cash availability, independent orders, repair
capacity and safe return remain open.
