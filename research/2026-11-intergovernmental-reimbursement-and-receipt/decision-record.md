# Design275 decision record

**Baseline and decision.** Design274 requires cash by authorized
payer and original date. MF and XR keep local duties and disputes
distinct. Choose the first budget's route when a locality owes
residents before another public body reimburses it. Use the
[working method](../FRAMEWORK.md) and [decision handoff](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md).

**Evidence and diagnoses.** Manchester's central grant, local
quarterly net spend, award timing and separate year-end return
provide a matched grant with an expenditure difference still open.
Copenhagen's named payer and former-office balance show how a
claim can remain open through a handoff. These observations
distinguish arrears, disputed liability, incomplete evidence and
reporting differences without establishing resident-level harm.

**Selection and rivals.** Prefer the lawful early undisputed
transfer and a bounded qualified bridge for mandatory work
when local liquidity cannot safely cover it. Reimbursement-only
can preserve stronger spending checks where a locality has an
expressly financed buffer and bearable denial risk. A qualified
central payer could pay residents directly while local officers
serve intake. This may remove one funding hop but risks distance,
loss of contestability or a common payment failure. Compare
all options with the same deadlines, scope, recovery risk and
resident access. The authored case selects a capped bridge and
separately preserves an early shortfall if the other payer fails.

**Authority, objections and migration.** Elected bodies set actual
sources and powers, including the bridge's losses and repayment
limits. Separate public liability review, lawful local choice
and actual resident receipt. A funder can demand unnecessary
records or hold unrelated amounts. A local office can misclassify
a claim or report an award as paid. Give independent reviewers
original, privacy-protected evidence and useful-time remedies.
Preserve original dates and accepted successors. Later disallowance
does not by itself authorize recovery from the resident. No
new authority or actual funded adoption is established.
