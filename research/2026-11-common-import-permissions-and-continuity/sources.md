# Original evidence on shared import conditions

| Primary source | What it establishes | Use and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [WTO Secretariat, Export prohibitions and restrictions, April 2020](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/covid19_e/export_prohibitions_report_e.pdf) | The report mapped pandemic-related export prohibitions or restrictions across 80 countries and customs territories as of 22 April 2020, including on medicines, protective goods and equipment. It explains limitations of notifications and other sources. | Permission to ship can change independently of seller willingness. This historical report does not prove misconduct, current trade rules or an individual contract's remedy. |
| [European Medicines Agency, methodology for critical medicines supply chain vulnerabilities](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/other/methodology-identify-vulnerabilities-supply-chains-critical-medicines_en.pdf) | The methodology separates active ingredient production and finishing, and uses detailed investigation of sites and supplier connections after screening. | Supports testing a shared dependency across apparent suppliers. No index weight, product conclusion, purchase price or recovery estimate is imported. |
| [European Medicines Agency, MSSG supply-chain recommendations](https://www.ema.europa.eu/system/files/documents/other/mssg-recommendations-address-vulnerabilities-supply-chain_en.pdf) | The recommendations address geopolitical risks, trade barriers, upstream supplier choices and use of safety stock where appropriate. | Supports explicit permission and stock assumptions. It does not establish that any specific stock or reserve contract in this project would work. |

The [constructed comparison](../settlement/common-import-permissions/README.md)
uses authored authority, supply, costs and receipt. It establishes no
actual political pressure, licence, ready supplier or patient outcome.
