# DP17–DP24. Public screening, queues and actual decisions

*Part of [C11/09](../09-public-digital-operation-and-continuity.md) · Design210 candidate.*

**DP17 — Assign responsibility for the route.** Each sector authority responsible
for a public function owns the material effects of automated scoring, ranking,
referral, intake filters and routing used by it or its contractor. A material
effect includes a reasonably foreseeable change in timely access, urgency,
the burden of producing proof, exposure to intrusive checks, or the ability to
challenge. Apply this rule to machine learning, deterministic systems and
nominally advisory outputs according to their use. Clerical assistance with no
such effect keeps ordinary controls. The same substantive access duties govern
manual queues. The task owner records the lawful purpose, affected routes,
responsible officers, grounds of priority and the actual alternative.

**DP18 — Protect receipt and the time in which a right is useful.** The ordinary
route preserves first protected receipt under AP and applies the sector's lawful priorities
including urgency, due time and relevant waiting. Preserve accessible intake,
proof assistance and direct urgent correction. A score, duplicate alert, missing
field or inferred identity alone cannot restart a clock, create a legal priority,
raise the burden of proof, or hold due protection. An authorized officer can
act on specific, independently assessable grounds under existing law. Promptly
verify an actionable lead where the reported facts indicate imminent harm and
apply the same lawful urgency rule. The sector must accept a timely lawful provisional route where one exists. When
genuine competing needs exceed capacity, use the existing scarcity process and
record each unmet duty. Neither a new deadline nor an automatic award follows
from the use or removal of a model.

**DP19 — Keep investigative leads separate from findings.** A task owner may
use a qualified referral to direct otherwise available checking time. Intrusive
proof, withholding, adverse findings and sharing for enforcement each need their
own lawful ground. Reviewers inspect the underlying evidence and record the
ground actually relied on. Conceal a ranking label from adjudicators where they
can work fairly from original evidence without it. Where a model output or its
derivation is material to what the state did, preserve it for independent review
and give the affected person enough explanation and lawful access for effective
challenge under AP/PD. Protect sources and investigative details only through
existing limited grounds and review. No portable presumption of suspicion passes
with a flag or with repeated copies of the same underlying observation.

**DP20 — Pay for added work and protect the ordinary route.** Before material
reliance, the sector owner sets service-specific protective limits and shows a
funded plan for ordinary, referred and challenged cases at ordinary and surge
loads. Count review minutes by skill, contact attempts, accessible routes, urgent
receipt, independent challenge and correction. Test simultaneous dependency
failure and confirm who can actually accept each duty in time. Spare capacity
reserved for one duty cannot be counted for another at the same time. Ordinary
ordering may itself use safe deterministic tools. It must work without the
implicated model, derived score and shared source or service where those could
cause the failure. A safe exception needs a named sector officer with power to
limit referrals, reassign paid capacity or seek lawful scarcity action. When
capacity is missing, limit discretionary routing first and report unmet duties.
Pay for staffing under the relevant appropriation. Recompute any claimed saving
against the full comparison before releasing staff or capacity.

**DP21 — Compare effects on everyone who needs the service.** Before reliance
and after material changes, compare the proposed route with a competent ordinary
route using comparable demand, rules and available skills. Count total requests
from first contact, completed and abandoned attempts where safely observable,
unreferred cases, attempted non-digital entry and people whose places were
displaced. Test serious missed cases among unreferred requests with lawful
representative review. Keep referral, corroborated findings, initial and final
dispositions, waiting distribution, missed useful times, avoidable proof and
delivered corrections separate. Publish safe denominators, scope, material
uncertainty and comparison limits. Use only lawful and necessary data, protect
small groups and disclose blind spots without creating a population register.
Material reliance requires a qualified benefit under the sector's lawful aim,
protected access and a funded alternative. Retain the competent ordinary route
where these conditions fail. Test and review new versions and new uses within
DP05–DP08. Pin thresholds, queue rules, review budgets and protective limits
with the version. A model update, expanded scope or staffing cut that can
change useful access needs fresh qualified review before reliance. A tool can
still be used for safe work outside the failed scope.

**DP22 — Act at each sector when a common fault is credible.** Apply DP09's
warning and useful-time rule to a credible material queue or screening incident,
including a shared input, extractor, model or threshold. The DP coordinator
relays minimum necessary dependency and protective information to competent
affected owners. Each owner takes scoped action under DP10 and its own law,
including limiting referrals, isolating the affected rule and opening the
tested alternate. Unaffected uses can continue. Where the common source was
materially relied on, use EV's actual lawful review and update routes. Identify
capacity conflicts before relying on mutual aid. Preserve receipt and valid
decisions through DP12. Restore only the scoped use that meets DP11 with proof
of usable receipt, cleared or accepted backlog, and safe operation under load.
Existing urgent clocks can require action before the DP09 reporting limit.

**DP23 — Correct the treatment actually received.** Under AP, B and sector
authority an accessible urgent or ordinary challenge can reach an independent
reviewer outside the implicated queue, without asking the person to identify
the software. The duty holder finds affected cases from lawful minimum records
and starts with those for whom time-sensitive harm can still be prevented.
Restore original receipt and priority, remove unsupported proof demands and
deliver any due assistance, payment, hearing or other lawful remedy. Account
for action already taken and notify the person through a safe usable channel.
Keep lawful evidence of the original treatment and the correction. The reviewer
can obtain protected queue and workload evidence without depending on the
implicated routing vendor. Where the same defect recurs, use AA/PD inspection
and SR remedies within their own powers.
Record open and failed remedies as such. The original decision maker, funder,
independent reviewer and remedy executor retain separate duties.

**DP24 — Keep authority and migration separate from performance.** An elected
sector law and its appropriations set scope, permitted priorities and powers.
Technical and procurement staff execute within that authority. Independent
reviewers and courts retain their own protected budgets, evidence access and
ordinary jurisdiction. Disclose scope, shared controls and material outcomes
to elected oversight without enabling case direction or release of testimony.
Commence prospectively under DP16 and actual sector rules. Map existing
queues, time promises, appeals, staff and contracts to lawful owners before
switching. Existing rights and urgent protection run through migration. Reopen
on loss of protected access, new affected routes or dependencies, excess review
load, missing independent relief or a better ordinary route.
