# Housing improvement and affordable thermal service

*Part of [TH safe home temperatures](../23-safe-home-temperatures-and-continuity.md).*

**TH05 — Test new buildings for usable protection before accepting
them.** Include heating and overheating in prospective design,
commissioning and major renewal. Prefer practicable exposure
reduction such as shading, ventilation, envelope repair and
thermal insulation where it works safely. Compare the remaining
need for active service and independently test it at credible
coincident loads. Treat secure nighttime openings, air quality
during smoke, accessibility, noise, moisture, fire protection and
resident control as real operating conditions. A design cannot
claim cooling from opening a window when that is unsafe or
impractical. Use a documented remedy and safe interim arrangement
when seasonal commissioning or later verification identifies
a defect. A sale, permit or energy label is not acceptance of
actual protection.

**TH06 — Retrofit the worst exposure without displacing residents.**
Local elected plans select an affordable sequence with a public
test of severity, time exposed, occupant needs, housing access
and feasible avoided peak load. Compare necessary repairs,
envelope improvements, active equipment and replacement against
the same indoor outcome. Reserve urgent work for the households
that cannot wait. The owner remains responsible for existing
repair and maintenance. Elected funding can purchase additional
performance or support hardship where it can verify receipt.
Keep owner, cooperative or common-fund obligations, capital,
repairs, resident assistance and recurring costs distinct.
Require grants and contracts to preserve rent and possession
protections, document verified performance and recover payments
on proved misuse under fair process. Reuse lawful H/RT/CB
repair, escrow and owner-hardship routes. Eligible work grants
or relocation costs do not create a rent surcharge or reset the
tenancy. Publish the tradeoff with waiting-list and rental supply.

**TH07 — Keep the essential service affordable in use.** Determine
the cost of maintaining safe conditions in the actual building,
including standing charges, fuel, routine service and equipment,
and check the household's usable resources. Apply EG essential
electricity protection and the enacted housing and personal
assistance route, including a timely manual route for tenants on
an intermediary meter. Verify that support reached the bill or
actual supply. Prior law protects the essential service from
loss for inability to pay, with separate safe engineering
isolation and owed-payment correction. Avoid counting the
same subsidy in both the electricity and household budgets.
Publish seasonal and date-specific cash, crew, equipment,
electricity peak, fuel, replacement and emissions requirements.
A claimed annual energy saving gives no winter or summer
peak capacity without a matched physical test. Phase in any
equipment retirement only with accepted working replacement
and paid overlap for continuing households.

**TH08 — Where a shared heat system is selected, govern it as a
bounded utility.** Before exclusive or compulsory access is
allowed, compare a shared system with repair and individual
service at the same reliability, total bill and ecological scope.
Use the existing sector regulator and elected siting bodies.
Name the accountable plant, network and seller or single
operator under an enacted charter. Review actual asset condition,
transparent standing and usage charges, open third-party supply
where safe and feasible, service quality, separately funded
essential protection and a lawful receiving operator on failure.
Allow safe voluntary exit or a reasoned statutory limit with
independent review of shared costs. No obligation to connect or
pay exists solely because a plan names a service district.
Give residents a direct route to challenge an outage, a denied
connection or an unaffordable essential bill. Cooling networks
use the same tests where their scope and physical operation
justify them.
