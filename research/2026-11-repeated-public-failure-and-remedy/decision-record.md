# Design197 decision. Execute individual relief and repair a proved common breach

*9 November 2026 · Research selection · Exact design196 predecessor.*

## Gap and selection

Design196 specified how a lawful successor actually receives control.
This edition addresses what those controls must deliver after a
binding judgment. B19 and E15 already require exact execution and
limited alternate record and payment paths. B20 and equality law
allow scoped collective and practice relief. The missing positive
step was a finite method to identify a recurring common breach,
verify funded repair and keep individual relief open.

Retain ordinary individual enforcement. Where necessary for a proven
existing legal duty, the competent court orders a finite repair plan
implemented by the responsible agency. It verifies actual receipt,
deadline and recurrence evidence. Choose 14 calendar days from usable
service for a plan and a 90-calendar-day ceiling on special supervision
with fresh review for any further finite period. Earlier useful-time
protection and hearing limits govern. These are research choices, not
empirical optima or grounds for a new delay. The legislature retains
law and appropriations. The standing justice and sector roles need
lawful funded executors and challenge.

## Real alternatives and objections

An individual-order-only approach keeps the court's role narrow and
can promptly serve particular claimants. It may leave the same
unlawful rule in use. Strong agency repair and legislative audit
avoid judicial administration but can fail to deliver a binding
right. An external operational manager can overcome entrenched
refusal where law permits, with serious accountability and capture
costs. Choose a court-checked plan and scoped independent evidence,
while the agency remains the executor. Do not add a supplier or
personal fault power by analogy to the foreign cases.

The strongest objections are judicial overreach, managerial defense
of low standards, costs of monitoring and the risk that a claimant
committee excludes the hardest cases. A written necessity finding,
independent appointment and challenge, protected ordinary intake,
explicit elected funding choices and the finite review interval
address these risks. They do not guarantee honest data or real
capacity. Record evidence of common cause and actual receipt,
including material objections. A court may require an effective
lawful remedy without making policy or cash available. A missed
judgment cannot be reclassified as compliance by narrowing the
metric. Protect private testimony and source evidence.
