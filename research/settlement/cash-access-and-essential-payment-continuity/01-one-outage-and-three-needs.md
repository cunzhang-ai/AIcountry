# One outage and three essential needs

*Design391 · Constructed case using abstract monetary units.*

On day 1, a district loses ordinary payment communications. Food and
medicine are in stock at reachable staffed sites. Three residents
face different barriers.

| Resident | Need and accepted route | Day 1 commitment |
|---|---|---|
| A | Buys food with cash at a shop with suitable change | A pays 4 from their own money |
| B | Buys medicine using a qualified offline card terminal with independent power | Card agreement reserves 3 of private settlement capacity |
| C | Has no usable offline card or cash and needs a qualifying prescription before the next morning | Clinical owner uses its voted merchant commitment of 5 from a public provision of 9 |

No route borrows another route's funding. Day 1 leaves 4 of public
provision. C tried to pay through an ordinary online channel, but the
instruction never reached acceptance. C's merchant is owed one payment of 5 under the public
commitment after proving fulfillment. Any replay of the old attempt
is held for reconciliation without a second charge to C.

On day 2, a local power outage outlasts the terminal's backup.
The pharmacy retains separate power for safe dispensing. Cash still
works for A, but B needs a second
prescription and has no cash. If B qualifies and the outlet accepts
a funded public commitment of 3, only 1 of public provision remains.
C's next qualifying prescription costs 5. The two prescriptions
require 8 of actual public capacity and the prior day left 4.
If no new source accepts in time, there is a combined shortfall of
4. The calculation establishes neither a priority between patients
nor who can safely wait.

If the merchant can no longer dispense safely, additional money
alone cannot meet either prescription. Test an alternate pharmacy,
qualified worker, records and actual patient receipt. If the public
payer can add 4 only after the necessary window, record the missed
need as a failure and keep any continuing duty and remedy open.
Restoration of ordinary payment links does not cancel an accepted
public commitment. This comparison does not price an actual network
or establish coverage for any district.
