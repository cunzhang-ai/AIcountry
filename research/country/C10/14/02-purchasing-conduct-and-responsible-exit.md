# BC06–BC10: change harmful buying, not merely the supplier's paperwork

*Part of [C10/14](../14-cross-border-buying-and-responsibility.md); design187 candidate.*

**BC06 — Prevent and stop the buyer's own contribution.** A buyer must take
reasonable feasible measures to prevent/cease its own conduct creating or
materially contributing to BC02 harm. Examine actual prices, deadlines,
uncompensated order/specification changes, payment withholding, recruitment
conditions and imposed production methods. A knowingly impossible safe/lawful
production schedule cannot be cured by requiring a supplier to sign a code.
Reasonably adjust price, volume, timing, payment or design, provide attributable
support, or stop the harmful act. No entitlement to an unlawfully cheap order;
no duty to continue an unviable business indefinitely or pay any demanded price.
Lawful collective bargaining and genuine safe lower-cost methods remain possible.

**BC07 — Repair attributable harm with ordinary causal proof.** An affected
person may recover for a proved BC duty breach materially causing/contributing
to actual loss, with B09–B12 causation, defenses, joint responsibility and
no double recovery. B11 full indivisible loss where its predicates are met is
not replaced by a convenient buyer-share cap. A reasonable preventable failure
to respond to linked harm may itself causally contribute; a label does not
immunize subsequent conduct. An adverse outcome, low price, audit failure or
commercial link alone proves neither breach nor causation. No automatic buyer
employment status, all-chain wage guarantee or liability for unrelated harms.
The actual employer and other wrongdoers retain their debts and duties.

**BC08 — Linked harm still needs a reasonable response.** Without own causal
contribution, a directly linked credible serious risk requires proportionate
feasible influence: seek specific cessation/repair, change feasible commercial
terms, cooperate lawfully with workers/other buyers, support capability or
consider safer sourcing. Specify a response and meaningful review time matched
to danger; an imminent grave threat cannot wait for a routine business cycle.
Mere formal distance is no defense to available reasonable measures. No strict
promise that a foreign actor obeys, unlimited investigation or duty to finance
all historic supplier liabilities. Assess the actual effect of attempted leverage;
repeated promises without material improvement cannot renew engagement forever.

**BC09 — Exit must not conceal either harm.** Stop the buyer's own harmful
contribution promptly. For remaining linked harm, compare credible improvement,
limited suspension and responsible termination with affected-worker/community
evidence and actual safety needs. Exit planning cannot excuse continuing grave
own contribution; engagement cannot become indefinite delay. Conversely, a
certificate failure is no automatic command to abandon every supplier or country.
Pay actually due accepted work/orders and attributable remedies; retain evidence,
notice consistent with immediate safety, and feasible transition support matched
to responsibility. State what cannot safely continue and who will actually
receive essential supply or remedy. No assurance of all foreign wages, replacement
jobs, consent or overseas enforcement that the buyer cannot provide.

**BC10 — Public buying carries the same duty and its actual source.** SC's
responsible sector executive and procurement staff include BC risks, feasible
safe terms, actual due payments and exit/backup costs in the authorized whole
commitment. Publicly controlled OE buyers cannot outsource responsibility through
a private tier. No lowest-price exception or unilateral unfunded instruction
to a weaker supplier. Independently assigned SC receipt verification, AA audit,
ordinary challenges and actual Treasury/local payers retain their own roles.
An urgent medicine order needs the real rights/safety/backup assessment, not an
invented BC waiver, forced supplier work or an automatic public compensation
promise. Existing due patient/service rights and competent prior-law emergency
powers continue within their own grounds; a sourcing duty creates no medicine.
