# FB31–FB35. Fund prevention across public budgets

*Design279 · Proposed extension to [lasting services and fiscal shocks](../62-lasting-services-and-fiscal-shocks.md).*

When one authority must spend now and residents or other services stand to
benefit later, the responsible elected bodies may jointly fund a defined
service through their ordinary budgets. The value to residents, the change
in use of other services and the cash actually released each need their own
account. Prevention can warrant explicit new spending even if no cash is
released elsewhere.

**FB31 — Identify the shared choice and keep the ordinary routes.** For a
material new proposal across budgets, the lead service owner presents the
harm to prevent, eligible residents, exact change and a funded ordinary
alternative on the same affected scope, dates and rights. Name each
authority's actual remit, the groups likely to gain or bear a cost, people
missing from intake, and risks of shifting a queue, bill or unpaid work.
Compare ordinary single-budget funding where it can do the job. Publish
who can provide evidence, contest the proposal or decline a service. Keep
ordinary benefits, consent, emergency response and independent remedies.
An expected saving gives no authority to triage by predicted cost.

**FB32 — Vote on the gross source before promising a place.** The lead owner
and Treasury submit a dated joint budget schedule under [DB](../60-democratic-budget-and-execution.md).
The Assembly and any competent local elected body authorize their own
maximum contribution, funding source, displaced use and named payer.
Use [MF](../../C04/08-funded-local-mandates-and-equalization.md) for existing
national/local duties and lawful advances. Compare zero offsets and a
plausible joint adverse path. Price gross start, overlap, usable ongoing
capacity, review and already committed tails. Name an accepting delivery
owner, reserve and evidence custodian. A participating authority may fund a
lawful narrower phase on its own authority, within its own resources and
coverage duties, if another authority declines. Bring any materially
changed scope back for an exact decision under T. The compact grants no
new mandate over an unwilling authority or charge on an unvoted budget.

**FB33 — Keep service gains available to residents.** The receiving agency
checks material changes in need and use against the comparison and actual
coverage. Freed operating capacity goes first to due work and independently
verified access gaps. A retained team can serve more people without lowering
payroll. Publish the actual redeployment and its effect separately. A cash
release enters a future budget only after [FB09–FB12](01-underspending-and-released-capacity.md)
qualifies the specific payment lawfully avoided, continuing and replacement
costs, available date and owner. The elected body decides disposition and
any lawful transfer. Count transfers once and do not assign the same shift
to two services. Where evidence points to lost access, fund correction and
keep that failure visible. No provider receives an automatic share of the
gross estimated offset.

**FB34 — Start with an offer people can keep.** For the first-budget housing
reference, select a bounded phased offer of ordinary secure tenancies and
independent, voluntary support under [HL](../../C09/25-housing-loss-and-secure-receiving.md).
Separate lawful priority, resident choice, secure housing and support consent.
Do not condition a tenancy on treatment, participation in evaluation, an
estimated savings score or avoidable service use. Keep first safe-night
receiving and ordinary access independently staffed. A phase opens only
with suitable accepted homes, qualified support, cash before commitments,
funded fallback and applicable recurring sources. A review date can limit
new enrolment. It cannot terminate existing tenancies, accrued claims or
duties under separate law. A failed prerequisite keeps the optional phase
closed and the ordinary route working, with any actual breach exposed.

**FB35 — Review receipt before expanding or reallocating.** Use the first
ordinary budget review after a full operating cycle, with earlier
useful-time correction for safety or access. Report who actually got a
safe home and kept it, service access, exits and reasons, paid and unpaid
burdens, cross-place effects, actual cash and missing coverage. Use
independent budget scrutiny and applicable audit, correction and court
routes. Keep sensitive evidence with lawful custodians and permit refusal
of optional monitoring. Compare the ordinary and expanded offers, including
people unable to accept. AI may help reconcile source and capacity schedules.
Humans remain responsible for findings, offers, decisions and remedies.
Adopt, narrow or stop new commitments by ordinary elected decision with
a priced continuity route for people already served.
