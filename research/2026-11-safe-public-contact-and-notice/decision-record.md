# Design338 decision on safe public contact and usable notice

## Baseline and choice

Pin Design337, AP ordinary receipt and delay, PD data rights, B16–B19
procedure and relief, and the working framework. Existing design protects
usable notice and physical correspondence. It leaves ownership of a
confidential receiving route, proof at a relay and the consequences of failed
delivery underspecified across administration and civil proceedings.

Select a free local public correspondence route with a scoped address or
routing reference, a separate rule for adverse notice clocks, and judicial
management of disputed civil service. The public office holds the delivery
duty. The person controls optional channels and mandates. Courts retain
procedural authority. Legislatures fund and authorize the duties.

## Alternatives and objections

Individual providers can adapt delivery case by case, with low initial
coordination costs and uneven coverage. A national legal mailbox could
standardize receipt, with a concentrated failure and disclosure risk.
Select a common legal duty with several receivers and a staffed physical
route. A single local receiver may be cheaper where it can prove independent
backup and protected staffing. Give the filing party a prompt route for
court directions and preserve lawful urgent orders when proof of ordinary
notice is missing.

The same-day and five-working-day access choices need costing in sparse
areas. Confidential forwarding creates records that can reveal a person
through metadata. A strict usable-notice rule can delay a good-faith claim.
Independent directions, limited disclosure and an auditable record answer
part of those concerns. The [primary comparisons](sources.md) provide
examples of public mail access, substitute addresses and judicial procedure.

## Adoption and reopening

Prospective coordinated legislation must name covered bodies, legal
receivers, payers, competent review and what counts as proof. Keep existing
filing dates, accrued claims, earlier guarantees, source-law limits and
valid notices. Fund alternate access, useful-time court capacity and a paid
handover. This is an assistant-authored design. Qualified independent and
affected review, enactment, cost and actual outcomes are open.

Before expansion, exercise one full review window with willing participants
who have unstable or confidential housing, legal aid, counter staff and
small claimants. Compare time to usable delivery, unsafe disclosures,
missed hearings, disputes over receipt and the burden on both parties.
Reopen if a shared dependency disables nominal alternatives, a provider
exposes location, enforcement advances without meaningful opportunity,
claimants face persistent delay or a simpler design protects both sides.
