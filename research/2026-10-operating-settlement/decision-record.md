New taxpayer exposure or social-floor change needs an Assembly vote. | A domestic currency with bounded monetary authority could supply domestic liquidity but still not imported essentials and could shift losses through inflation or favored credit. | Shared territorial allocation | Local councils decide ordinary lawful matters; a statutory interlocal forum prepares evidence, affected-party alternatives and voluntary agreements; the Assembly decides a **material unresolved** common allocation under prior competence, with payer and executor named. | A permanent elected region may give affected residents more direct control where a stable shared area exists, but no AIcountry geography, electorate or financing currently justifies inventing it. Local unanimity can become a veto, while Assembly reservation can overload national politics and dilute local voices. |

These are not universal optima. The external-currency package, parliamentary
districting, and Assembly-reserved territorial decision are conditional reference
choices open to rejection by an affected lawful public. The selection does not
absorb a domestic issuer's lender power, create a regional electorate by technical
mapping, or give an unelected protector authority to settle an election.

## Joined power, resources and continuity

All three choices depend on the same budget, records and execution capacity.
The Assembly controls general law and new public risk, but standing essential
payments and election/court operations need *actual* usable cash and channels.
Bank resolution cannot spend a service appropriation, and Treasury cannot count
an insurer's fund as its reserve. A failed correspondent or vendor can block both
benefits and a court's practical remedy. Election evidence and remedies therefore
need lawful custody and assisted/offline paths that remain usable during a payment
or identity outage. A court may decide legal entitlement, electoral process or
territorial competence, but cannot issue foreign currency, invent a vote count
or allocate general water use. A material shared-resource choice needs an elected
decision under ecological and rights constraints; national funding and local
service duties continue while that decision is delayed.

One authored combined failure illustrates the dependency: a bank and common
digital provider fail while a national election is contested and drought limits
housing growth in two localities. The receiver may use only its enacted fund and
loss order. Treasury records unpaid claims and protects the separately enacted
essential and electoral schedules within cash actually available. Election
officials and courts preserve original evidence and contested filings without
granting a new term by delay. Local water and housing duties persist; no new
irreversible allocation is approved because the joint forum cannot agree.
Assembly members can debate new funding, a bank bridge and a common resource plan,
but each needs its own lawful vote and reasons. This is a logical stress case,
not an observed simulation or proof of resilience.

The sources in the three analyses are primary institutional materials from BIS,
FSB, IADI, IMF, OSCE, the Venice Commission, International IDEA, UN-Habitat,
UNECE and the Council of Europe. They identify general mechanisms and rights
questions, sometimes drawing on related underlying evidence. They neither
endorse this joint package nor supply an AIcountry balance sheet, electorate or
territory. Material objections remain: bounded insurance may leave vulnerable
uncovered depositors; distributed election custody can expose privacy or local
capture; standing funds can become self-protecting; national resource decisions
can neglect intensely affected places. Reasons and dissent cannot be erased by
averaging those conflicts into a single performance score. Fresh objections and
events require renewed review even if file hashes match.

Design 8 changes C03–C04, C06, C09 and C14. Affected consumers include the
proposed A1–A9 authority chain, founding schedule, election and court law,
essential-obligation and tax rules, bank/deposit/payment contracts, local

The 16 September source manuscript and existing claims are preserved; these

publication permissions. A real transition would need article-level conflicts,
creditor/depositor/property and worker rights, pending cases and permits,
subannual cash, staffed receivers and courts, record custody, alternate operators,
historic claims, funded overlap and independent challenges. New regional powers,
bank loss priorities or election competence cannot self-approve by using their
proposed procedures.

**Evidence qualification:** document comparison, official standards and authored
failure logic only; no original AIcountry observations or independent qualified
review. **Research selection:** the three bounded chains enter the working
reference design. **Legal authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:**
unknown, including foreign clearing, bank assets, reserve cash, available courts,
obedient transfer officers, territorial maps and ecological capacity. **Observed
outcomes:** none. English/link/section checks verify artifact integrity only.

The next bounded real-world choice is whether an affected public in a lawful
jurisdiction wants to compare this reference package with domestic-currency,
different democratic-continuity and elected-regional alternatives. The first
deliverable would be a joint legal-and-control map for one actual adoption context:
who holds cash, bank liabilities, poll records, court access, security command,
land and water claims, and who can replace each holder. Its completion requires
original observations, affected-party challenge, current-law review and a costed
contested-transfer/payment/resource exercise. Without that setting, retain the
package as qualified research rather than pretending a new desk scenario can
establish readiness. Reopen the choices if actual cash/clearing makes the
monetary route impossible, if distributed election safeguards share a controller
or fail a contested handover, or if national resource reservation causes backlog,
exclusion or non-delivery compared with a legitimate regional model.
