# PD01–PD04: people, responsibility and lawful purposes

*Design 96 ordinary-data research candidate; not enacted law, funded readiness or
demonstrated superiority.*

**PD01 — Persons and scope.** A living person has enforceable privacy and
data-protection rights against public, private and cooperative controllers acting within
actual jurisdiction. Personal data are observations, identifiers or inferences about an
identified or reasonably identifiable person, including linked or pseudonymous records,
voice, image, location, neural/biometric signals and consequential scores. Apply to
organized or materially consequential collection, inference, use, disclosure, sale,
retention or erasure in paper, speech, devices and future media; a format change or
model copy does not remove the duty. Anonymous aggregate data fall outside only when
reidentification is not reasonably feasible with likely means. Purely personal/household
interaction is outside this ordinary controller regime, but separate harm and disclosure
law still applies. Public availability is not blanket permission. A fact about several
people creates overlapping interests, not exclusive property in another person's memory
or testimony. Protect overlapping or unfamiliar collective interests under their own
law; nonidentifying aggregate use still faces equality, safety and political-power
duties. Neither a copied account nor a new medium creates a fresh disclosure mandate.

**PD02 — Who answers.** The actual person/body deciding purposes and essential means is
the controller; joint deciders name their respective duties and remain answerable to the
person. A processor acts only under a recorded intelligible scoped instruction,
security/return/deletion terms and reviewable subcontracting; it becomes a controller
for its own reuse. Each public service authority remains the controller of its own
lawful records, even when a shared technical operator stores them. A legal duty follows
a materially directed domestic activity/person and actual establishment/connection, not
universal website visibility or a server address. Foreign transfer and enforcement
require PD29 and C08/17. No one acquires an unlimited “ownership of data” that displaces
other people's rights or public evidence duties. Material domestic establishment or
intentionally directed domestic services/monitoring supply the proposed connection; mere
worldwide website visibility or citizenship of a person abroad does not alone create
universal jurisdiction. Joint controllers disclose responsibility and remain reachable
for their own jointly determined conduct under B liability, not guilt by corporate
association. A processor needs prior documented authorization for a new subprocessor,
bound equivalent obligations and a challenge/exit route; a hidden vendor term is
insufficient.

**PD03 — Minimum necessary design.** Before collection, name the concrete purpose,
lawful base, needed fields, source, recipients, access roles, transfer, deletion/review
date and a responsible human office. Use the least intrusive workable means; keep data
accurate for its actual purpose, secure, separated by purpose and retained only as long
as justified. A standing purpose/retention arrangement may cover equivalent routine acts;
there is no new permission form or registration for every encounter. A new use needs its own compatible-purpose analysis or new lawful base and
notice; neither sale, a model update, acquisition of a company nor a revised privacy
notice silently enlarges old permission. Data used in a legally consequential decision
retain source/version and correction history under the applicable sector record rule,
not a permanent public biography. Publishing a new privacy notice does not cure a
missing base. Compatible continuation must remain within the original lawful scope and
reasonable safeguards; a materially new purpose needs its own independently valid base,
sensitive ground where required and notice. Minimize data without destroying proof
needed for safe care, earned claims or executed correction.

**PD04 — Exhaustive ordinary lawful bases.** Processing needs one recorded base and
demonstrated necessity: (a) free, specific, informed, affirmative and revocable consent
for an optional purpose; (b) data strictly needed to perform a service the person
actually requested or to take requested precontract steps, excluding unrelated
surveillance hidden in terms; (c) a specified enacted legal duty; (d) a defined public
task/power under enacted law that fixes purpose, eligible categories, authorized
recipients, review and retention; (e) protection of a person's vital interest where
timely consent is genuinely unavailable, limited to the event and later review; or (f) a
private actor's documented legitimate interest after necessity, reasonable expectations
and a rights/less-intrusive alternative balance, with notice and PD07 objection. A
public authority cannot invoke (f) for its public powers, and (f) alone never authorizes
a sensitive-data shortcut, covert political targeting or a new coercive search. Neither
IP ownership, a public grant, purchased data, a user account nor “better AI” supplies a
base.
