# Necessary care: consequential acts and irreversible therapy

*Canonical UA07–UA09; part of [C08/23](../23-necessary-care-and-unknown-will.md).*

**UA07 — Prior act-specific judicial permission.** For a new consequential act,
an ordinary independent court must find clear and convincing evidence that:
no current ascertainable choice or sufficient prior actual authority resolves the
act after serious support; every defensible personal interpretation/partial value
has been applied as a constraint rather than invented consent; identified serious avoidable
harm threatens this person's health, bodily integrity, essential functioning or
basic living conditions; and waiting for likely communication would cause material
harm. Consider safe delay and actual less intrusive, more reversible alternatives.

Expected protection must clearly and substantially outweigh this person's burdens,
uncertainty and irreversible loss. Preserve known partial values; no fiscal,
family, demographic, social-usefulness, normalization or convenience objective.
Name the exact proposed act, actual accepting qualified receiver, lawful payer,
monitoring and safe handoff. No worker/cash cannot become assent or create clinical
competence. The court applies law to the person; it cannot enact a coverage,
scarcity priority, tax, general guardianship or future-treatment discretion.

**UA08 — A hard therapeutic case is not silently excluded.** A specified invasive,
materially risky or irreversible therapeutic act may be authorized only to treat
an identified serious condition, with two relevant qualified clinical opinions,
at least one genuinely independent of the proposing provider's material control/
conflict. Obtain additional relevant expertise when necessary; two signatures
from one interested controller are not independence. Explain actual condition,
benefit, uncertainty, consequences of nonintervention, irreversibility and why a
less irreversible effective option does not adequately protect the person.

The court expressly decides the higher-stakes question before action, with UA13–16
representation, evidence, appeal opportunity and named procedural/aftercare scope.
No nontherapeutic enhancement, normalization, donor harvest, experimental research
or reproductive-policy objective qualifies. A procedure-specific necessary
anesthetic and its inherent temporary physical control must be expressly assessed
and authorized, ending at the earliest safe point in necessary recovery.
It cannot authorize induction over credible opposition, forced transport,
postoperative confinement or unrelated restraint; no closed placement follows.

**UA09 — Therapeutic reproductive consequences require an express exception.**
A court may authorize nonemergency treatment of this person's serious actual
medical condition with unavoidable reproductive consequences, including necessary
loss of fertility or pregnancy-ending effects, only under UA07/08's heightened
case. Explicitly record that physical consequence, actual alternatives/timing,
relevant reproductive expertise and why no adequately protective less intrusive
option exists. Pregnancy, disability or anticipated care cost alone is not a
pathology/indication; benefit solely to a fetus, relative or population does not
supply authority over this person's body. Actual refusal/credible opposition bars UA.

This is the narrow prospective lawful-therapy exception expressly reconciled with
RP01/RPO01, not inferred consent. Elective abortion/contraception/sterilization,
reproductive use/selection, retrieval/preservation/use of gametes/embryos for
reproduction, donation, research or another person still need actual specific
authority. Necessary therapeutic removal/destruction of diseased tissue or
pregnancy material and proportionate case-specific clinical pathology may fit
this narrow treatment ground; no later reproductive, storage or research use
is licensed. A court cannot relabel a
reproductive objective as cancer care or wait for deterioration to evade limits.
Prosecution retains P burdens; a credible lawful-UA basis must be addressed under
ordinary defense rules. No new offense, retroactive authority or automatic guilt.
