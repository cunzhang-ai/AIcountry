# Copenhagen. What the heat grant paid and what the household still needed

## Municipal recovery and administration

The Social Committee's 21 June 2023 record reports Copenhagen's first
heat-pool grant and the decision on its successor. The municipality
reviewed 53 awards for the first national pool, of which 16 met the
criteria wholly or in part. These are awards reviewed for municipal
recovery. They are not all applications or the count of people in need.

| Item in the first-pool account | DKK |
|---|---:|
| Reported gross grant from the pool | 53,896 |
| Audit cost included in the grant | 11,500 |
| Awards carrying a repayment requirement | 23,770 |
| Awards without a repayment requirement | 18,626 |
| Separate state reimbursement on the latter | 9,313 |
| Municipal compensation on the committee's stated basis | 9,313 |

The source reconciles the grant as 11,500 plus 42,396 in awards.
Removing the audit, repayable awards and separate reimbursement leaves
9,313 on its stated compensation basis. This is the committee's
account of the programme, with no inference that repayable awards
had been collected from residents. It estimated 60–80 hours and DKK
18,000–28,000 for the separate review and application work, excluding
additional effort elsewhere. Subtracting that estimate from the stated
compensation gives a negative difference of DKK 8,687–18,687 within
this scoped calculation. Do not deduct the audit again or treat the
estimate as a complete municipal cost.

The administration estimated about DKK 100,000 in possible claims and
DKK 20,000 in compensation for the second pool before case-handling
cost. The committee approved declining to apply and also asked the
national government to simplify the criteria. The record explicitly
separates this later public recovery from continuing resident
eligibility. It records no matched dates of household payment or
heat restored. [Committee decision and Table 1](https://www.kk.dk/dagsordener-og-referater/Socialudvalget/m%C3%B8de-21062023/referat/punkt-5)

The accompanying annex reports that earlier guidance changes required
reconsidering 700 decisions. By 21 April 2023, it records 650 examined
with any reconsideration complete, and two awards after earlier refusals.
These are status figures for the reopened group. They do not establish
the result of all 700, an unmet-need rate or actual payment timing.
The reopened cases are separate from the pool's 53 reviewed awards.
[Background annex, pages 2–3](https://www.kk.dk/sites/default/files/agenda/77851191-857f-434f-b35a-fe9525123f63/28577ea3-3403-4f20-967f-eaae4af336df-bilag-3.pdf)

## The building and tenant boundary

HOFOR's customer information explains that a heat exchanger separates
the district network from a building's heat system. Its interruption
guidance advises checking that building equipment works after the
network returns. Its billing information says tenants in multi-unit
associations are usually indirect customers, with household heat use
accounted for through the building's heat allocation process. These
are separate operator descriptions. They do not show that any household
in the municipal grant record suffered a physical outage or whether
its home became safe. [HOFOR on interruptions](https://www.hofor.dk/privat/fjernvarme/spoergsmaal-svar-fjernvarme-privat/fjernvarmeafbrydelser/)
· [HOFOR on billing and indirect accounts](https://www.hofor.dk/privat/fjernvarme/foelg-dit-fjernvarmeforbrug/)

The evidence supports testing whether a simpler, timely public payment
route and a distinct building receiving check improve the design. It
does not determine either the reserve size or an actual viable first
energy cohort.
