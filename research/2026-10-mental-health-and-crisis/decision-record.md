# Decision record: mental health, substance use and crisis power

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 53 research selection; not enacted law

## Baseline and the consequential question

The [design-52 predecessor](predecessor-design-52/README.md) preserves the
prior current country. Its C07 health topic lists mental health and permits
mental-health restriction or treatment without consent under an
individualized lawful ground. It names no complete voluntary service,
accepted crisis response, substance-use route or rights-preserving
alternative. C08 provides general safeguards against coercion but does not
say how a clinical contact differs from rescue, arrest or confinement.
The question is how the reference country supplies useful care while
preventing a diagnosis, AI prediction or missing bed from becoming a
second-class legal status or an open-ended coercive power.

People affected include those with psychosocial disabilities, people who
use drugs, children, carers, peers, clinicians, emergency responders,
neighbors, crime victims and people harmed by unsafe or absent services.
None has participated in an AIcountry jurisdiction. This is one authored
analysis; it is not independent inspection, public assent or actual law.
Values in conflict include liberty, equal legal capacity, relief, safety,
privacy, family and community life, professional responsibility and fair
use of limited staff. A diagnosis can be clinically useful without being
a lawful basis for detention; harm to others remains independently
answerable under ordinary law. Values cannot be settled by one risk score.

## Competing diagnoses and coherent packages

An apparent crisis failure could be lack of affordable help, a coercive
family or employer, trauma, unsafe housing, substance toxicity, provider
scarcity, discriminatory policing or a real imminent threat. Different
responses follow. Original accounts from users and nonusers, referral
and response times, medicine supply, housing and benefit records,
independent incident evidence, actual liberty orders and aftercare would
test these explanations. No real AIcountry observations exist.

| Package | Plausible benefit | Serious objection or failure |
|---|---|---|
| Retain generic mental-health entitlement and individualized involuntary powers | Clinicians may act quickly in a dangerous episode under familiar categories | An unavailable voluntary service can funnel people into coercion; diagnosis and prediction may substitute for specific conduct and independent proof |
| Police-led crisis and custodial hospital capacity | Creates a visible responder when danger is feared | Criminalizes distress, concentrates force, discourages help-seeking and can turn bed shortage into detention |
| Family or market-led care with no public crisis residual | Allows plural personal and cultural support | Income, family power and unpaid labor determine access; isolated people and abusive homes lack a receiver |
| **Selected: funded voluntary community network plus diagnosis-neutral immediate safety law** | Person-chosen care, peer and clinical support, overdose response and real aftercare are primary; imminent danger uses a narrow general rule, not psychiatric status | Needs substantial staffing and may still be misused as disguised commitment; hard cases of refused help and serious danger remain |
| Absolute prohibition of every protective physical intervention | Strongly guards liberty and avoids a diagnostic exception | May leave no lawful short rescue when immediate serious physical harm is observable and voluntary communication cannot work |

The selected [C07 service](../country/C07/11-mental-health-substance-use-and-crisis-care.md)
provides accepting voluntary pathways; [C08's legal boundary](../country/C08/09-mental-health-crisis-liberty-and-consent.md)
removes diagnosis-specific civil commitment and forced treatment from the
reference while retaining only general, conduct-specific immediate
protection under necessity and review. [C06](../country/C06/15-mental-health-and-crisis-finance.md)
counts real capacity and overlap; [C13](../country/C13/18-crisis-support-without-diagnostic-coercion.md)
tests two linked failures. The alternative rejecting all emergency
intervention remains a live moral and legal objection; a general safety
route must not be a relabeled psychiatric detention. The choice does not
settle general drug-possession policy by clinical paperwork.

## Primary support and what it cannot prove

The [WHO/OHCHR mental-health and law guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240080737)
argues for person-centered community services, equal rights and
elimination of coercive practices. WHO's [community mental-health
guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240025707) sets out
crisis, outreach, peer and supported-living service models. The
[Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Ch_IV_15.pdf),
especially Articles 12 and 14, is a primary rights comparator on legal
capacity and liberty; actual treaty obligations require a real state
and treaty inventory. The [WHO/UNODC drug-treatment standards](https://www.who.int/publications/b/53363)
address evidence-based ethical treatment. WHO's [community opioid-overdose
guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241548816) supports
trained bystander access to naloxone as part of emergency response.
These sources are not AIcountry statutes. They do not certify a proposed
general safety exception, determine every clinical choice, establish local
service capacity or prove better human outcomes. The ethical and legal
boundary between immediate rescue and coercion needs public scrutiny.

## Power chain, objections and continuity

The Assembly enacts the C07 benefit and public budget, C08 liberty and
consent boundary, general safety grounds, data protection, professional
standards and independent remedies. The health purchaser and regional
networks fund and deliver care; qualified clinicians make patient-specific
recommendations; local civilian responders address a crisis; police use
only their separate lawful powers; courts test coercion and enforce
remedies. Public-health and evidence services may analyze de-identified
patterns within lawful purposes but do not maintain a coercive risk list.
Trace appointments, money, source records, acting capacity and execution
under a hostile minister, a captured provider and a police service that
refuses to yield a case. Neither a model vendor nor a clinical regulator
can change the legal threshold by adjusting a score.

Serious objections remain. A person may refuse all offered help during
imminent danger; the selected short general rescue may be seen as too
coercive or too weak. A police-free default can fail when a real violent
threat exists; a police-led default can make help-seeking dangerous.
Community services may disperse responsibility or leave remote people
without skilled support. Peer workers may be asked to carry risk without
pay or backup. A diagnosis-neutral rule may still be applied mostly to
disabled or marginalized people unless original case data, independent
review and actual remedies expose the pattern. Drug policies, youth
privacy and public-safety obligations require separate public decisions.
These objections cannot be resolved by averaging a satisfaction score.

An adopting jurisdiction must reconcile current mental-health, medical
consent, disability, child, drug, police, emergency, criminal, housing,
benefit and privacy law, including any court orders and treaties. Existing
patients, pending liberty orders, medicines, beds, staff, claims, appeals
and services continue under valid law until a prospective lawful transition
with accepting voluntary care, housing, counsel and safe emergency
response exists. No research text releases people into danger or creates
a new power to detain them. Facility closure is not deinstitutionalization
if people become homeless, jailed or dependent on an abusive household.

## Result and next bounded test

Evidence qualification: international guidance and project comparison,
not affected participation, independent review or original local data.
Research selection: shift the reference from possible diagnosis-specific
coercion to voluntary community care plus a tightly bounded general
immediate-safety route. Legal authorization: absent. Operational readiness:
absent. Observed care, liberty and safety outcomes: absent.

The next bounded test in a real jurisdiction is a witnessed crisis path
covering a remote request, after-hours failure, refused intervention,
observable imminent danger, a mistaken AI score, overdose response and
the first week after discharge. Compare the selected network with
retained involuntary care and a stronger no-coercion rival using original
accounts, current law, staffing, independent incident review and actual
follow-up. The legitimate public decides any binding change. Reopen if
the general safety rule becomes disguised detention, unassisted people
cannot get care, a rival preserves rights and safety better, or newly
affected people reject the selected tradeoff with material reasons.
