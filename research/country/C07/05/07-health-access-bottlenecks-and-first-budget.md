# Fund the stage that prevents people from receiving care

*HG21–HG26. Candidate rules for a health access improvement in the first budget.*

**HG21 — Measure access from attempted contact through care.** Each
clinical network records, on the applicable clocks, attempts that
could not reach an authorized intake, accepted requests, assessment,
referral, qualified treatment and actual receipt. Preserve original
dates across sites and providers. Record reasons for delay, offers the
person could not use, nonresponse, known unmet need and independently
verified correction. Track the applicable clinical priority and privacy.
A booked appointment or case removed from the queue cannot substitute
for delivered care. No one must join a survey to make a request.

**HG22 — Test the source and the affected population.** Use original
records, independent patient accounts and lawful sampled evidence
to compare waiting, financial barriers, travel and other causes.
Keep stated denominators, reference windows, status flags and missing
populations visible. Elected choices, clinical evidence and reported
experiences retain their distinct roles. A national share cannot
authorize an individual decision or justify an age, residence or
wealth filter. Include people outside ordinary intake and review
whether the public offer reaches them.

**HG23 — Buy capacity at the actual bottleneck.** On the same useful
dates and spending envelope compare changes to financial protection,
assisted entry or transport, and the qualified treatment path.
Price actual incremental clinical time, premises, support, ordinary
care, patient effort, and the accepting service after referral.
Credit an additional treatment only when all necessary stages are
qualified and independently verified. If the same staff appear on
another due roster, retain that assignment until paid cover is
accepted. Do not buy only a visible intake target when no qualified
next stage can deliver. An urgent need follows its own clinical law.

**HG24 — Give the elected decision real alternatives and limits.**
Where qualified evidence indicates a waiting bottleneck, prefer
an independently funded end-to-end capacity repair over a fee
change alone or an assessment-only expansion. If inability to
pay, travel or communicate is the binding barrier, compare and
fund the relevant remedy before attributing a gain to slots.
Preserve due payment protection and sector obligations. The network
proposes exact costs, incidence and omitted needs. The elected
authority sets the material budget and scope. Clinicians retain
safe prioritization and patient consent. A metric cannot change
eligibility or authorize a treatment.

**HG25 — Follow losses and keep the ordinary route usable.** Before
new reliance, verify staffing, accepted onward care, paid review
and the next service window. People can challenge a missing case,
wrong clock or unusable offer directly with the existing outside
reviewer. Keep provisional and urgent protection on its own legal
scope. If one stage fails, maintain severable work, identify who
remains unserved and fund or seek lawful correction. Record actual
delivery and harm. A new provider or return to ordinary service
carries old dates, protected evidence and open remedies.

**HG26 — Review distribution and displace the rule when evidence changes.**
Publish matched measures of actual access, time, affected groups
and independent remedies alongside the financial account. Compare
the intervention with an equally protected ordinary alternative,
including harms from a displaced service or staff member. AI may
help reconcile cases, translate public evidence or compare dated
scenarios. It cannot diagnose, determine clinical priority or
count assumed treatment as receipt. Reopen on worsening access,
unreachable intake, repeated late care or a stronger funded choice.
