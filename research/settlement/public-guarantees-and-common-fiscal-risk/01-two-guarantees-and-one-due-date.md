# Two guarantees and one due date

*Design389 · Constructed comparison with [primary records](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-public-guarantees-and-common-fiscal-risk/sources.md).*

An existing nine-unit guarantee supports a transport operator's
eligible obligation. A proposed seven-unit guarantee supports
a separate supply company. A single market interruption can
trigger both by day 4. A lawful guarantee source has twelve
settled units on day 4. Four units needed for ordinary
services on the same day are funded separately and restricted
to that purpose. Neither promise gives authority to use them.
A five-unit collateral recovery may be settled on day 12,
with no earlier enforceable cash available in this example.

| Choice | Result under the declared assumptions |
|---|---|
| Examine each call separately | Both 9 and 7 fit a 12-unit source alone. This understates the shared risk. |
| Approve both at full amounts | Joint calls need 16 on day 4, leaving a gap of 4. Even if all 5 arrives on day 12, the earlier shortfall survives. |
| Narrow the proposed cover to 3 | The joint calls need 12. This fits the declared case only if the exact narrower guarantee is separately authorized and accepted and the cash settles. |
| Require more lawful funded cash or choose direct continuity | The Assembly can consider either. A promise to decide later cannot protect an earlier binding date. |
| Promise no guarantee, but expect a rescue | Keep the possible service impact visible. A later elected rescue is a separate choice with its own purpose and payer. |

In a variant, a national counter-guarantee reimburses the first
issuer's same nine-unit payment on day 8. Record nine due from
the issuer on day 4 and nine from the national payer on day 8.
Consolidated external exposure for that underlying payment is
still nine, and the national reimbursement cannot pay day 4.
The example gives neither the central authority nor the
transport operator a right to charge ordinary service sources.
