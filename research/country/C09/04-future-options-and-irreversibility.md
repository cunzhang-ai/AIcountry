# C09. Future options and hard-to-reverse public choices

*Part of [C09 land and ecology](../C09-land-and-ecology.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

People who will live with today's losses may be too young to vote or not yet
exist. Present decision-makers cannot know their preferences or claim their
consent. Nor may an imagined future public justify denying current people
housing, care or political rights. The country therefore protects **future
options** through lawful floors, an explicit account of hard-to-reverse effects
and a decision by the elected authority competent for the affected scope.

## When the duty applies

Prior law defines a material long-horizon choice by the scale and persistence
of a loss that cannot be restored within a relevant human or ecological period:
for example, depletion of a shared aquifer, destruction of habitat, long-lived
contamination, loss of essential-system redundancy or a binding fiscal promise
without a credible maintenance and payment path. A long service life or a large
price alone is not proof of irreversible harm. The test includes cumulative
small approvals and effects displaced across borders. An accrued benefit,
judgment or existing service duty is not a discretionary project this test
can cancel. An agency cannot split one project, narrow a map or promise an
unproven future repair to evade it.
Affected people may challenge classification under C03's prior-law scope rule.

## What must be decided before commitment

The proponent and responsible public authority disclose the present stock,
source observations and uncertainty; plausible future paths under action and
inaction; a repair, lower-use and staged alternative; who receives benefits
now; and who bears loss, maintenance, debt and restoration later. C06's common
account counts money, skilled people, land, water, energy, imports and the same
reserve only once. C11 retains original data and rival interpretations. The
comparison keeps ecological conditions, rights, usable services, livelihoods
and political freedom separate rather than trading them through one score.
Unknown damage is shown as unknown, not as zero or a confident AI forecast.

Existing constitutional and statutory rights, ecological duties and lawful
claims set enforceable floors. Where an action would cross a floor, ordinary
majority approval cannot cure it. Within those limits, the accountable local
council or Assembly specified by prior law chooses the risk; a national common
obligation stays with the Assembly. It gives public reasons for rejecting a
less damaging feasible alternative, the remaining uncertainty, the people
excluded from present benefits and the capacity reserved for maintenance or
repair. Where feasible, it stages a reversible step with a real stop point.
A sunset cannot reverse pollution or recreate a lost habitat, and monitoring
alone is not a funded remedy.
If loss is not repairable, the authority states that residual loss plainly
and justifies it within lawful floors; it cannot book a hypothetical repair.
An Assembly may change a statutory floor only through prospective law and
rights review, not through a permit, appropriation or unpublished instruction;
a constitutional floor requires its protected amendment route. Existing
claims and restoration duties survive any valid prospective change.

Children and younger residents can be heard with support; affected workers,
Indigenous and other rights-holders, downstream communities and people abroad
may supply relevant observations through lawful safe routes. Their current
rights and distinct claims are not replaced by a generic future advocate.
Opposition, eligible public-interest challengers and affected people can seek
source access, correction, interim protection and review of competence, floors
and required reasons. A court may stop unlawful action or require a fresh
choice, but cannot select a preferred energy mix, site or tax. No unelected
commissioner, scientist or AI receives a general veto or claims to speak for
unborn people.

The authorization names the operator, monitoring source, notice trigger,
funded restoration or safe exit, and lawful successor for permits, debts and
pending claims. A private assurance must be collectible in plausible failure,
not merely written as a bond. A public liability enters C06's account even
if payment is far off. Emergency first-hour protection under C06/C10 remains
bounded; it does not grant a permanent extraction or development right. A
material warning triggers the preauthorized safety response and a fresh
competent elected choice before any further irreversible stage; missed review
does not approve continuation. A changed government can revise a lawful
policy prospectively, but cannot erase accrued duties or call irreversible
damage a rollback.

This rule is a research selection, not an adopted environmental limit or a
finding that a particular project should be refused. The [decision record](../../2026-10-future-options/decision-record.md)
compares rival institutions and records the missing evidence. For an actual
jurisdiction, test one contested long-lived project with original place data,
current service needs, alternative costs, affected participation and a lawful
competence map. Revisit the rule if it suppresses essential present provision,
misses cumulative harm or gives review bodies an unaccountable policy veto.

[NR03/NR14](15-finite-resources-and-mineral-rights.md) now applies this duty to
finite mineral depletion and public-owner receipts. Actual existing payable
NP/source core precedes discretionary replacement capital; a qualified express
budget/Act may choose bounded further present use with admitted future loss.
No resource valuation, earmark or bond proves restored capacity or future consent.

[ER01–ER05](04/01-ecological-loss-and-replacement.md) governs the
ecological substitute that an irreversible stage relies on. Verify each
protected function, exposure window, additionality and funded successor.
An ecological hold leaves existing services and elected budget duties payable.
