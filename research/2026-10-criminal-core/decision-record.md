# Criminal power must have a narrow defended boundary

14 October 2026 · Integrated design 61 · Working research selection, not enacted law

## Decision, baseline and authorship

Select C08 P01–P20/K01–K12 as candidate general responsibility, harm elements,
criminal procedure, finite sanction and delivery rules. Differentiated protection
and bounded penal power remain the chosen architecture; core statutes now state
what must be proved and what happens at expired authority. Retain victim support,
civil repair and prevention on their own grounds. No software, source manuscript,
current constitutional hold, publication or actual law is amended.

current Markdown files and inventory, 251 hashed artifacts. Current overview,

criminal code/procedure and everyday-justice E1–E3/J1–J8 were inspected for the

## Diagnosis, coherent alternatives and selection

The gap is substantive law as well as service access: a generic fault rule can
criminalize a bad outcome, a job title can hide missing personal attribution,
unauthorized-access language can suppress research, custody labels can conceal
indefinite restraint, and an enforcement office can profit from punishment.
Navigation and extra police funding cannot correct those legal incentives.
A code alone also cannot supply qualified counsel, original evidence or safety.

Compare retaining/funding the modular draft, expanded deterrence/incapacitation,
community restoration first and differentiated bounded criminal power. Retain/
repair avoids disruption but leaves these ambiguities. Broad incapacitation
may stop some imminent violent acts but exposes error, capture and custody harm.
Community-first can preserve relationships but a dependent person may lack safe
refusal and local leaders may shield serious or official abuse. Select the
bounded model: serious harms remain prosecutable, prevention/support do not
wait for guilt, and every criminal element has a defended independent route.
The rivals and retain/no-change remain legitimate public choices, not averaged
into one favorable score. No crime-reduction effect has been observed here.

## Material choices and incompatibilities

P01–P05 state intention, recklessness, express gross negligence, result causation,
actual-duty omissions, personal participation, substantial-step attempt and
properly raised defenses without burden reversal. AI deployment requires actual
control, duty, ability and fault evidence; model/person labels cannot establish
guilt. Corporate criminal sanctions remain a needed separate prospective
schedule, not implicit vicarious liability or pooled fictional intent.

Minimum responsibility **16** displaces the source's **14**, without seriousness/
maturity exceptions; pre-18 conduct retains child proceedings and five-year
aggregate custody ceiling. Retaining 14 is a substantive rival: a higher threshold
limits punitive childhood exposure but requires real safety and support for
serious harm below it. The choice favors that protective boundary, not a claim
that neuroscience determines one legal age. Victim and youth participation is
missing. Criminal, civil, clinical, franchise and sexual-protection ages differ.

P09 removes the source's further 24-hour police-extension option and sets a hard
24-hour defended first hearing; warrantless exceptional searches also receive
review within 24 hours. P10 adds seven-day first remand, renewals up to 14 days,
and 90/180-day aggregate pretrial ceilings with specific grave-case reasons.
These normative limits have no measured docket/geography calibration. A release
does not guarantee safety; another custody label cannot solve failed capacity.
In-person presentation is the default; any remote option needs lawful equivalent
safeguards and a usable in-person review request. Treaty compatibility and actual
independent hearing capacity require fresh competent review before adoption.

K01–K12 define core violent, coercive, exploitation, property, corrupt, digital,
torture and obstruction harms. Most maxima retain predecessor values. Newly
explicit ceilings for reckless/gross-negligent death, threats/control, forced
exploitation, torture and obstruction are original provisional choices, not
estimated optimal punishments. Digital access needs harmful purpose; proportionate
safe research/reporting is protected. Mere dissent, diagnosis, poverty, wrong
forecast or unproved allegation is not a crime. Specialized child sexual-law,
corporate/fine/community, limitation, territorial and international schedules
remain incomplete; no law-by-analogy or complete-code claim follows.

P11–P15 retain the stronger September disclosure and torture/coercion/derivative
exclusion candidates, including meaningfully unchallengeable evidence excluded
even alongside other proof. No fresh independent approval is inferred. Appeal,
innocence reopening and civil repair have distinct grounds. P16–P20 impose finite
core aggregation, credit custody, protect subsistence, separate humane corrections,
require actual release and receiving duties, and bar punishment quotas/rewards
and pursuing-office fine/forfeiture income. A policy target is not personal guilt.

supports one responsibility threshold, age-at-act and full child protection before
18, while commending higher thresholds; it does not prescribe our exact design.
Official texts were retrieved on 14 October. The direct CAT page/PDF and UN CRC
PDF retrievals failed; no successful inspection of those downloads is claimed.
The [official UN CAT text](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-196818/)
and [OHCHR reporting guidance](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=yGqjgB3vvakAPydUBDnVFAk9TmUJKm5B%2FfbetNTXmg5AqW%2FDNYdmz5Lo9n8UBiBG384S3%2FnBc3SVJVYIu1IIiw%3D%3D)
were checked through official indexed material for the statement exclusion and
separate derivative question. Our broader rule is explicitly original. Applicable
binding obligations for an actual country remain unknown. No primary harm/case/
cost observations or new public participation exist.

Objections: narrow harms may leave serious coercion uncharged until special laws
are complete; protection/civil routes continue and gaps stay explicit. Hard clocks
may release an alleged dangerous person when a court fails; preserve actual narrow
safety routes, fund independent capacity and retain the policy tradeoff rather than
invent indefinite detention. A higher age or finite maximum may not satisfy victims'
accountability values; meaningful support and lawful custody remain, but their views
cannot be fabricated. Strong suppression can remove probative material; retaining
it would reward coercion/control, so the broader candidate is retained pending
comparative review. More rules may burden claimants and courts; normal reports need
no full dossier, duties stay with institutions and capacity is not assumed.
