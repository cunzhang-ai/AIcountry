# Design381 decision. Share specific input risks and budget their joint cost

## Baseline and selection

Design380 already requires disclosed price and FX adjustment clauses
under SC13, gross appropriation and ordinary continuity. It protects
households against essential price and availability losses under I36–I41.
The gap is how to select a price risk allocation for essential contracts,
test its shared public exposure and avoid a later negotiation after
a supplier gains leverage through residents' dependence.

Select [SC44–SC49](../country/C04/05/11-essential-contract-costs-and-paid-continuity.md).
Use a bounded component formula where material external costs qualify.
Fix the base, index, bounds, dates and fallback before award. Keep
firms' controllable risks, effective competition, separate ordinary
protection and lawful alternative supply. Treat wider renegotiation
under its actual authority, including challenge and existing expiry.

## Alternatives and objections

Firm prices can offer simpler budgeting and stronger incentives to
control costs. They may demand a bid premium or fail where suppliers
cannot finance shocks. Retain this route where viable at lower whole
cost. Full cost reimbursement can stabilize fragile supply but can
pass controllable and related-party costs to the public. Broader
indexing can be simpler but overstate exposure. Direct, cooperative
or other independent provision remains a real rival with its own
staff, cash and transition costs.

A precise index may still be a poor proxy. The risk from observable
costs is partly transferred to the public; a thinner margin leaves
less room for new services. Consider protected evidence and bidder
challenge without requiring unrelated private information. Small
providers may need simpler priced terms and timely ordinary payment.
Do not presume that an indexed award protects pay, physical supply
or independent performance. Assess material rights separately.
