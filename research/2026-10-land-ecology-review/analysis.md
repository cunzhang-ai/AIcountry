# Land, ecological limits and the authority to make spatial tradeoffs

2 October 2026 · Supporting analysis for AIcountry country-design C04–C09/C13–C14 · Research proposal, not adopted law

## The national-design question

Who makes a binding, contestable choice when housing, food production, water,
energy, transport, ecosystem protection and climate safety compete for the same
places and physical capacity? The question is not answered by showing that each
sector has a planning clause. The current [integrated design](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md)
gives elected local government land-use competence, national government the rights
floor and major networks, and C09 a material account and project participation.
The [status register](../DESIGN-STATUS.md) correctly says that geographic and
ecological capacity is unverified. But neither specifies the ordinary authority
that reconciles a local housing refusal, a regional water limit, a national grid
connection and an ecological obligation before those individual decisions are
made. The same physical site, water flow or construction workforce can therefore
be implicitly promised several times. A project hearing held after a corridor is
chosen cannot give affected people real influence over the earlier distribution
of burden.

This is a **design gap**, not evidence that an actual country is short of land or
water. Scarcity can arise from physical limits, damaged ecosystems, absent
infrastructure, exclusionary rules, concentrated ownership or unaffordable access.
Those explanations require different remedies and cannot be collapsed into a
single national construction target or a single ecological price. The existing
[sector manuscript snapshot](../2026-09-readiness/source-documents.json) already
contains useful fragments: `housing.housing-land` Articles 2, 5–6 cover planning,
acquisition and development infrastructure; `food.food-water-act` Articles 3–5
specify a provisional scarcity allocation and reserves; `energy.climate-energy`
Articles 1–3 separate carbon, habitat and water and require project alternatives;
`infrastructure.infrastructure-act` Articles 1–5 cover appraisal, maintenance and
network access. They are proposed sector texts with legacy AI-lawmaking references,
not a reconciled national allocation of democratic authority.

The external literature supports the relevance of linked territorial planning and
physical limits, not any specific AIcountry institution. [UN-Habitat's territorial
planning guidelines](https://unhabitat.org/international-guidelines-on-urban-and-territorial-planning)
address integrated, connected and inclusive territories. The [IPCC AR6 Working
Group II summary](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/)
assesses adaptation limits and stresses that land use, infrastructure, ecological
integrity and inequity interact. [FAO's land-and-water assessment](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1565470/)
documents pressure on agricultural land and water at a global scale; it supplies
no AIcountry catchment measurement. The [Aarhus Convention text](https://unece.org/environment-policy/public-participation/aarhus-convention/text)
is a primary example of access to environmental information, participation and
justice; it does not establish treaty status or domestic legal applicability here.

## Rival explanations and coherent choices

| Choice | Mechanism and democratic control | Strength | Likely failure or cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retain C04/C09 plus sector permits | Elected local councils decide land use; sector agencies decide water, ecology, energy and transport within national laws; budgets disclose physical accounts. | Preserves local knowledge and limits new bureaucracy. | No accountable decision reconciles cumulative regional demand or an upstream/downstream conflict. A locality can block housing whose workers and infrastructure it benefits from; national agencies can pass costs among sectors. |
| Central national allocation authority | A national expert body fixes land and resource maps, quotas and corridors, with local consultation and project appeal. | Can compare the whole territory and compel a needed network. | The expert body acquires a distributive power it cannot justify by technical expertise alone. It may impose displacement, misread local conditions, become captured by the cabinet or owner interests, and make appeal after mapping too late. Public ownership of all land does not remove these risks. |
| **Selected for recommendation: nested democratic spatial and resource planning** | Assembly enacts common rights, resource and ecological duties and conflict rules; elected local governments make local plans within them; legally defined functional areas jointly plan shared catchments, housing/transport systems and networks. Unresolved distributive choices return to elected institutions, with reasons, minority positions, rights review and funded execution. | Makes shared limits and tradeoffs explicit while retaining local choice where effects stay local. Allows plans to be revised with observations and public objection. | More coordination and evidence capacity are needed. A regional process can be dominated by affluent landowners or infrastructure operators; Assembly override can become habitual centralization. No measured feasibility or consent exists. |

The third choice should displace the current assumption that separate accounts and
participation in each project are enough. It need not create a permanent new tier
of general government. Boundaries differ by the problem: water catchments, housing
and commuting areas, grid service areas and ecosystems do not coincide. A single
fixed regional map could hide relevant affected people. The Assembly must define
the legal scope and replacement of any joint planning body, not allow it to enlarge
its own jurisdiction. Local election remains meaningful for choices whose costs and
benefits are local. National law can override a local refusal only within a defined
national or cross-boundary competence, with public reasons and judicial review;
there is no universal local veto or universal national fast track.

## Candidate national settlement and decision chain

1. **Name the material and rights boundary before allocating.** A public baseline
   distinguishes land availability from buildable, serviced and accessible land;
   water withdrawal from ecological flow and quality; electricity generation from
   grid and backup capacity; food availability from soil, labor, supply chains and
   people's ability to buy or receive it. Record existing titles, tenancies,
   customary or collective claims, public assets, permits, pending obligations and
   historic dispossession. Map uncertainty rather than inventing a national
   carrying-capacity number. The Assembly, under protected rights, enacts the
   obligations and the method for setting contested limits. Scientific evidence
   informs actual biophysical constraints; it does not decide how society distributes
   losses or whose livelihood counts.
2. **Connect national rules to functional-area plans.** The Assembly legislates
   basic domestic water protection, necessary ecological safeguards, national
   infrastructure and housing-access obligations, climate-risk disclosure and
   review routes. Local elected authorities propose site-specific land use, housing
   capacity, transit, access and infrastructure. For a catchment, connected housing
   market or network crossing local borders, the affected local councils jointly
   prepare alternatives in an authorized planning forum. Include tenants, workers,
   farmers, Indigenous or customary claimants where applicable, children through
   appropriate representation, and communities bearing risks, including those
   outside the administrative boundary. Participation begins while alternatives
   can still change, not just at the permit stage. The forum's technical secretariat
   has no independent mandate to make a final distributive bargain.
3. **Resolve actual disagreement by public authority.** The plan states capacity,
   siting alternatives, cumulative effects, who gains and loses, maintenance and
   adaptation costs, funding, mitigation, monitoring and dissent. Each elected
   authority decides within its competence. If a shared-area decision cannot be
   made, the unresolved alternatives and local objections go to the Assembly or
   the lawfully designated elected regional body for an explicit decision. The
   national executive cannot acquire approval by letting a clock expire. A court
   reviews legality, evidence duties, procedural fairness, property and rights,
   but does not choose the preferred housing density or water allocation as
   general policy. Emergency powers remain temporary and reviewed, not a way to
   bypass an ordinary unresolved plan.
4. **Protect people through the transition.** Owners do not hold a veto over all
   shared needs; planners do not erase title, tenancy, livelihood or cultural ties
   through a map. Acquisition or relocation requires a lawful public purpose,
   necessity tested against feasible alternatives, independent valuation and
   review, compensation where law requires it and practical rehousing before
   removal except immediate danger. Displacement assistance must include access to
   work, school, care and community relationships, not only a cash amount. Existing
   permits and service contracts are inventoried and continue until lawfully
   modified; dangerous or unsustainable activities may need a reasoned transition,
   remediation and fair treatment of reliance interests. Restoration security and
   the cost of decommissioning belong with the approving decision.
5. **Bind plans to delivery and revision.** The national/local fiscal settlement
   must identify capital, workforce, materials, grid and water connections,
   operating and renewal budgets, reserves, and which authority delivers each
   remedy. A plan cannot count the same water or contractor capacity for mutually
   exclusive projects. Publish indicators and trigger review when measurements,
   climate exposure, population, supply chains, court findings or affected-party
   objections materially change the assumptions. Revisions preserve pending claims
   and distinguish new promises from existing duties. Lack of money or data requires
   transparent triage and a democratic adjustment, not a silent waiver of rights.

For drought or contamination, the general plan is complemented by a *preadopted,
reviewable scarcity procedure*: protect life-sustaining household access and
scientifically grounded ecosystem needs, disclose the available balance and
uncertainty, then apply legislated criteria to other uses with stated impacts on
food, jobs and prices. An agency may implement a lawful temporary restriction;
it cannot invent a permanent priority among farming, industry and housing during
the emergency. People need accessible relief and water delivery if a meter, permit
or utility fails. Energy shortages similarly require prior continuity duties,
service priorities and lawful decisions; a low carbon count is insufficient if the
grid cannot reliably support essential services. Food security requires domestic
production, trade, transport, reserves, safety and affordability to be assessed
together, with no presumption that forced self-sufficiency is always feasible.

## Authored cross-system failure test

Assume a connected region proposes homes near a transit line. Construction needs
water treatment and a new grid connection. An upstream council benefits from power
revenues but bears a reservoir impact; downstream residents, food producers and
habitat rely on the same flow. A flood assessment also makes part of the proposed
housing site unsafe. A national target, four separate permits and a monetary budget
can each appear compliant while the plans consume the same water and shift flood
risk to a poorer locality.

Under the recommended chain, the relevant functional-area plan must show feasible
housing sites and sizes, water balance, grid capacity, food/ecology impacts,
displacement, flood exposure, and alternatives such as repair, reuse, demand
management or a different location. The affected councils and public can challenge
the framing. If councils cannot agree, the elected authority with lawful shared
competence chooses a package and funds its obligations; independent review tests
rights and procedure. If the water, site or workforce cannot be made available,
the proposal cannot truthfully be called deliverable. Existing housing and water
claims continue; the Assembly must confront the shortfall. This is an **authored
case**, not measured AIcountry geography or proof that the proposed forum would
withstand capture.

## Integration, objections and limits

**Recommended correction to the working design:** strengthen C09 from a commitment
to integrated measurement and project participation into a specified multi-level
allocation authority. C04 should distinguish local land-use choice from common
catchment, network and housing-market decisions, including a lawful elected route
when councils disagree. C06 should connect capital and maintenance funding to
the same physical plan. C07's housing, food and water guarantees require location
and service access, not only nominal quantity or cash. C08 supplies property,
administrative and environmental remedies. C13 should test a compound spatial
conflict; C14's founding inventory and legal migration should include land,
water, permits, liabilities and cross-boundary obligations. The design status should
record both the selection and the absence of territory-specific evidence.

The strongest objection is that a new plan could license technocratic vetoes and
delay urgently needed homes or clean energy. The response is limited, replaceable
jurisdiction; early comparison of alternatives; fixed duties to give reasons and
decide; and an elected resolution path when coordination fails. Yet process costs
and delay remain real, and rights review cannot be streamlined away merely by
labelling a project urgent. Another objection is that compensating existing owners
protects unjust historical allocations. This design does not treat all property
claims as morally equal or freeze current value; it requires a lawful, contestable
assessment of present rights, historic dispossession, tenants and public gains.
The eventual balance is a value and authority decision for an affected public,
not a result inferable from land-cover data. Environmental requirements may also
displace pollution or extraction abroad through imports; the plan must expose those
effects and treaty duties, while the broader external-dependency architecture is
reviewed separately.

relevance of integrated planning, adaptation constraints and procedural standing;
they do not validate AIcountry's proposed bodies, thresholds or service capacity.

**Next bounded question:** in a specified lawful jurisdiction, can the responsible
public body assemble a single defensible regional baseline and compare one housing/
water/energy/ecology conflict under its actual rights, finance and participation
rules? The output should be one public alternatives account with dissent, delivery
and appeal paths; completion requires original local measurements and actual affected
people's participation. This recommendation must be reopened if that comparison
shows the joint process systematically blocks essentials, masks exported harms,
cannot fund remedies or is controlled by the interests it is meant to constrain.
