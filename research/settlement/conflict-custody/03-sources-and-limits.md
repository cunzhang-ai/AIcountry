# Inspected sources and explicit limits

15 October 2026 · Part of [conflict custody](README.md)

- [ICRC English compilation of the 1949 Geneva Conventions](https://www.icrc.org/sites/default/files/external/doc/en/assets/files/publications/icrc-002-0173.pdf):
  actual treaty text inspected, including common Article 3, GCIII status/doubt,
  transfer/treatment, civilian-court conditions, escape protections, criminal
  confinement and release, plus GCIV civilian-internment provisions. This is
  source text, not a full current treaty-party, protocol, customary-law or
  case-law assessment for our own hypothetical country. The database's separate
  attempted treaty PDF endpoints returned JavaScript HTML, not usable PDFs;
  the compilation supplied the inspected text instead.
- [Human Rights Committee General Comment 35 (2014), official rendered text](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=kg9V7WDnUjN0cH4CTgGQT%2BmDrcs8HcJ6GkrZ2RJd7AA0FNZZvQSc00d0sLsNtEUBLSnPXdtLYI2tuQdGzvXS%2BQ%3D%3D):
  inspected arbitrariness, published grounds, review, records and the complementary
  roles of human-rights and humanitarian law. This interpretive source supplies
  no national detention power and no complete extraterritorial case-law review.
- [Hague V (1907), primary treaty text reproduced by Yale Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/hague05.asp):
  inspected Articles 11–14 and 20 on neutral duties, distinct arrival categories
  and treaty applicability. The page reproduces a historical US State Department
  translation; it is not a 2026 government statement or present party/custom survey.

These are three documentary source families, not three independent outcome
studies or affected-party voices. Linked texts were read online; no complete
publisher-byte archive is claimed. The dated record states the inspected
scope and preserves local predecessor identities separately.

The 24-hour judge, seven-day full-status/child renewal, 30-day adult renewal,
clear-and-convincing status proof, limited noncustodial discipline and narrower
powers are proposed national choices, not universal treaty rules. Three
calendar months and 90 actual days are different bounds; source text does
not establish the safety of our shorter combined criminal ceiling.

No actual conflict, party status, territorial jurisdiction, recognized state,
qualified lawyer/clinician review, detainee consent, independent appointments,
funded safe capacity, foreign receiving agreement or better-life outcome has
been verified. Treaty/case-law and operational review could require material
revision; publication checks cannot certify legal compatibility or readiness.
