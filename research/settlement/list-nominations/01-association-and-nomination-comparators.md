# Association and nomination comparators

[List nominations](README.md)

German federal law provides a concrete institutional comparator.
Section 21(1), (3) and (6) requires a member or delegate assembly
for party constituency nominations, secret voting, proposal rights,
candidate presentation and a record of the procedure. Section 27(5)
applies specified provisions to state party lists and requires
attestation of secret voting for list order. Section 21(4) also
provides a specified constituency procedure for one leadership
objection and a new binding vote. This proposal uses neither a
leadership-triggered rerun nor the German public seat rule.
[Official section 21](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwahlg/__21.html)
· [Section 27 in the Returning Officer's English text](https://www.bundeswahlleiter.de/en/dam/jcr/4ff317c1-041f-4ba7-bbbf-1e5dc45097b3/bundeswahlgesetz_engl.pdf)

The Venice Commission and OSCE/ODIHR identify concentrated control
of candidate access and list ranking, including leadership screening
or ratification under ostensibly direct member voting. They also
defend party freedom from government interference and discuss
representation risks. Their guidance on party-statute disputes favors
internal exhaustion before civil court access. Here the constitutional
stakes and short public nomination calendar warrant a direct route for
a concrete statutory injury. Ordinary party disputes remain separate.
[Joint Guidelines, paragraphs 156–164](https://odihr.osce.org/sites/default/files/f/documents/8/1/538473.pdf)

These are an institutional example and official normative guidance.
They do not estimate how often candidates are excluded, which system
has better representatives or the cost and effect of this proposal.
The next empirical test needs an actual notice, protected roster and
receipt system, observed inclusion and dissent, independent execution
time and real cost.
