# Compare three arrangements under the same appointment failure

The Design259 arrangement uses nine core selectors for constitutional
and electoral legal-source appointments and adds three judges for the
ordinary formation. The ordinary appointment requires eight affirmative
votes of twelve and one per category. The core legal source needs six of
nine and one per category. All numbers and losses in this comparison
describe the proposed country, with availability counted after prior
lawful alternates. Each arithmetic pass also assumes a qualified
candidate, usable review and willing votes in the necessary categories.

| Declared condition | Shared core plus three | Separate ordinary formation | Separate all three formations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five core seats unavailable, with 1 judge, 1 practitioner and 2 lay selectors remaining | 4 core and 7 ordinary votes possible. All three legal-source pathways are blocked. | 4 core votes possible. The twelve separate ordinary selectors can consider a candidate. | The affected constitutional pool has 4. The other two can consider a candidate if actually staffed. |
| All three core lay seats unavailable | Core has six possible votes, ordinary has nine. Both lack the required category. | Core still lacks a category. Ordinary can consider a candidate with a staffed lay category. | Only the affected core is blocked if the other two lay pools can actually serve. |
| One controller makes the indispensable record or authority to spend unusable for every formation | All affected processes wait for a lawful path. | The ordinary process survives only if its own path works. | Additional appointers still fail if they depend on the same missing path. |

Separate ordinary selection uses 21 principal slots across the two
formations, against 12 shared principal slots at baseline. Fully separate
selection uses 30. Term-matched alternate slots and public participation
support are additional. These counts measure administrative scope and
cross-holding, with no claim about euros or the eventual availability of
people. Some qualified judges would spend time on selection rather than
hearings. A qualified, genuinely shared administration could still
lower total costs. A single vulnerable office cannot be credited as
three timely routes.

A standing working judge may hear cases within their actual jurisdiction
during a blockage; this cannot fill an appointing seat. If one selected
hearing route has two urgent duties and only one staffed slot, the case
schedule exposes one duty even where the new ordinary appointment can
eventually proceed. Ordinary access also depends on legal aid, case
handling and actual execution. No assumption turns an appointment
possibility into a timely hearing, a certified vote or a filled post.
