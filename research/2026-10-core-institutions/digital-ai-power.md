# Public digital power without a compulsory national platform

2 October 2026 · Supporting analysis for AIcountry C02–C06/C08/C11–C14 · Research recommendation, not law or deployment

## One national institutional decision

**Choose a layered, publicly governed and replaceable digital infrastructure for
public acts, while keeping legal authority with elected lawmakers and responsible
service institutions.** Do not make a single identity, data lake, AI service,
vendor or application the gate through which a person must pass to vote, receive
essentials, challenge a decision or form a public commitment. Common protocols
and minimum service duties can enable cooperation among institutions; they do not
make all communication or private life a state record.

The present [country design](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md) already rejects AI sovereignty,
requires provider exit, non-AI routes and replaceable civic media. Its [status
register](../DESIGN-STATUS.md) correctly marks those claims as untested. The gap is
the **institutional ownership and decision chain** for the shared layers beneath
each service: identity proof, authorization, data exchange, translation, discovery,
payments, audit and appeals. If the same controller authenticates a resident,
curates the facts shown to a caseworker, ranks civic speech and hosts the only
appeal channel, formally separate ministries can still share one effective veto.
Conversely, every agency building an incompatible system can make a benefit,
correction or lawful transfer unusable across boundaries. These are design
hypotheses about failure paths, not measured conditions in an AIcountry state.

The 16 September [sector manuscript snapshot](../2026-09-readiness/source-documents.json)
offers partial rules. `digital.privacy-act` limits processing, gives correction
and challenge rights and requires alternatives; `digital.ai-public-service` sets
task-level mandates and human escalation; `economy.market-finance` requires
contestable procurement; `digital.ai-legislature-act` supplies research-publication
controls. Several retain the older AI legislature or assume an already founded
state. This analysis does not enact, translate or replace those texts. The current
[working method](../FRAMEWORK.md) and [decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md)
are proposed research checks; an AI output is neither an independent public review
nor a vote.

## Different diagnoses and institutional options

| Architecture | What it solves and who controls it | Failure that matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Retain sector systems with contractual safeguards** | Each service ministry chooses its provider, with privacy law, procurement exit and an offline counter. The Assembly controls each mandate. | Shared cloud, identity, payment, translation and complaint providers may hold a cross-ministry veto that no one ministry can see or replace. Incompatible records can also defeat portability. This option has lower coordination cost and may remain preferable if real dependencies are small. |
| **One integrated state platform** | A national agency owns a universal credential, linked data store and service/AI interface. This can simplify cross-service verification and centralize security spending. The cabinet and agency can make rapid changes. | One actor can join sensitive domains and control access, surveillance, service delivery and political participation. A single outage or captured operator can become a national failure. An independent agency label does not remove its funding, appointment and credential dependencies. |
| **Selected research option: layered, plural public infrastructure** | The Assembly sets rights, permissible purposes, duties and funding; service authorities retain decisions and records; a public technical operator maintains common exchange and authentication standards but cannot set eligibility or censor speech; plural providers and assisted/offline routes remain usable; regulators and courts can inspect and order remedies. | Separation adds costs, security interfaces and coordination burdens. Apparent plurality can conceal the same cloud, financier or key custodian. A paper alternative can be too slow to protect a person. This option requires more than open formats. |

The selection does **not** constitutionally freeze a particular protocol, universal
ID, open-source license, cryptographic scheme or number of providers. Technology
can change and the whole arrangement can be challenged. The enduring proposed
commitments are public control of rulemaking, purpose-limited data power,
operationally usable exit, accessible equivalent routes, and remedies independent
of the challenged provider. A real electorate might reasonably choose a more
central service architecture where its risk, capacity and democratic controls
justify it; that value tradeoff cannot be settled from an authored table.

## Proposed chain of authority and protection

1. **Law first, technical standards second.** The elected Assembly defines rights,
   eligible persons, legally consequential acts, permitted and prohibited data uses,
   compulsory disclosures, remedies, oversight powers and appropriations. Cabinet
   and accountable civil servants implement. A public standards office can publish
   versioned, open interface and security specifications within that mandate,
   through notice, reasons, affected-user testing and review. Neither the office,
   a vendor nor an AI can change the legal meaning of residence, consent,
   disability, political standing or an entitlement by changing a schema.
   Opposition, local government and affected users can propose alternatives and
   challenge exclusion; there is no incumbent-only route to standard setting.
2. **Identity is a claim for a purpose, not a universal permission to exist.**
   Separate legal status, evidence of a fact, authentication of an act, and
   authorization to access a service. A proportional level of assurance depends
   on the action and its harm, with multiple ways to prove eligibility and safe
   correction of errors. No public service or ballot is lost solely because a
   person lacks a phone, biometric, commercial account or one credential.
   Anonymous or pseudonymous lawful speech need not use an entitlement credential.
   Registries may still need to prevent duplicate payment or votes; the legal
   purpose, data minimization, access rights and independent correction must be
   specified rather than solved by a national life log.
3. **Data exchange is scoped and inspectable.** Each agency remains responsible
   for its records and lawful purpose. A common exchange layer conveys the
   minimum authorized fact or evidence needed for a defined transaction, with
   source, transformation, recipient, retention, access record and correction
   path. Statutory authority, not nominal consent, is needed for compulsory
   exchange; freely given consent may support genuinely optional use but cannot
   be extracted as the price of unrelated necessities. Staff and systems can
   verify a relevant claim without silently joining every health, welfare,
   education, police and political record. Protected audit records are retained
   only as lawfully necessary; individuals can see and contest consequential
   data about them without exposing other people's sensitive testimony.
4. **Automation has a named public decision owner.** A service authority may use
   AI for analysis, translation, scheduling and reversible routine execution
   under a registered legal mandate. For liberty, a subsistence benefit,
   essential service, franchise or comparable consequential deprivation, an
   unattended system cannot be the final authority. A responsible officer must
   have the power, time, evidence and training to question the system, give
   intelligible grounds and direct interim protection or correction. A ceremonial
   human click is insufficient. Lawful urgent measures retain prompt adversarial
   review. The person can challenge source facts, rule application and the
   system's interpretation through an accessible channel that does not depend
   on the same model. Rules should define what can proceed automatically,
   what requires prior human judgment and what must never be delegated; the
   burden cannot be hidden in a generic “human in the loop” slogan.
5. **Audits and remedies follow power across the stack.** The data-protection
   authority examines processing, an independent auditor examines procurement
   and public performance, sector reviewers examine professional/benefit duties,
   election bodies protect the ballot and courts decide legality and rights.
   They require authorized access to records, relevant model behavior, suppliers
   and shared controllers; confidential details can be protected without making
   a consequential public decision unchallengeable. Public reasons and aggregate
   error/service measures must be intelligible, with privacy protection.
   Appointments, removal, funding, information access and execution of orders
   need the C03 safeguards. A different AI model reviewing the same data and
   sponsor is neither an independent institution nor affected-party participation.
6. **Prove continuity before a critical dependency is used.** Procurement must
   disclose beneficial control and subproviders, protect lawful access to code or
   specifications needed for audit, and arrange timely export of data *and legal
   relationships*, records, mandates, keys and unfinished cases. Exit is tested
   with an alternate operator or maintained public capacity, staffing and a
   funded overlap; open formats alone do not operate a service. A compromised
   AI or vendor can be paused, credentials revoked, evidence preserved and people
   notified while the valid law, benefit and appeal remain in force. A manual or
   alternate route must be able to receive an application, issue an effective
   receipt, deliver an urgent remedy and later reconcile records without double
   commitments. Emergency shutdown does not grant the operator new legislative
   or coercive authority.

**Non-digital equivalence** means a person can obtain the same legal standing,
application date, intelligible reason, protection against deprivation, challenge
and effective remedy through accessible assisted, paper, in-person or other
appropriate routes. It does not require every medium to look alike. Fund the staff,
language access, physical sites, time limits and record reconciliation that make
the alternative usable for disabled, rural, displaced or digitally excluded
people. Track delays and outcomes by channel; a nominal desk open once a month is
not equivalent when a benefit expires today. Conversely, an offline office should
not be exempt from privacy and error controls.

## Future agents, mediation and the right to change the architecture

Public communication may use speech, images, collective practices, personal
agents or future media. Common infrastructure should carry the *legal effect* of
an act where necessary, without requiring every native expression to become
standard text. Keep expression, interpretation, delegation, assent and execution
distinct. An agent's mandate states principal, scope, duration, resource bounds,
reserved decisions, subdelegation and revocation; copies do not multiply votes or
budgets. An authentication signature proves an attributed act under a method,
not comprehension, truth or valid political authority. An offline agent may act
only within prior bounds and expiry; disconnection cannot be treated as consent
to a changed mandate.

Mediators can frame, rank, translate and hide choices. Their governance is not
solved by neutral file exchange. People must be able to inspect a consequential
summary, contest omitted or transformed meaning, reach another mediator or
direct channel, and preserve unfamiliar concepts as unresolved. The Assembly's
lawfully enacted text and authorized records govern binding public acts. New
media can be admitted for exploration before they qualify for commitment or
official publication. A later public decision may replace the exchange
architecture itself, with transition of pending claims, delegations, cases and
records. Today's contract cannot claim to encode every future social practice;
protected rights and democratic authority cannot silently disappear during a
technical migration. The earlier [interaction contract](../2026-09-collaboration-foundations/interaction-contract.md)
is useful research on these semantics, not enacted public procedure.

## An authored failure and the remaining objection

Suppose a contractor supplies identity verification, cloud storage, case triage,
translation and the only online appeal portal to several ministries. Its parent
also funds a dominant civic discovery service. After a lawful election defeat,
it rejects some residents' credentials, downgrades opposition notices, and blocks
the incoming cabinet's access to pending benefits. Each ministry's contract has
an export clause, but no alternate operator has practiced restoring the records
or receiving appeals. A second AI model sharing the contractor's data and cloud
repeats its assurance verdict.

The selected arrangement requires the public institution—not the vendor—to hold
the valid decision and succession mandate, independent custody of authenticated
records and lawful keys, separate civic publication and appeal routes, a funded
alternate operator or manual capacity, and cross-system scrutiny of common
control. Courts and investigators can order access, but a remedy succeeds only
if people and systems can execute it promptly. If the same political coalition
controls the standards office, regulator, courts, budget and alternate operator,
the architecture still fails. This is an authored stress test, not an observation
or a readiness certificate.

The strongest objection is that decentralized custody and multiple routes can
increase cost, inconsistent service, cyber exposure and confusion. The proposed
common standards and purpose-limited exchange answer some coordination problems,
but they do not prove security or affordability. Some functions may need one
shared operator; the public must then confront its concentration explicitly,
separate its policy and operational authority, preserve independent access and
test recovery. Another objection is that mandatory explanation or public audit
could disclose sensitive data or security details; protected reviewer access,
scoped reasons and reviewable withholding are preferable to absolute secrecy or
universal publication. A human official may reproduce an AI error or prejudice.
Training, workload, independent challenge and outcome measurement matter as much
as the formal person signing a decision.

## Support, integration and bounded continuation

[UNDP's Universal Digital Public Infrastructure Safeguards Framework](https://dpi-safeguards.org/framework)
describes safety, inclusion and structural risks across the infrastructure life
cycle; [OHCHR's digital-risk policy brief](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/issues/development/dfi/OHCHR-DFI-Digital-Policy-Brief-Sept-2024.pdf)
identifies surveillance and exclusion risks from linked digital ID systems.
[NIST's 2025 Digital Identity Guidelines](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/)
separate proofing, authentication and federation, and discuss security, privacy
and accessibility. [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)
is a voluntary lifecycle risk-management reference, currently under revision.
[OECD's Digital Government Outlook 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/digital-government-outlook_0496b2bc-en/full-report/strengthening-digital-public-infrastructure-and-data-governance_2c7323c7.html)
discusses shared infrastructure, data governance, interoperability and resilience.
These are primary institutional sources for the general risks and design questions;
none endorses this model or measures AIcountry's capacity. They were inspected
on 2 October 2026. No real affected person or independent public reviewer
participated in this analysis.

**Changes needed in the main design if selected:** C11 should name the Assembly,
service authorities, technical operator, regulators and courts as distinct owners
of law, decisions, protocols, audits and remedies. C12 should require scoped
identity/data exchange, mandate and mediation checks across future media. C02,
C03 and C08 should make effective non-digital standing and challenge explicit;
C04/C06 should fund local assisted access, alternate operators and continuity;
C13 should test a common-provider capture/outage; C14 should include digital
credentials, records, legal mandates, vendor contracts and pending cases in
founding/handover migration. The status register should identify the unreconciled
`digital.*` and procurement clauses and costs. This would be a **research
selection**, not an amendment to current publication permissions or law.

**Evidence qualification:** only the current document gap, local manuscript
provisions and cited external source positions were inspected. **Legal
authorization:** absent; the old AI-exclusive clauses remain in the unrevised
manuscript. **Operational readiness:** absent; no service inventory, ownership
map, capacity test, case data, offline staffing or budget exists. **Observed
outcomes:** none. The main design and status baselines inspected for this note
were SHA-256 `8513013a92d6f74312d32029702c800dd0dda7d6b15f8327ffeb4d5fb4517229`
and `2726cd2c2c9e8dd4abc7b02ab81ecfd5b830616d40564ed097796ac60872800f`.
The working method and decision template remain proposed; a later revision of
those documents or new events would require fresh review rather than transfer
of this note's qualification.

**Next bounded question:** in a specified lawful jurisdiction, which real
controllers jointly hold identity, records, payments, AI, hosting, civic
discovery and appeals, and can an essential service continue through a tested
change of provider and a person without a digital credential? The deliverable
would be one affected-service dependency map and witnessed transition exercise
with cost, rights impact, dissent and executed remedies. If actual evidence shows
that the layered design worsens exclusion, cannot secure data, cannot fund
equivalent channels or only recreates common control, reconsider it against the
integrated-platform and sector-autonomy alternatives. Until then, maintain
existing lawful services and claims; do not turn a research preference into a
mandatory identity system.
