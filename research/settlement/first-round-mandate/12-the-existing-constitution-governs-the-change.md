# The existing constitution governs the change

*Design547 · Denmark as a constitutional comparator for a lawful retrofit.*

Denmark already has parliamentary government, rights and local
self-government. Compare the proposed destination against actual Danish
institutions with workable repairs. This comparison uses the Danish
Constitutional Act as the legal baseline. It selects neither a jurisdiction
for adoption nor an interpretation binding on Danish authorities. Give the
Faroe Islands and Greenland their actual representation and applicable
rights in every affected decision. A Copenhagen operating example supplies
no authority over the rest of the Realm.

Improve independently held records, accessible correction and fact-finding where law permits. The candidate would replace final parliamentary adjudication for defined election disputes. Sections 28 and 31 address membership and electoral structure, including territorial representation. | Keep the existing electoral law for the intervening contest and the amendment decision. Add the proposed one-year resident franchise and linked candidacy prospectively through constitutional change and implementing law. ## What happens if reform loses

Under section 88, if the Folketing passes an amendment proposal and the
government pursues it, a general election follows. The newly elected
Folketing must pass the proposal unchanged. A referendum on it follows
within six months of the final passage. Approval requires a majority of
those taking part and affirmative votes from at least 40 percent of all
eligible electors. Royal assent is also required. A material amendment
to the text cannot substitute for the unchanged second passage.
[Constitutional Act, section 88](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/1953/169)

Price continuity across the actual interval between the election and
the new Folketing's competent sitting. Section 32 sets the predecessor
seat-vacancy event at the election. The candidate's different Assembly
continuity rule supplies no authority in this interval. Arrange any needed
lawful ordinary or provisional supply in advance under sections 45–46.
Keep authorized ministry and career operation within its current scope.

For a constructed electorate of 1,000,000, suppose 700,000 vote and
390,000 support the proposal. Even though affirmative votes make up
more than half of participation, the vote falls short of 400,000.
The proposed resident franchise, new appointment rule and final review
powers therefore do not start on that authority. Compatible ordinary
funding, repair and existing deadlines continue. A person who has been
a resident for one year has the actual predecessor vote rights and
legal remedies until the new rule has lawfully commenced. The proposal
may be brought again only through the competent route.

In a second constructed branch, 410,000 of 700,000 support the proposal.
The two numerical conditions fit. Verify unchanged passage,
the original referendum result, royal assent and the exact commencement
before treating any new authority as operative. Preserve separately due
benefits, staff and records through the transition. Fund an independent
and lawful route for review in the intervening period rather than
depending on the proposed future court. Two arithmetic branches test
the distinction between public support and legal effect. Neither
represents an actual proposal, vote, authorized budget or public finding.
