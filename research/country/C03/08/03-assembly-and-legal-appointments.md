# Constitutional review: Assembly and mixed legal sources

*Canonical CJ05–CJ06; part of [C03/08](../08-constitutional-review-and-public-control.md).*

**CJ05 — Assembly source.** Three of nine seats derive from the Assembly, one
per cohort. Appointment requires both ceil(3N/5) affirmative votes, where N is
full statutory membership including vacancies, and ceil(O/3) affirmative votes
from the protected non-government cohort. Absence, abstention and an unfilled seat
do not lower either denominator. If O is zero, this source cannot appoint.
Every candidate must meet CJ04; no source inherits another source's vacant seat.

The governing cohort comprises cabinet participation and publicly recorded
coalition or confidence-and-supply agreements. One policy vote or occasional
support does not alone make someone a governing member. Publish the named
non-government seat positions and denominator when the process opens: O counts
occupied seats not established to be governing under the closed definition above.
An unsupported allegation or unresolved classification does not subtract a seat
from O. Initially vacant seats do not enter O or supply its approval, but remain
in N. Classification disputes receive prompt external review before an appointment
takes effect; no office may withhold classification to lower the threshold.
A position's lawful successor may supply approval only while outside government.
Subsequent vacancy does not reduce O; a member now in government cannot supply
opposition approval. Individual switches do not retrospectively count votes. A genuinely new government
or governing agreement requires published reassessment and a fresh vote;
previous candidate approvals do not transfer. Disputed classifications receive
external CJ12 review, without a minister's power to declare critics inauthentic.

Named Assembly votes and reasons are public. Shared nomination control or
funders across nominal opponents remain visible risks, not a basis for removing
lawful opposition votes by ideological certification. This is a bargaining
constraint; it cannot prove independent judgment or prevent coordinated capture.

**CJ06 — Mixed legal source.** Three seats derive from a nine-member selection
body: three judges elected by judges through a contestable secret peer vote,
three legal practitioners similarly elected by practitioners, and three paid
lay electors selected by the inclusive CJ07 draw method. No court president,
ministry or professional association owns a closed nominations list. The first
receiving register records every eligible self-nomination and challenge.
Professional rolls and exclusions are published with private-data protection
and CJ12 review; unregistered lawful relevant practice has a direct route to
prove statutory eligibility instead of buying guild membership.

For an inaugural professional three-seat election, each eligible voter may approve
up to three candidates; the three highest totals win. Thereafter an election for
m actual vacancies permits up to m approvals and selects m highest totals.
Publish each term/remaining-term assignment before voting, allocating higher
totals to longer available terms; a public draw resolves equal totals or terms.
This selects the body, not a constitutional judge.
Members serve six years, nonrenewable; one per category expires every two years.
Initial two/four/six-year category terms are drawn before nominations. Replacement
fills the original term under the same category's process. In each professional
election, the next m consenting eligible finishers become term-matched alternates
in the same longest-term-first order; publish this consequence before voting.
Lay alternates are the next pre-drawn eligible people assigned to the same term
slots. Too few eligible consenting finishers leave alternate places unfilled.
No person holds two body votes or primary/alternate places simultaneously.
No accusation alone activates an alternate. A member cannot assess or vote on their own
candidacy or a direct personal conflict; use only the preselected eligible
alternate. The fixed denominator stays nine. Failed substitution leaves a
selection failure, not a conflicted vote or an ad hoc selector.

A constitutional appointment requires six affirmative votes of the fixed nine,
including at least one from each category, with at least six lawful members
present. The body hears open qualified candidates and publishes reasons, conflicts
and actual votes. It cannot bind the candidate pools of CJ05 or CJ07. Existing
ordinary-judge selection may share this body only through express prior law,
conflict separation and accounted simultaneous capacity, never by assumption.
Category veto and professional clustering are retained costs; failure does not
lower six votes or transfer the seat to Cabinet or the constitutional court.

The current [OJ06–OJ10](../../C08/25/02-selectors-appointments-and-founding.md)
proposal uses a separate ordinary formation. The earlier shared formation
remains a comparison. Core CJ06 retains its nine seats, six-vote threshold
and original terms and alternates. [EA21–EA25](../09/06-independent-electoral-selection.md)
assigns prospective electoral legal-source appointments to a third formation.
Apply the corresponding migration rules to live selection and hearing duties.
