# PT01–PT06: distinguish an asset, its owner and permission to transfer

*Part of [C08/26](../26-property-transfers-and-secured-credit.md); proposed ordinary law.*

**PT01 — Scope and hierarchy.** Apply to domestic land rights, ordinary goods
and transferable private claims within B41–B52's actual forum/law. Distinguish
ownership, possession, custody, security, occupancy, contractual use, public
restriction and collective/downstream rights. M payment/deposit/finality, NB
custody/securities/clearing, KR knowledge rights, family/estate, H, NF and LA
retain express special rules; PT does not create a missing special exception.
A body, personal data, vote, public office, clinical choice or agent mandate is
not ordinary saleable collateral. A digital representation identifies only
rights actually established under its source law/instrument, not every thing it
depicts. Retain unfamiliar meanings and disputed classification for safe review.

**PT02 — The actual holder's rights and limits.** A lawful holder may possess,
use, exclude and transfer within others' rights and elected law. Ownership
does not authorize unsafe operation, eviction, ecological injury, forced labor
or immunity from tax. No planning permission, paid tax, incorporation, map,
AI match, long occupation or new register entry independently creates title.
Lawful original production/acquisition must identify its actual inputs and
source permissions; processing stolen goods cannot automatically cleanse them.
Possession is rebuttable evidence, not a duty on an absent/displaced holder to
prove permanent online visibility. B38 true-holder restoration after a void/
fraudulent transfer remains, with actual defenses, notice and independent proof.
This Act creates no merchant-entrustment, general bona-fide-purchase or adverse-
possession exception to someone else's ownership. A repaired or borrowed item
does not become the repairer's or borrower's stock to sell. Actual B04 authority
or lawful attributable holding-out needs separate proof, not possession alone.

**PT03 — Ordinary goods.** A transfer needs lawful disposition power, a real
agreement or other exact legal source, and identifiable existing goods. Default
title passes on actual agreed delivery, including a third-party custodian's
verified acknowledgment that it now holds the item for the buyer. Parties may
clearly choose earlier/later passage for identified existing goods; do not
infer it from payment, an invoice, generic inventory or an agent copy alone.
Unidentified/future goods create a delivery claim until actually identified to
the agreement and lawfully transferable. The same item cannot be wholly conveyed
twice: a later buyer obtains only the transferor's remaining right. Title, risk,
paid price and possession are separate; a consumer supplier bears accidental
delivery loss until actual agreed receipt, except the consumer's proved own
causal wrong. A title-retention clause securing price uses PT07–PT12, not a
secret ownership device to defeat the buyer/estate rules. No universal transfer
register, obligatory notary or internet account for ordinary household goods.

**PT04 — Ordinary claims and assignments.** An actual holder can assign a
transferable identified claim or objectively determinable future commercial
receivable; the latter takes effect only as it actually arises. Give the new
holder protected original proof of scope, amount, defenses, partial interests
and prior assignments; one debt cannot become multiple full payment duties.
The debtor may discharge by proper payment to the old creditor until receiving
usable authenticated assignment/payment-direction notice, and may demand
reasonable verification without losing defenses or being charged twice. After
verified notice pay the actual entitled recipient; genuine competing notices
permit court-directed protected escrow, not double payment or automatic default.
Accrued defenses and same-contract setoff survive; no new cross-client or
restricted-public-fund setoff. Statutory nontransferability and personal duties
remain. Ordinary outright claim transfers compete by their actual lawful time;
a later assignment cannot convey an already assigned share. For a future claim,
the earliest binding lawful outright assignment reserves only the actually
arising transferable right, subject to a previously binding valid security
agreement. That assigned portion is unavailable to later security grants;
advance notice alone without a binding security agreement cannot cure the
later grantor's missing power. A security agreement reserves only its actual
burden, not full ownership; competing valid securities still require PT10's
public-effectiveness rank. These private source interests require actual proof;
a clean security search does not certify that no assignment exists.
An assignment used as security follows PT07, whatever its label; NB custody/market settlement has
its separate actual law. A uniform commercial-receivable filing regime remains
a genuine rival, not an exception silently borrowed from the security register.

**PT05 — Land conveyance and competing applications.** A new voluntary
conveyance, land mortgage or expressly created permanent private easement needs
an accessible authenticated instrument identifying actual parties/authority,
parcel/right/share, terms and retained rights, and lawful registration. Paper,
supported oral execution with an independent paid record/witness, and future
media can establish the same deliberate act; no technology proves consent.
A sale contract alone creates B duties, not completed land ownership. A valid
executed instrument lodged at protected first receipt reserves its position;
among competing authorized instruments for the same disposable right, earliest
valid lodgment prevails on lawful registration. A missing-authority filing has
no priority. Knowing dishonest collusion cannot acquire priority by racing an
injured party. No private paid reservation slots or priority by party influence.
Registration completes the new title prospectively; the filing position is not
retroactive ownership, a tax-owned day or permission to evict. A later defeated
buyer keeps actual seller/remedy claims, not invented additional land.

Existing lawful unregistered, customary/collective, family, trust, tenant and
court-created rights are not extinguished by first registration. Hear and show
them as qualifications where material; disputed boundaries are not precise
because a model draws a line. A registrar cannot grant first ownership by
calling a contested parcel vacant. B/H/LA and ordinary courts govern actual
rights, possession and compulsory acquisition. No registration of ordinary
residential tenancies is required for H protection.

**PT06 — Shared property and practical use.** A holder may transfer/pledge
only their own actual share unless separately authorized. Distinguish shared
title from co-residence, an adult tie, unpaid care or access assistance. Shares
follow actual lawful agreement/source and B-family/estate adjustments; if joint
acquisition is proved but its proportions remain unproved after fair evidence,
presume equal shares rebuttably. Do not presume joint acquisition itself.
Ordinary preservation costs and actual agreed use require fair accounting;
unilateral improvements cannot force another holder into a new loan. An affected
holder may seek consensual division/buyout or ordinary-court partition on fair
valuation, notice, practical divisibility and protected child/tenant/collective/
ecological interests. A sale is not automatically the first remedy. Safe home
receiving and separately owed claims persist, without forcing indefinite unpaid
private shelter. Common-building structural repairs, safety and shared-service
costs follow actual lawful ownership terms, H/technical duties and B judicial
relief; no manager/majority may sell another unit or alter shares by maintenance
vote. Missing special building terms stay visible rather than authorizing a
new private municipality or using this clause to seize dissenters' homes.

[CB01–CB24](../../C09/14-shared-buildings-and-neighbor-rights.md) now supplies the
previously missing common-building decisions: necessary maintenance despite
vote deadlock, defined owner cost/asset authority, resident funded-use ballots,
protected shares/funds, actual repair/access and independent redress. PT06
private-unit/share/partition limits remain; a CB majority or new charge cannot
rewrite old mortgages/title, impose a lien or give a manager public sovereignty.
