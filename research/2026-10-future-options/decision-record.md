# Future options in irreversible public decisions

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 36 research selection; not enacted law

Prepared by an AI assistant without an affected electorate, youth or future
interest claimants, Indigenous rights-holders, courts, operators or a lawful
territory. The exact [design-35 predecessor](predecessor-design-35/README.md)
is preserved. This selection changes a working country design only.

## Defect, values and competing diagnoses

C01 promises meaningful options to future generations. C06 counts long-lived
obligations; C09 names ecology and future-generation representation; C14 reports
future capacity. None yet gives one operative rule for deciding a material loss
that later voters cannot reverse. A present majority can approve the visible
benefit while later people inherit depleted water, maintenance, debt or
pollution. Yet a future-interest veto could block urgently needed present
housing or let a self-appointed expert displace democratic choice. The value
conflict is real: protection of later freedom versus current need and equal
political control. No single discount rate or national happiness score resolves it.

The [UN Declaration on Future Generations](https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/annex-ii-declaration-future-generations)
recognizes long-term needs and intergenerational effects; it does not enact
AIcountry's law. [Wales's statutory sustainable-development duty](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2015/2/section/5)
is a comparator for considering long and short terms, integration and affected
people. The [German Federal Constitutional Court's 2021 climate decision](https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2021/bvg21-031.html)
illustrates judicial scrutiny where present emissions shift severe constraints
onto later freedom. These are three distinct legal and policy contexts, not
empirical proof that one template improves lives or fits this unformed country.

## Coherent institutional choices

| Package | What it could protect | What it could fail to protect |
|---|---|---|
| Retain current sector accounts and general C14 reporting | Low administrative load; elected discretion | Irreversible loss can be approved before the term-end report, split into small permits or hidden outside a sector map |
| Give a future-generations commissioner a veto over material projects | A clear early stop against short-term politics | Selection, evidence, funding and removal could be captured; an unelected veto could deny current needs or claim to represent people not yet born |
| Require a national ballot for each long-lived commitment | Direct visible authorization | Complex repeated ballots can reward sponsors, bypass local competence and omit nonvoters or external affected people |
| **Prior-law future-options duty at competent elected scope** | Protects legal floors, makes alternatives, losses and restoration visible before irreversible action, with challenge and elected choice | Evidence and participation cost time; majority can accept a damaging lawful risk; no present institution can fully represent future preferences |

The selected [C09 rule](../country/C09/04-future-options-and-irreversibility.md)
uses a prospective materiality test tied to persistence, non-restorability,
cumulative effects and affected scope. The responsible elected authority
must examine no-project, repair, lower-use and staged paths and state how
maintenance, debt and restoration will be delivered. Enforceable rights and
ecological floors remain law; beyond those floors the Assembly or competent
local council makes the contested value choice. Courts enforce competence,
reason-giving, evidence access and existing law, not a preferred project.
No new permanent commissioner or central score is selected.

## Authority, distribution, capture and continuity

The Assembly defines the trigger, floors, local powers and review route by
prospective law; voters can replace its members. Local councils decide only
inside assigned competence. Operators and C09 stewards provide source
observations; C11 preserves versions; C06 tests common capacity and fiscal
obligations. Opposition and affected people can challenge omissions; C08
courts can give interim relief where later correction is useless. Inspect the
entire appointment, budget, evidence, permit, review and enforcement chain.
A captured majority could narrow the trigger, a captured steward could hide
losses, and a nominally independent reviewer could share the operator's
provider. Publication and a court order alone do not deliver restoration.

Presently affected people include those needing jobs, homes and services as
well as children, tenants, workers, Indigenous and other collective-rights
holders, downstream users and people across borders. A youth hearing provides
current young people's views, not consent from unborn people. Neither a
representative body nor an AI can infer the latter's preferences. A private
bond must survive insolvency and ecological delay; a public guarantee and
unfunded future liability belong in C06's common account. Existing permits,
property interests, treaty duties, accrued claims and pending cases survive
an authority change until valid law assigns funded successors. Staged approval
must identify a real stop before the irreversible step; expiry does not undo
completed damage. Emergency protection stays within C06/C10 prior-law powers.
An enacted material-warning trigger requires lawful interim safety and a fresh
competent elected choice before the next irreversible stage; silence does not
renew permission.
Where repair is physically impossible, the residual loss remains explicit;
promised monitoring or an imaginary offset is not restoration capacity.

This selection is compatible with C03's scoped authority, C06's commitment
account, C08's court powers, C09's sector duties, C14's public conditions and
candidate A11; those are its principal consumers. It transfers no competence
and changes no current permit or entitlement by publication. If legitimately
adopted, a new statute must inventory existing approvals and restoration
security, apply its trigger prospectively subject to higher law, preserve
accrued claims and name funded successors for pending monitoring and repair.
This research record cannot authorize itself or retrospectively annul a permit.

The strongest objection is that the new process can delay essential present
provision. Apply it to material, hard-to-reverse choices, use existing C06/C09
records, and let elected bodies choose within enforceable floors; compare
repair and lower-use alternatives with the unmet need, rather than imposing
an automatic moratorium. A second objection is that reason-giving merely
legitimizes present exploitation. Keep source access, affected standing,
interim review, actual restoration funding and C14's later public account;
repeated unlawful or harmful losses reopen the constitutional floor and the
choice of institution. Neither safeguard establishes that future welfare is
secured. A guardian-veto advocate can still reasonably dissent from the
selected democratic balance; its capture and legitimacy costs remain unresolved.

## Status and bounded next test

Evidence qualification: the cited official sources establish comparators and
legal possibilities; the gap in C01/C06/C09/C14 is an internal design finding.
Research selection: a prior-law future-options duty at the competent elected
scope with enforceable floors and no general guardian veto. Legal
authorization, operating readiness and observed outcomes: none. No affected
people or independent reviewers participated, and no original project or
resource data was inspected. This is not a national ecological standard.

For one real contested project, the competent public authority would need a
lawful territory and trigger, original stock and harm observations, current
need, alternatives including no build and repair, fiscal and restoration
capacity, excluded and external people's accounts, and an enforceable review
path. Compare the selected duty against sector-only scrutiny and a bounded
independent guardian with their full operating costs. The role, budget ceiling
and authority to run that test remain unassigned. Reopen the design if it
allows cumulative irreversible harm, delays basic rights without benefit,
shifts costs abroad, or lets a reviewing body choose policy without public
control. A reasoned no-change result remains possible.
