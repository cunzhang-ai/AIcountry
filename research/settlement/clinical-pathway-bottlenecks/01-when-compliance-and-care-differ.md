# When legal compliance and a timely start differ

*Design517 · Observed administrative benchmark and first budget disposition.*

The Danish Health Authority's fourth-quarter 2025 publication records covered diagnostic and treatment starts in domestic public hospitals. It distinguishes starts within maximum waits, later starts where the rules were reported as met, and cases where the rules were not met. The national counts from Tables 1, 1a and 1b reproduce the following results.

| Observed episode scope | Total | Within time | Later, rules met | Rules not met |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Diagnostic starts | 55,059 | 52,582 (95.50%) | 2,458 | 19 |
| Treatment starts, first and follow-up | 13,350 | 12,080 (90.49%) | 1,264 | 6 |
| Both episode categories | 68,409 | 64,662 (94.52%) | 3,722 | 25 |

The counts show 99.96% reported legal compliance and 94.52% starting within time among these episodes. Use the published tables for reproducible counts. Some prose percentages differ; the [source record](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-clinical-pathway-bottlenecks/sources.md) preserves that discrepancy. In the late but compliant subset, diagnostic records mostly cite patient wish (2,296 of 2,458), while treatment records mostly cite health (859 of 1,264). Capacity is the recorded reason for nine diagnostic and 43 treatment starts in that subset only. The data do not establish the reason for the 25 noncompliant starts or all delays, and group timings cannot be subtracted to estimate the interval from diagnosis to treatment. The same person may appear more than once. Cases not started in the period and overseas transfers require distinct evidence. Treat participation, patient wishes and clinically justified timing as real choices and judgments. Ask independently whether a recorded decline reflected an impractical offer.

## Compare where the recommended pathway loses time

A separate [Danish Health Data Authority workbook series](https://sundhedsdatabank.dk/sygdomme/kraeft-menu/kraeftmonitorering) reports recommended pathway times. The following rows are drawn from Region Zealand (Sjælland) within one retained workbook vintage. The percentages and counts appear as published.

| Pathway and clock | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lung cancer, referral to first attendance (OF1) | 62% of 677 | 37% of 707 |
| Lung cancer, referral to initial surgery (OF4A) | 53% of 58 | 59% of 49 |
| Colorectal cancer, diagnostic completion to initial surgery (OF3A) | 44% of 108 | 33% of 78 |
| Colorectal cancer, referral to initial surgery (OF4A) | 68% of 107 | 68% of 79 |

These are distinct completed indicator populations and clocks. The total surgical-pathway percentage for lung cancer can rise while first-attendance performance declines. Likewise, colorectal surgery preparation can be a local audit candidate despite unchanged total-pathway percentage. They are diagnostic leads about the observed service scope. They do not establish a shared patient trajectory, dropout rate, exact numerator or causal capacity deficit. Check local changes in pathway timing definitions and case mix before interpreting movements.

## Decide what the first expansion can credibly buy

**Select a time-limited pathway audit and funded receiving repair on the evidence available.** In the observed scopes, open a lung first-attendance audit and a separate colorectal pre-surgery audit before placing any larger eligible expansion in the budget. Require the responsible region to cost an accepting alternate at a defined origin and receiving service, with safe transport, interpretation, original dates and actual follow-up. Check open requests and unsuccessful transfers separately. The evidence warrants checking treatment-stage delay and actual receiving; it does not justify an across-the-board expansion of diagnostic or treatment places. Keep existing urgent treatment and access protections funded.

Compare three exact alternatives. Where a usable slot exists and booking, travel, cost or communication blocks transfer, favor resolving that limit with ordinary qualified staff and approved support. Where qualified observations show the specific receiving service has no available timely capacity even after access and booking repair, bring a staffed local or independently accepting alternate increment to elected choice with original demand, capacity and donor burdens. Where medically appropriate time or genuinely informed patient choice explains the remaining delay, maintain those protections while verifying follow-up. If unresolved, publish the actual unknown, fund only a qualified narrower change and keep lawful review and interim protection.

Follow the same people through a useful offer, acceptance or free refusal, timely actual start and the next necessary service where legally observable. An institutional alternative wins only on the effect it can support for the same population and obligations. AI can help reconcile dates, compare proposals and spot a failed handoff. It cannot treat a legal referral as completed care or make a clinical decision.
