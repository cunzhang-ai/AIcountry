# PQ01–PQ03. Reserve only the acts that need prior permission

*Part of [fair licensing](../32-fair-licensing-and-portable-competence.md).*

**PQ01 — Start with what people do and who can be harmed.** Keep lawful
work open unless a sector Act reserves specified acts or protects a
defined title. For a new or materially expanded restriction, the Assembly
must identify the harm, affected people including third parties, and
why ordinary liability, public information, voluntary certification,
inspection, or a protected title would leave serious harm insufficiently
prevented. Consider cumulative barriers to entry, including actual wait
and price. A title restriction alone cannot reserve the work or require
membership. Existing employment, civil protection, safeguards against
misrepresentation and liability for actual harm apply at every level.

**PQ02 — Keep the people who benefit from restrictions from controlling them.**
The sector regulator publishes its proposal and an independent
proportionality opinion before an elected decision. A statutory public
panel appointed through the existing V10 qualified process supplies
that opinion. The appointment slate includes technical, service-user,
and competition or equality competence. The panel excludes members
with a material current stake in the restriction, publishes interests,
and uses funded predesignated replacements. People who use or pay
for services, entrants, small operators, workers and incumbents can
contest the factual basis through accessible, protected routes. A
trade body or school may submit evidence. It cannot set quotas, pick
individual examiners or certify its own restriction. The Assembly
records reasons on less restrictive options. Ordinary courts can
correct an unlawful rule or application within existing powers.

**PQ03 — Reassess a material barrier while protecting continuity.** The
law sets a review date no later than five years after commencement or
the last completed review. The independent panel reports whether the
rule reduced serious harm and what it cost in actual entry, service
access and waiting, with confounding changes and missing evidence
shown. Give affected people a route to seek earlier review when
documented harm, exclusion or material change warrants it. The Assembly
retains, narrows, replaces or repeals the reservation through law.
The law reserves committee and floor time for a recorded decision
within 90 calendar days of the review due date, including an
opposition proposal to narrow the restriction. If the panel report
is missing, the committee records the missing evidence, funds a
replacement review and sets its date. It cannot treat missing
evidence as proof of proportionality. A missed report is challengeable under ordinary review and gives
neither the panel new powers nor an applicant authority to perform a
reserved act. Any continuing restriction still needs a lawful basis.
During revision, preserve existing lawful permissions and pending
claims under explicit transition rules. Address an evidenced immediate
hazard under the existing safety power, with useful-time review.
