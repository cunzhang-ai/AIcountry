# Wales. Survival and healthy years differ across the same places

The Welsh Government's October 2026 wellbeing report describes a
recovery of overall life expectancy at birth to pre-pandemic levels
in 2022–2024 and a continuing decline in healthy life expectancy.
This shows why the two outcomes must be compared separately.
It does not attribute either change to the wellbeing framework.
[Welsh Government report, healthier Wales](https://www.gov.wales/wellbeing-wales-2026-healthier-wales-html)

The Office for National Statistics gives the following period
estimates for the most and least deprived Welsh area quintiles.
Both outcomes use years at birth in 2022–2024. Differences are simple
subtraction of the published rounded endpoints.
[ONS April 2026 bulletin, section 3](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/healthandsocialcare/healthinequalities/bulletins/healthylifeexpectancybynationalareadeprivationenglandandwales/between2013to2015and2022to2024)

| Measure and reported sex | Most deprived areas | Least deprived areas | Endpoint difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Life expectancy, males | 73.6 | 81.9 | 8.3 |
| Healthy life expectancy, males | 49.7 | 67.8 | 18.1 |
| Life expectancy, females | 78.4 | 85.1 | 6.7 |
| Healthy life expectancy, females | 47.3 | 66.3 | 19.0 |

The ONS regression-based slope index of inequality is a distinct
measure. These endpoint gaps are not that index. The estimates
describe areas, not a person's income, forecast lifespan or the
cause of the differences. Confidence intervals and survey methods
matter. England and Wales use different deprivation classifications
and group sizes. Do not use these area rows for an England–Wales
ranking. The ONS and Welsh Government reports share underlying
source evidence rather than providing two independent observations.

The OECD report distinguishes outcomes, gaps and resources for
future wellbeing and shows differing data vintages by indicator.
Its framework is a way to compare what is supported, without
treating all areas and time periods as directly comparable.
[OECD How's Life? 2024, chapter 1](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-s-life-2024_90ba854a-en/full-report/component-5.html)
