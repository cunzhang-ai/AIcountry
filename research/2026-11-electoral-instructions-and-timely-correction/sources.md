# Evidence for electoral instructions and timely correction

| Primary source | What the record shows | Use and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Federal Communications Commission, FCC 24-104 forfeiture order](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-104A1.pdf) | The 2024 order describes prerecorded calls shortly before the New Hampshire primary. The message imitated President Biden's voice, encouraged potential voters not to vote in the primary and used misleading caller identification. The FCC imposed a penalty under the applicable caller-ID rules. | Verify the source and the practical election instruction separately. The order does not measure changed turnout, establish actual correction reach or supply a content-removal rule for the proposed country. |
| [Elections Canada, Electoral participation and digital literacy](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?dir=int&document=dig&lang=e&section=vot) | The official voter guidance directs readers to the election administrator for operational facts about where, when and how to register and vote, with separate information about digital verification. | A usable authoritative fact route can be distinguished from judging political argument. The guidance does not establish the effectiveness of the proposed separate relay or legal power. |

The [matched comparison](../settlement/electoral-instruction-correction/README.md)
uses authored timing, groups, reach and public cost. The evidence
qualifies the mechanism and an existing practical distinction. It
does not qualify actual funding, affected participation or outcomes.
