# Original evidence and limits: income security, not an unconditionality verdict

Inspected 3 November 2026 · Design 135 research · No participant records accessed

## Kela: experiment implementation and official evaluation summary

[Basic income experiment](https://tietotarjotin.fi/en/information-package/158070/basic-income-experiment),
published 16 September 2020, edited 6 March 2023; historical implementation/evaluation
sections remain on the live page. Read directly; source family FI-BI-2017–18.

In 2017–18, 2,000 selected unemployed people aged 25–58 received €560 monthly.
Other benefit applications persisted; some payments deducted a matching amount.
The payment was tax-exempt; affected other benefits retained their pre-deduction
tax treatment. First-year group employment effects were absent; subsequent
interpretation is complicated by the 2018 activation reform. Survey respondents
reported better wellbeing; interviews showed varied experiences. Pension benefit
exclusions limited sampling. Participation was compulsory, under Finnish law.
The page reports a €20m experiment budget, without a complete cost reconciliation.

**Our limits:** no survey nonresponse analysis, participant data, complete
tax/service account or original legislative text was independently inspected.
Official descriptions are neither a consenting electorate for AIcountry nor
authority to repeat compulsory recruitment. The page's historical rules are not
current-benefit advice. Amounts are euros then, not country CU or Danish DKK.

## Later original authors: income and recorded health

[Hämäläinen, Simanainen and Verho (2025)](https://tietotarjotin.fi/en/publication/1296603/health-effects-of-cash-transfers-evidence-from-the-finnish-basic-income-experiment),
*Journal of Public Economics* 250, 105480,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2025.105480), published 17 September 2025.
Read the original author abstract at Kela, including search-index retrieval
after direct retrieval timed out; full publisher text returned 403. No claim to
have reviewed full methods, confidence intervals or raw records. VATT/Doria,
Kela and publisher versions concern the **same experiment**, not independent
replications or independent reviewers of this proposal.

The authors report average income increasing 9–11%, through prior-period benefit
overlap and earnings not reducing the payment. Register analysis estimates
psychotropic medication use 8–11% lower, with suggestive outpatient mental-health
visit changes and no detected effects on other health outcomes. Most recipients
still claimed unemployment benefits with obligations; the authors principally
attribute the health findings to higher income.

**Our inference:** this source cannot isolate unconditionality as the mechanism
or establish universal clinical improvement. Less medication use alone is no
complete health measure. The national financing, service supply, older-person
rights, lasting work response and whole-population effects remain unidentified
here. No prospective savings enter the country source account.

## What remains a design hypothesis

IC's independence preference and its adequate-targeted rival are value/institutional
choices. The accompanying CU calculation only exercises unchanged Y06/Y07
interfaces under explicit authored cases; it does not estimate any foreign
trial effect or country benefit/tax incidence. Existing primary snapshots remain
unchanged. New evidence and objections must be checked before final reuse;
no monitoring, independent affected judgment or current-law authorization exists.
