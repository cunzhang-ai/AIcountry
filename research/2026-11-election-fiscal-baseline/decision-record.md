# Design347 decision on election fiscal information and the opening week

## Baseline and choice

Pin Design346, HT01–HT17, ER, GP, DB, OL, JC and the working framework.
Existing policy covers commitment inventories, proposal costing and the
opening liabilities of an authorized transition. Add a date tied to the
first possible vote, independently issued changes and an opening account
that reconciles directly to the public election baseline.

Select a common versioned baseline, protected revision and a seven-day
opening account while original duties continue. Preserve confidential
optional costing and full elected control. The project assistant authored
the choice. Treasury supplies originals, Assembly analysts issue the
baseline, sector owners execute and independent review remains separate.

## Alternatives and objections

A one-time pre-election forecast can inform debate with less work.
It leaves late changes and first-week cash poorly explained. Briefing only
the successor protects confidentiality but gives voters and opposition less
scope to test promises. Select a dated public account and proportionate
changes with protected evidence and separate first-week execution.
A simpler publication schedule remains viable where it gives equally
useful evidence.

The [official comparators](sources.md) support pre-election publication
and parliamentary costing. The proposed timings, political independence,
effects on attention and data protection need evidence. Frequent versions
can confuse people or create openings for selective leaks. Preserve originals,
publish changes in accessible form and keep each official role narrow.

## Transition and reopening

Enact prospectively with funded analyst capacity, actual evidence access,
alternate custody and independent review. Preserve election, formation,
service and accrued-claim dates. Affected participation, actual budgets,
hostile handover performance and lawful adoption remain open.

Exercise a late liability before first voting and a disputed call during
the first week in office. Include eligible contestants, independent
analysts, Treasury, local providers and affected service users. Check who
could see the information in time, what funds actually arrived, and
whether both services and political choices survived. Reopen on selective
release, false headroom, missed duties or simpler equivalent practice.
