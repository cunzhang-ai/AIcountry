# Primary records on disputed responsibility and essential receiving

1. England, Department of Health and Social Care, *National Framework for
   NHS Continuing Healthcare and NHS-funded Nursing Care*, July 2022
   revised edition, corrected 2023, Annex E paragraphs 2–8. For its
   specified non-discharge context, the guidance discusses continuation
   of existing funding and care, separate urgent action and the
   limits of the bodies' statutory powers. It separately addresses
   people who lack an existing arrangement.
   [Official publication](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64b0f7cdc033c100108062f9/National-Framework-for-NHS-Continuing-Healthcare-and-NHS-funded-Nursing-Care_July-2022-revised_corrected-July-2023.pdf).
2. England, joint local government and health Ombudsmen, Hampshire
   County Council decision 25 008 975, 10 February 2026, paragraphs
   57–71 and 87–90. They attributed just over three weeks of avoidable
   hospital delay in one case to the handling of a funding dispute
   without prior interim funding or formal escalation. They
   distinguished later staffing and assessment time and did not
   find the dispute period alone established avoidable health
   deterioration. They agreed separate remedial actions with the
   bodies. The findings on funding procedure and the assessment of
   eligibility in the later dispute have distinct scopes.
   [Official decision](https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/adult-care-services/charging/25-008-975).

These primary records show a source-specific institutional distinction
and a case finding. They do not estimate the prevalence of disputes,
supply capacity or effects of the proposed AIcountry route. The
constructed case has separate dates, costs and task assumptions.
