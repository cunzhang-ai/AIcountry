# Danish courts in 2025. A smaller pending stock and different waits

## What the courts observed

The 2025 annual report and March 2026 release describe Danish district
courts and appeals within their own scopes. They draw on one
administrative source family.

| District court indicator | Observed direction or value |
|---|---|
| Pending lay-judge criminal cases, end 2023 to end 2025 | About 8,900 fewer |
| Pending ordinary civil cases, same dates | About 1,400 fewer |
| Annual average completed lay-judge criminal cases, 2024 to 2025 | 10.7 months in each year |
| Annual average completed ordinary civil cases with a main hearing, 2024 to 2025 | 22.9 to 23.2 months |
| Average age of pending ordinary civil cases, end 2024 to end 2025 | 16.7 to 17.4 months |
| Average age of pending lay-judge criminal cases, same dates | 11.4 to 8.0 months |

The annual report also records a decline in fourth-quarter lay-judge
criminal case time from 12.2 to 10.1 months. This is a different
comparison from the annual mean. About 885,000 cases of many types
closed in 2025, including enforcement and other proceedings. This
count does not measure complex hearings or actual relief. The
report says overall weighted district activity was unchanged from
2024 and notes lower inflow from prosecutors in some categories,
a change to case classifications, and scheduled time per closed
lay-judge case complex rising from 5.1 to 5.4 hours. It notes that
the latter was yet to be reflected in the weights. Its attribution
includes several possible causes. The figures do not isolate the
effect of extra staffing or reform, and a shorter stock can coexist
with longer waits in part of the population.

## Keep the plan, operating budget and legal help separate

The 2024–2027 agreement at 2024 prices and wages provides a
programme cost profile of 460.3, 545.6, 632.4 and 674.4 million
DKK, totaling 2,312.7. The programme includes continuing earlier
funding, not only the extra budget above 2023. The separate reported
2025 increase in the overall appropriation level above 2023 is
239.5 million DKK. It is not the 2025 programme cost of 545.6.
The planned funding scope and reported expenditure in current
prices are different views. The agreement also contemplates
procedure changes and needs separate authorization for them.
[Agreement, table 1](https://www.justitsministeriet.dk/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/aftaletekst-om-ny-fleraarsaftale-for-domstolene-2024-2027.pdf)

The 2025 outturn report records separate accounts. Amounts are
million DKK at the report's current basis. The reported budget
includes supplementary amounts then proposed. Receipts below are
shown as positive.

| Account | Budget | Outturn |
|---|---:|---:|
| Courts §11.41.02 gross expense | 2,422.9 | 2,454.4 |
| Courts §11.41.02 receipts | 6.4 | 23.7 |
| Courts §11.41.02 net expense | 2,416.5 | 2,430.7 |
| §11.42.02 publicly funded litigation and lawyer assistance expense | 616.8 | 625.8 |

The first account's net overrun of 14.2 follows from 31.5
additional expense and 17.3 additional receipts. The legal help
line has an additional 9.0 at its own scope. Neither variance
alone prices a reform. The report gives 2,603.4 overall full-year
equivalents for 2025, compared with 2,491.7 in 2024. It does not
convert these into a court, defense or executor roster. The budget,
new staffed work, lawful access and an executed result all require
their own evidence.
[Annual report, tables 1 and 11](https://domstoldk.euwest01.umbraco.io/media/5phdqi4m/aarsrapport-2025-for-danmarks-domstole.pdf)

The subsequent [police and prosecution agreement](https://www.justitsministeriet.dk/pressemeddelelse/ny-aftale-for-politi-og-anklagemyndighed-sikrer-milliardstyrkelse-af-indsatsen-mod-oekonomisk-kriminalitet/)
and [corrections programme](../lawful-custody-capacity/01-danish-appropriations-and-capacity.md)
have their own authority, periods and cost boundaries. A stronger
upstream case service is a potential source of more court work.
It cannot be counted as the downstream capacity to hear those cases.
