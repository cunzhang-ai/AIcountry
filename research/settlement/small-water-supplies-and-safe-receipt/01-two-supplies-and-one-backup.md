# Two affected supplies and one backup

*Design390 · Constructed comparison with [primary records](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-small-water-supplies-and-safe-receipt/sources.md).*

A small school supply and a separate group supply serving tenants
have a credible safety problem after the same storm. The health
authority issues appropriate restrictions on day 1. Both groups
need actual safe water on day 1 and again on day 2 unless a
qualified safe return occurs. Some residents cannot reach a
collection point. The school can receive an independently accepted
bulk delivery. All quantities and safe-use conditions here are
assumed to be professionally verified for the case.

The school route costs 3 units per service window. The tenants'
accessible route costs 4. Independent assessment and review
cost one additional unit in the first window. Eight units are
settled on day 1. The supplier disputes final cost and any
recovery will arrive after day 2. Local or national actors use
only the applicable existing, funded emergency or service authority.
The public owner retains its continuing response.

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| Warn and require private action | People know about the restriction. Receipt by those unable to collect water remains unverified. |
| Eight units and two genuinely accepted routes | Pay for both day 1 deliveries and independent review. Keep both groups' day 2 demand visible. |
| Eight units but a shared vehicle cannot make both deliveries | Even with funding, the unserved group remains an open duty. Seek a timely lawful alternative and use only the applicable priority law. |
| Operator declares the repair complete | Independent health evidence is still needed. Fund day 2 replacement if return has not qualified. |
| No further settled cash for day 2 | Day 1 delivery does not supply day 2. Another seven units and accepted receiving are needed on the stated unchanged facts. |

A qualified early return for one supply could lower the next
window's requirement, while the other retains its own protection.
A public advance does not decide the ultimate bill. This comparison
does not estimate actual hazards, staffing or appropriate amounts.
