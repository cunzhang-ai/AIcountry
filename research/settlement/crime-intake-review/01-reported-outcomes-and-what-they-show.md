# Recorded theft outcomes are not the same measure as effective investigation

The Home Office report for the year ending March 2026 publishes
these selected outcome percentages for police-recorded offenses in
its stated coverage. This is a snapshot of assigned outcomes for the
recorded cohorts, including cases not yet assigned an outcome in the
underlying denominator. The types below are distinct comparison
rows. The selected outcomes do not sum to 100%.
[Home Office statistical report, section 2.5 and Table 1.2](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/crime-outcomes-in-england-and-wales-2025-to-2026/crime-outcomes-in-england-and-wales-2025-to-2026)

| Type | Closed with no suspect identified | Charged or summonsed |
|---|---:|---:|
| Residential burglary | 72.4% | 5.0% |
| Vehicle offenses | 83.2% | 2.8% |
| Shoplifting | 52.7% | 23.2% |

The report notes that evidence availability and apprehension in the
shop can help explain the different shoplifting outcome. These
figures provide no count of unrecorded reports, wrongly closed cases,
missed reasonable leads, convictions or actual restitution. Do not
infer misconduct in an individual case or turn an outcome percentage
into a charge target.

The College of Policing describes reasonable proportionate inquiry
both toward and away from an identified suspect. The Crown
Prosecution Service describes a route to review a qualifying
noncharge decision and the possibility of requesting further
investigation. These are institutional comparators. The proposed
AIcountry rule includes a separate useful-time path for a plausible
unacted lead before an investigation is closed.
