# PCO11–PCO16. Give major laws a real decision after commencement

*Design355 · Part of [public conditions](../04-public-conditions.md).*

**PCO11 — Bind review to the law and its practical effects.** An adopting
Act requires a published review schedule for each new or materially
expanded primary measure that changes access to a protected public
service, authorizes a material compulsory burden, or commits material
recurring public resources. Apply the published Assembly materiality
criteria to the package, including related Acts and amendments that
operate together. Technical corrections within existing effects need no
separate review. The Clerk publishes the scope and any omitted-scope
objection before the ordinary final vote. The Assembly determines
contested scope on the public record; the Clerk cannot veto the bill.
A missed schedule keeps the statutory default review date and remains
challengeable.

**PCO12 — Set dates and observation before implementation.** The
schedule records who and what are affected, the inherited baseline,
lawful alternatives, original sources, delivery obligations, and the
cost of observation and review. Record how eligible people outside
the main delivery channel will be heard. The candidate default for the
first Assembly disposition is the earlier of 24 months after the first
scheduled legal commencement of the material measure and 36 months
after enactment. If the measure has no scheduled date, use the
enactment limit. An Act may set an earlier date. A long outcome horizon
can justify a separate later test, with reasons and funded evidence.
Maintain the first decision for missed starts, early effects and
unresolved exposures. A rename, recodification, delayed launch or
change of operator does not reset the old scope. A genuine expansion
gets its own scoped review without retiring the predecessor review.

**PCO13 — Put independent evidence on the same calendar.** The
responsible ministry supplies delivery and cost records. At least
60 calendar days before the due decision, publish a qualified
independent comparison, relevant missing evidence and any ministry
response already received. The Assembly funds a named reviewer from
existing audit, evidence or qualified external capacity with protected
appointment and access under its lawful mandate. The evaluated
operator cannot appoint, direct or defund the reviewer in an individual
case. A reviewer with a shared controller or materially conflicted
role is replaced through the predesignated funded route. Use PE for
withheld records and actual independent access. Invite safe direct
evidence from people excluded by the measure and protect sensitive
records. Use PCO01–PCO03 for denominators, original observations,
uncertainty, attribution and distinct rights. AI can reconcile records
or compare stated options; it cannot certify the effect or determine
whose rights can be traded away.

**PCO14 — Give elected members a real choice even if a report is missing.**
The Clerk reserves a hearing and a recorded disposition by the original
date. The subject committee may put one concrete alternative to a separate
recorded vote. Reserve a separate route for one alternative endorsed
by at least one tenth of the full Assembly. Each member may endorse
one text for this route. If several qualify, take the most endorsements,
with a public neutral lot on a tie. Provide paid drafting and fiscal
scrutiny. These votes use the ordinary rules for legal effect.
Each proposal identifies the exact operative text or named further
verification, affected rights, source and start. The Assembly may
retain with reasons, amend or repeal through ordinary law, or order
time-limited further verification with a dated return and interim
duties. If usable evidence is incomplete, identify what remains
unknown and make the decision that the available evidence and
authority support. A missing report or missed vote stays visible.
Use ordinary procedural remedies and the earliest lawful replacement
sitting. There is no new independent power to order a political result.

**PCO15 — Preserve faster safeguards and lawful continuity.** Keep the
earliest applicable original dates for a qualified regression finding,
urgent protection or individual remedy. A shared hearing or vote may
satisfy both schedules only when it actually covers each question with
adequate notice, evidence and opportunity to challenge. PWR and any
shorter emergency or source-law expiry continue in their own scopes.
A delayed review gives no new power, expenditure or consent. Lawfully
continuing statutory duties and accrued claims survive. A change sets
exact prospective texts, due dates and funded successors. Count a remedy
only when affected people receive it. A change of government carries
the pending evidence, agenda time, money and duties into the next term.

**PCO16 — Price scrutiny in the same budget as the policy.** Before the
final vote, show shared staff, opposition access, witness support,
secure records, ordinary court capacity and the cost of any continuing
case. If reviews collide, share evidence and hearings only within
compatible scopes. Preserve separate decisions where measures can be
changed independently. Schedule optional starts to fit available
review and service capacity. A staffing gap cannot cancel an enacted
deadline or a resident's right. The Assembly publicly records overdue
reviews, reasons, available alternatives and funded dates for recovery.
Reopen on serious contrary evidence, observed exclusion, a missed
transition or a materially changed obligation.
