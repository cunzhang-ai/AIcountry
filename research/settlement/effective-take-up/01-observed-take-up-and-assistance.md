# Observed take-up, assistance and evidence exchange

## An estimated entitlement can remain unreceived

Great Britain's Department for Work and Pensions estimated that
62% of people entitled to Pension Credit received it in the financial
year ending in 2024. The estimate uses a model and survey linked to
administrative data. Its comparison with the preceding year was
statistically uncertain. It is a national estimate for this benefit,
not a list of entitled nonrecipients or an explanation of why
individuals did not receive it.
[DWP statistical report](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/income-related-benefits-estimates-of-take-up-financial-year-ending-2024/income-related-benefits-estimates-of-take-up-financial-year-ending-2024).

## Practical assistance can change enrollment

Finkelstein and Notowidigdo's randomized experiment involved
about 30,000 older people likely eligible for SNAP who had
not enrolled. Over nine months, 6% of the control group,
11% of the information group and 18% of the information plus
application assistance group enrolled. People brought in by the
interventions differed from the typical enrollee in the control
group, including on income and health. This supports testing
accessible help as a real rival to administrative automation.
It does not show that outreach reaches the people with the
highest needs or estimate any effect of automatic payment.
[Original published experiment](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/aaFinkelstein_Noto_QJE_August_2019%20%281%29.pdf).

## A life event can carry limited proof and a chosen application

The Canada Revenue Agency's privacy impact assessment records that
Yukon birth registration can transmit information for a newborn
benefit application if the person consents, with separate application
routes available. The registry transmission satisfies proof of birth.
This is a defined channel for application and evidence. It establishes
neither the whole entitlement nor the best policy for care disputes,
household status or safe delivery.
[Canada Revenue Agency assessment](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/protecting-your-privacy/privacy-impact-assessment/canada-child-benefit-related-benefits-v2.html).

## Rules and statistical inference can be treated separately

Finland's Kela describes automated routine decisions made under
law without discretionary case judgment and excludes machine-learning
or statistical decision rules in that route. Its published scope
includes some adverse actions. TU draws a narrower boundary,
keeping qualified human authorization and adverse or disputed
decisions outside the batch-release route. Kela's policy statement
does not measure how often an automated decision reaches the
right person or corrects an omission.
[Kela description](https://www.kela.fi/automated-decisions).
