# WS26–WS32. Deliver safe drinking water beyond the public network

*Design390 · Proposed ordinary law within [water allocation and service](../16-water-allocation-and-service.md).*

**WS26 — Identify operators and people outside the network.** A supplier
providing drinking water to other households, tenants or a facility's
users from a small private, public or community source must register its
covered service and accountable operator before new use. Provide assisted
registration and a dated transition for existing suppliers. Include users,
uses, source and alternate access without publishing personal locations
or sensitive infrastructure details. Support voluntary confidential
testing and advice for solely household self-supply. No compulsory
household listing, home entry or new pumping right follows. A resident,
tenant, worker or visitor can report an unsafe or missing supply without
operator permission, registration, proof of ownership or a utility account.

**WS27 — Match oversight to exposure and protect independent evidence.**
The competent public-health water reviewer sets risk-based surveillance
under adopted health standards. Name actual sampling scope, methods,
qualified labs, timing, known uncertainty and treatment and maintenance
records. Consider shared source or treatment, exposure patterns, repeated
failure and people with specific access needs. Require proportionate
preventive plans from covered operators, with funded technical assistance
where authorized. Separate operator checks, independent sampling and
safety decisions. A clean sample or unknown unregistered scope does
not establish safety. Give affected people usable results and direct
challenge with appropriate privacy. Use only lawful consent or separate
lawful inspection authority at private premises.

**WS28 — Act on a credible threat in useful time.** An operator reports
a credible contamination or loss promptly and preserves original
observations. The independent health authority decides evidence-based
warnings, specified safe uses and any necessary temporary restrictions
under TD07 and existing emergency law. Warn actual users through
accessible direct and offline routes as appropriate. Protect health and
personal contact records. The competent water or local sector owner
begins safe receiving before the next necessary use, using prior lawful
authority and based on the actual hazard. A notice alone does not make
water available. Avoid identical instructions for materially different
hazards. No general search, coercive treatment or eviction power follows.

**WS29 — Give affected people an accepting, paid alternative.** The
supplier arranges safe temporary supply within its lawful duty. The
local Water Service Director keeps the essential residual obligation
when the supplier is unknown, unsafe or cannot deliver in useful time.
Use the named national fallback where applicable. Count qualified
water, transport, access, containers, an accepting provider and
actual usable quantities and dates. Test the first and next delivery,
including tenants, pupils and people unable to collect or prepare
water. Preserve safe sanitation and other independently due services.
Act within actual available lawful sources. A dispute about cost or
operator control cannot close a resident's essential claim.

**WS30 — Separate protection from the final bill and capital choice.**
Use a funded interim service, owner duty or lawful advance before
disputed recovery where needed. Price independent testing, transport,
operation, repair, long-term monitoring and qualified alternative
supplies against the same needs and dates. Compare support and repair,
shared provision and safe connection without assuming universal
conversion is best. Apply elected tariffs and subsidies under WS12–WS15.
Preserve lawful recovery, defenses and independent review. No special
charge to a tenant, assumed unpaid help or forecast recovery pays
today's supply. Keep household support separate from private asset
improvement and eventual liability.

**WS31 — Restore only after an independent safety decision.** The
health reviewer verifies source and use conditions, qualified repair
and representative evidence under the applicable standard. Check actual
users, sample dates, sustained safe operation and a paid plan if the
risk returns. The operator cannot certify its own reopening. Keep safe
interim access through a delayed or disputed reopening under existing
duties. Record affected periods, accepted receipt, continuing limits,
remaining needs and independent challenge. An end to a warning does
not erase an earlier missed delivery or prove every tap received safe
water. Transfer records, duties and claims to an actual successor.

**WS32 — Keep the duty inspectable and replaceable.** Adopt prospectively
with paid registry and sampling, operator assistance, health review,
local receiving and national fallback. Preserve source and title law,
privacy and earlier obligations. Publish safe totals for known and
unknown coverage, repeat failures, notices, missed supply and verified
repair. Elected bodies set oversight and fallback budgets and can
replace service leadership under ordinary law. They cannot order a
particular safety finding. Users can seek prompt independent review
and a funded delivery order under WS16. AI may compare observations
and routes. It cannot infer
safety from a missing sample, authorize a restriction or certify receipt.
Reopen on excluded users, common lab or payer failure, repeated
nonreceipt, unsafe repair or a better qualified local method.
