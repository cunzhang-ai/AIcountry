# Decision record: reproductive agency and a child's independent standing

5 October 2026 · Integrated design 50 research selection; not enacted law

## Baseline, question and people affected

The [design-49 predecessor](predecessor-design-49/README.md) preserves the
working country before this choice. C02 protects bodily autonomy and a
child's standing; C07 promises a lawful route to sexual and reproductive
services; C08 protects individual status and family duties. Yet a person
still cannot locate the chosen authority, service and remedy for avoiding
or continuing pregnancy, abortion, safe birth, infertility care or an
assisted-reproduction parentage dispute. The earlier
[personal-status decision](../2026-10-personal-status-and-care/decision-record.md)
explicitly left reproductive and parentage choices open. The bounded
question is how the country protects bodily agency and children while
assigning clinical, status, funding and review powers without a household
head, ministry or AI gaining a reproductive command.

This is one project-authored analysis under the proposed
[framework](../FRAMEWORK.md) and
[decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md), not affected-person
participation, independent review or law. People affected include those
who can become pregnant, partners, adolescents, children born through
different arrangements, disabled people, donors, gestational and intended
parents, clinicians, carers, religious and ethical dissenters, and people
whose tax and service claims share the same budget. None has been heard
in an AIcountry jurisdiction. Values in conflict include bodily autonomy,
health, child welfare and identity, equality, privacy, conscience,
potential life, plural family forms, fair public cost and democratic
legitimacy. Those conflicts are normative as well as empirical.

## Rival diagnoses and coherent choices

Unmet reproductive need may arise from a legal ban, provider absence,
coercion by family or state, unsafe care, travel and cost, lack of privacy,
or a disputed status rule. They call for different remedies. Original
patient journeys, clinician staffing, adverse events, denials, records,
court cases and confidential affected accounts would distinguish them;
absence of a recorded complaint is not evidence of consent.

| Package | Mechanism and possible benefit | Serious objection or failure |
|---|---|---|
| Retain generic bodily right and future health statute | Leaves local value choices open and avoids premature numbers | A written right can coexist with no accepting clinician, hidden coercion and a child stuck between registries |
| State demographic and family plan | Could coordinate birth, care and pension projections | Treats people as fiscal inputs; coercion, exclusion and political control threaten bodies and children |
| Predominantly private clinical and contract route | Offers provider diversity and negotiated arrangements | Income, insurer, partner or clinic may determine access and parentage; consent and child identity can be sold or lost |
| **Selected: rights-protected reproductive choice, public clinical access and independent child status** | Protects informed choice and timely care while separate clinical and civil processes safeguard the child and contested duties | Needs expensive staff, privacy, independent review and a legitimate public choice on developing life and emerging techniques |
| Restrictive pregnancy-outcome law with narrow medical exceptions | Gives greater statutory weight to developing fetal interests | Can require intrusive proof, delay care, punish patients and transfer medical decisions to police or partners |

The selected [C08 route](../country/C08/06-reproductive-autonomy-and-parentage.md)
protects bodily choice, permits safe abortion on request under clinical
standards, bars coerced outcomes, and keeps a child visible while adult
status is disputed. The [C07 service](../country/C07/09-reproductive-health-and-early-life.md)
names an accepting clinical chain from contraception to postpartum and
newborn care; the [C06 account](../country/C06/12-reproductive-care-finance.md)
counts it with other commitments. The
[C13 stress test](../country/C13/15-reproductive-care-and-disputed-status.md)
asks whether privacy, transport, refusal and parentage remedies work in
one person's life. This selection does not average the rival normative
positions into a hidden compromise or use a model score to choose them.

## Primary sources and their limits

The [WHO abortion care guideline](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789240039483/)
is an official evidence and policy comparator for quality care; it does
not by itself enact national law. [WHO's maternal and newborn postnatal
guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240045989)
addresses routine care and respectful follow-up, while its
[2025 infertility guideline announcement](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-11-2025-who-issues-first-global-guideline-on-infertility)
identifies equity, informed decisions and remaining evidence gaps around
third-party reproduction. The [Convention on the Rights of the Child
text](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text)
is a primary treaty comparator for nondiscrimination, identity, child
interests and voice; actual applicability requires a real state and treaty
inventory. [OHCHR information on contraception and family
planning](https://www.ohchr.org/Documents/Issues/Women/WRGS/SexualHealth/INFO_Contra_FamPlan_WEB.pdf)
addresses coercion and informed choice. These sources support relevant
questions and safeguards, not the assertion that this exact institutional
package is optimal or universally accepted. No AIcountry source patient
observations, current legal event or population baseline exists.

## Authority, continuing duties and objections

The constitution in this reference protects bodily integrity, equal
standing and access to effective remedy; prospective Assembly law states
the clinical package, safety and consent rules, professional obligations,
parentage, adoption, records, funding and review. C07's purchaser and
clinical networks deliver; professionals judge patient-specific care;
C08's registrar records facts and its courts decide contested status;
C06 allocates resources; C11 protects original evidence and privacy.
No provider, household member, insurer or demographic authority can
change law or make a patient or child disappear by editing a record.
The complete appointment, funding, information and execution chain must
be tested against a hostile minister, unsafe provider and absent court.

A legitimate adopter must reconcile existing health, medical-consent,
criminal, family, parentage, adoption, inheritance, data, disability,
child-protection, insurance and local-service law, including applicable
treaty duties. Existing pregnancy care, children, parental orders,
maintenance, consent records and pending claims continue under valid
law until a funded successor and fair process exist. A new right cannot
be called operational before actual staff, transport, medicine, emergency
cover, privacy, court and safe child-care capacity exist. A status dispute
does not suspend immediate newborn access; a new legal category cannot
retroactively erase a child or a donor's accrued protection.

Serious objections remain. The reference abortion choice may not give
developing fetal interests the weight some affected people regard as
right; a restrictive rival may instead impose severe bodily and health
harm. A clinician's conscience can conflict with patient access; the
state owes a real alternate provider and cannot evade the conflict with
a paper referral. Infertility coverage can divert scarce clinical staff;
excluding it can entrench wealth. Third-party reproduction can help
families while exposing donors, gestational people and children to
exploitation and missing records. These choices require real democratic
participation and evidence; the selection of one reference package is
not a claim of unanimous moral settlement. The exact rules for donor
anonymity, embryo storage, third-party gestation and novel genetic
technology remain separate prospective decisions, with existing law
and child protections continuing meanwhile.

**Evidence:** official medical and rights comparators plus authored
analysis, without original AIcountry observations or affected-party
participation. **Research selection:** a bounded reproductive-autonomy
and care route with independent child identity and status. **Legal
authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** no jurisdiction,
public, staff, clinical network, privacy and court capacity or budget.
**Observed outcomes:** none; no claim of superiority follows.

The next bounded test is an affected-public and professional review in
one real possible jurisdiction, following time-sensitive care, a
conscientious-refusal handoff and a disputed child's status through actual
law, providers and courts. A competent sponsor would need lawful access,
privacy protections and an approved time and cost limit; none is
authorized here. Reopen the research choice if care remains unreachable,
coercion or stigma rises, child identity is lost, professional duty is
unsafe, or a rival rights package better protects lives and agency.
