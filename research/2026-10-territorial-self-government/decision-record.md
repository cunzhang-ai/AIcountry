# Design 28 decision: plural self-government and territorial status

4 October 2026 · Research selection for the integrated country design; not law

## Decision and baseline

The [design-27 predecessor](predecessor-design-27/README.md) and its
`SHA256SUMS` pin the immediately preceding working texts. The current
[research framework](../FRAMEWORK.md) and [decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md)
guide this comparison; neither is an adopted constitutional procedure.
Design 27 allocated national and local tasks and sent unresolved cross-local
resource allocations to the elected Assembly. It did not give a distinct
people or territorial public a usable way to seek enduring autonomy or a
change of status. Nor did it clearly separate minority rights that travel
with a person from collective historic claims or a proposed border vote.

**Selection for design 28:** protect portable cultural and language freedom,
retain distinct lawful collective claims, and establish a prior, reviewable
route to initiate and negotiate territorial status. A proposal or first vote
does not itself change sovereignty, public ownership, citizenship or debts.
Ordinary service boundaries remain statutory; protected self-government or
sovereign change needs the prior valid constitutional route and a separately
defined affected-area authorization, with applicable international law and
collective rights respected. Existing law, rights and services continue until
a valid, resourced transition begins. This is a candidate for a real founding
public, not an assertion that this project can settle any actual territory.

## Why this is a country-design question

This is not solely a map or referendum technique. If the national majority can
refuse every status claim, equal political standing can mask permanent
subordination. If one local majority can transfer sovereignty immediately,
people inside and outside its proposed border can lose land, services,
citizenship or voice without consent or remedy. Culture may survive without
territorial power, while a distinct collective land or treaty claim cannot
fairly be converted into a generic individual service. These are different
values and legal relationships; none is proven by a count of accounts,
petitions or AI agents.

The empirical questions are separate: which people and rights actually exist,
who can represent them, which area and networks are involved, how services
and liabilities would transfer, and whether fair review can operate under
pressure. This project has no original observations of those facts.

## Alternatives compared

| Package | What it would do | Strong reason for it | Material failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retain design 27 and negotiate ad hoc | Use local councils, Assembly allocation and ordinary petitions | Fewer new institutions; flexibility where no status conflict exists | A national majority or cabinet can indefinitely reframe a durable self-government claim as a water-allocation complaint; rules change after seeing the claimant and map |
| Uniform individual rights only | Protect language and equality while rejecting distinct territorial or collective routes | Avoids compulsory identity and entrenched local elites | Cannot answer a genuine land, treaty or self-government claim; central control remains the default even when it is the disputed harm |
| Automatic local exit after one vote | Let a defined area vote itself into autonomy or independence | Strong local leverage against central refusal | An incumbent can choose the map and franchise, erase internal minorities, transfer shared assets and leave cross-border duties unresolved; a referendum cannot itself supply lawful external recognition |
| Fixed identity-based federation | Allocate permanent territorial units and group institutions now | Can guarantee durable self-rule where peoples and territory are known | This project knows neither; fixed labels can force identity, strand dispersed people and arm regional patrons with power over dissenters |
| **Layered negotiated status, selected** | Portable rights and distinct claims; accessible initiation; affected-scope evidence; negotiated alternatives; preauthorized final decision and funded succession | Gives a peaceful route without treating the first map or ballot as complete consent | National veto through amendment, captured mediation, local minority exclusion, cost and legal uncertainty remain serious |

The selected package is not a compromise average. It rejects the claim that one
national or local electorate always exhausts affected scope. A lawful adopter
may choose a different constitutional federation, status rule or no change
after people with standing compare them. Direct territorial independence and
the allocation of historic rights remain live disagreements, not issues an
AIcountry research vote can settle.

## Selected operating chain and rank

1. **Founding first.** A legitimate founding or predecessor process must state
   the protected rights, who may initiate a status proposal, how conflicting
   maps and rights-holders enter, what any first ballot means, the final legal
   route and the affected-area authorization. This process cannot approve itself
   retroactively once a claim is visible.
2. **Initiation without compulsory identity.** Residents, affected councils and
   peoples with applicable distinct rights may present a proposal through
   accountable representatives or accessible petitions. The first receiver
   records it and an independent route hears exclusion or forged representation.
   A government credential is not the sole proof of a community's existence.
   Prior law supplies objective support conditions, an auditable receipt,
   a public Assembly agenda and funded participation; refusal to register
   or negotiate is reviewable. A deadline creates no consent or new border.
3. **Competing scope and alternatives.** Keep original local testimony and
   source records; compare several maps and nonterritorial, fiscal, service and
   constitutional options. Identify people living in enclaves, downstream users,
   shared workers and creditors, holders of prior collective rights and the
   national public carrying continuing obligations. No one statistic or AI map
   decides their relative standing.
4. **Negotiation and correction.** Elected national and local authorities and
   independently chosen affected rights-holders negotiate in public with safe
   exceptions. Facilitation is jointly selected, funded and replaceable;
   opposition and internal dissent can challenge framing, translation and
   suppression. Courts enforce process, rights and existing obligations, not
   a preferred political bargain or an invented border.
5. **Final authorization.** Statute may change ordinary service districts and
   local competences within protected rights. Constitutional self-government
   or sovereignty changes follow the previous valid constitutional route and
   the founding charter's separately defined affected-area decision. A ballot
   distinguishes authorization to negotiate from approval of exact final text;
   it discloses effects before voting. The design does not displace any
   applicable international-law path or claim that a domestic vote alone
   settles international status.
6. **Continuity.** Before commencement, lawmakers identify lawful successors,
   cash, personnel, records and remedies for rights, land, water, debt, tax,
   pensions, services, courts, payments, defense and external obligations.
   Pending claims and environmental duties do not reset. When negotiation
   fails, current law and services continue with public reasons, usable
   challenges and space for a later peaceful proposal.

This adds proposed [A15](../authority/05-candidate-provisions-A10-A15.md)
to the candidate legal package. C01–C04, C06–C07, C09–C11 and C13–C14 carry the selected
design; [F11](../founding/04-stress-F11.md) tests a combined political, water,
service and security failure. C06's whole-country account is extended for
territorial succession; the existing constitutional amendment rule remains a
continuing dependency and is not automatically rewritten by A15. A real
jurisdiction would need article-by-article source-law,
treaty and private-rights review before any enactment.

## Sources and limits of support

The [ICCPR, Articles 1, 2, 25 and 27](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html)
distinguishes self-determination, equal individual rights, political participation
and minority culture/language. The [UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous
Peoples, Articles 3–4, 19, 26 and 46](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/iplands/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/UNITED-NATIONS-DECLARATION-ON-THE-RIGHTS-OF-INDIGENOUS-PEOPLES.pdf)
is a primary reference for Indigenous self-government, consultation, land and
the limits on reading the declaration as a license to dismantle states.
The [Supreme Court of Canada's *Reference re Secession of Quebec*](https://scc-csc.lexum.com/scc-csc/scc-csc/en/item/1643/index.do)
illustrates a domestic distinction between a clear demand to negotiate and
unilateral legal effect; its holding governs Canada, not AIcountry or every
self-determination case. The [Venice Commission's Revised Code of Good Practice
on Referendums](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/?pdf=CDL-AD%282022%29015-e)
informs the concern for clear questions, impartial administration and known
legal effects. These sources support the importance of the issues and supply
comparators. They do not select this candidate's thresholds, affected electorate,
mediator, map, title system or final settlement. They were inspected on
4 October 2026; no real affected-party participation occurred.

## Objections, status and next bounded question

The strongest objection is that a dual authorization route can give a national
majority a practical veto over a subordinated area. A duty to negotiate and
judicial process review might produce records rather than a just outcome.
Conversely, allowing a local declaration alone can make regional minorities,
downstream communities and creditors bear an unchosen settlement. Distinct
rights-holders may reject a state-designed representation procedure even when
it appears inclusive. Mediation and translation can control what counts as a
reasonable option. The reference keeps those objections explicit; it does not
certify the selected route as superior.

**Research selection:** design 28 includes this layered route. **Legal
authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** none established for any
territory, electorate, review body, service operator or cross-border agreement.
**Observed outcomes:** none. **Dissent:** the central-veto and local-exclusion
objections remain unresolved. No current software permission, constitutional
hold or old manuscript was amended.

The next bounded question is whether a real affected public and lawful
jurisdiction can identify the applicable collective rights, competing maps,
representative routes, shared obligations and legal status procedure without
either level controlling all evidence and remedies. A documented case with
original accounts, current law, alternatives, dissent and funded service
continuity could revise or reject this candidate. More invented territorial
numbers alone would not.
