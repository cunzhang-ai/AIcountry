# A flood, ten households and two cash dates

This is an authored exercise in one catchment, with assumed resources
and voluntary choices. Its ten displaced households comprise six who
can return after inspected shared works and four who cannot safely live
on the affected sites at the tested hazard level. Two of the four rent,
and two own. There is an accepted receiving place for three of the four.
The fourth household wants a local accessible dwelling after a funded
upgrade and needs a temporary extension meanwhile. These are case
assumptions. An actual recovery would have to establish each choice,
interest, risk and available dwelling through its own process.

| Due in this case | Work or support | Spendable source and units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Safe temporary housing through day 60, accessible claims help and essential continuity for all ten | 5 from the prearranged public source |
| Day 30 | Shared mitigation that can make six homes safe subject to independent inspection and service acceptance | 7 from the same source |
| Day 60 | Voluntary acquisition and movement payments, including independently usable moving support for tenants | 5 from a separate recovery transfer |
| Day 60 | Receiving service and housing work, including the accessible upgrade | 2 from that transfer |
| Day 60 | Temporary extension for the fourth household through the planned completion and acceptance of its dwelling on day 90 | 1 from that transfer |

The first source covers 12 units and the separate transfer covers 8. The
stated work uses all 20. Assume necessary continuing operation for return
and host services is already priced in the continuing service budget in
this bounded case. If that assumption fails, reopen the comparison and
price the new cost before relying on the plan. Insurance of 6 units may
become payable to an owner on day 150. The insurer has disputed the claim.
No early public source includes it. Any lawful same-loss offset depends on
the actual receipt, recipient and loss. Temporary housing and tenant
moving aid cannot be treated as payment of the owner's loss.

On-time transfer still requires inspection and actual receipt. Assume
the six returns pass inspection and the residents accept by day 60.
The other three with ready places can move if their own arrangements
are acceptable and they receive due movement payments. Keep the fourth
case open through actual receipt of its accessible place.

If the day 60 transfer arrives on day 120, the early source has zero
uncommitted units. Assume the three ready places are held, the host can
complete its work as scheduled if paid, and all four households need
their movement payments before they can use the places. The case prices
2 extra units for temporary housing through day 120. That covers three
households from day 60 and the fourth from day 90. A separately authorized
day 60 bridge of 3 units can pay the planned host work and first extension.
The extra 2 units require an additional lawful source. They cannot be
repaid from the same 8 without reducing the movements budget. If the
terms permit, use the later 8 to repay the 3-unit bridge and pay the
remaining 5 for movement. This delay costs 22 in all, of which 5 must be
accessible at day 60 despite the grant delay. If a place is no longer
held or an extension falls through, seek a funded substitute and record
the unmet duty and available relief. Keep a case open while safe
accommodation is unfunded or undelivered.

A property-only offer can reach an owner while tenants lack usable
accommodation. Repair in place could serve six under the assumed
conditions and would expose four to intolerable residual risk if applied
to all ten. A mixed plan gives the six households a return route and
the other four a supported choice. It depends on timely resources, durable
services, consent and independent checks of the places. Any public use
of the vacated sites needs a lawful steward and funded safe-use terms.
No compulsory purchase or use restriction is inferred from the exercise.
