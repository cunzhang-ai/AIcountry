# Constitutional review: voting, assignment and urgent protection

*Canonical CJ09–CJ10; part of [C03/08](../08-constitutional-review-and-public-control.md).*

**CJ09 — Five actual votes.** Final nationwide constitutional disposition requires
five affirmative votes of the fixed nine seats, with quorum five. This covers
invalidation, binding merits rejection, national constitutional electoral disposition
and the chosen remedy. No appointment-source presence condition gives three seats
a boycott veto over six lawful judges. At five sitting judges all five must agree;
at six through nine the requirement stays five. The chair has no additional vote.

A proposition is binding majority reasoning only if five actually join it. Five
may agree on a precise disposition for different reasons; publish that limited
holding. A clerk or AI cannot combine incompatible opinions into five votes.
A four–four split, fewer than five agreeing or fewer than five lawful judges
produces no final disposition, not a judgment that a law is constitutional.
Publish the unresolved questions, votes, prior effects, interim orders and next
lawful hearing. Earlier law and orders retain only their previous legal effects.
This can preserve harmful status quo and privilege an incumbent; it is not a
neutral outcome or proof that the constitution has protected the claimant.

**CJ10 — Assignment and finite interim power.** Before the judicial year, publish
specialization and auditable seat rotation for three-judge duty panels and full
hearings, including reserve activation and external conflict routing. Case
receipt determines the next lawful panel; exceptions have public reasons and
CJ12 review. Judges electing a presider cannot give that person a case-selection
veto. Replacement during merits deliberation requires a fresh adversarial hearing
of material issues, not private adoption of a predecessor's unexamined vote.

A three-judge duty panel may unanimously issue party-specific preservation or
urgent temporary protection for at most seven continuous days from its first
related operative order, pending the full court. No serial panel/order resets
that ceiling. Extension requires five full-court votes and a reasoned bounded
order. The panel cannot invalidate a statute nationwide, determine a national
result, choose a government or renew a mandate. An ex parte order also obeys the
shorter applicable B17/E14 hearing limit; this is not seven days without defense.
Ordinary case-specific courts retain their separate lawful jurisdiction, with
orders reconciled openly rather than contradictory secret stays.

Nationally operative provisional suspension needs five votes and demonstrated
risk of irreversible constitutional harm, a narrow scope and public reasons.
Its operative expiry is at most fourteen elapsed days, with an adversarial return
hearing within seven days and any shorter B17/E14 limit controlling. Renewal
requires fresh five-vote reasons before expiry, at most fourteen days each and
fifty-six aggregate days from the first related national suspension. New parties,
case numbers, panels or labels do not reset the same operative measure's ceiling.
No missed hearing or silence continues it. After expiry, only the previous law's
existing effects and separately authorized individual relief remain; this may
restore harmful operation and cannot guarantee a fair national contest. The
ceiling prevents an indefinite provisional regime without final merits, at that
explicit cost. These durations are uncalibrated design choices.
No constitutional referral, vacancy, urgent order or funding shortage extends
P09's twenty-four-hour first custody review, remand limits, clinical protection,
election dates or another shorter clock. If the competent court cannot protect
a person in useful time, record the failure and apply the existing release or
protection duty; constitutional docket priority does not manufacture capacity.
