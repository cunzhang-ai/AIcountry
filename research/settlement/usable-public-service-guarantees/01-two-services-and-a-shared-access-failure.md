# Two due services through a shared access failure

Two residents have the same protected receipt deadline in each
window. One needs assisted travel to a clinical appointment and has
no spare cash. The other needs an interpreter for a service review
and could advance the cost. Each consents to a suitable replacement.
A common dispatch and payment provider fails. Two advertised
alternates use that controller, so neither qualifies. The clinical
and review offices remain competent to receive each resident.

In window 1, an independent accessible driver and interpreter are
available. Both will take a direct public commitment and are free
to serve before the deadline. The driver will take payment from a
resident but no advance reaches that resident by the last departure.
The interpreter also takes payment from the other resident. By
window 2 the driver has another protected booking. An independently
staffed visiting clinician can deliver the first service at home
if that change is agreed and arranged in the first window. It is
stipulated to be safe and acceptable. The interpreter can serve again.

| Route compared | Window 1 on time | Window 2 on time | Design consequence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Reimburse residents | 1 of 2 | 1 of 2 | A resident without spare cash cannot use the first offer. |
| Direct booking | 2 of 2 | 1 of 2 | Payment buys the first trip but cannot free a driver in window 2. |
| Direct booking and agreed visiting service in window 2 | 2 of 2 | 2 of 2 | The second path needs its own qualified worker, consent and funded start. |

Use illustrative units. Ordinary services cost 9 in each window.
Window 1 separately needs 1 for independent review, 1 for
dispatch standby, 3 for travel, 2 for interpretation and 1 for
reserving the visiting route, for a total of 17. Window 2 needs
9 for ordinary services, 1 for review, 1 for standby, 2 for
interpretation and 4 to deliver the visit, also 17. The first
reservation cost is nonrefundable and does not pay the second
invoice. Provider commitments are recognized in their own windows.
If window 1 has only 16 with the ordinary, review and direct paths
protected, the visiting reservation does not qualify. If window 2
then lacks lawful qualified travel or a visit, the clinical duty is
unmet. Keep the case open even though the resident paid nothing.

The three routes compare the same stipulated people, clocks and
provider offers. Reimbursement and direct booking are less costly
here if they omit the visit, and each leaves a service unmet.
Neither private willingness to pay nor an AI-generated alternate
creates an available worker. A missed case retains its original
date and may reach an independent remedy. This case establishes
no national costs, actual receipt or valid appropriation.
