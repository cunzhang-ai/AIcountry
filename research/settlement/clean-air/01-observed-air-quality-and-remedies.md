# Observed standards, monitoring and remedies

## Health guidance and a legal limit have different functions

The WHO's 2021 global guidance recommends an annual average of
5 micrograms per cubic metre for fine particulate matter
(PM₂.₅) and uses a separate daily recommendation and frequency
convention. Its table separates pollutants and averaging
times. These are health-based guideline levels. A jurisdiction
still needs an enacted standard, a qualified measurement method,
a lawful transition and funded response. AQ does not assign a
universal statutory concentration or treat a guideline as proof
of no harm. [WHO 2021 guidelines, Table 3.26](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/345329/9789240034228-eng.pdf).

## Place-specific measurement and plans are separate duties

The EU's 2024 ambient-air directive provides for air quality
plans and roadmaps, short-term action plans and access to
review in its defined scope. Its annexes address siting of
sampling points, including areas of high exposure and vulnerable
populations, and information for corrective planning. This shows
how a regime can separate measurement, legal compliance,
acute protection and cross-sector plans. It establishes no
observed reduction attributable to the proposed design.
[Directive (EU) 2024/2881, Articles 19, 20 and 27 and Annexes IV and VIII](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2881/oj/eng/pdf).

In *ClientEarth* in 2015, the UK Supreme Court gave a mandatory
order requiring the Secretary of State to prepare revised air
quality plans on a defined timetable and deliver them to the
European Commission by 31 December 2015. That is an example
of a timed judicial remedy under the legal regime then
applicable. The judgment establishes the order. It does not
prove later attainment of the limits or the efficacy of every
plan measure. [UK Supreme Court judgment, paragraph 35](https://supremecourt.uk/uploads/uksc_2012_0179_judgment_9d0e42c87f.pdf).

## An accounting treatment cannot erase the observed event

A June 2026 US Environmental Protection Agency description of
the exceptional events rule explains that some monitoring data
may be excluded from specified regulatory determinations.
The agency also describes tools for understanding actual smoke
conditions. The regulatory treatment is scope-specific. AQ uses
the distinction to keep real exposure, warnings and accessible
protection visible under any authorized accounting treatment.
[EPA explanation](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-releases-tools-assist-air-agencies-exceptional-events-demonstrations).
