# Late assistance and a remaining loss

In this constructed example a public agency owes a resident
accessible transport to a protected appointment. It fails to
deliver by the lawful deadline. No individual negligence is
established. The resident pays 6 for reasonable accessible
replacement transport but arrives too late for the original
appointment. The next suitable appointment and its paid transport
are offered and accepted. The missed date and any unresolved
nonfinancial loss remain recorded. The authority's offered new
trip does not reimburse the earlier expenditure.

An existing lawful payment of 2 for the same expenditure
actually reaches the resident. Under the proposed covered
claim, assume the independent adjudicator finds breach,
causation, eligibility and proof for the full 6. The residual
award is 4. A promised payment of 2, had it not been received,
would have required verification and reconciliation rather than
deduction at the point of award.

| Same payment window | Required units | Authorized source |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Qualified replacement service and continuing work | 7 | Ordinary service funds |
| Independent intake and review | 1 | Separate review source |
| Residual amount to the resident | 4 | Separate authorized remedy reserve |

A 3-unit remedy reserve leaves 1 outstanding if partial
payment is lawfully possible. Keep the balance open and pursue
funded correction. With a 4-unit remedy source and actual
receipt the direct loss is paid. Neither state has restored
the original deadline. A flat credit of 2 could underpay this
resident while paying someone who experienced no material
loss. Ordinary tort remains available under its own grounds.
The bounded statutory path addresses the direct cost without
having to identify a negligent employee. If the person cannot advance the fare, the funded urgent route
can book it directly where available. If both routes fail, record
that failure and the missed appointment. The lack of a fare
receipt establishes no equivalent service or absence of harm.
The initial expense claim alone would leave this resident without
a monetary award on these facts. No liability or actual funding
follows from the exercise.
