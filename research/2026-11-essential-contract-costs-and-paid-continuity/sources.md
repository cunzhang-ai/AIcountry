# Primary procurement comparators for allocating input risk

1. UK Cabinet Office, *The Sourcing Playbook*, June 2023 edition,
   section on inflation and indexation. The guidance proposes
   identifying underlying cost drivers before award, considering
   what the supplier can manage, and matching the index and its
   weight to the cost exposure. This is official design guidance
   for its own institutions.
   [Published edition](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64901fcc5f7bb700127fac5e/Sourcing_Playbook_Final.pdf).
2. UK Government, *Risk Allocation and Pricing Approaches*,
   sections 6.1–6.2 and appendix on choosing an index. The note
   distinguishes firm and indexed prices, input and output indices,
   and actual published values and forecasts. It is part of the
   same policy family, without independent empirical replication.
   [Official guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-sourcing-and-consultancy-playbooks/risk-allocation-and-pricing-approaches-guidance-note-html).
3. EU Directive 2014/24/EU, Article 72(1)(a), consolidated text
   as of 1 January 2024. It specifies the terms for modifications
   anticipated by clear initial review clauses, including price
   revisions, and bars changes to the overall nature of the
   contract under that route.
   [Official consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2014/24/2024-01-01/eng/pdf).

These are institutional comparators. They do not identify actual
local costs, bidders, willingness to bear risk, cash dates or
continuity. The constructed comparison uses its own prices,
eligibility, amounts and dates.
