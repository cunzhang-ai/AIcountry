| Alternative | Practical tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Automatic fixed-fraction release with ordinary support | A predictable endpoint limits unequal discretionary decisions. Individual protective measures carry the work of addressing exceptional current danger. |
| Selected halfway presumption with a judicial exception | The state must establish a narrow present risk and the insufficiency of lesser measures. Continuing custody and recall still bring risks of unequal prediction, court workload and delayed receiving. |
| Full finite sentence with discretionary or humanitarian release | Gives greater weight to execution of the original punishment and can permit exceptions. A person may remain imprisoned without a strong practical opportunity to establish that custody has become unnecessary. |

The selected presumption makes halfway a liberty deadline unless an
independent court has ordered continued custody on tested facts. The same
legal state at the deadline determines whether release carries conditions.
Conditional release discharges the original custody authority when its
fixed window ends, including custody already resumed by recall. This
accepts a public-safety tradeoff. After the endpoint, ordinary protection
and freshly established offenses carry the response to later harm.
The adult twelve-month and child six-month limits build on Q05. The halfway
effect, continuation periods, recall endpoint and proof rules are project
choices requiring an actual public decision.

Victim information, safety and support need their own receiving roles.
Court decisions, independent counsel, corrections, local housing and
clinical care require actual paid capacity at the same time. A bed shortage
in the community cannot supply a prison ground. Conversely, a projected
decline in prison occupancy does not itself supply usable housing, staffing
or funds. The full incidence across households, victims, staff and public
budgets remains open.

## Original source and interpretation

The Council of Europe Committee of Ministers adopted Recommendation
Rec(2003)22 in September 2003. I inspected the publisher-hosted text in the
browser, including the introduction and relevant appendix paragraphs on
release models, preparation, employment and housing, reconsideration,
conditions, noncompliance and safeguards. The recommendation distinguishes
discretionary and mandatory release, discusses individualized conditions,
calls for timely preparation and gives the person access to reasons and
independent challenge. It also proposes conditions and revocation options
that differ from RL. Its text informs comparison. It provides neither
observations of this project's outcomes nor a basis to estimate a local
release effect. The local capture attempts both returned HTTP 403.

## Continuity and the remaining decision

The Act would need actual commencement, sentence-by-sentence migration,
lawful leniency and an independent route for correcting disputed time.
Its scope preserves the separate jurisdiction of foreign and tribunal
sentences. Old credits, release dates, protection, received claims and
paid OP/T successors need active reconciliation. The attached cases test
the written joins. [Adverse cases](adverse-cases.md)

The written decision is selected for research. Authority, paid operation,
independent professional and affected-party participation, comparative
safety and better lives remain open. The next choice should address the
most consequential remaining country need or source conflict against a
real whole alternative. Reopen RL on material evidence of disparate
detention, failed safety, remedy delay, inaccessible support, shared
controller capture or a better release architecture.
