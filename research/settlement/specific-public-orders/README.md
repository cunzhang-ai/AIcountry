# Two urgent orders, with different acts needed

*Design295 · Constructed concurrent execution and budget comparison.*

A competent court has issued two enforceable orders. A requires
a two-unit payment received by hour 6. B concerns an already
established entitlement to an available accessible temporary
place. It requires a specific confirmation of the prior decision
before the provider's dispatch cut-off at hour 6 and actual
receiving by hour 8. Its provider and transport cost three units.
The placement remains suitable and voluntary in the declared case.

The implicated original office misses the last reliable preparation
point at hour 4 and withholds the confirmation route. The ordinary
deputy cannot use an independent credential. A qualified alternate
payer can still perform A. That payer's record and payment authority
does not include B's confirmation. An executor in the priced reserve
has the task authority, an independent credential and an accepted
slot, conditional on the court's scoped execution decision. A and
B need overlapping paid time. An executor may be qualified for both
kinds of act and still cannot occupy both slots at once.

| Alternative | Cash required in the window | Result under the declared blockage |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Record and payment reserve only | 5 | A can be delivered. B has money and a place, but its lawful confirmation cannot be supplied by the cut-off. |
| Same reserve plus scoped substitute execution | 6 | A and B can both be delivered with independent credential, accepted performance and a separate staffed slot. |
| Direct ordinary route with a funded independent sector deputy | 7 | A and B can both be delivered if the deputy has a preexisting lawful assignment and independent proof. Prefer ordinary operation when it actually qualifies. |

The sixth unit buys the separate execution slot and scope in this
example. At five available units it cannot be credited. If the
provider withdraws, the alternate confirmation alone cannot
establish B's delivery. A temporary placement received after
hour 8 leaves the original missed interval visible. A valid
stay or an unresolved eligibility decision precludes the declared
substitute act. Preserve earlier independently lawful protection.

Both orders retain their dates and available remedies. A later
recovery claim supplies no current funding. A next call needs
independent rights and records, a new qualified slot and cash on
its own clock. If a real public function repeatedly lacks a
qualified operator, compare ordinary statutory repair and funded
reorganization. A court-supervised plan may expose the failure
but a broad court-appointed manager could acquire control over
resources and cases unrelated to the order. The case neither
selects that structure nor claims it has been implemented.
