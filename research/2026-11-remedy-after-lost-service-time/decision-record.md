# Design445. Repair a remaining loss after a missed public deadline

Design444 is the pinned predecessor. AP, SR and ordinary civil
liability already provide protection and enforcement. The added
choice is a narrowly enacted statutory claim against the public
body for proved material loss after breach of a time-sensitive
essential service duty. The initial scope is reasonable replacement
expenditure. Individual staff fault is unnecessary. This revision
used AI assistance and a parallel scope review.

Ordinary restoration and tort retain existing routes but can leave
direct costs of a missed deadline unresolved. A flat credit is
simpler but poorly aligned to materially different harm. Select
an independent, bounded claim with access to proof, an authorized
remedy source, and no diversion of the service budget. The
legislature defines the duty and source. The court or authorized
reviewer determines relief within its remit. The payer proves
receipt. Original claims, stronger sector remedies and lawful
deadlines survive migration.

[Sources](sources.md) and the constructed test do not establish
actual adoption, independent qualified review, affected participation
or funds. Next compare one observed service failure with actual
substitute receipt, direct expenditure, independent casework and
continuing ordinary service in the same budget window. Reopen on
unfair proof burdens, displacement of services or better effective
redress.

The main objection is distributive. A resident with cash may buy a
substitute and prove a loss while a resident without cash may miss
the same appointment with no eligible expenditure. Existing paid
protection and direct booking take priority. Record failed direct
booking and uncompensated loss separately. The initial class leaves
this inequity open when the preventive route fails. Compare a
direct-booking guarantee or a separately justified loss category
before extending the claim. Avoid shifting liability from the duty
owner to an individual officer or asking the implicated payer to
decide its own disputed award. Reviewers need independent access to
the due date and proof of receipt. Keep detailed case records
protected and publish safe counts of unmet duties and unpaid awards.
