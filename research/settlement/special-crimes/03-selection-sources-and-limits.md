# Selection, primary sources and limits

*Part of [special harms](README.md); consulted 15 October 2026.*

Three substantive models remain distinct. **Broad custody/strict regulatory
criminality** may promise swift incapacitation but risks punishment without
fault, overcharging and large hidden custody costs. **Predominantly civil/
restorative response** can reduce penal harm but still needs an answer for
serious coercion, safe refusal, unavailable assets and powerful repeat actors.
**Differentiated bounded penalties**, selected for this draft, retains
serious criminal accountability with nonpenal protection, no-sanction
comparison, specific proof and finite usable sanctions. This selection is
a reasoned value hypothesis, not observed superiority or a universal optimum.

The [ICCPR primary text](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html),
Articles 9, 14 and 15, provides the liberty, fair-trial/finality and legality/
leniency context. Article 11 concerns inability to meet a contractual
obligation; it does **not itself prescribe our penal-fine expiry rule**.
The candidate's broader needs floor, age/peer rules, units, maxima, sealing
and limitation periods are independent proposed choices, not treaty numbers.

The Committee on the Rights of the Child's
[General Comment 24](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=SIJ2TxyNUciJOAuRgNhwHiXhFqktfxJGtFsaYhZKyyCrNSaek3J6em4ZHOmhz0MKx1NAzELYiQw3gVEZB5v%2FiQ%3D%3D),
paragraphs 12, 22–30 and 89–90, addresses harmful criminalization of children,
consistent minimum responsibility, age doubt, under-18 process and prompt
liberty review. It does not choose K13's consent age/24-month exception,
our sexual-material definitions or the Q sanction figures. Current source
inspection supports these bounded references, not a full 2026 global-law review.

Material objections remain: age/month cliffs; price and liquid-asset valuation;
pressure to accept community work; economic penalties passed to workers/users;
concealed organizational control; vague sector duties; long-delayed abuse
proof; unequal impact of records; and an expiry that may reward concealment.
The draft responds with specific elements, prosecution burdens, advice,
no-conversion caps, public-duty continuity, published predicates and narrow
independent tolling. These safeguards are not demonstrated effective.
Affected children/survivors/accused, workers/users, disabled/poor people,
defenders, clinicians, inspectors and judges have not participated.

Legacy migration needs each applicable old clause, pending case, sentence,
fine, time bar, asset and support claim mapped; source law/software remains
unchanged. New offenses cannot apply retroactively; leniency, actual liberty
and safe receiving require independent lawful execution. Existing applicable
other law is neither repealed nor newly validated by this research.

Next draft family/civil age, agency and limitation, then cross-border and
remaining regulatory schedules. Reopen this selection on an ambiguous harm,
unsafe care, coercive work, discriminatory means/records, captured enforcement,
unsupported actual cost or a less harmful effective model. Original
observations, qualified independent legal review, legitimate adoption,
operational readiness and observed outcomes are all still absent.
