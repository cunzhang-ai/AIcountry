## Alternatives and objections

Independent project decisions keep control near delivery and may fit
less constrained markets. They can miss competition for the same underlying
trade, materials or inspector. A staged calendar tests combined requirements
and leaves lawful local choice, but deferral costs money and delays service.
A larger publicly staffed inspection or delivery team can reduce reliance
on thin markets; it takes recurring funds and can pull scarce workers from
existing duties. Compare smaller competitive lots, alternative delivery and
funded training alongside both structures.

Forecasts can be wrong in both directions. Require actual verification for
public binding release, while retaining uncertainty for private demand and
price. The public calendar could facilitate collusion, leak confidential
bids or make local governments dependent on central permission. Publish
safe aggregates, provide protected evidence access, preserve legal powers,
and reject cross-bid disclosure or presumed control of private contracts.
Keep the ability to challenge supplier overlap and independently verify
service receipt.
