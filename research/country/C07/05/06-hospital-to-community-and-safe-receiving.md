# HDC01–HDC06. Join discharge to actual community receiving

*Design364 · Part of [health service and patient control](../05-health-service-and-patient-control.md).*

**HDC01 — Start planning early and separate the decisions.** For a person
with foreseeable material needs after an inpatient stay, the treating team
appoints a named transition lead while those needs can still be met in time.
Keep clinical readiness to leave inpatient care, the person's choice,
destination suitability, accepting services and payment distinct.
Use the person's stated preferences and accessible supported communication.
Ask about relevant housing, disability, care and transport needs without
requiring an unrelated life dossier. The hospital owns its clinical and
handover work. The local housing and care authorities retain their statutory
decisions. Planning neither creates a diagnosis nor determines entitlement
outside the competent source.

**HDC02 — Verify a usable route before counting a transfer as safe.** For an
arranged transfer, document the actual destination, access, required qualified
help, medicines and equipment, transport, the accepting person for each
function, and the first and next useful deadlines. The responsible clinical
team verifies the plan within its competence and records unresolved work.
Check any willing unpaid help separately and with consent, including
availability, training and lawful alternatives. No family relationship,
address, reservation or referral supplies an accepting provider. Agree
what the person can use and verify actual arrival and first required care.
A planned handover does not close the prior service or transfer a missed
deadline. Preserve distinct service duties through the next accepted act.

**HDC03 — Protect liberty and offer a qualified alternative.** Where the
preferred route cannot operate, compare a paid, clinically appropriate
temporary destination with a voluntary, safely staffed short stay and
available in-place support. Explain tradeoffs, times, costs to the person
and independent challenge. If the person lawfully chooses to leave,
offer usable advice, harm-reduction support and safe follow-up within
existing duties. Record what was accepted and what remains unavailable.
No route conditions essential protection on unpaid help or a waiver.
The transition plan creates no power of detention, forced treatment or
unlawful eviction. Any applicable emergency or supported-decision route
keeps its separate grounds and review.

**HDC04 — Fund handover and the next patient together.** Health and local
service budgets name the eligible temporary provision, lawful payer,
ceiling, usable cash, accepting workers and the next decision date.
Where prior law permits a capped bridge, pay for the specified receiving
before intergovernmental reimbursement is settled. Preserve continuing
entitlements and reconcile each actual cost once under MF. A hospital
slot freed by transfer becomes capacity only if safely staffed and
actually available for the next need. Check the existing ward, transport,
community work and independent review together. A budget cannot count
the same worker or room twice. If there is no funded, qualified path,
record the exposed duty and escalate through ordinary clinical, local
and elected correction.

**HDC05 — Escalate before the first threatened act.** The transition lead
maintains usable contact and a next-action schedule for unresolved care,
access, payments or housing. Bring a threatened failure to the competent
hospital or community duty owner and the appropriate independent
review route before useful protection is lost. Offer immediate lawful
interim help where available. Do not let a payment dispute, referral
loop or planned date replace urgent assessment. If someone remains
voluntarily while a transfer is blocked, revisit the plan at least
each elapsed 24 hours and sooner when needs or risk change. A review
interval authorizes neither retention nor postponement of an earlier duty.

**HDC06 — Verify transfer, handback and lost service.** Record actual
receiving, missed or refused acts, continued rights, and the funded
successor for still-open work. Protect scope, privacy and original dates
through a change of destination, payer or provider. Publish safe aggregate
delay and access evidence, including people without a home and those
without willing informal care. AI may compare routes or flag missing
acceptance. It cannot establish consent, clinical readiness or successful
discharge. Reopen on repeated unsafe transfers, unmet ward demand, coerced
acceptance, a failed backup or a simpler qualified community route.
