# Core harms: elements before punishment

*K01–K12; part of [C08 criminal core](../12-criminal-core-and-procedure.md).*

Each row requires all stated elements plus [P01–P05](01-responsibility-and-defenses.md).
Maxima are candidate **upper limits in years for natural persons**, not
mandatory prison terms. P16 requires the no-sanction/noncustodial comparison.
They are normative draft choices, not fitted evidence of safety or adopted law.
A missing specialized schedule gives no power to fill a gap by analogy.

Serious injury is substantial danger of death or lasting substantial impairment. Prosecution proves nonconsent, dishonesty, unlawfulness, aggravation and
causation where the row requires them. A complainant need not have physically
resisted or reported immediately. P11–P15 protect meaningful defense; civil
or protective findings neither establish guilt nor disappear on acquittal.
Calling a service critical, a network private or a person dangerous does not
substitute for K10's precise evidence. Protected data/security interests need
published law; a vendor's unilateral ban or terms-of-service breach alone
cannot make access criminal. Labor withdrawal and lawful disclosure
retain protected routes; an independent serious unlawful harm still needs
its own elements. Protective necessity never gives a broad police warrant.

**Source and limits.** K01/K03/K05/K07–K10 broadly retain the predecessor's
25/10/2, 15/5, 5/10, 3, 5, 7 and 2/5 maxima. Its digital access/disruption
boundary is narrowed through express harmful purpose, fault and material acts.
K02/K04/K06/K11/K12 introduce explicit candidate ceilings; the source had no
complete standalone schedule. Those ceilings and K05's seven-day aggravation
are uncalibrated value choices. Newer wording is not evidence of improved
safety. All need proportionate public/practitioner comparison before adoption.

[K13–K24](../15-special-crimes-and-bounded-sanctions.md) now extends the
ordinary draft to child exploitation, privacy, physical/sector dangers and
public integrity, under the same P protections. This is **not a full code**:
sector predicates/administrative sanctions, treaty/international crimes and
complete cross-border rules still need reconciled prospective statutes.
Existing specific claims and protection continue through lawful receivers;
this omission cannot create immunity under an actually applicable other law.
No current software, source manuscript or constitutional boundary is amended.
