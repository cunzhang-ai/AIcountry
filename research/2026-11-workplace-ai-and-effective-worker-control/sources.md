# Primary sources on automated management and worker decision rights

1. Germany, *Works Constitution Act*, sections 76 and 87. Where the
   matter is not prescribed by statute or collective agreement,
   section 87(1)(6) gives the works council co-determination over
   introduction and use of technical devices designed to monitor
   worker conduct or performance. Section 87(2) provides for a
   conciliation decision in place of agreement. Section 90 addresses
   information and consultation in planning, including use of AI.
   This is a distinct existing institutional comparator.
   [Official German text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html)
   · [Official English translation](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_betrvg/englisch_betrvg.html).
2. Directive (EU) 2024/2831, articles 7–11 and 13. Its platform-work
   scope includes restrictions on data use, transparency, human
   oversight, review, and worker information and consultation. It
   distinguishes particularly significant decisions involving the
   employment or contractual relationship or account. The scope and
   powers differ from the proposed domestic labor law.
   [Official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2831/oj).
3. Rani, Pesole and Gonzalez Vazquez, *Algorithmic Management practices
   in regular workplaces*, ILO/JRC, 2024. The original case-study
   research examines logistics and healthcare establishments in Italy,
   France, India and South Africa. It reports examples of streamlined
   work as well as risks to job quality and surveillance. It does not
   establish an average causal effect or a policy threshold.
   [Original report](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC136063/JRC136063_01.pdf).

The proposed domestic scope, forum test and worked hours are authored.
The sources do not establish local legal compatibility, representative
capacity, payroll execution or actual benefits from this proposal.
