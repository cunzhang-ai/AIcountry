# C06. Financing equal standing and accessible remedies

*Part of [C06 public finance](../C06-public-finance.md); integrated design 55 research, not enacted law.*

The Assembly must fund the equality floor it enacts in existing services,
workplaces and markets. Count accessible buildings and transport,
interpretation, trained staff, reasonable accommodations, alternate human
channels, safe complaint intake and time to correct an exclusion. A tax,
health, housing or school ledger cannot count a nominal place that the
person cannot use as delivered service. The fiscal account distinguishes
general design changes from an individual's accommodation and records
real opportunity costs, alternatives and an enacted undue-burden boundary.

Independent inquiry needs a disclosed staff and data budget, secure evidence,
outside audit and court access. Labor, housing, clinical, education, finance,
electoral and police reviewers need their own trained staff and an actual
executor; one ombudsperson cannot be booked as the reviewer for every
sector at the same time. Claimant and respondent advice, translation,
privacy-protecting disclosure and order execution have distinct costs.
Some accommodations are cheap while others require new workers, vehicles
or buildings; an abstract anti-discrimination right creates neither cash
nor immediate physical capacity. Temporary equivalent access and an elected
capacity decision must be visible when full delivery is delayed.

National equalization cannot reward localities that exclude high-need people
or undercount them in a needs formula. Funding for targeted repair requires
original evidence, affected participation, a published duration and review;
it is not a permanent unexamined entitlement for a favored contractor.
Disaggregated analysis needs safe collection and nonuser evidence, with
privacy and a route to correct categories. No budget, population, current
law, provider inventory or observed equality outcome exists here.
