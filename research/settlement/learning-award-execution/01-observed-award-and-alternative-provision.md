# Two English education decisions and their limits

## Award in force, provision delayed

In Ombudsman decision 24 021 963, a learner started full-time mainstream
school in September 2024. The amended plan had specified six hours of
independent tuition weekly for the academic year, until the next annual
review. The council reported that the team that finalized the plan had
transferred the case without commissioning the tuition. The investigator
found that around sixteen weeks passed without the specified tuition
before provision began in mid-January 2025. Partial provision continued
into May. The provider's records showed approximately eight additional
hours in June and July. The Ombudsman found the later hours did not
restore the lost provision and recorded agreement to a GBP 1,100
symbolic payment. Actual payment is outside this record. The case
concerned the lost tutoring component, and other education at school
continued. The reported weeks are approximate and no complete weekly
record supports an exact total of missed hours. [Final decision,
paragraphs 20–38](https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/education/special-educational-needs/24-021-963)

## An alternative offer can be suitable on its own facts

In decision 25 011 094, the investigator found on the balance of
probabilities that the council arranged suitable alternatives during
the investigated January 2024 to March 2025 period. They considered
individual needs, intensive one-to-one teaching, a staged return
and objections to specific providers. The investigator separately
found fault in the lack of a timely update of the formal plan.
Some matters after March 2025 were outside the investigation because
of their connection to the tribunal appeal. The finding on suitability
is scoped to the arrangements examined. It cannot establish full
actual attendance, all support received, or how the learner ultimately
did. The case gives a reason to test suitability and learner access
rather than mechanically require the same duration in every setting.
[Final decision, paragraphs 11–12 and 50–56](https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/education/special-educational-needs/25-011-094)

These are two case decisions from the same institution. They do not
estimate a national rate, prove the cause of the earlier financial
gap in Leicestershire or establish the effect of this country's
proposed arrangements. The reported learners' experiences are
mediated by the decision records. They are not participation in
AIcountry's design. [Source scope and custody](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-learning-award-execution-and-recovery/sources.md)
