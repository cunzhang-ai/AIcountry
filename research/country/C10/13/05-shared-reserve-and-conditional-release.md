# RC21–RC24. Fund a shared reserve that can be released safely

*Design218 · Part of [civil rescue](../13-civil-rescue-and-incident-command.md).*

**RC21 — Plan the necessary service before choosing how to share it.**
For a material shared standby commitment, each commissioning authority
and service owner state the tasks, people and places exposed, useful
arrival and receiving limits, and the supplying area's protected home
service under applicable law. Test plausible concurrent demands,
including prolonged activity, a lost downstream receiver, the next
request and a shared controller, road, fuel, grid or communications
failure. Include domestic and foreign aid where genuinely available.
Price local prevention and retention, a pooled reserve and a physically
independent alternate route against the same protection. Prefer pooled
cover for risks that it can actually carry together. Fund separate
capacity or a service-preserving alternative where the relevant failures
coincide. State where protection cannot yet be established. Sector
judgment, elected appropriations and protected independent functions
retain their own authority.

**RC22 — Make each paid standby commitment precise.** Use RC10 agreements
and existing service and budget contracts. Identify the actual dispatch
owner, competent rested crew, equipment, eligible tasks, place, response
window, recipient, independent routes, safe release and replenishment
conditions. Publish material funding and coverage limits safely. Keep
four distinct states in the operational record. A capability can be
listed, held for a compatible task and window, lawfully dispatched,
or received. Record the later time it can safely serve again. An asset
can serve different nonoverlapping windows or a valid conditional pool.
A shared promise must state its unmet-demand rule. It cannot be counted
as independently ready in two incompatible plans. For paid availability
use safe witnessed acceptance or equivalent operating evidence before
reliance and after material change. Pay readiness, use, cover, restoration
and liability under their actual lawful sources. A prospective reimbursement
does not supply cash or a rested team at dispatch.

**RC23 — Confirm the sending and receiving paths in useful time.** The
actual dispatch authority accepts an exclusive dispatch for the relevant
window under the agreement and release conditions. The receiving duty
officer confirms an actual staffed place or safe field receiver, current
access and accepted timing. The donor officer verifies protected home
coverage, including any actual accepted lawful substitute. A prepared
standing acceptance can supply facts within its current scope. Use direct
live verification if the facts change. Move to a usable alternate plan by
the last safe start if receipt or release cannot be relied on. A first
request, dashboard indication or AI match does not reserve or dispatch a
crew. For conflicting lawful requests, use enacted resource-specific
priorities and qualified judgment under RC/CP, protecting donor duties
and materially equal claimants. Where a material tradeoff falls outside
those powers, use existing narrow protection and the Assembly route.
Where capacity is absent, retain the unmet claim and seek available
lawful help. A failure to agree creates no new dispatch authority.

**RC24 — Keep a usable local route through a communications split.**
Prepare and test alternative communication and offline operational
records under RC, DP and OC. Before loss, identify a single competent
release authority for each dispatch window or divide the available
capacity into genuinely nonoverlapping scopes. Use dated acknowledged
handoffs and a protected local decision trail. A late duplicate request
or stale availability record does not renew an expired hold. If safe
exclusivity or reception cannot be confirmed, treat that shared option as
unverified and use a verified alternate or existing lawful urgent route.
Existing urgent authority within its own scope needs no new signature.
Restore shared records after reconnection, including unresolved claims
and any inconsistent orders. Data convergence cannot retrospectively
authorize a dispatch or undo a loss.
