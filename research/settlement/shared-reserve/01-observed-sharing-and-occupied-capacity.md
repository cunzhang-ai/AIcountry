# Two ways that a listed response capacity becomes unavailable

## Australia. Concurrent demand can exhaust mutual aid

The Royal Commission examining the 2019–20 bushfire season described
existing bilateral and national arrangements. It reported national
coordination of the deployment of over 7,305 interstate and international
personnel through the interstate arrangement, with some requests still
unfilled. Its evidence distinguished reports of availability from the
ability to forecast against local commitments. It also identified that
bilateral activity fell outside the national coordination centre's view,
that resources were described differently across systems and that fatigue
and advance notice could limit effective mobilization. It recommended a
national register and stronger interoperability. This is historical
inquiry evidence about assistance and limits. The deployment total is
neither a concurrent headcount nor a measure of all domestic aid.
[Royal Commission report, chapter 6, paragraphs 6.30–6.62](https://www.royalcommission.gov.au/natural-disasters/report)

The country design uses interoperable scoped records, joined risks and
actual release. It leaves open whether one national system or locally
maintained records provide stronger operational visibility in a given
context. A register cannot itself restore available hours or donor cover.

## Northern Ireland. A receiver can keep a crew out of service

The Northern Ireland Audit Office measured hospital handover from
arrival to physical transfer of care. The service standard was 15 minutes
for that step and a further 15 minutes to prepare and leave. Handovers
taking more than an hour rose from 9% in 2019/20 to 34% in 2023/24.
For 2023/24 the audit reported 123,059 operational hours lost above the
target, equivalent to 25% of operational capacity, and valued that capacity
at £13.2 million. The valuation is an estimate of lost operational
capacity in that period. It is separate from recoverable budget cash or
the cost of a second vehicle. The audit links the problem to hospital
and community constraints as well as ambulance availability.
[Northern Ireland Audit Office report, sections 1.3–1.11 and executive summary](https://www.niauditoffice.gov.uk/publications/ambulance-handovers-northern-ireland)

The two observations describe different institutions and dates. They
qualify distinct failure paths and do not estimate a national reserve
size, common intervention effect or return from the proposed design.
