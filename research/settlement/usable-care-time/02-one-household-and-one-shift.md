# One child, one late shift and a paid alternative

*Design199 · Authored four-week scenario using published route boundaries.*

## Keep the household and its needed times the same

One carer has a two-year-old. Ordinary care on four other weekdays
is seven hours a day. Each Tuesday's accepting day provider covers
08:15 to 17:00. The carer's scheduled paid shift ends at 18:10. Allow
30 minutes for the journey to the provider and ten minutes for handover
and ordinary delay. The necessary pickup horizon is 18:50. These are
explicit scenario assumptions, not actual Copenhagen opening times,
work hours or journey measurements. No private driver or unpaid
relative is available by assumption.

Tuesday needs 1 hour 50 minutes beyond the place's existing close.
Over four identical weeks the gap is 7 hours 20 minutes, although
total needed weekly care is 38 hours 35 minutes. A nominal 40-hour
offer can fail. Copenhagen's published combination route has a
ten-hour average weekly minimum. This example falls below that route's
threshold even with documented work need. Its other routes are real
alternatives if a provider, journey and actual fee are accepted.
Helsinki's occasional route could cover it in principle subject to
actual acceptance and its own terms. [Published boundaries](01-copenhagen-and-helsinki.md)

## Put staffing and payer on the same clock

| Choice under the same needs | Useful-time consequence | Who supplies what |
|---|---|---|
| Retime work | Ending by 16:20 leaves 40 minutes to arrive and complete pickup by 17:00 | Employer accepts the change and accounts for 110 minutes of earlier release versus the original shift end. If the original task still needs coverage, record accepted relief or other real production cost. No involuntary transfer of the shift |
| Extend the existing setting | The child stays with an agreed familiar group until pickup. Normal receiving is available through 19:00 | The existing care authority buys the named later interval and staffed handoff, uses ordinary fee treatment in the candidate and verifies the family can complete the journey |
| Move to a shared late session | Alternative may serve several small requests if the transfer starts while ordinary care is still staffed and both legs work | Paid safe escort, accepting specialist or care team, communication and handoff time. Staff cannot supervise both sites at once. An unsuitable transfer leaves the claim open |
| Give cash or ordinary daytime hours only | No known accepting carer between 17:00 and 18:50 | Cash can support a real chosen provider. Neither cash nor another morning hour settles the uncovered interval |

For one targeted model suppose six same-age children actually need the
same window and are eligible to remain together. Under NC02 the group
of six requires at least two qualified adults present through the
session. Check each child's actual support and safe room conditions
separately. In the authored late rota, the two adults work
16:50–19:10, including ten minutes of paid transition before 17:00
and ten minutes of paid close work after the planned 19:00 care end.
No break or other group is assumed during these hours. Account
4 hours 40 minutes of late-role work per session, or 224 staff-hours
for 48 sessions, and count any overlap with a genuinely retained
paid shift only once. A separately reserved worker for the same
period would take 112 standby staff-hours over 48 sessions if
availability consumes that full time. Value standby and activated
work according to actual lawful terms without double counting.
Add required training, leave cover, supervision, travel, access,
premises and supplies. These are a conditional workload schedule
and separate source lines, with no wage quote, recruit or financed
offer. Six children's paid-care hours are not six simultaneous staff
shifts. A second center with the same substitute cannot promise
that person during the same window.

If one of the two adults is missing and no qualified replacement can
arrive, the stated same-age ratio permits at most four children with
the one available adult even before safety and other needs are assessed.
At least two do not have the promised place under that rota. Apply
existing safety and scarcity criteria and find real alternatives.
No manager may mask the loss by averaging across hours, blaming the
family or silently excluding children with more needs.

## Failed pickup, separate remedy and succession

A 20-minute travel disruption pushes pickup to 19:10, beyond the
normal care window. The ten-minute closing assignment is work
already allocated and cannot itself prove accepted extra care.
The provider activates protected contact and lawful supervision,
replans and pays the worker assignment, and seeks the competent
local or FC fallback and safe return. The child stays supervised
until an authorized handoff. If ordinary transport also fails,
record its actual dependent claim and use a paid accepting MO
route if available. Do not claim that both duties have been met
because one system found a later arrival. If an employer caused
a material late change, TW/WL review of notice and consent remains
available. A justified refusal, hours delivered, loss of pay and
review have their own scopes.

Use a dated care request, an accepted window, lawful spending and
actual pickup as separate evidence. If a court orders relief,
track timely receiving, lateness and remaining claims under SR.
Keep the ordinary placement, original deadlines and qualified
workers through local or provider handover under OP/T and HT.
AI can propose alternative times and flag a double-booked worker;
no suggested slot amounts to an accepted shift or family consent.

The bounded next test compares one observed late window with the
same accessible alternatives. Observe matched intake, staff availability
and wages, refused or missed windows, actual journey, bill and child
handover. Include people who did not apply, if they choose to participate,
and test another claim on the same holiday backup. Use a paid intake,
an existing representative decision and protected independent review.
Reopen on a worse child experience, recurring loss of work/rest,
displaced ordinary provision, failed staffing or a simpler equal outcome.
