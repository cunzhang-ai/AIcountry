# Financing, shortages and reference scenarios

## 3. One financing perimeter and a public choice over shortages

Consolidate national/local budgets, controlled entities, capital, maintenance, existing
debts, guarantees and tax relief. Internal transfers cancel on consolidation. Public
service wages appear in service costs, not again as a separate national wage bill.
Pensions and other cash benefits are transfers; procurement and services are different
uses. Refinance maturing debt as a financing flow, not a second service expenditure.

The national guarantee schedule and local delivery allocations are adopted together.
Equalization covers standardized need less standardized own-source capacity under a
published formula. An explicit funding gap remains a national political responsibility;
local authorities retain their lawful duties and challenge route. Treasury cannot change
eligibility or hide a shortfall by refusing to release an already authorized payment.

Before adding a benefit, ministry or service expansion, show the incremental recurring
cost, staffing, capital/maintenance, available cash by due date, effect on other claims
and transition. Recurring programs need recurring financing or a stated sustainable
financing path; a one-time asset sale or assumed automation gain cannot satisfy it.

When resources fall short:

1. Delivery authorities and treasury disclose the actual gap and imminent unmet needs.
   Do not wait for the annual report or turn a missed payment into a closed case.
2. Execute existing lawful contingency/continuity arrangements and immediate protection
   duties. Their limits and cash must be real; they cannot renew their own authority.
3. Cabinet submits a revised package with revenue, lawful borrowing, resource reallocation
   and genuinely deferrable expansion alternatives. Show effects on groups and future
   maintenance. Across-the-board cuts are a political choice, not a neutral formula.
4. Assembly decides policy and funding under rights constraints. Opposition can introduce
   alternatives; independent adjudication can order lawful remedies. Reviewers cannot
   manufacture nurses, currency or a new tax. Treasury and providers must execute valid
   orders within the applicable powers and expose failures.
5. If no adequate lawful solution exists, retain the unmet claim, record the breach,
   protect against arbitrary prioritization and pursue the responsible remedy. Neither
   a budget ceiling nor declaring an emergency makes deprivation acceptable.

Avoid expansion commitments whose delivery depends on untrained staff or unbuilt
capacity. That rule governs new promises, not unilateral withdrawal of existing rights.
Capital investment may be necessary to secure those rights; calling it discretionary
does not make postponement harmless. A real schedule needs subannual cash and delivery
planning; the annual illustration below cannot establish it.

## 4. Common reference scenario and alternatives

The model is an existing hypothetical population of 100,000 residents, including 20,000
children and 80,000 adults, with 15,000 older adults already inside that adult count.
There is no claim of a founding electorate or empty territory. All residents are assumed
covered for this illustration. Omitted visitors, migration and changing population must
be added for real planning. Years 1–3 are model periods, not calendar forecasts.

GDP is assumed constant at 3,000 million CU. Revenue rises from 900 to 915 to 930 million
CU through declared receipts, not an estimated tax function or growth prediction. The
receipt lines are personal, corporate, consumption, land, pollution and other recurring
revenues. Their household incidence and behavioral feasibility remain unknown. No assumed
separate payroll receipt may be added to fund the same benefits twice.

Opening debt is 600 and liquid public assets are 150 million CU. The assumed cash-buffer
floor is 100; it is a model constraint, not a legally privileged claim over essentials.
Interest is 5% of opening funded debt. Each year 100 of maturing principal can be refinanced
and up to 60 of additional debt issued. These are invented financing conditions, not lender
commitments or a safe debt rule. Borrowing precedes drawing available cash in the model.
Unfunded obligations carry as arrears; no unmodeled loan or recovered debt balances them.

| Package | Service and income choice | Primary outlays, years 1/2/3, million CU |
|---|---|---|
| B: retain capacity | Existing service staffing, maintenance/capital 40 annually, cash support 100 annually | 880 / 895 / 910 |
| S: expand services with income floor | Meet the authored growing service workload; capital/training-related investment 60 annually; retain cash support 100 | 900 / 934 / 968 |
| U: add unconditional cash | B plus 600 CU per resident annually; existing cash protection retained | 940 / 955 / 970 |

Recurring costs include staff, operations, routine maintenance, administration, payment
channels, oversight and training operations; capital is separately counted. These are
envelopes without measured unit costs. Assumed staff availability includes recruitment
and retention that have not been established. S is not qualified merely by allocating it.
No capital quantity is a validated number of dwellings, schools or hospital beds.

The illustrative 100 million cash-support envelope contains 45 for old age, 15 for
disability and 40 for other income security, without claiming adequacy or assigning
every person to one exclusive benefit. Assumed distribution to the bottom 40%, middle
40% and top 20% of residents by initial income is 70/25/5 million. U adds 24/24/12 million.
Those shares are authored, not microsimulation results. They show gross cash distribution;
net incidence and service benefits are not established. No net welfare ranking follows.
