# One local budget and three service commitments

*Design200 · Reproducible authored examples in millions of planning units.*

## Count the same grant once

Apply the preserved NF23 formula for this illustration only. Assume
standard common cost N of 120, representative capacity C of 80,
actual annual local cash of 80 at the representative rate, and no
other same-duty receipts. The general grant is max(0,120 − 0.75×80)
= 60. Assign 60 of local cash and 60 of general grant to the common
floor. The remaining 20 of actual local cash retains its locally
elected optional scope, subject to actual law. This is a possible
elected allocation, not a compulsory proportional formula.

| Common service | Standard cost | Allocated local source | Allocated general grant | Remaining sector source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early care | 36 | 18 | 18 | 0 |
| Essential mobility | 24 | 12 | 12 | 0 |
| Schools | 60 | 30 | 30 | 0 |
| Total | 120 | 60 | 60 | 0 |

The 18 is the general-grant component available for the care cost,
with the actual care budget and advance still needing verification.
Neither the entire grant nor the entire local tax capacity can be
credited against each service. A ministry cannot subtract a grant
from a net cost which already includes that source. The locality
can choose a different lawful mix if every continuing duty is financed
and received. NF cash/deferral scope and the TN numerical downgrade remain.

Now assume elected law assigns 12 more of recurring care cost and
initially finances it with a scoped sector source outside the old
common grant. The common floor plus addition costs 132, funded by
60 of local allocation, 60 of common grant and 12 of sector funding.
If new law later moves the addition into N without a change in C, the
benchmark general grant becomes 72. The sector line for the same
cost and period ends when its lawful paid replacement begins. Total
national annual funding for those duties is 72 in either arrangement,
before any genuinely distinct transition expense. Paying both the new
72 and the old 12 for the same cost would overstate sources by 12.
Ending the old 12 before the new cash is available can leave that
service unfunded. Existing arrears remain owed through either switch.

## Change the distribution, then the date

Suppose two equal-population places receive a new annual duty with
qualified local incremental costs of 12 and 4. A population-only
distribution of the same aggregate 16 yields 8 and 8, leaving the
first place 4 short. The other 4 is an excess relative to its stated
cost, with its actual lawful use governed by its own budget. A
need-matched distribution can close this particular gap with 12
and 4 if the source and costs are valid. No conclusion follows about
the distribution of all existing grants or the adequate funding for
either real locality. Unequal costs may be warranted, disputed,
or caused by avoidable operating choices. Those explanations call
for different evidence before assigning a payer.

Return to the first table and assume the benchmark 60 comes in
12 installments of 5. At the first payroll and supplier date the
locality has 5 of legally usable own cash and 8 due. On-time grant
receipt leaves 2 after those payments. If the installment does not
arrive, the gap is 3 despite annual apparent balance. The
undisputed grant of 5 still falls due on its source date. Use the
actual authorized payer and any lawful alternate, preserving the
amount not yet received and its original deadline. An independent
order can enforce an available lawful path without inventing
appropriation or a private loan. Later receipt does not erase the
delay or any care, transport or supplier claim. Record whether
actual services continued separately from settlement of the grant.
If Cabinet withholds the installment from an opposition council,
the protected calculation and due instruction remain reviewable.
The authorized independent route can execute the amount where
the law and funds permit. Record deliberate withholding separately
from cash failure, with election and ordinary powers unaffected.

If annual actual local cash instead falls to 48 while the assessed C
and grant remain 80 and 60 under this scenario's law and vintage,
the aggregate usable source is 108 against 120. Assume the 48 is
available for the floor and no optional commitments already bind it.
The floor still lacks 12 after using the local source. Determine
whether this is a timely correction, an authorized shock instrument
or a durable source change. The whole 32 difference between C and
collected cash is not automatically payable as a higher formula grant.

Finally assume money reaches the local account while two services
count the same licensed backup driver for the same hour. One worker
cannot do both. Check the first due trip, the claimed second route,
the effect on the supplying place and protected prior-law priority.
Actual receiving, qualified staffing, cash and legal authority
each keep a separate outcome. An unresolved service does not close
as referred or funded.

## What would discriminate among the alternatives?

For the same month and places trace one costed new or continuing
duty from prior law to local allocation, usable cash, actual payroll,
safe accepted staff and receipt. Include the common backup and an
opposition council or independent reviewer who can challenge
withholding. Compare timely ordinary implementation, a real source
shock and transition with the strongest retained public arrangement.
Identify exactly whose claim is missed if either place lacks workers
or usable reserves. A simpler arrangement can win if it funds and
delivers the same duty with equal local choice and correction.
