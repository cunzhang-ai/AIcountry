# Environmental duties and recoverable money in Alberta

## A decision about proceeds

In the 2019 Redwater case, the Supreme Court of Canada directed
that proceeds from the sale of Redwater Energy's assets be used
for its end-of-life obligations. The majority distinguished the
estate's regulatory duties in that case from provable debts and
distinguished the estate's duties from personal trustee liability.
The dissent viewed the provincial and federal rules as inconsistent.
The decision answers a legal dispute about the assets. It does not
establish that the proceeds were sufficient or when protection
reached affected sites. CLN05's prospective registered site charge
is the country's own narrower proposal, rather than a restatement
of the Canadian rule.

## Security, spending and actual progress

Alberta's Auditor General reported in March 2023 that the oil and
gas regulator had processes for managing non-oil-sands liabilities,
but some did not adequately mitigate closure risk. The audit found
that the previous security system did not meet its objectives and
identified deficiencies in checking the sustainability of the orphan
fund and monitoring transfer conditions. It also identified a risk
that spending requirements encourage work on lower-risk sites.
These are findings at the audit period's scope, with later work on
the framework separately described.
[Alberta Auditor General report, summary and sections 3–6](https://www.oag.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Liability-management-oil-gas-mar2023.pdf)

The Alberta Energy Regulator's November 2026 publication reports
2025 closure quotas and outcomes. The industry quota was CAD 750
million and licensees reported CAD 912 million of eligible spending.
The orphan levy was CAD 144.45 million and the Orphan Well
Association's closure expenditure was CAD 154 million. The levy
figure and expenditure have distinct scopes. The difference does
not establish a deficit or a recovery rate. The regulator also
reports 77,932 inactive wells in 2025, compared with 82,837 in 2022.
These counts do not track individual downstream harm.
[Alberta Energy Regulator performance report](https://www.aer.ca/data-and-performance-reports/industry-performance/liability-management-performance-report)

The regulator estimated active and inactive oil and gas closure
liability at CAD 38.0 billion at the end of 2025. It reduces the
estimate using the assigned cost of a completed reported activity,
which can differ from actual spending. Method revisions affect the
comparison over time. Do not subtract the year's quota or cash
from the estimated stock as a reconciliation of actual remediation.
[Alberta Energy Regulator liability method](https://www.aer.ca/data-and-performance-reports/industry-performance/liability-management-performance-report/estimated-liability-and-licensee-capability)
