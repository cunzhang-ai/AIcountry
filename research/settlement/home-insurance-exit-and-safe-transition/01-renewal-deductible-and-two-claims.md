# Renewal, the deductible and two concurrent claims

*Design373 · Published United States market evidence and a separate constructed comparison.*

## Observed market differences

The Federal Insurance Office's Figure 14 compares the 20% of
analyzed ZIP Codes with the highest expected annual building losses
from specified climate-related perils with the lowest 20%. For
2018–2022, average inflation-adjusted premiums per policy were USD
2,321 and USD 1,277, respectively. That is an observed USD 1,044
difference, or about 82% relative to the lower group. Average
nonrenewal rates were 1.61% and 0.90%, a difference of 0.71
percentage points. These are market aggregates from the report's
covered insurers and policy forms. Its modeled peril measure and
standard policy data exclude flood. It does not measure flood
coverage, estimate individual affordability, or isolate a causal
effect of risk on pricing. The figures below are independent
illustrative units.

## Two distinct windows

A household's replacement premium is eight units due by renewal
day 10. Five units are safely available for the premium. A separately
authorized bridge of three units received by day 9 closes the renewal
gap. If it arrives on day 11 the original offer expires in the case.
After the timely payment the household has only one unit for a
four-unit deductible. The policy is valid, but a covered loss still
leaves a three-unit barrier to repair. Suitable works priced at four
units could be accepted by day 30. Any reduction in the deductible
or the next premium requires actual amended or replacement terms.
The later work does not secure the first renewal. In the case,
a supported voluntary move also cannot be received by day 10.
An actual safe place and paid transition could make it preferable
for longer exposure. Existing shelter and household protection
remain on their own sources and dates.

For a separate catastrophe test, two valid claims are each due
12 units by claim day 5 after deductibles. The primary insurer can
settle 14 in total by day 4. Two units of expected reinsurance arrive
only on day 7. The proposed public layer has a ten-unit legal
ceiling. Eight settled public units leave two units of claims unpaid
on day 5. Ten settled public units by day 5 cover both amounts
on the stated terms. If those ten arrive on day 6, ten remain
unpaid at the original cutoff. The test states a combined cash
gap without changing either claimant's legal priority. Actual
repairs and safe occupancy require separately usable delivery.

| Policy option | What must work |
|---|---|
| Ordinary renewal and direct support | Clear the actual premium and any repair barrier on time. Retain cash aid, safety and service options where qualified cover cannot be bought. |
| Targeted time-limited bridge and durable risk reduction | Verify safe occupancy and the next policy or safe receiving. Name payer, limits, completion, accepted changes and the effect on residents. |
| Bounded public risk layer | Verify loss order, collective exposure and timely backing, especially when a common source fails. The ceiling does not deliver cash. |

The example supports targeted transitional assessment and protects
ordinary resident duties while a durable option is decided. It
cannot determine market-wide affordability, an actual premium
subsidy, reserve size, or whether a specific property should move.
