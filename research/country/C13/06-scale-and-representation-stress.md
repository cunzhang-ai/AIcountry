# C13. Can the same political form reach different places?

*Part of [C13 lived experience](../C13-lived-experience.md); integrated design 55 research exercise, not an observed country.*

Test the reference national Assembly/local-council form against three
invented settings without treating any as AIcountry's actual geography.
A compact urban polity has dense travel and one major service area; a
separate elected regional layer may duplicate the same staff and tax base.
A dispersed island polity has intermittent transport and communications;
a resident may formally vote but lose access to nomination, hearings, a
court and essential care. A large multi-region polity has several durable
labor markets, catchments and historical claims; the national Assembly may
face more material interlocal allocations than its opposition-accessible
docket can decide.

For each setting, map the same person's vote, safe organizing route,
ordinary service, shared-resource dispute, evidence and executed remedy.
Count election workers, accessible travel, interpreters, Assembly hearing
time, local/region staff, money, alternate communications and court reach
once. Simulate an incumbent who benefits from a boundary or backlog and a
shutdown of the favored digital channel. Compare the three C14 form
packages and a lawful retain/no-change option. The test fails if an
unrepresented place's claims disappear, a nominal region has no elected
control, or a national bottleneck awards power to cabinet by delay.

These are discriminating design tests, not proof that one form is best.
Actual founding requires real population and service maps, affected-party
participation, lawful authority and validated capacity. No scenario can
choose a people or ratify a constitution on their behalf.
