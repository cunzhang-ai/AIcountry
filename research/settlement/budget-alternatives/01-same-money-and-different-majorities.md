# Same money, different majorities

A 100-seat Assembly has three vacancies. The final threshold is still 51.
There are 44 Cabinet-aligned members, 32 service-reform members and 21
rural independents. The scenario treats them as internally uniform only
to make the arithmetic inspectable. Ten designations qualify an alternative.
On the same earlier-law baseline, every package retains six units of
continuing obligations. Four additional units and one qualified
commissioning slot are usable in the decision window.

| Exact package | Use of the four optional units and the slot | Designations | Indicative yes |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| G1 | Preventive bridge maintenance where existing protection is met | 44 | 44 |
| S1 | Add accessible disability-payment navigation | 32 | 53 |
| R1 | Add rural clinical receiving capacity | 21 | 21 |

All packages spend ten units and claim the same single slot. S1 plus R1
would spend fourteen units including the continuing six and would require
two simultaneous slots. The independently positive reasons for each
option cannot justify adopting both from the same resource. An AI can
flag this conflict and expose assumptions without combining the texts.

If the Cabinet alone puts G1 to a yes-or-no vote, 44 votes fail and
there is no budget despite the elected majority willing to approve S1.
Under BCV, S1 reaches the first final vote. If the same 53 members
affirm that exact text, it passes. R1 was actually considered and
recorded before it was displaced by the incompatible S1. Its sponsors
can still pursue a subsequent lawful proposal, without spending the
four units or commissioning slot a second time.

| Countercase | Required result in the authored case |
|---|---|
| Three members retract their support in the binding vote | S1 has 50 and fails. G1 and R1 get their votes in order and also fail. |
| S1 is replaced late by S2, which needs a different funding source | S1's counts do not carry. If the new text cannot complete fresh scrutiny and designation, defer it and vote on unaffected texts. |
| S1 and G1 tie on indications, and the public tie order puts G1 first | G1 receives the first vote. If G1 fails and S1 receives 53 final yes votes, S1 still passes. |
| Thirty-nine G1 voters leave after the indicative count | With 58 members present the final presence requirement is still met. If seven more leave, 51 is just sufficient. With 50 present, the sitting cannot approve any package. |
| Five members submit several variants through multiple agents | They remain five actual members and can each designate only one exact text. The protected gate remains ten. |

The case assumes all standing obligations are separately payable. A
rejected budget by itself does not remove them. An enacted but unfunded
new line remains subject to its own lawful source and release conditions.
