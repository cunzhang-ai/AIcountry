# Evidence for usable time and public participation

| Primary source | What was observed | Scope and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Office for National Statistics, Time Use in the UK, March 2023](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/bulletins/timeuseintheuk/march2023) | The historical survey used two allocated diary days and distinguished paid work, unpaid household work, care, travel, sleep and other activities. Published estimates use reported main activities and omit secondary or passive activity. The release describes incomplete diary responses and weighting. | Time diaries can distinguish important uses. The published means do not measure protected or contiguous free time or the effectiveness of the proposed participation routes. |
| [U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-25-107239](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107239/index.html) | GAO examined 51 selected benefit-related information requests and found the instructions did not cover all burden-reduction guidance. All 51 described compliance costs, while 10 described psychological costs and two learning costs. The selection targeted large estimated public burdens. | Record learning, stress and timing in addition to tasks completed. These are counts of reported costs in a selected administrative sample, not personal time-use estimates or prevalence across all programmes. |

The two residents, clocks, staffed alternatives and cost units in
the [comparison](../settlement/usable-time/README.md) are authored.
The observed studies do not establish the proposed relative effects,
accepted participants, legal authority or full public cost.
