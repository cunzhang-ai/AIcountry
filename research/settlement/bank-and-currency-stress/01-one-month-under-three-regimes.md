# One month of bank failure and scarce foreign settlement

*Design483 · Constructed comparison of inherited, external and domestic arrangements.*

Start with the inherited claims of the [opening budget
comparison](../monetary-commencement/01-one-budget-three-currency-arrangements.md).
Extend the calendar to 30 days. L is the predecessor unit, F the
contractual foreign unit, and N a new domestic unit. This is an authored
path, not an observed jurisdiction or a forecast. The comparative
branches make different arrangements explicit on the same opening
endowments, obligations, shocks and protected service.

## The common month

| Due | Claim and cutoff outcome to observe |
|---|---|
| Days 1 and 15 | Treasury owes two 8 L payrolls. It starts with 16 L, including an independently safeguarded second tranche. The first instruction's status is unknown at close on day 1. Independent records on day 2 show it failed and the first 8 L is available to the alternate payer. The second tranche stays separate. Record the first payroll late even if it is eventually accessible. |
| Days 2, 3 and 11 | On day 2 a failed bank leaves covered deposit claims of 6 L. The insurer delivers a 2 L advance on day 3 by the original advance cutoff. The 4 L draw has a lawful source and fits the 7 L facility with 3 L already drawn, but misses the day-11 balance cutoff. The 4 L remains a claim until payment. These dates are assumed under the applicable predecessor scheme. |
| Days 5, 12 and 23 | Health needs 4 F at the first medicine cutoff, a foreign-law public creditor is owed 3 F, and a second medicine lot needs 4 F. Across the full month, 8 F is legally committed and owned for these three uses under a prior publicly authorized schedule. No after-the-fact diversion or creditor forbearance is assumed. |
| Days 6 and 25 | Verify one paid home-delivery route per timely lot and usable medicines by the treatment date. A staffed local reserve covers one missed lot. It cannot cover two. Preserve every missed patient journey and actual household burden. |
| Day 30 | Carry forward any unpaid principal, uncovered care, unresolved loss and original review duties. No forecast recovery, bank recapitalization or foreign swap counts as cash. |

A common bank failure disables the ordinary first-payroll route. An
exchange shock raises the constructed local price of a monthly essential
basket from 4 L to 5 L under the assumed price pass-through. Fixed wages
lose purchasing power in all three branches because those wages and
prices are still denominated in L. Independently funded direct grocery
relief buys one basket for the person whose first pay is late, if a
retailer accepts the funded order. At the second household call on
day 14, one funded basket remains due. The first retailer cannot
replenish and there is no paid alternate in the base branch. Record
missed grocery receipt even if the payment for the order is final.
A funded alternate with one verified basket would restore actual
access. Neither grocery relief nor insured
deposit access pays the employment claim. Record the affected
household's paid wage, accessible deposit, delivered groceries and
uncovered cost independently. The price path is held fixed to isolate
delivery mechanisms; a real comparison must vary domestic and external
prices, cross-currency contracts, expectations and confidence.

## Three arrangements and one foreign access failure

The 8 F consists of two 4 F tranches. The shared foreign intermediary
fails before day 5. In all branches, the first tranche clears on a distinct
accepted route. The second tranche needs an actual alternate with
accepting counterparties and control of originals. Assume foreign
priority permits 3 F for the day-12 creditor and leaves 1 F restricted
to the second medicine lot. The lots are indivisible at their cutoff.
Where the alternate fails, the whole 4 F remains outside usable custody
for day 12 and becomes accessible on day 16. The unpaid creditor claim
is then paid late by its lawful retained earmark in the fixture. Its
interest or breach remedies still need their own record.

| Arrangement | Day 12 | Day 25 and household exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Available lawful predecessor | Alternate foreign clearing only from day 16. Debt is late. An independent L route pays the first wages on day 2 and second on day 15. | First lot arrives. Second is 3 F short. The one local reserve pays for physical replacement. |
| Explicit external arrangement | A preaccepted independent correspondent provides access by day 12. Its fees and collateral need separate prior funding. Debt is on time. If both names share the blocked controller, this advantage disappears. | Same 3 F funding gap and reserve use. Existing wages still require the actual L route. No foreign institution is assumed to insure legacy deposits. |
| Staged own currency | Same foreign route and late debt as the predecessor. Issuing N helps neither cutoff. Older payroll and deposits retain L. Ordinary L access and the end-of-month obligations continue. | A newly authorized N retailer route could reach a future beneficiary if it buys the required basket at an accepted price. This adds no current food stock or care slot. With the same merchant dependency it adds no resilience. |

In all three branches there is a **3 F shortage** for the second lot
despite 8 F against 11 F due. An actual, lawfully owned and independently
cleared 3 F by day 23 changes the budget comparison. Medicine still
needs acceptance and local receiving by day 25. If the first tranche
also loses actual access, the local reserve covers only one missed lot
and the other creates an uncovered treatment need. The insurer has
4 L of legal undrawn headroom against 4 L of unfunded covered exposure
in this scoped test. Its legal ceiling
passes while due-date liquidity fails. Assume the draw settles on
day 18 and the accepting payer delivers it that day. The accrued
arrear is cleared, but timeliness does not improve. Outstanding
public facility principal is now 7 L. Projected estate recovery
cannot reduce it. An unrecovered loss and a missed health outcome
remain separate exposures.

## A second call and a blocked Assembly sitting

Test a separate adverse branch. The first lot arrived on time, so
the staffed reserve remains unspent. The last 3 F actually arrives
on day 22. Its ownership permits either a full second lot when paired
with the existing 1 F earmark or retaining it for a separate energy
invoice due on day 27. The delivery officer cannot fulfill both.
Before the shock the Assembly could have enacted a bounded rule
for this eligible 3 F, based on accepted receiving, treatment
urgency, published criteria and independent challenge. An available
local medicine reserve covers the lot, while no qualified alternate
covers the energy call. In that branch the voted contingency uses
the 3 F for energy and accepts the reserve for medicines. If the
reserve fails before the import cutoff and the funds are still
available, reassess within the enacted terms. If the release is final
or the cutoff has passed, seek separately funded relief and record any
missed treatment. Never reclaim a final payment through the old rule.

When the ordinary sitting is blocked, keep the other legally
effective duties. A new priority or source requires a competent
alternate sitting with usable opposition and protected review.
Absent prior authority or a valid new decision, disclose the
unallocated 3 F and each uncovered obligation. A temporary bank
order cannot decide this allocation or turn a statutory limit into
cash. Return to lawful procedure without restarting the claims.

For a prospective fifth-week supply cohort, compare an accepted
F contract on the external rail with a priced N contract on a
separate domestic route for the same local goods. Under loss of
both F receivers, the N route has an actual advantage only if the
provider accepts N and can deliver independently of imported inputs.
Under loss of the domestic merchant channel with the F rail intact,
the external contract has the opposite advantage if it has an
independent supplier. These are alternative new commitments. Neither
rewrites L payroll or F debt. Record overlap, costs and displacement
and price both routes before a decision. If the lawful predecessor
can procure the same independent route, its capacity also qualifies.

## Choose protections before selecting the regime

Keep the lawful predecessor in this reference if all residual risks are
recognized and urgent mitigation operates. Prefer the paid independent
foreign route where it protects the needed cutoff without displacing
the reserve or judiciary. That choice can be made within more than one
monetary regime. If only the external package can provide that route at
acceptable total cost, put that package to a separate elected decision
with its actual counterparties, price effects and reduced policy
discretion. Consider new N commitments only after their own readiness
and a fresh whole-package comparison. This exercise chooses continuity
and separates two changes that can be made without changing the unit:
independent settlement and funded physical reserves.

Do not transfer insurance capacity, money held for payroll, client
property or a monetary reserve by moving a line in the budget. Apply
the separate competent insolvency authority and creditor order. Record
an unpaid insurer balance, any unrecovered public loss, and households
or creditors still carrying harm. First use the applicable original
payout, retail and court routes, with protected funding and an
accepting executor. Only a competent elected instrument changes
ceilings or priority. Timely relief and settlement figures are published
in safe form with direct contest. AI can reconcile dates and expose
inconsistent assumptions. No AI output authorizes a guarantee,
debt write-down or scarce foreign release.
