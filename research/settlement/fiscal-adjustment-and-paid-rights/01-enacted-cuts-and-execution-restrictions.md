# A budget cut and a payment block are different events

## An enacted rule can affect people outside the visible cut line

The 1 March 2013 U.S. sequestration order followed federal law. The
Government Accountability Office reported an initial reduction of
USD 85.3 billion in federal budgetary resources and examined 23
agencies. This is budget authority, with documented varied effects,
rather than an equivalent amount of direct losses or foregone outlays.
The study describes delayed or reduced services, deferral of investments
and short-term mitigation. It cannot separate all causes.
[GAO-14-244](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-14-244)

An April 2013 OMB report addressed a different statutory test.
It found no additional discretionary-cap sequestration required under
section 251. Its scope explicitly separates the Joint Committee
mechanism under section 251A. Keeping the two legal triggers distinct
reconciles the apparently different accounts.
[OMB final report, introduction](https://github.com/cunzhang-ai/AIcountry/blob/edition-1/research/2026-11-fiscal-adjustment-and-paid-rights/original-sources/omb-final-2013.pdf)

A subsequent GAO report uses updated HUD data. About 41,000 fewer
very-low-income households received the covered rental vouchers at
the end of 2013 than at the end of 2012, down 2.2 percent, primarily
attributed to sequestration. The earlier estimate was about 42,000.
The later analysis excludes 35 agencies under the Moving to Work
demonstration. Housing authorities generally limited reissue of
vacated vouchers rather than terminating current ones. This is
evidence of reduced practical entry, with no census of all unmet
households or separate estimate of harm for each family.
[GAO-14-452, appendix IV, pp. 75–76](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-14-452.pdf)

The U.S. federal Judiciary reports that staffing in federal public
defender organizations fell from 2,779 in October 2012 to 2,358 in
March 2014. It also reports delayed panel-attorney payments in its
2013 operating account. Different definitions, times and other budget
conditions limit attribution and comparison. The record identifies a
funded dependency of meaningful justice, beyond courtroom funding.
[Defender Services, 2014 report](https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/reports/annual-reports/directors-annual-report/annual-report-2014/defender-services-annual-report-2014)
· [2013 operating report](https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/reports/annual-reports/directors-annual-report/annual-report-2013/sequestrations-impact-judiciarys-programs-and-operations-annual-report-2013)

## A material delay has to be explained in the operative account

In its July 2025 Head Start decision, GAO found a withholding of
funds from expenditure in violation of the U.S. Impoundment Control
Act. Its account used HHS grant-system data because the agency had
not supplied apportionment schedules and the public schedules were
unavailable. It compared the same January 20–April 15 window across
years using the corresponding year's funds.

| Scope | USD disbursed |
|---|---:|
| FY 2024 funds during the 2024 comparison window | 2,397,037,861 |
| FY 2025 funds during the 2025 comparison window | 1,571,620,771 |
| Difference | 825,417,090 |

The later figure is about 65.57 percent of the earlier figure.
GAO also reported a later recovery to similar aggregate disbursement
by June 9, 2025. These observations do not give the amount legally
due at each provider, the number that actually lost service or
the share of the gap caused by any single directive. A later
aggregate recovery cannot verify earlier local payroll, care or
support. GAO describes its finding and ongoing litigation separately.
[GAO B-337202, July 23, 2025](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-337202)

GAO's October 2026 decision about a different child-care programme
provides a useful counterexample. Its scope was the discretionary
Child Care Access Means Parents in School programme in FY 2025.
The ordinary grant obligations were near fiscal year-end in line
with the academic calendar. GAO reported approximately 99.51 percent
of the USD 75 million allocation obligated, compared with 99.34 and
99.32 percent in FY 2024 and FY 2023. It found no violation for
FY 2025 given the programme's authority, actual records and timing.
Obligation percentages for this programme are different measures
from Head Start's dated disbursements. They cannot be averaged.
[GAO B-337813, October 7, 2026](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-337813)

Use the first history to test shifted costs and dependence. Use
the two decisions to distinguish policy withholding from permitted
scheduling under actual law. These findings do not supply an
AIcountry legal power, a full national counterfactual or an estimate
of the fiscal rule's long-term effect.
