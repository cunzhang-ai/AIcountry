# IC13–IC14: investment must change a useful capability

*Part of [C05/09](../09-income-capital-and-democratic-investment.md); design88 candidate.*

**IC13 — Separate a funded project from a growth assumption.** An investment
offer names actual purchasers/users, inputs, title, sites, permits, qualified
people, build period, commissioning, operating cost, maintenance, renewal and
failure. Compare repair, demand reduction, diverse imports and domestic supply
on the same useful service and horizon. An AI productivity forecast, a higher
tax base or nominal GDP/FTE multiplier supplies none of these inputs. External
trade, ecology, imported harms and displaced local work remain part of the choice.

The authored example reserves 50 million CU from *realized* nonbank retained
cash after the bank ringfence and already offered public finance. That cash
is conditional on receipt of the model's public/private payments, some of
which remain unfunded. It is not currently available capital. Reservation
changes the use of an existing claim; it creates neither income nor GDP.
Build uses 35 million imports and 15 million domestic own-account formation;
the domestic amount is not a second F-to-F cash payment. Actual firm-level
payroll, supplier bills, FX settlement and title still require their own sources.

Assume 50 MW rooftop equipment, 1,200 equivalent annual hours and a 20-year
life solely to expose the accounting. If accepted, 60 GWh displaces at most
7.2 million CU of a named existing imported-energy input. It provides **zero
firm-MW credit**. Fifteen O&M roles are reassignments within the existing F
wage bill, not free extra labor or demonstrated unchanged other production.
Imported operating inputs cost 0.3 million and annual depreciation is 2.5 million.
Roof rights, grid, safety, storage, interconnection, actual energy demand,
qualified rosters and their complete costs are unknown. These authored numbers
are not engineering estimates or a proposed national procurement.

**IC14 — Keep dates, sources and failure distinct.** There is no output credit
while building or delayed before acceptance. After commissioning, depreciation
is a cost; cash reserved for eventual renewal is neither a current replacement
purchase nor a new lending source. Keep import savings within the original
input scope; do not also reduce its coefficient and promise the same money
as household bill relief or a Treasury dividend.

The selective-tax annual cross-sections have funding gaps of 367.730 million
CU before the project, 383.176 while building, 364.617 with accepted output
and 369.712 with zero output after commissioning. New taxes, service cuts or
free guarantees do not close the residuals. These cross-sections recalculate
demand/profit/finance but do not link every year's opening cash, debt, price,
workforce or loss balances into a financed trajectory.

The accepted example reduces imports from 813.978 to 807.204 million while
raising domestic GVA from 2,731.911 to 2,739.222 million. Thus a higher
required nominal GVA can reflect onshoring rather than greater physical
infeasibility. Retain FD's equal-price accounting; retire its fixed nominal
capacity comparison as a test or ranking of physical supply. Capacity now
requires the actual task, quantities, skills and reliable delivery account.
No nominal benchmark becomes achieved productivity.

At failure, owners recognize impairment, keep maintenance/renewal and legal
claims visible, and use paid warranty, repair, alternate supplier or safe
closure only under actual enforceable contracts and sources. The zero-output
branch deliberately leaves its repair costs and recovery receipts unknown;
it establishes no guaranteed backstop. A delayed project can use cash without
yet depreciating or delivering output. Private/cooperative risk cannot be
transformed into public debt by calling failure a national emergency.
