# Who may become a viable Assembly candidate?

3 October 2026 · Supporting choice for C03/C14 · Research proposal, not enacted election law

## Decision, baseline and why it matters

The bounded question is who controls a **viable position** in AIcountry's proposed compensatory proportional Assembly election. Equal ballots do not give the public a meaningful choice of lawmakers if a small party leadership can alone determine every winning list place, while the route outside a party is too costly or structurally unwinnable. Equally, turning all party nominations into state-supervised primaries could let an incumbent decide which opposition is legitimate. This is a design diagnosis about the existing text, not an observation about a real election.

The inspected predecessor is [integrated design 9](COUNTRY-DESIGN-design-9.md), SHA-256 `d9917fa27af43f0cedf0822a68cc211f0b42286105d0d58ff633c5e3662dd3f0`, and its [status](DESIGN-STATUS-design-9.md), SHA-256 `29f972bf5c44501829da7b56cf012020728fbe9f6e87e2b4c757c1813e819929`. C02 selects an adult citizen and settled-resident franchise with equal candidacy. C03 selects compensatory proportional representation, protected opposition, campaign access and an independent-candidate route; the earlier [democratic candidate](../2026-09-democratic-control/democratic-constitution.md) adds a provisional national party threshold and independent route. Neither names the constituency/list ballot relationship, who ranks compensatory-list candidates, or whether a party can substitute candidates after voting. The [membership review](../2026-10-membership-review/analysis.md) changes who may stand; it does not establish how an eligible newcomer reaches a ballot or a seat. The [continuity correction](../2026-09-democratic-continuity/continuity-candidate.md) warns that formal opposition and multiple nominees can share control. The [working method](../FRAMEWORK.md) and [decision template](../DECISION-TEMPLATE.md) were consulted, not treated as adoption authority.

This is one AI-assisted analysis under the same project controller. No voter, party member, independent candidate, administrator, affected minority or genuinely independent reviewer participated. No party, district, population or current law is identified. The objective is a conditional reference mechanism and a test that could displace it, not a claim that an AI-designed ballot binds anyone.

## Coherent alternatives

| Package | Where the gate sits | Benefit and serious risk |
|---|---|---|
| Retain design 9's unspecified compensatory model and objective candidate registration | Party and election law later determine viable list positions. | Preserves flexibility and imposes no uncosted ballot redesign, but lets future officeholders decide the very gate through which replacements enter. The existing right to stand does not prove access to an electable place. |
| Closed compensatory lists with a legal internal-democracy floor | A party selects and orders its list through published member procedures, with court review for rule breaches; voters choose the party, not its candidates. | Supports coherent party teams and can intentionally place underrepresented members in viable positions. But members can be filtered by party leadership, and direct voter control over individual lawmakers is weak. Heavy state regulation of internal democracy could become an opposition-screening tool. |
| **Selected: voter-influenced compensatory lists and protected nonparty entry** | Parties/nonparty groups choose a published pool; voters determine party seat shares and can separately influence which list candidates fill top-up seats. Named nonparty candidates contest constituency seats. | Preserves broad proportionality while reducing leadership control of list rank and leaving entry outside party endorsement. Preference campaigns can reward wealth, celebrity or local patronage, weaken party cohesion and be harder to count; nomination into the pool remains a gate. This is a hypothesis needing genuine public choice and testing. |
| Candidate-only preferential representation without party compensation | Voters rank individual candidates; no party list gate governs elected names. | Makes personal choice strong and can reduce party-rank dependence, but it is a different overall electoral settlement with altered proportionality, district size and coalition incentives. It cannot be silently grafted onto C03's selected compensatory premise. |

International IDEA distinguishes closed from open lists and mixed-member proportional systems, and stresses that the complete electoral system changes political incentives; it does not certify a universal best ballot. Its [electoral-system database](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/electoral-system-design-database) defines list and compensatory mechanisms. Its [open-list study](https://www.idea.int/sites/default/files/publications/open-list-proportional-representation.pdf) describes voter choice alongside risks of intra-party money competition, ballot complexity and weaker control of representation measures. The [OSCE/ODIHR–Venice Commission party guidelines](https://odihr.osce.org/sites/default/files/f/documents/8/1/538473.pdf) defend party autonomy while noting that list ranking can be controlled by closed networks; they also call for a fair independent-candidate route and equitable access to public support. These are primary comparative analyses and standards, not observations of AIcountry or evidence that the selected combination will work.

## Selected ballot and authority chain

Select a **two-vote compensatory reference** so the candidate mechanism can be evaluated as a whole, with no seat count, district map or threshold invented here. One ballot chooses a named territorial candidate, who may be party-backed or independent. A separate list ballot chooses a party or qualifying nonparty civic list and may include an optional preference for one named candidate on that list. All valid list ballots, including those without a preference, determine proportional Assembly seat entitlement under the preadopted compensatory rule. Territorial winners fill seats credited to their declared list where applicable; the remaining entitlement is filled from its compensatory candidate pool. An independent territorial winner keeps a personal seat under the prior-law allocation rule; their later affiliation cannot retroactively transfer the ballot's list votes. The complete formula must specify overhangs and duplicate candidacy before any first contest; no party office or administrator resolves a disputed seat by discretion.

For compensatory seats, **recorded candidate preferences determine the order among eligible nominees on the same list**. Where candidates have no preference votes, or preference totals tie, a list's previously published fallback order applies. A voter may cast a list-only ballot without reducing its weight for seat entitlement; such a ballot leaves personal ranking to voters who expressed a preference and to the disclosed fallback for unresolved places. This gives voters actual leverage without an arbitrary preference threshold, but a small number of preference ballots could reorder a list when most voters choose list-only. Before adoption, an affected public should compare this rule with a preannounced flexible-list threshold and an all-candidate-preference ballot using the same example votes, accessibility needs and audit effort. If the low-participation case defeats fair representation, the no-threshold version should be changed rather than protected by this research selection. The required ballot must show how a territorial vote, list vote and optional preference affect distinct outcomes; a print and assisted-voting route must permit reconciliation without a single digital provider.

The **Assembly**, through the valid protected electoral-law route, sets the formula, timing, objective eligibility, list registration, campaign support, disclosure, appeals and succession before the affected contest. Incumbent-benefiting changes do not apply to the current officeholders' next election under C02/C03's protection. The **election authority** receives timely party, civic-list and independent filings; checks only legal eligibility, identity/evidence and disclosed process compliance; publishes candidate names, affiliation and default list order; and cannot judge policy or ideological fitness. A **party or civic list** sets its platform, membership and nomination method under its published charter; it cannot after the legal cutoff replace or reorder names for political convenience. A death, voluntary withdrawal or proven ineligibility uses an exact prior-law vacancy process with notice and challenge, not a private relisting. No group is compelled to endorse a person it rejects on lawful and fair grounds.

The minimum public boundary around party autonomy is narrow: freedom to organize and criticize, nondiscriminatory individual eligibility, accessible publication of nomination rules and deadlines, truthful account of who controls the final pool, consistency with the group's own announced rules, and an internal complaint route that does not consume the entire election period. Member nomination contests must disclose substantial organized funding and coordinated spending under the same lawful privacy and anti-retaliation safeguards as public campaigns; otherwise the campaign-money gate simply moves inside the party. The election authority does not run a party's internal vote or appoint nominees. A **competent independent court** can urgently review a rejected filing or serious process violation, order preservation of evidence and restore a still-useful opportunity to compete. It should not select a party's preferred ideology or infer a candidate's victory from an administrative deadline. Court, filing and correction capacity need the protected funds and records route already proposed in C03.

A newcomer has two usable pathways to test, not an ornamental exception: seek a place on a party or civic list under published rules and voter preference, or run as a named independent in the territorial contest. Voters may also form a nonparty civic list under objective, proportionate registration rules; without this group route, proportional seats remain monopolized by registered parties. Petition/signature and deposit rules must be capable of being met without a patron and independently appealable, while preventing fraudulent or duplicate entries. Neutral public information, campaign support and usable media access must reach credible newcomers and independent candidates as well as incumbents, under a law that accounts for actual support without making prior office a prerequisite. Significant control of ads, funding and distribution remains disclosed with protection for vulnerable small contributors. Employers, platforms or public contractors must not use their gateways to suppress a lawful nomination or candidate response.

Once lawful results establish the Assembly, its members occupy public office by the election result, not by continuing party favor. Expulsion from a party does not by itself vacate an elected seat; vacancy and succession follow the prior published law. A compensatory-list vacancy uses the remaining lawful candidate order fixed from that election, not a newly selected loyalist; a territorial independent's vacancy requires its own lawful replacement route. This choice must be reconciled with the constructive-confidence process, because the governing coalition may change when members leave a party. A losing party leader or executive has no power to withhold certification, records, treasury access or command transfer. The existing C03/C14 [contested-transfer circuit](../2026-10-operating-settlement/democratic-institutions.md) remains required, including count evidence and executable court orders; candidate-list rules alone cannot defeat common capture.

## Limits, migration and next bounded test

The selected mechanism changes two things in the main design if integrated: **C03 should name the list vote, candidate preference and viable nonparty routes**, and **C14 should require a pre-election entry-and-succession exercise** alongside its contested-transfer exercise. It affects C02's equal candidacy for citizens and settled residents, election and party law, campaign finance, public media, printing/accessibility, court jurisdiction, compensation formula, list vacancies, and the budget for administration and counsel. The older citizen-only candidate D1 and D2's unspecified compensatory/independent rules remain predecessor proposals; this note cannot quietly transfer their reviews to the new electorate or ballot. Existing legal rights, party associations, pending cases and public obligations continue until lawfully revised. A transition must publish exact old-to-new party and independent eligibility, protect accrued terms, fund parallel voter education and ballot testing, and set a date before which no incumbent may change the next contest's rules.

Major objections stay open. Preference ballots may intensify intraparty rivalry and expense; nonparty lists can be sponsor fronts; the ballot can confuse people or exclude voters with limited time, language or access; direct preference can weaken party measures for women, minorities and persons with disabilities; a single territorial independent cannot automatically solve a national list gate. Conversely, closed lists with genuine internal member control may better support coherent programs and representation in some setting. The state cannot respond to these risks by examining political belief, banning dissident parties or requiring every association to adopt one internal governance model. The selected package should be rejected if its independent path is only nominal, if count reconciliation is unreliable, if party competition becomes unfinanceable for ordinary candidates, or if a different democratic model demonstrably preserves effective replacement with less control over association.

**Evidence:** current text and cited institutional analyses were checked; there is no original local electoral observation. **Research selection:** the two-vote, voter-influenced compensatory package is conditionally preferred for the reference design; closed lists and candidate-only representation remain live competitors. **Legal authorization:** none; applicable constitutional and research-publication boundaries remain. **Operational readiness:** unknown; no electoral commission, court, party system, list roster, print ballot, funded public access, district map or tested count exists. **Observed outcomes:** none; multiple AI drafts are not independent public approval.

The next bounded question for a lawful adopting community is whether ordinary voters and challengers can understand and use the two votes and optional preference, and whether a newcomer can reach a realistic contest without a party patron or wealthy sponsor. An independently supervised test would compare the selected ballot with a closed-list/internal-democracy ballot and a flexible-list threshold under the *same* mock votes, accessibility conditions, budget and adversarial nomination cases; parties, independent aspirants, underrepresented groups, election staff and voters must participate. Completion means publishing ballot errors, candidate/seat outcomes, costs, objections, reviewer dependencies and the correction available before an actual election. This research has no budget or public mandate to conduct that test; failures or a better-performing rival would reopen the conditional choice.
