## The diagnosis that changed the design

Design 5 could assign a valid health appointment, school place, care visit,
housing offer and cash payment without anyone having to show that their times and
locations work together. It could finance housing, water, energy and food through
separate accounts while no authorized elected body settled a shared land or
catchment conflict. It could declare a civil right without a usable intake,
funded help, interim relief and an executor of judgment. It also depended on
external currency and imported essentials without fully joining foreign
commitments, displaced harms and reception duties to democratic control. These
are textual/system-design gaps, not measured failures in a real country.

The distinct causal explanations remain important. Some non-use comes from
conflicting schedules; some from absent workers or cash. Some housing scarcity
comes from physical limits; some from exclusionary rules or ownership. Some legal
failure comes from missing rules; some from cost, delay or enforcement capture.
Better coordination does not create resources, and a new tribunal does not make a
bad law just. The selected changes expose these differences before declaring a
claim fulfilled.

## Coherent selections, rivals and incompatibilities

| Question | Selected reference arrangement | Rival retained and main objection |
|---|---|---|
| Usable social provision | National effective-access duty under enacted service standards; local place-based plans; voluntary cross-service convenor; separate sector decisions and rights. | Separate sectors plus a navigator are cheaper but may only route people among unusable offers. A unified national welfare agency could coordinate command but centralizes eligibility, data and provider control. The selected duty needs real staff and must not compel family care or a common dossier. |
| Shared land and ecology | Assembly sets rights, physical and ecological duties and competence; elected local councils choose local land use; scoped functional-area plans compare shared alternatives; unresolved distribution returns to an elected body with reasons and review. | Isolated permits retain flexibility but can double-promise water or infrastructure. National expert allocation can compare the whole map but gives technical officials an unearned distributive mandate. Joint planning can itself delay housing or be captured. |
| Ordinary law | National civil/procedure floor with express local supplementation; a claim travels from accessible intake through legal help, voluntary resolution or court, interim relief, appeal and verified execution. | Retaining separate manuscripts avoids disruption but leaves forum and execution gaps. A uniform central code may be simpler across boundaries but can erase local self-government and make one captured center decisive. Legal help and enforcement need funding. |
| External relations | Accountable interdependence: Assembly mandate for material commitments, mapped supply and external harms, lawful protection intake and reception, with cabinet negotiation and ordinary cooperation under prior law. | Autarky can reduce one dependence but may sacrifice essential goods, land and labor. Unrestricted executive openness can provide fast trade yet conceal liabilities and freeze democratic choices. Partners cannot be bound by this proposal. |

These choices are compatible only under a **single account and authority map**.
Connected-service plans need the spatial plan's sites, transport, water and grid
capacity. Courts can protect an individual's access or property right but cannot
choose general housing density or create a nurse; elected institutions make and
fund that choice under rights review. Cross-boundary planning may not use a
technical clock to defeat local voting, and local refusal may not erase national
rights. Foreign contracts cannot secretly reserve the same cash or material for
another use. The outside claims process cannot imply universal jurisdiction or
give a nonresident a national ballot. Where those duties compete, the Assembly
must disclose and decide the lawful tradeoff instead of letting each sector count
the same worker, site or payment.

## Support, objections and migration

The supporting analyses cite WHO, ILO, UN disability and human-rights instruments,
UN-Habitat, IPCC, FAO, UNECE, OECD and UN treaty bodies as **comparison sources**.
They establish relevant problems, rights concepts and methods; none observes this
country or endorses the combined package. Several are institutional guidance and
share underlying data families. The authored household, regional water and legal
claim examples test logical dependencies, not field resilience. There was no
independent legal, engineering, clinical, financial or affected-party review.

Material objections remain. Effective-access review may become paternalistic case
management; people may refuse coordination and keep separate entitlements.
Provider payment can reward easy cases; contracts must count non-users and
high-need people without reducing care to a single metric. Planning can become
a veto against needed homes; affected parties must contest alternatives early,
while a competent elected body must issue a reasoned decision. Land acquisition
can preserve historic wealth or erase legitimate reliance; title, tenancy,
customary claims, compensation and reparative questions need actual lawful
adjudication. A national civil core may displace useful local law; express grants
and a published conflict rule limit it but cannot prove the balance is best.
Outside-harm review can burden domestic workers or give little relief abroad;
it needs an identifiable decision, control, legal standing and resources.

Design 6 changes C04, C06–C10, C13 and C14. Affected consumers include the
older civil, administrative, family, housing, food/water, energy, infrastructure,
procurement, budget, migration, treaty and security manuscripts; the A1–A9
authority candidate; elected local governments; service providers; courts and
treasury; households and firms. No old approval transfers. A real migration needs
an article-level hierarchy and conflict map, safe records, staff and physical
capacity, financing for legal aid and care coordination, continuity of pending
claims and permits, independent appeals and actual receipt of remedies. The
three-year public budget has no measured line for these additions. Costs and
liabilities must be reconciled rather than assumed away.

## Qualification and next bounded design work

**Evidence:** local textual review and official conceptual sources only; no
AIcountry service, land, case or external-account observations. **Research
selection:** the four arrangements above enter the current reference design.
**Legal authorization:** none. **Operational readiness:** unknown, including
workforce, sites, water, courts, international cooperation and cash. **Observed
outcomes:** none. Successful link and wording checks establish artifact integrity,
not better lives.

The next *design-level* priority is to choose the still-thin operating models for
health/education/care institutions, digital and AI public power, and prevention,
criminal law and rehabilitation, then reconcile them with this shared budget and
authority chain. That is a bounded architectural task; adding unrelated case
studies is lower value. Even after a reference choice, an actual adopting public
must supply jurisdiction, participation, original observations and lawful review
before any readiness or superiority claim. Reopen this selection if a simpler
arrangement supplies usable access and remedies with less domination, if linked
plans obstruct essential construction, or if affected people reject the values or
authority allocation.
