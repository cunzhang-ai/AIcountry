## Values, diagnosis and coherent alternatives

Equal practical political standing, dissent, bodily safety, privacy and others'
usable public access are commitments. Actual scarcity, finite judges/police,
property and unknown future media are constraints. Proposed protections might
reduce discretionary gatekeeping; effectiveness, burden and safety are not
observed. Exclusion may instead chiefly arise from private ownership/funding,
public-space shortage, intimidation, inaccessible courts or captured staff.
A new statute alone cannot solve those structural sources.

| Whole alternative | Benefit/mechanism | Real burden and possible defeat |
|---|---|---|
| Retain constitutional/LF/RX safeguards and repair current practice | Less new process; actual trained officers/courts may protect dissent | Underlying event/force source and notice/facilitation remain unspecified in this candidate; no free faithful interpretation |
| Prior permission | Advance conditions can coordinate scarce roads/medical protection | Incumbent veto, delay, fees, selective application and exclusion can make opposition impractical |
| Compulsory minimal notice with genuine spontaneous exceptions | Earlier information, still formally a right | Can function as permission, identify vulnerable groups, punish leaderless action or reward well-connected organizers |
| Administrative material restrictions with actual immediate judicial challenge | Faster ordinary logistics and fewer initial court applications | State can destroy a short expressive opportunity before a challenger reaches court; formal appeal is no actual restoration |
| Selected free assembly/voluntary notice and prior-court material planned restriction | Ordinary gathering needs no approval; state carries burden when it seeks a material nonconsensual assembly-specific restriction | Actual judges/help/rotas cost capacity, courts can be captured, voluntary coordination can miss risk; no empirical superiority |

Select the last option provisionally, preserving the real rivals rather than
absorbing their advantages. CS16's simpler-route comparison matters: no separate
assembly board, licence, universal notice database or judge for routine gatherings.
A prior ordinary court is an additional step only for the state's attempted
material nonconsensual planned restriction, justified by irreversible opposition
loss and incumbent timing power. It is a choice with costs, not more-formal-is-better.
