# Who decides when territories share a resource?

3 October 2026 · Bounded national-design analysis for C04/C09 · Research recommendation, not enacted law

## Decision and baseline

The [country design](../COUNTRY-DESIGN.md) selects nested democratic planning but
leaves a consequential phrase unresolved: if local councils disagree, a “lawfully
competent elected body” decides a shared land, water, housing or infrastructure
choice. **For the reference design, make the elected national Assembly the default
final public authority for a material interlocal conflict that existing law and
voluntary agreement cannot settle.** Local councils retain ordinary land-use and
service powers; a time-limited joint planning body can prepare alternatives and
execute a valid agreement, but cannot impose a new distributive bargain on
nonconsenting people or localities. The Assembly decides only the residual common
choice by an explicit law or plan-approval statute with funding, reasons, review
and named delivery duties. A directly elected regional authority remains a
possible later replacement for a demonstrated durable shared area, subject to a
separate legitimate creation process. This recommendation makes the existing
design's decision-maker identifiable without inventing a regional map or electorate.

This is a research choice, not a claim that every project should wait for a
parliamentary vote. Ordinary permits and technical standards operate under
preadopted law. The Assembly's reserved role is triggered by a **material,
unresolved distributional choice** among local governments or against common
rights and resource duties—not by any minor cross-border effect or a party's
strategic objection. The distinction itself needs enacted thresholds and an
independent challenge route in a real jurisdiction. The [authority candidate](../AUTHORITY-AND-TRANSITION.md)
A2 already gives the Assembly general lawmaking and local councils express
competence; it has no regional legislature. The earlier [land/ecology review](../2026-10-land-ecology-review/analysis.md)
established the cross-sector conflict but did not settle who has an electorate,
tax authority and final vote. Existing water, housing, energy and infrastructure
clauses in the [September manuscript snapshot](../2026-09-readiness/source-documents.json)
are sector proposals with legacy AI authority, not a territorial settlement.

## Four coherent models and the selected combination

| Model | Legitimate decision route | Benefit | Material failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retain local councils and sector permits** | Each elected council decides within its area; water, energy and other agencies apply their own law. | Proximity and limited institutional change. | Affected people across a catchment or housing market never choose one common allocation. The first permit can consume scarce capacity; local veto and central agency permits can each hide the other. |
| **Direct national decision for all shared projects** | National Assembly votes each material siting and allocation. | One existing electorate and explicit national responsibility. | It may be too remote and overloaded, turn technical detail into party bargaining, and subordinate local knowledge even when no national choice is necessary. |
| **Standing directly elected regional authority** | An electorate within a stable statutory region chooses a regional council with defined planning, funding and oversight powers. | People sharing repeated obligations can replace their own decision-maker. | A catchment, labor market and grid do not share one boundary. Defining the electorate can exclude downstream or neighboring affected people; a permanent tier costs money and may compete with local/national rights and budgets. No real AIcountry territory establishes the case for it. |
| **Temporary functional compact** | Participating elected local councils delegate a bounded shared task; each approves contributions and may replace delegates under law. | Flexible boundaries, local knowledge and less permanent bureaucracy. | Council unanimity can give an incumbent or resource owner a de facto veto; delegates may not represent affected nonmembers. It cannot levy taxes or bind another locality merely by agreement. |

**Selected conditional combination:** use a temporary, statutory interlocal forum
for evidence, alternatives, consultation and voluntary commitments; leave final
authority with each elected council for its own lawful competence; reserve the
unresolved common decision to the Assembly. The Assembly must legislate scope and
funding, not merely accept a technical forum's “optimal” map. This keeps the
compact's informational advantage without granting its non-universal delegates
sovereign authority, and uses the national electorate already posited in C02
without declaring that it perfectly represents every locally harmed person.
Where a real, durable functional area later warrants direct regional election,
the applicable constitutional or statutory process can transfer *specified*
competences prospectively with the affected electorate, finance and remedies.
No central office, compact or software system can create that regional authority
by changing a map or claiming coordination necessity.

## How affected scope and authority would be established

1. **An enacted jurisdiction rule precedes the dispute.** The Assembly defines
   types of shared effects, who can request an interlocal review, what records
   must be produced, the ordinary legal competence of each council and sector
   authority, and which residual choices require an Assembly decision. Affected
   residents, councils, customary or collective claimants and organizations
   with a lawful interest can challenge a boundary that omits a material impact.
   Independent review settles *competence and process*; it cannot secretly pick
   the land or water allocation. A technical body supplies measurements and
   uncertainty, not the political boundary by its own decree.
2. **The forum represents effects, not only registered owners.** Elected councils
   in the mapped shared area nominate accountable delegates and can replace them;
   meetings, evidence and dissent are public subject to protected personal data.
   Tenants, workers, farmers, children through appropriate representation, people
   with disabilities, historically displaced groups and neighboring communities
   receive early access to alternatives and a way to contest the map. Title,
   contract or political membership is not the sole test of being affected. A
   person outside the eventual voting jurisdiction may have participation,
   environmental and legal standing without being invented as a member of a
   different national electorate. Cross-border impacts invoke actual treaty and
   neighbor duties; this research cannot assign another polity's vote.
3. **The elected decision is bounded and reasoned.** A compact within existing
   lawful powers needs each participating council's public assent, specified
   contributions, term and exit/continuity rules. If it cannot agree on a
   material choice, its competing plans and objections go to an Assembly docket
   that the executive cannot suppress. The Assembly hears affected councils and
   people, publishes alternatives and cumulative effects, and votes the precise
   shared allocation, conditions, payer, implementing authorities, expiry/review
   and remedies. An ordinary majority cannot waive protected rights; a change
   to the constitutional distribution of powers uses the valid protected route.
   No project is approved by a missed deadline, and a regulator, court or AI
   cannot make the Assembly's value choice.
4. **Regional election is a real alternative, not a hidden assumption.** If
   repeated shared decisions justify a regional council, its establishing law
   must first state the territory, why that boundary captures the material
   effects, the local-resident franchise and equal vote, election administration,
   opposition rights, powers, financing, term, judicial review and interaction
   with local councils. Affected people contest inclusion and exclusion before
   the first election; the incumbent cannot redraw the boundary for the next
   contest. Enfranchised local residents under the chosen C02 settlement would
   vote in the region, while other affected people retain rights and standing.
   The future public may reject a permanent tier if geography changes quickly.

## Obligations during overlap, refusal and physical scarcity

**No competence vacuum.** Existing local service, housing, water, environmental,
property and permit duties remain with their current lawful authorities until
an enacted decision names successors. National essential guarantees and funding
equalization remain national responsibilities. A compact's creation does not
transfer an unfunded hospital, housing or water claim into an ambiguous joint
body. Every shared plan must have a duty matrix: the legal rule, asset or permit,
owner/operator, funding authority, workforce, deadline, affected locality,
reviewer and executor. The consolidated budget counts the same water, land,
network connection, worker and cash once. National compensation/equalization
addresses a locality that bears a national benefit's cost; beneficial localities
cannot free-ride by leaving maintenance and displacement to another. A compact
cannot tax; new levies and transfers need the Assembly and, where delegated,
local democratic approval.

**No veto through inaction.** A filed, publicly registered dispute starts the
review timetable; a cabinet cannot delay the trigger by withholding recognition.
Failure to agree or vote leaves existing lawful plans and permits in force, but
does not authorize a *new irreversible* allocation or expansion merely because
the clock expired. The Assembly must disclose the unresolved claims and hold a
reasoned hearing on a publicly available proposal, including opposition and
local alternatives; no rule can force it to enact one option. If housing or
life-sustaining water obligations are already unmet, the responsible national
and local authorities must use lawful interim delivery, protection and shortfall
procedures funded under existing authority. A court may enforce an existing duty,
stop an unlawful irreversible act and require an accountable response, but cannot
create a water source, tax or final territorial plan. Deliberate refusal and
physical impossibility are separately recorded. Repeated paralysis is evidence
for reconsidering the territorial model, not implied consent to a project.

**No private or technocratic veto.** Landowners and permit holders receive
property, notice, fair process and lawful compensation, not an absolute right
to block all public need. Historic titles, customary or collective claims,
tenancy and prior displacement are investigated rather than erased by cadastral
convenience. Housing need must be tested against safe, serviced and reachable
sites, not a raw parcel count. Water use must respect measured ecological flows
and quality as well as life-sustaining access; science constrains what is
physically available but does not allocate remaining use by itself. Network
operators, major owners and public planners disclose control and conflicts;
independent evidence access and appeal prevent a proprietary model or permit
database from deciding the boundary. Displacement requires lawful purpose,
necessity, practical rehousing, other livelihood supports and remedies under C09.

**Continuity and migration.** A later Assembly allocation or elected region
does not retroactively erase vested claims, pending hearings, contract duties,
restoration security or judgments. The transition instrument maps each to an
identified successor and funding source. Existing permits continue unless
lawfully modified for safety or ecological reasons with reasons, reliance
assessment and challenge. Staffing, archives, local assets and debts cannot be
assigned to a new regional body without lawful transfer and financing. An
interlocal compact that expires must return data, ongoing maintenance and
claims to named authorities; exit cannot make a service disappear. Construction,
renewal and decommissioning costs are costed together, including workforce and
timing. No scenario numbers can substitute for an actual catchment, grid, title
register or public budget.

## Evidence, objections and the bounded next decision

[The European Charter of Local Self-Government](https://rm.coe.int/european-charter-of-local-self-government-/1680b0fbec)
is an official comparator for legally defined local powers, consultation and
adequate financial resources; it does not bind or found AIcountry. [UN-Habitat's
territorial planning guidelines](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/download-manager-files/IG-UTP_English.pdf)
join land use, infrastructure, environmental conditions and implementation
finance. Its [comparative legal study](https://unhabitat.org/comparative-legal-research-on-spatial-planning-hierarchies-at-various-administrative-levels)
identifies the relevance of explicit mandates and horizontal/vertical coordination
across existing countries; it does not prove this hybrid best. The [Aarhus
Convention text](https://unece.org/environment-policy/public-participation/aarhus-convention/text)
is a primary example of information, participation and justice in environmental
decisions, not an AIcountry treaty commitment. These sources were inspected on
3 October 2026. They support examination of the problem, not the invented
authority chain or any claimed outcomes.

The strongest objection is that national Assembly reservation can turn every
regional problem into central party politics, while the people most affected
have only a diluted national vote. The limited trigger, local preparation,
opposition and affected-party access, published local dissent and rights review
mitigate that problem but do not remove it. A directly elected regional council
could be superior where shared geography and obligations persist; that choice
cannot be honest without geography, an electorate, resources and a legitimate
creation route. Conversely, a compact may be faster, but unanimity can let a
wealthy locality or infrastructure owner block others. The selected route does
not equate speed or more government tiers with better lives. It should be
rejected if an actual jurisdiction demonstrates a more accountable and workable
arrangement under its affected people's judgment.

**Precise main-design changes if integrated:** (1) In C04, replace “an elected
body with that competence” with the default Assembly-reserved final decision
for material unresolved shared allocations, preceded by a lawfully scoped
interlocal forum and affected-public participation; direct regional election
requires a separate valid establishing act. (2) In C09, state that pending
deadlock preserves existing duties and blocks automatic new irreversible
authorization, with named interim service/funding responsibilities and no
technical or owner veto. Update the status register's territorial-authority
row and legal migration dependency accordingly. Do not treat this supporting
note as itself changing C04/C09 or the authority candidate.
