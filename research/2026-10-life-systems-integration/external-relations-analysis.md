# External interdependence is part of the national settlement

2 October 2026 · Assistant-authored supporting analysis · No enacted foreign policy

## Gap and competing diagnoses

In [design 5](COUNTRY-DESIGN-design-5.md), C06 selects a conditional external-
currency operating package and C05/C07 require critical imported inputs, while C10
provides only broad peace, treaty, migration and refugee principles. The country
could therefore promise domestic guarantees whose payments, workers or materials
depend on outsiders, then treat the consequences abroad as a footnote. Conversely,
foreign agreements could lock in borrowing, digital infrastructure or resource
extraction without the same democratic scrutiny as domestic law. This is a textual
gap, not an assertion about a real territory or partner.

Three diagnoses suggest different choices. An **external supply** diagnosis asks
whether critical goods, payments and routes will exist during shocks. A **democratic
authority** diagnosis asks whether a cabinet or lender can commit future budgets
without effective public control. A **reciprocity** diagnosis asks whether domestic
well-being is purchased by shifting harm to foreign workers, displaced people or
ecosystems. A single import-reserve number cannot answer all three. Values include
peace, life, self-government, fair exchange, refuge and responsibility to outsiders;
the tradeoffs depend on actual resources, geography and legal relationships.

| Arrangement | Attraction | Major failure |
|---|---|---|
| Retain thin foreign-policy principles | Avoid invented treaty partners and external budgets. | Guarantees and trade/finance duties can be decided in separate silos with no shared account or remedy. |
| Strategic self-sufficiency | Reduce exposure to withheld imports or payment access. | A small or resource-limited polity may lack essential inputs; compulsory local production can waste land, energy and workers or damage rights. |
| Unrestricted openness managed by cabinet | Rapid negotiation and potentially cheaper imports. | Private contracts, debt and executive side deals can bind public services or shift rights costs without public authorization. |
| **Selected: accountable interdependence** | Treat foreign supply, treaties, migration and external harm as one democratic and physical account, with diversified capacity and lawful cooperation. | Needs negotiation capacity, transparent records, reserves where justified, and partners willing to cooperate; no unilateral design can guarantee recognition or supply. |

This selection is a reference planning architecture, not an adopted trade regime.
It does not mean all agreements need a referendum or that every foreign affected
person receives a national vote. It does mean a cabinet cannot hide public debt,
essential-service dependence or coercive border action inside a commercial contract.

## Proposed national correction

The Assembly should authorize material external commitments before they bind public
taxes, guarantees, security, rights, critical infrastructure or enduring resource
use. Cabinet negotiates within a published mandate with protected disclosure for
genuine security and commercial needs. An adopted law identifies implementation,
fiscal exposure, non-derogable protections, affected communities, review and lawful
exit or continuity. The public account includes public entities and contingent
guarantees, including external debt and investor commitments; a secret side letter
cannot create new domestic taxing or coercive authority. Ordinary low-impact
administrative cooperation can proceed under prior statute and audit rather than
Assembly micro-approval of every transaction.

Foreign supply policy should compare viable domestic capacity, diversified
partners, repair/substitution and justified reserves for critical medicines, food,
energy components and payment systems. That is a risk and resource decision, not
a mandate for autarky. Import contracts and export strategy must be assessed with
the external-currency cash path and domestic workforce/land/ecological account.
The physical and human-rights costs borne abroad should be disclosed and addressed
through lawful procurement, due diligence, cooperation and remedies where the
country controls the relevant actor or transaction. A domestic “green” balance
cannot count a displaced mine or unsafe care-worker recruitment as a free input.

For displacement and migration, an individual claim to protection needs safe intake,
competent assessment, translation, independent appeal and reception capacity.
Border contracts or third-country processing cannot be used to make a prohibited
return appear to be someone else's decision. Long-term habitual residence counts
for the working ordinary franchise as in C02, while basic rights apply from the
start. Local authorities need funded reception responsibilities; diplomatic
cooperation does not erase their or the national government's duties. No number
of future arrivals, treaty membership or external partner is assumed.

Outside communities harmed by state-funded projects or cross-border pollution
should have notice, evidence access and a competent challenge or diplomatic route
proportionate to the effect, with safe testimony and protection of existing
jurisdictional rights. They do not automatically obtain a veto over domestic
elections. The originating authority remains responsible for lawful response and
repair even when a contractor or foreign affiliate operates the project.

## Sources, objections and limits

The [UN Charter](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text) supplies
peaceful-settlement, cooperation and sovereign-equality principles. [UNHCR's account
of the Refugee Convention](https://www.unhcr.org/about-unhcr/overview/1951-refugee-convention)
describes non-refoulement. The [UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights](https://www.ohchr.org/documents/publications/guidingprinciplesbusinesshr_en.pdf)
provide a comparison for state duties and business impacts; the [UN International
Law Commission's work on transboundary harm](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/9_7.shtml)
shows legal categories for prevention. Their precise binding force depends on
the actual jurisdiction and treaties. They are primary legal and institutional
sources, not a legal opinion or proof of the AIcountry package.

The strongest objection is that external impact review can become a broad,
unfunded veto that harms domestic workers while leaving foreign victims without
real remedies. The candidate therefore ties review to identifiable decisions,
control and legal routes, and requires reasoned tradeoffs and budgets. A second
objection is that transparent treaty mandates can weaken negotiation; limited
confidentiality may be justified, but hidden authority and fiscal liabilities
must remain contestable. A third is that critical reserves can divert scarce
resources from present care; they must compete in the same budget. No foreign
state, trading partner, migrant, worker or affected community reviewed this work.

The proposal changes C06, C09, C10 and C14; it depends on the monetary, ecological,
membership and security choices. Existing treaties, debts, contracts, claims and
protection duties continue unless lawfully changed. No legislature, budget,
supplier, reception service, neighboring government or legal jurisdiction exists
here. The next bounded question, once an affected public identifies an actual
jurisdiction, is to map its existing external obligations and three indispensable
cross-border dependencies, including who can interrupt them and who bears the
harm. A result showing secure supply and adequate accountability under a simpler
arrangement could justify retaining it. A credible alternative treaty or monetary
model, affected-party objection or observed external harm reopens this selection.
