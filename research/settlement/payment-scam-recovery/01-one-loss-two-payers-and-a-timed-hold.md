# One loss, two providers and a timed hold

A consumer approves a payment of 100 units after a verified deception.
The amount is within the hypothetical statutory incident ceiling. At
protected receipt, 65 units have left the initial receiving account.
The account contains 35 attributable available units and 25 unrelated
units with a separate lawful basis. An authorized officer confirms the
grounds, imminent onward risk, lawful amount and deadline. A scoped
hold can preserve 35 units. The other 25 remain accessible. No finding
of wrongdoing by the account holder is assumed.

| Scenario | Consumer cash restored by reimbursement and actual return | Ultimate provider net cost, sending / receiving | Scope |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 35 lawfully returned before reimbursement | 100 | 32.5 / 32.5 | Sending provider pays remaining 65 and collects the other's share separately |
| Sending provider pays 100 before 35 is lawfully recovered | 100 | 32.5 / 32.5 | Gross provider payments and recovery are recorded separately |
| 35 temporarily held but no timely lawful extension or return | 100 | 50 / 50 | Release the hold at expiry. Held money supplies zero recovery |
| No identifiable funds remain, or the onward foreign recovery fails | 100 | 50 / 50 | Eligible initial domestic loss is paid. The domestic order has no assumed foreign effect |
| Other provider has paid nothing when the first payment is due | 100 | Sending cash outflow 100, with 50 due from the other provider in the no-recovery case | The consumer does not wait for the contribution |

A targeted temporary restriction starting at hour 0 expires at hour
4 without a prior lawful extension. Moving the case at hour 3 to a
new officer does not grant four more hours. A qualified judge can
decide a distinct preservation request within adopted authority.
Only proof of lawful title, a valid disposition and actual settlement
can turn the 35 into a returned amount. Without an extension, the
institution must make held funds usable again. Reporting that an order
was issued is not settlement or release.

If the illustrative ceiling were 80 instead of 100, the same 35 recovered
after an 80-unit reimbursement would first pay the consumer's uncovered
20. The remaining 15 would offset provider outlays. If nothing were
recovered, the consumer would still have an uncovered 20, with any
separate civil claim preserved. The statutory cap is a real distributional
choice and cannot be described as full recovery in every case.

In an innocent-holder countercase, suppose a source error made the 35
appear linked. Correct it and remove the restriction as soon as the
error is established. The 25 separate funds were never eligible for
the hold. If the temporary restriction caused two units of proved
direct fees or interest, the responsible provider pays those charges
under the candidate rule. That cost is separate from the claimant's
reimbursement, and there is no debit for someone else's deception.
Original evidence, payer and holder remedies remain separate.

This fixture assumes that funds, responsible people and independent
routes actually exist where named. Insolvency or outage can defeat a
reimbursement route. A statutory claim would survive, with no new
Treasury or deposit-insurance coverage inferred.
