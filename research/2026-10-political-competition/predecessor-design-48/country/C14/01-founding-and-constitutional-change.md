## Lawful founding and legal hierarchy

For a real country, establishment would require a legitimate territorial and membership
settlement, participatory constitutional ratification, an audited opening balance sheet,
lawful transition of obligations and functioning service/remedy institutions. Existing
inhabitants, creditors, workers and neighboring communities do not disappear because a
new design is published. A gradual lawful adoption by existing communities is also a
possible use; a wholly new sovereign state is not the only path to learning from this work.

The selected [legal hierarchy](../../2026-10-legal-hierarchy/decision-record.md)
protects the country's democratic and rights functions without freezing every
untested policy. A legitimately founded constitution fixes the electorate's
protected boundary, equal vote and viable opposition, lawful replacement,
rights and executable review, elected local government's existence, the
basic essential-access and ecological duties, and limits on emergency and AI
power. The elected Assembly specifies ballot mechanics, service entitlements,
provider and funding duties, tax and monetary rules, public evidence, ordinary
justice and local/shared competences through amendable framework statutes.
Annual budgets, local enactments, regulations and contracts act only within
those laws. A research edition or official statistic has no lawmaking rank.
The [delegated-rule choice](../../2026-10-delegated-rulemaking/decision-record.md)
adds an effect-sensitive chain: statute defines authority and scrutiny,
material instruments need an affirmative Assembly decision, narrower routine
rules receive published notice and a callable elected review route, and
urgent instruments have a prior-law trigger and expiry. General legal effect
cannot be introduced through guidance, dynamic external standards or a model
update. These are reference rules for a future lawful adopter, not current
permissions for this project's software or any existing state.
The [proposed legal provisions A1–A15](../../AUTHORITY-AND-TRANSITION.md) express
the division for future legitimate review; none is current law.
The first Assembly cannot enact the election law under which it was chosen;
its exact initial ballot, count, dispute and handover rules need a legitimate
founding or predecessor authorization before polling. Later Assemblies may
revise future elections within the protected competition and timing limits.
The [first government-formation rule](../C03/06-government-formation-and-caretaker.md)
also needs prior lawful adoption: nomination, investiture, dismissal, a repeat
poll and caretaker powers cannot be chosen by the cabinet whose tenure they
control.
For the C03 reference this includes N, D, a reviewable district map, nominee
pool and list-cap disclosure, the no-threshold quotient count, original-evidence
recounts, vacancy and repeat-poll rules. An uncovered list-backed territorial
plurality, tied count, exhausted pool or independent by-election must be
explained and tested before the first ballot; an election authority cannot
complete those choices after seeing votes.
The [national-form choice](05-scale-and-national-form.md) compares the
Assembly/local arrangement with elected regions and issue-scoped joint
services using actual affected people, geography, legislative capacity and
resource obligations. The C03 count is valid only if the prior lawful
founding choice establishes its full electoral parameters and usable
democratic control; a failed reference does not authorize an improvised map.
The [founding sequence](../../FOUNDING-AND-STRESS.md) separates that first lawful
contest from later framework statutes and service commencement.

## Public constitutional change

After legitimate founding, ordinary constitutional amendments may be proposed
by one third of the full Assembly or by an elector petition. A 1% petition
opens an accessible public drafting/hearing route; a final exact text signed
by 5% of the C02 electorate reaches a referendum without an Assembly veto.
The Assembly route needs two thirds of its full membership and a majority of
valid yes/no referendum votes. The direct petition route, which lacks the
Assembly supermajority, needs three fifths of valid yes/no votes; this guards
against an incumbent using a sponsored petition to bypass the Assembly
threshold, but may also obstruct a deserved reform. Both routes need at
least 90 days of public consideration of the final text, a clear ballot and
balanced, independently challengeable information. The reference puts the
ballot within 180 days of lawful certification, subject to a narrow
court-reviewed postponement. The Assembly has 60 days to publish its view
of a citizen text. There is no turnout quorum in this reference. These
numbers are provisional hypotheses, not enacted thresholds or proof that
ordinary people can meet them.
The charter itself must protect the petition thresholds, at least a one-year
collection period, objective verification and a court remedy; later election
law supplies accessible methods but cannot make a live initiative impossible.
The Assembly may publish an alternative, not replace the petitioners' exact
ballot text or use a simultaneous conflicting vote to cancel it.

The exact amendment states its effects on rights, offices, public resources,
existing claims and commencement. A court reviews procedure and whether the
change would defeat equal political standing, practical opposition, periodic
competition, executable remedies or peaceful replacement; it publishes
reasons and cannot treat policy disagreement as constitutional invalidity.
Changes to elections, tenure, appointments, review, emergency power or the
amendment process cannot govern current officeholders or the contest choosing
their immediate successors. The old procedure judges its own replacement;
a cabinet cannot call a self-authorizing referendum. A later system may alter
institutional forms while preserving those democratic functions. A genuine
constituent replacement is a separate affected-public and legal question,
not a research edition's or incumbent's automatic power. Petition,
referendum, review and handover need protected staff, cash, original records
and usable opposition channels; a paper right to amend is not public control.

Constitutional protection of essential access is a duty to establish, fund,
deliver and review a basic floor, with equal treatment and feasible urgent
help, not an assertion that a charter creates absent homes, clinicians or
foreign currency. Statute must name concrete coverage, standards, payer,
provider, interim route and remedy. A court can correct unlawful exclusion,
preserve accrued claims, order available lawful protection and expose an
unfunded duty; it cannot set a new tax or choose the nation's preferred
allocation. Elected institutions must respond publicly to shortages and may
revise future statutory guarantees through express prospective law within
constitutional rights. Appropriations, administrative categories and AI
outputs cannot repeal a claim silently. Material election and oversight
changes need prior publication, independent review and prospective effect
that does not reward the current officeholders' next contest; cumulative
changes cannot disable protected democracy by crossing several statutes.

## Territorial status and shared constitutional authority

The founding charter must state whether and how a territorially concentrated
public can require negotiation over autonomy, a new region, boundary change or
independence. It must define eligible initiators, the way competing maps and
overlapping peoples are heard, the legal effect of any ballot, the competent
review forum and the authorization needed for each class of final change.
Neither the first Assembly nor a temporary administrator may invent those
rules after a territorial demand or vote is visible. The C04 candidate protects
a right to initiate a fair process and genuine negotiation; it does not purport
to grant unilateral secession or extinguish any applicable international right.

For a future lawfully founded AIcountry, a proposed ordinary boundary or service
competence change follows enacted statute, affected-scope participation and
review. Constitutionally protected regional competence or national sovereignty
would require the preexisting constitutional amendment route plus an affected-area
authorization defined beforehand. The precise affected-area electorate and any
distinct rights-holder consent are founding choices, not figures this project can
derive from a map. A single regional majority cannot erase minority or Indigenous
rights, while the national government cannot answer a sustained clear demand
with silence, retaliation or an endlessly stalled committee.

A first vote may authorize negotiation only. Any final ballot must display the
negotiated legal text and material conditions, including individual citizenship
options, cross-border service access, land and ecological claims, public assets and
debt, pensions, courts, contracts, defense, foreign obligations and the starting
date. Clear, accessible, balanced information and original count evidence are
needed for both sides and internal dissenters. A failure to agree keeps existing
law and duties in force, requires a public account of disputed terms and leaves
legal challenges and later peaceful proposals open; it cannot manufacture assent.

This candidate is a comparison for a real founding public, not a binding answer
to every people's self-determination claim. It must be reconciled with then-current
law, treaties and the affected people's own forms of decision before adoption.
