Design 9 gave equal candidacy, compensatory elections, worker voice and a

control every viable compensatory-list position despite formal entry rights.

time despite a nominal income and service floor. These are diagnoses of the
working text, not measured social effects. Equal political standing and usable
freedom are value commitments; whether the selected mechanisms improve either
is an empirical and public-choice question.

## Coherent alternatives and selected mechanism

| Question | Selected working design | Serious alternatives and tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|
| Who controls electable Assembly names? | Two-vote compensatory reference: territorial candidate vote, party or qualifying civic-list vote, and optional preference for a list candidate. List votes set proportional entitlement, published preference/fallback rules fill top-up seats, and independents may win territorial seats. Objective filing, published nomination control and fast exclusion remedies protect a practical route to candidacy. | Keep design 9's unspecified list gate, which preserves flexibility but may transfer all electable rank to party officers. Closed lists with enforceable internal democracy can support coherent teams and representation but may still concentrate rank and invite state interference. Candidate-only preferential elections strengthen personal choice but change proportionality and coalition formation. |
| How does the social settlement protect time? | Enacted national hours, rest, scheduling and leave floor; independent bargaining can improve it. Portable pooled care-leave income, job protection where there is a job, distinct self-employed and nonworking claims, and funded replacement staff connect leave to actual use. | Retain the existing generic labor/social floor, avoiding premature promises but leaving time unprotected. Fix one uniform numerical schedule and employer-funded leave, which is simple to state but may fail essential shifts, small firms and workers outside standard employment. Bargaining alone permits contextual choice but leaves unorganized workers exposed. |

The election mechanism does not select its own legal threshold, map, seat count,
overhang rule, nomination cutoff, vacancy formula or public funding amount.
The older democratic candidate's party threshold remains provisional. A party
cannot alter published viable order after the lawful cutoff or unseat a member
through expulsion alone; the state cannot decide ideological fitness. Voters
must be able to understand the distinct ballot effects and obtain an independent,
timely remedy before exclusion becomes irreversible. Preference voting can
reward money, celebrity and patronage, and a civic-list filing rule can become
an incumbent's exclusion tool. Those objections remain live, not averaged away.

The time mechanism does not infer a universal hour limit or benefit rate.
September's pinned labor candidate contains numeric provisions, including a
40-hour ordinary week, a 48-hour upper week, weekly rest and 20 paid annual
days; design 10 does not silently ratify them. Employers, pooled insurance and
the public budget have different liabilities. Paid carers, care recipients,
relatives, self-employed workers and people outside paid work have distinct
interests. Bargaining must not substitute for a national floor or for actual
inspectors and adjudicators. A shorter nominal schedule cannot deliver time
if care, travel, unsafe staffing or an income shortfall consume it.

Both choices draw on the same C06 account: election staff, court time, media
access and public campaign support compete for money and skilled people with
labor inspection, leave income, care places and qualified shift replacements.
Neither source-of-funds labels nor automated processing create capacity.
These costs must be reconciled with essential services, monetary and material
constraints before readiness can be claimed. A prospective first election and
labor transition must preserve existing voting, contractual, wage, pension,
insurance, collective-agreement and care claims under lawful successors.

## Evidence, objections and authority

The supporting comparisons use primary institutional material from
[International IDEA](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/electoral-system-design-database),
[OSCE/ODIHR and the Venice Commission](https://odihr.osce.org/sites/default/files/f/documents/8/1/538473.pdf),
and the [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/ilo-helpdesk-tools-and-resources-business-working-time)
for mechanisms and relevant constraints. They do not demonstrate outcomes for
this unformed country or establish that the combination is best. Source
institutions and AI outputs may share evidence families. New events, contrary
observations and objections need checking before any final decision, even if
the cited pages remain unchanged. The actual prior constitutional and ordinary
law in an adopting jurisdiction has not been identified or reconciled.

An incumbent could still capture candidate entry through district boundaries,
filing burdens, funding, media, courts, ballot printers or the officers who
execute a remedy. Optional preferences could leave party officers in control
when most voters omit them. The full appointment, removal, information, funding
and execution chain must therefore be tested together with protected opposition
and the contested-transfer circuit in C03. A formal judicial right is insufficient
if the court cannot hear and enforce it before ballot finalization. For time,
large firms could evade the floor through contractors; a public employer or
insurer could deny benefits through delay; care leave could shift an impossible
workload onto colleagues or family. Inspection, cost incidence, service access
and enforceable correction must be observed, not inferred from enactment.

**Evidence qualification:** comparative institutional sources and authored
text/logic checks only. **Research selection:** the two conditional arrangements
enter integrated design 10. **Legal authorization:** none. **Operational
readiness:** unknown for election personnel, accessible ballots, count software,
independent courts, campaign funding, inspectors, benefit finance, care workers
and replacement shifts. **Observed outcomes:** none. A research-index change
does not adopt a rule, and parallel AI drafting does not add a mandate.

## Continuity and next bounded action

The affected consumers are C02–C03/C05–C07/C11/C13–C14, the democratic candidate,
membership and continuity reviews, the old labor-protection, insurance and
pension candidates, and any future election, employer, care and fiscal law.
Their prior conclusions do not automatically transfer. A lawful migration
would publish complete ballot and succession rules before the protected cutoff;
audit real nomination and campaign routes; verify non-digital access, count
reproducibility, timely review and enforcement; and preserve an ordinary
election if the replacement is not ready. Labor migration would inventory
existing agreements, accrued leave, wages, employer obligations, insurer funds,
care contracts and pending claims; name receiving actors and fund overlap before
enlarged entitlements commence. Safe testimony and employment records require
protected access and correction, not permanent public exposure.

The next bounded question is whether an affected public in a lawful adopting
jurisdiction prefers this ballot and time package to closed-list or candidate-only
elections and a different work/care allocation, when shown the actual cost and
capture chains. A useful deliverable would be a witnessed first-election filing,
ballot/count, exclusion-remedy and succession exercise, paired with an original
employer/worker/carer/care-recipient account of hours, leave financing and
replacement staffing for one essential sector. The competent public authority
must set scope and budget; none exists for this research project. If that
participation or mandate is unavailable, keep the choices conditional and
receive specific contradictions or rivals. Reopen the design if viable opposition
still depends on a patron, if preferences worsen effective access or representation,
or if the time floor produces unfunded care gaps or loss of income relative to a
lawful alternative. No elapsed deadline supplies consent.
