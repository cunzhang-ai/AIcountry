# Turning a task saving into paid time with care still covered

Design328 · Constructed comparison for [AT18–AT22](../../country/C05/18/05-technology-gains-and-paid-time.md)

A home-care operator changes a scheduling and documentation process.
The trial removes 100 paid task-hours per roster cycle at the same
specified output and quality. In the authored case, independent review
takes 24 hours and correcting errors takes 16. The recurring net is
60, and training uses 20 of those already paid hours in the first
cycle. Only 24 of the remaining 40 can be assigned to the actual
weekend care window with qualified, willing staff. The same workers
keep their regular weekly earnings under each lawful option.

There are 36 additional weekend care-hours to cover, while ordinary
visits retain their existing staff. An independently available and
accepting provider costs 2 illustrative cash units per hour. An extra
30 units are available for the first cycle, of which 6 pay instructor
and training costs separately from the trainees' retained wages.
The new tool's cost is covered on the stipulated existing authority
for this comparison. In a real choice it needs its own gross account.

| First-cycle arrangement | Qualified care and paid release | Additional cash |
|---|---|---:|
| Offer all 40 hours as shorter paid duty | All 36 care-hours require outside receiving, in addition to the 40 hours of release | 78 |
| Assign every net hour to care on paper | Only 24 qualify in the window. Twelve care-hours are uncovered unless a paid alternate is used. | 6 without the alternate |
| Bargain a mixed roster with separate care cover | Assign 24 qualified hours, pay an accepted provider for 12, and deliver 16 hours of real paid release. | 30 |
| Keep the ordinary roster and procure all additional care | Uses a qualified comparison route if it accepts all 36 hours. No change-related training or release. | 72 |

The mixed roster fits the stipulated first-cycle constraints. In a
second equivalent cycle with training complete, and the same qualified
assignments, 36 hours could be offered as paid release if the separate
12 care-hours and their 24 cash units are still covered. That is a
separate qualification. The second-cycle money and workers are not
established by the first roster.

If correction work rises from 16 to 32 hours in the first cycle, net
capacity after training falls to 24. The proposed 16 hours of new
release has no support in that stressed case. Preserve any release
and pay already due, retime only under actual lawful terms, and seek
paid cover or report the unmet obligation. If the separate care team
also fails, 12 care-hours remain uncovered even if other general
staff time is available. A successful trial cannot override consent,
qualification or a due care visit.
