# Person-level needs, work and private control

*Design 58 authored cases; values below are monthly fictional CU, not adequate local prices.*

Assume an adult personal basket of 1,000, child basket 600, housing share
300, 50% withdrawal above a 20% personal-basket earnings disregard, and
10% employee contribution. Individual tax after that contribution uses
0% through 1,000; 10% from 1,000–2,000; 25% from 2,000–5,000; 40% above
5,000. Contributions are restricted insurance funds. The assumptions do
not implement a tax law or price a complete needs basket.

| Situation | Selected calculation | Consequence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Adult with no usable resources | Personal plus housing need 1,300 | Ordinary top-up 1,300, independent of employment or spouse's nominal wealth |
| Gross work income 1,000 | Contribution 100; no personal tax; net 900; taper on 700 | Top-up 950; after-tax resources plus aid 1,850 before housing/personal expenses |
| Other person's account is inaccessible | Count only the claimant's actually usable resources | A shared address or high partner wage does not turn an inaccessible account into the claimant's money |
| All personal components offered in kind | Credit at most 800 in the authored 20% cash-share case, with housing actually supplied | Person-controlled 200 remains; a patron cannot buy away every independent expenditure or political option |
| Child receives 400 safe support | Child need 900 minus actual 400 | Top-up 500; missing owed support is not received cash |
| Older eligible person, basic 1,000 and earned pension 250 | Credit basic plus 200 of earned pension | Top-up 100 plus full pension payments; accrued earnings claim is paid, not cancelled |
| Accessible assets 12,000 | Buffer 3 × 1,300; twelve-month excess draw | Monthly assessed draw 675; the same blocked assets count as zero usable draw, with correction |

Necessary paid support for a child enters the payer's recognized need and
the recipient's received resources once, within the recipient's actual
needs. No second earnings deduction or national receipt is created.
The fixture's support flows cancel between payer and child in the national
resource account. This does not require a worldwide gift register or a
compulsory family dossier; complex unsupported arrangements need protected
ordinary evidence, interim help and independent interpretation.

## A smooth taper does not solve every loss

For fixed recognized needs, wages from zero through 12,000 are compared
with a one-CU increase, under three fixed expense assumptions. The smallest
retained after-cost gain is 0.27 CU. That is a conditional arithmetic result,
not observed employment participation, fair wages or an optimal rate.

A separate program starts charging 600 necessary care cost at gross earnings
2,000. If the floor still uses the previous expense record, moving from
1,999 to 2,000 reduces available resources by 599.595. Timely recognition
of that cost in need restores a 0.405 arithmetic gain, but adds a due
public payment and creates no carer. An adjustment paid late is not timely
protection. Household tax, benefits, care, housing and source-law thresholds
must therefore be compared as one journey, with actual payment and acceptance.

The three-month asset buffer, pension disregard, cash-share case and tax
bands need public/affected review and full costing. The model is silent
on capital-income distribution, employer levies, participation, consumption
prices, service quality, nonclaiming people and safe data administration.
Their absence cannot be turned into zero cost or proof of superiority.
