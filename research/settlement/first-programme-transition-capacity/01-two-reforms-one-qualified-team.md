# Two funded reforms competing for one qualified team

In a constructed first budget, a benefit-payment migration and
a mobility booking change each have money for both preparation
and receiving. Both require the same qualified reconciliation
team. The two changes are optional and their competent authority
may choose different dates. The original benefit, mobility and
review duties already apply.

| Work in the first window | Qualified reconciliation hours | Independent review hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Existing due work and unresolved old cases | 4 | 1 |
| Benefit migration and overlap | 5 | 1 |
| Mobility change and overlap | 4 | 1 |
| Both reforms plus existing work | 13 | 3 |
| Confirmed first-window availability | 10 | 2 |

The roles are distinct. The same source-law priorities and existing
services apply to each option. Assume that benefit migration can
be kept ready for lawful receiving and that delaying it would
leave a documented manual payment failure unresolved for longer.
The old mobility route can continue in this window at its already
funded capacity. Each reform fits alone. Their concurrent release
exceeds confirmed staffing by three reconciliation hours and one
review hour, although cash is available. Overtime that would
violate protected rest, unqualified staff and review by an
implementer are unavailable.

Select benefit migration in the first window, using nine
reconciliation hours and two review hours. Keep mobility
preparation separate where its scope and receiving remain intact.
In the second window reserve four reconciliation hours and one
review hour for the standing baseline and two reconciliation
hours and one review hour for benefit exceptions and handback.
Adding mobility would take ten reconciliation hours and three
review hours. With ten reconciliation hours and two review hours
again available, the mobility start still fails. Obtain one
independently staffed, authorized review hour or keep the
mobility release pending while preserving the old service.
A hypothetical extra review hour has no effect on the first
window's reconciliation deficit. A lawful binding start would
require a competent correction and protection on the original
deadline.

Retain-and-repair preserves both old routes and avoids release
work, while leaving the documented benefit defect to its existing
repair path. Parallel release can be feasible with genuine paid
backfill in both roles. Sequencing buys time and exposes the
exception workload left by the first change. The comparison
does not estimate real staffing or the size of either benefit.

As a separate recruitment test, two units each expect eight
candidate-shifts, with six shared candidates and two unique to
each. At most ten distinct offers can be filled on those
assumptions. If four of the shared shifts are needed for
existing services with no backfill, at most six are available
across the units and at most four to either unit. These are
bounds. Count only confirmed, qualified and accepted assignments.
