# Hidden intake failures and a care deadline

*Design392 · Constructed comparison. Amounts are abstract units.*

An intake contractor serving two councils has stopped recording some
supported oral requests as applications. An agency dashboard counts only
accepted forms. A carer reports the failure on day 0. A known patient
will miss a due visit on day 1 if no qualified service receives the case.
The carer can report the pattern. Sharing the patient's private details
requires consent or a separate lawful authority.

| Path | Result under the stated assumptions |
|---|---|
| Ordinary agency review alone | Staff see only the accepted forms. A person who knows of the omission can still seek immediate AP or court relief. The missing group remains outside the dashboard. |
| Ombuds investigation with no separate urgent route | The common failure can be found later. A later recommendation cannot supply the day 1 visit. |
| Protected referral plus scoped inquiry | With lawful consent and actual acceptance on day 0, the existing care authority spends 4 from its lawful service source on an accepting qualified team. The Ombuds spends 3 from its separate investigation allocation. Review of the pattern continues after the visit. |
| Investigation and service both depend on one pot with 6 available | Joint cost is 7. Record the shortfall of 1. A complaint cannot transfer an investigation appropriation to a care provider, and a hearing cannot fund the gap. |

The inquiry compares protected first-receipt records and contractor logs
with an agency export. In the bounded sample, the agency export contains
six of eight independently verified applications. This identifies two
additional cases for a lawful check. It does not estimate population
coverage or establish either person's eligibility. After ordinary
fair reply, the Ombuds recommends repairing oral intake, reviewing
unrecorded cases and verifying the outcome of those due a service.

The councils must explain their decisions by the response deadline.
If they reject the recommendation with reasons, the disagreement remains
visible and can reach the elected scrutiny docket. If they do not
respond, the court can enforce the statutory response without adopting
the recommendation. If they accept, a software change only proves a
system change. A case remains open until the competent owner determines
the claim and verifies any due relief. A confirmed common implementation
error can trigger LX through its competent duty owner. People retain
their separate remedies and deadlines throughout.
