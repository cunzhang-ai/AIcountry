# IV33–IV38. Sequence construction against the capacity that exists

*Design356 · Part of [public investment](../63-public-investment-and-asset-renewal.md).*

**IV33 — Put public and other demand on the same capacity calendar.**
For the annual national investment budget and before a material public
release, Treasury and affected local budget offices combine regional
demand by trade, stage and usable work window. Include protected
maintenance, binding public projects, regulated utility work and
credible private demand. Separate accepted commitments, authorized
options and projections. Show missing scope and uncertain private
starts explicitly. Match practical travel, qualifications and safe
hours, critical materials or plant, site access and the actual
independent inspection and acceptance window. Treat an annual total,
permit, appropriation or bid as insufficient on its own. The calendar
creates neither a permit nor control over private starts or workers.

**IV34 — Qualify offers against simultaneous calls.** Procurement
officers obtain the proposed delivery chain and accepting availability
for scarce stages. Where two contracts offer the same underlying
subcontractor, specialist or inspection window, establish an actual
non-overlapping schedule or count the conflict. Reconcile owners,
lawful reserve and primary users even where a provider has different
public names. Keep service operators, independent inspectors and
contractors in their own roles. Publish aggregates, uncertainty and
schedule dependencies. Auditors and authorized parliamentary review
may inspect necessary evidence in protected custody. Bidders receive
fair access to common forward demand information. Avoid disclosure of
rival prices, payroll identities or live bid strategy. Use planning,
published statistics and lawful confidential market evidence without
requiring a universal registry of private activity.

**IV35 — Vote for a feasible sequence, not a list of starts.** The
Assembly or competent local council compares simultaneous release,
staging, and a supply-expanding or less resource-intensive rival.
Compare actual service dates, safety, interim losses, reserve,
gross cash, prices, local opportunity costs and affordable exit.
State the protected work, exact optional stages, release dates,
limits and contingency before binding contracts. Authorize
independently useful preparatory work within its own ceiling.
When the same crew can cover only one project, vote on the tradeoff
and publish who waits and what service continues. Treasury reconciles
the account, but the competent elected bodies retain their spending
and delivery choices. A jointly funded timetable takes each required
consent. An unresolved clash may hold an optional release within
the responsible owner's authority. It cannot silently reduce an
existing protection, seize labor or reprioritize a private contract.

**IV36 — Increase capability with funded routes and real lead times.**
Where recurring shortage warrants it, compare paid training with
protected mentor cover, portable verified competence, smaller
competitive contracts, qualified alternate methods and an accountable
public delivery team. Count the capacity removed from origin work,
time to qualification, independent inspection, actual retention and
later work. Give new and small suppliers equal access. A standby
contract buys precisely defined time and capacity. Its cost, scope,
expiry and lawful release are explicit. Use existing labor, safety,
competition and procurement law. No pooled timetable can fix private
prices, share competitors' bid terms, compel work or let a contractor
approve its own safety. AI can compare schedules and flag collisions
using accountable source data. It cannot award, certify or bind work.

**IV37 — Recalculate on new evidence, then follow the service.** Before
the last useful point to change a stage, reconcile the actual crew,
materials, inspection, usable cash and continuing services. An
earlier private start, insolvency, unavailable inspector or tighter
seasonal window can reopen a result. Use prior authorized repair and
safe alternatives. Hold only optional stages whose conditions have
failed. For binding work, apply its lawful performance, replacement
or exit route, including claims and gross cost. Report services
delayed, affected people, failed inspections and original dates.
Verify actual independent acceptance and staffed service before
crediting an opening. At a later release, use the capacity still
available after prior use and recovery. Recheck the next budget.

**IV38 — Keep the schedule challengeable through changes of control.**
Provide opposition, workers, affected users and smaller suppliers
an accessible route to contest omissions, unrealistic availability
or exclusion before the binding decision. Use existing audit, PE and
court powers within their scope. Publish the resolution or unresolved
risk alongside the voted sequence. A successor office inherits the
approved version, exact reservations, changed dates and accrued
obligations with funded custody. Reopen if delay shifts harm to a
weaker locality, construction supply changes materially or a simpler
service route protects the same needs at lower full cost.
