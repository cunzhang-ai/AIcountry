# Compare timed support and recovery within one term

Two learners each have an operative award for two matched support
sessions every week over a three-week comparison. All four sessions
are due in suitable windows. The first budget already funds those
four ordinary slots each week. An ordinary worker becomes unavailable
for two first-week sessions and one second-week session. Any ordinary
provider invoice savings settle after the second week. Assume all
remaining ordinary sessions are usable and received. Separately, one
extra third-week session can help address a first-week learning gap.
The learner, educator and reviewer judge that it helps with the gap but
cannot recreate the original opportunity. No historical observation is
used as a scenario input.

The region can supply up to two suitable reserve sessions in the first
week and one in the second. Protect the existing work of other learners.
Each used reserve session costs one budget unit, with its full standby
cost already included in the scenario's recurring budget. A qualified
third-week recovery session also costs one. A finite first-week bridge
of two units is already authorized. A further lawful one-unit ordinary
source is usable at week two, and a separate one-unit recovery source is
usable at week three when a gap exists. Funds are eligible only for the
stated scope. Later settlement can repay the bridge under a separately
approved plan.

| Choice | Current support received by week | Remaining exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary commissioning only | 2, 3, 4 | Three missed sessions. One later recovery session may help. |
| Regional reserve, two units of bridge only | 4, 3, 4 | One missed session. Unusable later savings cannot support week two. |
| Local owner plus paid reserve and usable second source | 4, 4, 4 | No missing current session in this case. No new recovery session is due. |
| Same paid reserve with a one-slot first-week capacity limit | 3, 4, 4 | One missed current session. One later recovery session may help. |

A directly staffed regional service is a structural rival. If it can
provide four suitable sessions each week, with an accessible local
site and independent review at a competitive full cost, it can also
cover this calendar. The authored case supplies neither comparative
cost nor evidence on learner preference for that arrangement. The
selected split preserves a viable school relationship and makes regional
capacity usable during shortages. It would lose its advantage if the
local route routinely fails or the regional service provides better
usable support through the whole year.

The example tests timing and constrained capacity. It does not estimate
real staffing, rates or effects. Each actual district budget must state
cash, usable provider hours and what other work its sources displace.
